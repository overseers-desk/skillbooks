# Test 20: where the candidate stands on a fact, and a way of presenting them to avoid

- Date: 28 November 2026
- Model: Sonnet 5.5, a fresh session started outside the repository for each round, with no context
- Under test: `sales-hire/hire-methodology.md`, the format of the candidate file under "What a run produces": `include` with `wanted`, `by-outcome` and `held-back`; `given-when`; a ruling written as a `pitfall` with `a-way-round`; and the must-not-conclude sentence that H closes with
- Rounds: two. The method's text was the same in both; the first prompt gave no figures, so two of its entries could not be written whole

## The prompt of round two

> You keep one candidate's file for her job applications, and you also write her applications, under the method in `sales-hire/` (read `hire-methodology.md` there; read nothing else in that repository and write nothing anywhere).
>
> Part one. She said three things. For each, show the entry or ruling you would write in the candidate file, with its fields, by the method as written, and quote the sentence of the method you relied on.
>
> 1. "I earn 140,000 now. Leave that off, unless the form asks what I earn."
> 2. "Wherever the lab is mentioned, I want my 12 patents from there with it."
> 3. "Don't make me sound like a consultant. Readers then think I never stayed to run anything. One draft was fine where it said I 'ran the plant for six years'."
>
> Part two. You are now writing a page for one post. The file holds what you wrote for the three items. The post's application form has no question about salary. The page mentions the lab once, in a list of employers. Say in a few lines what the page does with each of the three, and what you do with the words "ran the plant for six years".
>
> Finish with anything in the method's text that left you unsure, quoting the words that were unclear.

Round one had item 1 as "Leave my salary off, unless the form asks what I expect." and item 2 as "Wherever the lab is mentioned, I want the number of patents with it."

| Item | Standard answer | Round one | Round two | Gap |

## What the agent was unsure of

- Whether a bare name in a list of employers is "wherever its subject is", and whether a wanted fact outranks keeping a passage at one grain. Minor: in both rounds it took the escape the text gives, the reason in the register.
- Whether a telling the candidate called fine may be reused word for word. It read "the words on a page are the writer's" as no. Minor.
- A fact entry needs `value` or `from`, so a wanted or held-back fact whose figure the candidate has not given cannot be written whole; round one wrote a question to her in `open`. Minor.
- Whether a remark that is partly about a draft becomes a pitfall, a case, or both. Minor, and outside the passages under test.

## Edits made in this session

`include` says where the candidate stands on a fact reaching a page, as `wanted`, `by-outcome` or `held-back`, and a held-back fact may carry `given-when`. A ruling on a way of presenting the candidate is written as a `pitfall` that says what it leads a reader to conclude, with `a-way-round` as a list of examples. H's must-not-conclude sentence is filled from what the file's pitfalls lead a reader to conclude.
