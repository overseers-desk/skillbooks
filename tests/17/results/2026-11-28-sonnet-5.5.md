# Test 17: what a writer adds to the candidate file where it is silent

- Date: 28 November 2026
- Model: Sonnet 5.5, one fresh agent with no context for each round
- Under test: `sales-hire/hire-methodology.md`, the sentence on a gap no default covers in "I — Interview", and the sentences on defaults under "What a run produces"
- Rounds: two

## The prompt

> You are writing one job application for a candidate under the method in `sales-hire/` (read `hire-methodology.md` there; read nothing else in that repository and write nothing anywhere). The candidate is away and cannot be asked.
>
> Part-way through, you meet four things her candidate file says nothing about. For each, say in one or two lines what you do, and exactly what, if anything, you add to her candidate file: which part of the file, and which fields you fill. Quote the sentence of the method you relied on.
>
> 1. She once ran a small side business. Her record mentions it, but nothing says whether it may appear on a page.
> 2. Whether the summary at the top is written as three labelled lines or as one paragraph.
> 3. Her record gives two different figures for the size of the team she led at one post, 12 and 15, and nothing says which stands.
> 4. Whether her languages are listed before or after her education.
>
> Finish with anything in the method's text that left you unsure, quoting the words.

## The reply, and the standard answer

| Item | Standard answer | Round 1 | Round 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, whether a venture may appear | Decided by the writer; one entry in `defaults`; a register row | One default, in `defaults` or in the subject's `rulings` | One entry in `defaults` with `about`; no `include` set; a register row |
| 2, the form of the summary | Decided for this reader; nothing added to the file | Nothing added | Nothing added |
| 3, which of two figures stands | Decided by the writer; one entry in `defaults`; a register row | One default, in that post's `rulings` with a note | One entry in `defaults` with `about` naming the post; the entries holding her words left alone |
| 4, the order of two lines | Decided for this reader; nothing added to the file | Nothing added | Nothing added |

Round 1 ran with "A gap about the candidate that no default covers" in place and showed the critical gap that was left: the text sent a writer's own choice into a subject's `rulings`, among the candidate's decisions. Round 2 ran after the text said a default is in `defaults` whatever it concerns.

## What the agent was unsure of

- The table gives `defaults` no field for "not yet confirmed"; the agent read being in `defaults` as that mark. Minor.
- Nothing says which of two equally good sources stands. Minor, and outside the sentences under test.
- "Whether a low-confidence assumption about the candidate may go on the page is one of the candidate file's defaults" points at a default that may not exist yet. Outside the sentences under test.

## Edits made in this session

The sentence on a gap no default covers says "a gap about the candidate". A default is in `defaults` whatever it concerns, with the subject it concerns in `about`, and `rulings` hold what the candidate decided; the row `defaults` says "about themselves" and no longer limits the part to what concerns several subjects or none.
