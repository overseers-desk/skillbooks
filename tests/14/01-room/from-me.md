# About me, for the job search

I'm Ingrid Solberg. I've been a nurse for sixteen years, the last six as deputy ward manager on a 28-bed acute medical ward at a district hospital. I want my own ward, or a clinical lead post in the community.

## How I'd tell it to someone hiring a ward manager

"I've run the ward for most of the last two years in all but name. My manager was seconded to the new-build project, and I did the rota, the budget meetings and the complaints. In that time agency spend on the ward fell by about a third, because I rebuilt the rota around the people we had and stopped booking agency for gaps we could see coming. Falls came down as well, after I moved the handover to the bedside. I'd like to do it with the title."

## What I want

- A ward manager post, or a clinical lead post in a community team. Not another deputy post.
- Within about forty minutes' drive of home. I won't move. I live in Ashby: Northfield is 25 minutes away, Calder Bridge about 35, Westport over an hour.
- Pay: I'm on £46,000 now. Below that is a no. Anything above £52,000 would be a real step.
- What matters most to me is what the post would teach me and where it leads: I want to be a matron in five years. Pay comes second.
- I look after my father two afternoons a week, Tuesdays and Thursdays. A post that needs me on site five full days every week, or regular nights, doesn't work.

## What I don't want on paper

- My father's care. It's nobody's business at application stage.
- That I applied for the manager post on my own ward last year and didn't get it.
