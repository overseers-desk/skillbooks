# CART — Preparing a Matter for the Event It Is Heading To

**Phases:** Cite, Argue, Render, Tender

**Direction:** Inbound and adversarial. A body of material has accumulated around a dispute. Someone has to walk into a hearing, a mediation or a conference able to answer anything the other side says, from one thing they can read while standing up.

CART governs how that material is organised and how the thing they carry in is built. It ends at that artefact and its rebuilds. What happens to the material after the event is outside it.

## The problem

Preparation accumulates. Each session adds what it found, and the record grows into several long documents that nobody reads before the event, least of all the person who has to speak. The failure is not disorder. It is surplusage: everything that was ever learned is written down somewhere, often twice, and the one thing needed in the room is buried in it.

Two habits produce it. Filing by subject gives facts, claims and operational notes their own documents, and nothing in that arrangement says which way a fact should travel between them, so the same fact settles in two places and both drift. And the instinct to be complete treats every fact as earning its place by existing.

## The four phases

### Cite

Every assertion carries where it comes from. Nothing is stated bare.

This runs at two levels and they are the same discipline. The register of material points at sources instead of retelling them, so it stays thin. The artefact points in the coordinates the forum itself uses, so a line can be spoken and the decision-maker can turn to it while it is spoken.

The forum's coordinates are supplied by the matter, not by this methodology. A tribunal may paginate a court book, number exhibits, letter annexures, or accept a party's own schedule of documents as the reference. Whatever it is, the artefact speaks it, because a reference the other side cannot follow is an assertion the other side can challenge.

A citation does two jobs at once, and the second is the reason to be thorough about the first. It holds up what our side says. It also impeaches what their side says, because the page being pointed at is often their own earlier document contradicting their present position. The same act defends and attacks.

### Argue

Decide what is run, what carries it, and what is held back.

Held material is not duplicate material. Much of what a matter accumulates is kept available and deliberately unused: the documents tendered only if a door opens, the answers prepared for arguments the other side may never run. A document sitting untendered is doing its job.

A reduction pass that treats anything unused as redundant will cut exactly this material. Before removing something as a duplicate, establish that it is a second copy of a fact rather than an option being kept open.

Held means not served. It says nothing about whether a document was ever communicated to the other side, and the two questions have different answers and different consequences.

### Render

The artefact is built from the two documents above it and is never where a fact is born.

The test is mechanical. Ask where a fact would be maintained if the artefact were deleted. If the answer is nowhere, it has been authored into the wrong place and belongs upstream.

Where something on the artefact is wrong, the fix goes upstream and the artefact is rebuilt. Editing it in place is how it becomes a second home for the fact.

Selection is adversarial rather than archival. Material earns its place by persuading the decision-maker, by answering something the other side will say or produce, or by being an inconsistency that can be put to them. Material that does none of those stays upstream, and the artefact says so in its own fine print, because otherwise a later session sets out to make it complete and rebuilds the long documents it replaced.

### Tender

What is actually put before the forum, and the trigger that releases each held item.

Every held document carries, in one line, the claim it answers and the moment it would be produced. A held item with no such line is mislabelled, because it cannot be found at the moment it is needed and will not be.

The artefact carries the trigger and not the reasoning behind the hold. The person in the room needs to know which document answers which claim and when to ask; the exposure behind each decision stays upstream.

## The three documents

Preparation sits in three documents. The number carries the order, and the order is derivation rather than chronology.

**1** registers the material: what is held, where it lives, and what each item is. Thin by design. When it starts to read as a narrative it has become a briefing under a new name, which is the failure to watch for.

**2** decides: what is argued, what carries it, and what is held back with its trigger.

**3** says how the artefact is built from the other two, and how to rebuild it when the matter moves.

The artefact carries no number, being the output of the three rather than a fourth slot.

There is no 4. The logic of preparing a matter does not change between matters, so a later one updates these three rather than adding to them. A fourth document means something was misfiled.

1 feeds 2, 2 feeds the artefact, and nothing flows back. A fact's home is wherever it enters, and every appearance downstream is a rendering rather than a second home. This is the one-home rule applied, not an exception to it: the rule binds authoring rather than rendering, and what it forbids is two documents maintaining the same fact with no derivation between them.

## Writing posture

Each document reads as though written in one pass by someone who already knew the end state. A reader should not be able to tell which parts arrived first. Traces of how a file grew are removed: currency notes, phase scaffolding, and lines recording when a question was settled.

Dates that are facts in the matter stay, and there will be many. The distinction is between when a document reached us and when a thing happened. A sweep that strips dates by pattern destroys the second kind with the first.

An order or notice that governs the proceeding is not accretion, however stale its deadlines. It is what a phrase like "the directions" points at, and it is registered in 1.

The artefact names people rather than roles, wherever a name exists, because a name is what gets spoken and recognised under pressure. The exception is a non-party whose name adds nothing and whose privacy is worth more than the precision.

## Retiring a document

Content moves first and the file goes second, in separate commits, each naming where its sections went. A reader of the history should be able to confirm that an information point was carried rather than dropped.

## Model allocation

Cite and Tender are Sonnet-tier: checking assertions against sources, and maintaining a register with triggers. Argue and Render are Opus-tier, turning on judgement about what persuades and what to leave out.
