You are producing a SPAR-P research profile of one media creator for the 2026 Reputation campaign. That campaign raises the public profile of the campaign's subject, a technology founder who built an AI system that runs his real multi-line business (an agri-tourism venue) end to end and now offers it to other organisations, ahead of a senior AI-leadership search. A media-creator profile judges whether this creator's channel is a good venue to feature that founder as a guest or subject, and rates the value to us.

Output file (create or overwrite): @@OUTPUT_PATH@@

CONTACT (roster row):
- name: @@NAME@@
- organisation / channel: @@ORG@@
- role: @@ROLE@@
- channel type: @@TYPE@@
- linkedin: @@LINKEDIN@@
- source_url (why they were included): @@SOURCE_URL@@

HOW TO WORK — research briefly, then write. You will NOT see the full catalogue, and you do not need to. A few broad searches, then write the file. Do not exhaust the context hunting for more examples; three to five concrete examples are plenty.

SEGMENT — media creator. A valid member runs their own channel (podcast, YouTube channel, newsletter, or blog) covering AI in business, AI agents and automation, entrepreneurship and founders, business automation, fintech, or crypto and web3, and features founder guests or founder stories. Global; English primary, Mandarin acceptable.

PRIMARY EVIDENCE IS THE CATALOGUE, NOT LINKEDIN. The evidence of what this creator covers is their own output: episode or video titles and the guests they feature. Build the relevance judgement from what the searches reveal about that output, and quote the real titles and guest names you find. Use LinkedIn only for identity, never as evidence of what they cover.

TOOLS — all via the Bash tool. Your research budget is SMALL; work from search snippets.
1. Web search (your main, usually only, source): /usr/local/ai/spar/bin/brave-search "query" 5
   Use two or three BROAD queries: the show name with "guests" or "episodes"; the host name with the campaign themes (AI, founders, automation); the host name with "AI" to find their own stated stance. Snippets carry titles and guest names. Do NOT search for guests one at a time — that burns the budget and fills the context before you can write. Avoid apostrophes inside the quoted query (write Lennys, not Lenny's); they break the search wrapper.
2. WebFetch (optional, usually returns almost nothing): these channel pages are JavaScript-rendered and come back near-empty. Try at most one, only if a search points at a plainly readable page, and never retry it.
3. LinkedIn (identity only, optional): browser-serialiser linkedin.com/parse-profile <slug>
   The session may be logged out; if it returns a login wall or nothing, skip it and note that in Verification corrections.

HARD STOP AND WRITE: after at most 4 tool calls in total, stop researching and WRITE the file, even if your picture is partial. Writing the file is the only success; an unwritten profile is a failure. A short, honest profile built from a few searches, quoting the real episode titles and guest names you found, is a complete success.

VERIFICATION RULES:
- Quote only titles and guests you actually saw in a result. Do not invent episodes, guests, subscriber or download counts, or a host thesis. If a reach number is not verified, say so.
- Do not infer the host's OWN stance on our themes from a guest having voiced it. A guest on a theme shows editorial range (signal 2); the host arguing it shows the host's stance (signal 3). Record which of signal 2 and signal 3 you actually found.
- Treat directory or estimate reach (Rephonic, ListenNotes, Social Blade) as unverified.

RATING — value to us, reach-gated. star_rating (1-5) = reach into OUR audiences (startup investors and founders; the broad AI-in-business audience) TIMES relevance from three signals, in rising strength:
  (1) topic: covers AI in business, AI agents and automation, or the owner-run-business operating world (SMB operations, hospitality, tourism);
  (2) guests: features founders, operators, or exited founders;
  (3) stated interest: the host's own expressed appetite for these themes.
Calibrate conservatively. On-theme but low-reach, or high-reach but audience-mismatched (general consumer or general tech with no founder, investor, or operator overlap), is NOT a 4 or 5. A mid-size channel with exactly the right audience can outrank a larger general one. Reserve 5 for strong reach into our audiences AND squarely on-domain with the right guest type. Rate on VERIFIED reach and fit, not on how much data you gathered; more data found does not by itself raise the rating. Justify the number against reach times the three signals.
