## What they have said publicly

**On open source supply chain security (Wilson Center blog, 16 April 2024):** Authored "How to Secure Open Source Software: The Dilemma of the XZ Utils Backdoor." Key arguments: (a) Open source is not inherently insecure -- "transparency" enabled Andres Freund's discovery of the backdoor; (b) approximately "96% of studied codebases contained open source code," making a pivot away from OSS economically unfeasible; (c) the XZ incident is "a heroic tale of averting a major crisis -- but also a cautionary tale." Recommendations: memory-safe coding per CISA's Secure by Design program, enhanced code reviews, corporate responsibility (citing the National Cybersecurity Strategy's call to shift "the burden for cybersecurity onto organizations most capable" of reducing risks), implementation of SBOMs as industry standard, active community support through code contributions, funding, or hiring maintainers, and a partnership model among open source communities, private enterprises, and federal agencies.

**On SBOM specifically (Wilson Center blog, 16 April 2024):** Recommended "Implementation of Software Bills of Materials (SBOMs) as industry standard" and "Increased vetting of open source code" as concrete corporate responsibility measures. This is the closest she has come to discussing provenance and attestation infrastructure.

**On cryptocurrency cybersecurity (Wilson Center, 31 March 2025, co-authored with Madison Binder):** "The Bybit Heist: What Happened & What Now?" -- analysis of the February 2025 North Korean hack stealing ~$1.5 billion in ETH, highlighting "the ongoing need for more security in the crypto industry."

**On AI-generated CSAM (Wilson Center article, co-authored):** "Combatting AI-Generated CSAM" -- reviewed misconceptions around AI-generated child sexual abuse material, its harms, and mitigating solutions. Part of a broader research portfolio feeding into "The Deepfake Files" educational game.
