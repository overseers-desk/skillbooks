## Institutional context

FIL is a structurally complex insurance-program buyer for its size. Five risk vectors stand out:

1. **Multi-country footprint.** Operations or supply across NZ, AU, China, Vietnam, Thailand, USA. Even if not all are own-entity subsidiaries, sourcing/manufacturing/agent presence in five additional jurisdictions creates marine cargo, contingent BI (supplier failure), and territorial coverage questions that single-country brokers routinely under-engineer.
2. **Heavy product liability.** FIL designs and manufactures load-restraint and dunnage products that are deployed in transit by third parties — failure of a restraint system in cargo movement implicates the manufacturer in resulting load damage, vehicle damage, or personal injury claims. PL exposure is structurally larger than for a typical packaging distributor.
3. **Blue-chip client concentration.** Named major clients include NZ Steel, BlueScope Steel, NZ Post, CourierPost. Loss of any one is a material BI / revenue-concentration event; large-customer indemnity terms in supply contracts (signed upstream of any insurance review) can also drive PL/contractual liability uplifts.
4. **Custom manufacturing and bespoke fabrication.** Site mentions cast rubber, polyurethane, engineered plastics, R&D — the production assets imply property and machinery breakdown exposure, plus IP/PI on the custom designs.
5. **Sustainability product line (mycelium, PHA, woodchip from waste).** Newer materials with less actuarial history; product recall and PL coverage adequacy is a likely gap for an incumbent broker who priced the program before these lines were added.

For a 35-year owner-operated family business of this footprint, the incumbent broker is almost certainly long-standing and the program may have grown by accretion rather than by structured review. This is precisely the buyer profile NRS's "director-led, multi-sector program advisory, complex program structuring" pitch is built for.
