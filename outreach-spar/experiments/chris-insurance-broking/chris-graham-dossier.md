## 9. Positioning gaps and open questions

This section documents the tension between NRS's different public-facing representations, as flagged in the campaign overview stub at `/home/weiwu/code/aesop/nrs-overview.md`.

### The broking-versus-consulting identity gap

Chris's LinkedIn headline identifies him as a "corporate insurance specialist" engaged in "complex program structuring and global insurer placement." This is unambiguously broking language — program structuring is the process of designing multi-tower insurance programmes for large clients, and placement refers to placing risk with insurers. The headline implies that NRS is a corporate insurance brokerage with global market access and specialist placement capability.

The NRS website makes no such claim. The four service lines are:
1. Risk Management & Governance Consulting
2. Property Valuation & Advisory
3. Tax Depreciation Schedules
4. Business Continuity & Operational Planning

None of these is an insurance product. Risk Management & Governance Consulting is a non-transactional advisory service. Property Valuation & Advisory and Tax Depreciation Schedules are specialist professional services typically provided by valuers, quantity surveyors, or specialist consultancies (services 2 and 3 are unusual additions to an insurance broker's offering and suggest either a referral or white-labelling arrangement, or genuine in-house capability — which is not clarified). Business Continuity & Operational Planning is adjacent to risk advisory but again does not involve insurance placement.

The AR/AFSL structure confirms that broking is legally authorised. The question is not whether Chris *can* place insurance — he can, under Advisernet's AFSL — but whether NRS's current client work is primarily broking (as LinkedIn implies), consulting (as the website implies), or a mix that neither source describes completely.

A CFO receiving an A-phase message from Chris needs to know which pitch is real, because the value proposition is entirely different:
- If NRS is a corporate broker, the pitch is: "We can restructure your insurance programme, get better terms from markets your current broker can't access, and you'll deal directly with a director."
- If NRS is a risk consultant, the pitch is: "We can review your risk governance framework, produce a property valuation, and design your BCP."
- If both are offered, the message needs to sequence them — typically broking opens the door for fee-based consulting that follows.
