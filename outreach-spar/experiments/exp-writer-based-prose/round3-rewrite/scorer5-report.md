## 1. Rubric

1. **Unit.** One sentence = one scored item; the subject line counts as a sentence; greeting and signature block are excluded; a showtime list counts as one sentence together with its introducing clause; `words` = every whitespace-separated token in the file.
2. **Nowhere (M1).** A definite or anaphoric expression whose referent the email has not introduced by that point — offer machinery ("the booking link", "the code", "the booking details"), a prior exchange ("Re:", an earlier note), an arrangement never set up ("the bus", "the hub", "the room", "the notice a bus run needs"), or a bare "it" with no antecedent; the reader's own possessions (their newsletter, their Facebook page, grandchildren under a restrictive modifier) do not count; each distinct referent counted once, at first use.
3. **Sentence scores (M2).** 0–5 in reading order: *confusing* = the reader cannot tell what is meant; *surprising* = the reader wonders why they are being told this; *interesting* = the reader is glad to have read it. Means reported to 2 dp.
4. **False (M3).** Recipient picked/chosen/one of a selected few, or seats "held in your name"/"set aside for" them; tickets said not on sale or not available publicly; a season end other than 4 October; a free offer outside 24–27 September.
5. **Unsupported (M3).** Any claim with no source either way — a previous email to this recipient, facts about the recipient's own organisation (its meetings, sub-groups, geography), "we are not advertising this offer"; distances/times that *are* in the sources (30 minutes from central Gold Coast, foot of Tamborine Mountain, showtimes, parking) count as supported; the sender's name and role are never counted.
6. **Sender-side (M4).** A sentence whose content is why the offer is made, how this recipient came to be written to, what is not on offer or not for sale, or the sender's own situation; greeting, sign-off and organisation-introducing sentences excluded; reader-side (what you get, what you must do, by when, from whom) not counted.
