## 1. Rubric

1. **nowhere** — count every expression that points at a thing as already shared (a link, a code, a bus, a list, an outing, a manager, a page, a meeting, "the cafe/horses/alpacas") which the email has not introduced by that point; introduction later in the email does not excuse the earlier use; definite and indefinite presupposition both count; the reader's own obvious possessions (their members, their families, their club) do not.
2. **confusing (0–5)** — 0 = plain on one reading; 2 = needs a re-read or a small inference; 4–5 = the reader cannot tell what is meant, what is referred to, or what to do.
3. **surprising (0–5)** — 0 = expected at this point in a cold email; 2 = an aside the reader did not need; 4–5 = the reader stops and wonders why they are being told this at all.
4. **interesting (0–5)** — 0 = nothing a recipient would act on or remember; 3 = a concrete, usable fact; 5 = the thing that would make them reply.
5. **claims** — false = contradicts the sources under the fixed rules (picked/chosen/held in their name; not on sale or unavailable to the public; season end other than 4 October; free offer outside 24–27 September); unsupported = asserted with no source either way (undocumented distances, "farm", prior correspondence, "not advertised anywhere", claims about the recipient's meetings or audience); sender's name and role never counted; a claim repeated verbatim in one email counts once.
6. **sender-side** — sentences whose content is the sender's side: why the offer is being made, how the recipient came to be written to, what is not on offer or not for sale, the sender's own situation or past letters; instructions, entitlements, times and deadlines for the reader are reader-side; greeting, sign-off, signature block and the one sentence introducing the organisation are not counted. Subject line, greeting and signature block are each scored as one sentence.
