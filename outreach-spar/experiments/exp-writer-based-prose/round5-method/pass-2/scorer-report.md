## 1. Rubric (applied unchanged to all 16)

```
1. Unit = subject-line text + each body sentence, in reading order; salutation and signature block excluded from scoring, sentence count and word count.
2. Nowhere: a definite/demonstrative expression whose referent the email has not yet introduced at that point; recipient-owned things (their club, village, group, members, staff, readers, segment) resolve, as do proper names, common-calendar definites ("the school holidays"), sender-possessives ("our lawn"), and close bridges (tickets→seats, shows→the season); later introduction does not rescue it.
3. Sentence scores 0-5 each for confusing / surprising ("why am I being told this?") / interesting; report means and the count of sentences at surp 4-5.
4. Claims false: picked/chosen/selected recipient, seats held in their name, tickets or shows not on sale / not available to the public, "opening week is set aside / not advertised" (all fourteen are on public sale on Eventbrite and Oztix), season end other than 4 Oct, free offer outside 24-27 Sep. Unsupported: no source either way.
5. Unsupported by rule: any distance or travel time not in the sources (30 min from central Gold Coast, hinterland, foot of Tamborine are supported); prior-contact narratives; "these tickets/seats are not advertised"; tree ages. Sender name and role never counted.
6. Sender-side: the sentence's main clause is why the sender offers it, how the recipient came to be written to, what is not on offer/not for sale/not possible, or the sender's own situation; telling the reader what they get, must do, by when or from whom is reader-side; greeting, sign-off and the opening organisation-introduction sentences are excluded.
```
