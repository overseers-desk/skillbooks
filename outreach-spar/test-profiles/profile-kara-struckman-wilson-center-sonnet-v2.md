## What they have said publicly

**On XZ Utils and the dilemma of open source security (Wilson Center blog, April 2024 — https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/how-secure-open-source-software-dilemma-xz-utils-backdoor):** Struckman argued that open source software remains trustworthy and valuable despite the XZ backdoor, and that the open nature of the code base was precisely what allowed the backdoor to be discovered before catastrophic damage. She identified the structural dilemma: nearly all modern systems depend on OSS, substitution is impractical, yet the software is maintained by unpaid volunteers who cannot be expected to sustain industrial-grade security review indefinitely. Her specific recommendations were: (1) implement manual or automatic code review of all submitted changes; (2) establish SBOMs (software bills of materials) as industry standards; (3) companies benefiting from OSS should actively support their open source communities by contributing code, funding maintainers, or employing them; (4) support liability frameworks that shift cybersecurity responsibility onto "organizations that are most capable and best-positioned to reduce risks" (a direct reference to shifting burden from volunteer maintainers to enterprises); (5) promote adoption of memory-safe coding languages. She is the sole author.

**On infrastructure concentration risk — the CrowdStrike outage (Wilson Center video and blog post, July 2024):** Struckman covered the CrowdStrike outage for the Wilson Center, identifying three key points: the importance of rigorous testing before software updates are pushed; the need to diversify infrastructure so society is not reliant on a single source for software or product; and what CrowdStrike's response demonstrated about incident communications. The diversification point is a direct parallel to OSS supply chain concentration risk — a second data point connecting her to the dependency-chain vulnerability theme.

**On cybersecurity in the cryptocurrency industry — the Bybit heist (co-authored with Madison Binder, article linked from LinkedIn, March 2025 — "The Bybit Heist: What Happened & What Now"):** "On February 21, 2025 North Korean hackers executed the largest cryptocurrency heist to date, stealing approximately $1.5 billion worth of ETH. This incident highlights the ongoing need for more security in the crypto industry." Co-authored with Madison Binder. The piece covers the Bybit heist mechanics and broader crypto cybersecurity context. Not directly relevant to the foundation's themes but confirms her continued engagement with cyber threat analysis after leaving Wilson Center.

**On AI-generated CSAM (Wilson Center article, February 2025 — "Combatting AI-Generated CSAM"):** Policy analysis of misconceptions, harms, complicating facets, and mitigation solutions around AI-generated child sexual abuse material. **On cyber policy under a second Trump administration (co-authored with Madison Binder, December 2024 — "Expectations and Priorities: Cyber Policy Predictions Under a Second Trump Administration"):** Analysis of likely policy shifts across CISA scope, offensive operations, space security, and AI integration in government cybersecurity.
