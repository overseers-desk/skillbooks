# SAGE invariants

Hard rules the rest of the methodology must not contradict. When a procedure, brief, template, or example here disagrees with an invariant, the invariant wins and the other is the bug to fix.

## I1. A parameter absent from the decisions table is design freedom

**Test:** for any "fixed", "decided", "never" or "always" in a brief, template, or derived document, can you point to the decisions-table row or dated ruling behind it? No row, no hardening.

What fails the test: a brief that fixes a group size the owner left open; a summary that upgrades a recommendation to a rule; a later run citing an earlier run's unruled default as policy.

**Enforcement:** every frame line in an arm brief carries RULED with its card or DEFAULT with its finding, and an arm reads an unmarked line as a default (`sage-G-game.md`, the arms); the reviewer gate checks briefs and sheets against the table; the phase documents state the rule at the point of temptation (brief-writing and absorption); this file is the tie-breaker.

## I2. Every kept design element carries a demand justification

**Test:** for each element in an arm sheet, absorbed sheet, or definition, is there a citation to a finding number, a taxonomy prevalence, a named comparable's practice, or stated buyer behaviour? "We have it" or "the corpus mentions it nearby" fails.

**Enforcement:** the arm briefs demand the citation inline; the reviewer strikes uncited elements; the absorption redline preserves each element's citation.

## I3. Judges receive only the reviewer cut

**Test:** did any judged sheet reach a judge without passing the reviewer gate? One yes voids the round.

Why: an unreviewed sheet wins on smuggled format, and the smuggled format then reads as ruled. The round that taught this was voided and re-run.

**Enforcement:** the game procedure routes sheets through the reviewer before blinding; the blind key lists the reviewed variants as the only sources; the review log is part of the round's record.

## I4. Quotable claim text carries no provenance

**Test:** read each numbered claim paragraph alone: does it contain a citation, a ratification note, a count of judges, or any grounding apparatus? If yes it fails; grounding lives in the internal table beside it.

Why: downstream agents lift claim sentences whole into outward messages, and internal apparatus travels with them.

**Enforcement:** the claims template separates claim text from grounding table; the reviewer gate reads the claims; outreach-side review catches leakage as a backstop.

## I5. Methodology documents name no run's product

**Test:** grep this folder for the product nouns of any completed run; zero hits. The methodology speaks in kinds (operator, offering, buyer, comparable); a run speaks in instances.

Why: a product noun surviving here rebinds the method to one market, and the next run inherits a frame it should not have.

**Enforcement:** the grep is run at authoring time against each finished run's vocabulary; this file is the tie-breaker when an example seems too useful to generalise. Abstract the example, keep its lesson.

## I6. The study reads demand and polices no one

**Test:** for any permission, licence, cover, role, record or piece of equipment the operator lacks today, does it reach the study only as the cost of delivering the option that needs it? The study reads each option as on sale tomorrow on the operator's own website; what selling it would take is stated and priced beside it ("this option needs every handler to hold a working-with-children check" passes), never a caution, a fault, a question to the owner about what staff or records hold today, a reason to shape an option around the requirement, or a bar on the option. The study holds alike for an offering never launched, one that may never launch, and one already on sale with a regulatory problem. And for any fact class about third-party standing (registration, licensing, accreditation, authenticity, awards) collected or asked as a study question, can you point to evidence that buyers or their intermediaries consult it when choosing? Without that evidence it appears only as source-list provenance, never as a question the study answers.

What fails the test: a survey verifying an operator's registration before reading its buyer appeal; striking an unregistered comparable from a frame; a codebook variable on official standing with no buyer-behaviour citation behind it. An operator trading without the right permissions is still a comparable, and its bookings are still demand: the survey describes the market, it does not police it. A card whose recommendation waits on an insurer, a review that lists a missing licence as a fault to fix, or an option withheld on a broker's letter fails the same way on the operator's side.

**Enforcement:** the methodology states the on-sale-tomorrow premise and names launch-checklist substitution among its failure modes; a ruling stands on demand alone, with the card's cost line beside each option; the survey procedure derives frame sourcing from buyer choice; the codebook author receives this rule in the drafting brief; the frame review strikes status-mark questions with no buyer-evidence leg; this file is the tie-breaker. First case: a run's survey opened with heritage-register verification no buyer asks for; the owner ruled it out on 2026-09-07. On the operator's side: a party run listed missing insurance and blue-card records as faults for the week, and the owner ruled on 2026-10-28 that the study may say an option needs a blue card but never that it cannot be sold without one.
