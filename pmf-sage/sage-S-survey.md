# SAGE Survey — the evidence, at review grade

Survey gives the run a real market to read, so that no later phase has to reason from internal opinion. It produces four bodies of evidence and, conditionally, a fence. Everything downstream cites this material by number.

## 1. The comparables corpus — learn from, never beat

Comparable operators anywhere in the world, studied to learn what sells and how it is sold. An operator on another continent is not a rival; it is a published experiment someone else already paid for. The corpus reads demand off supply: an element common across operators is read as the market rewarding it. It therefore describes the market as buyers meet it, not as an authority would audit it. An operator trading without the right permissions is still a comparable, and its bookings are still demand. Third-party status marks, registration, accreditation, authenticity claims, matter to the study exactly as much as buyers demonstrably weigh them when choosing, and no more.

The discipline is a content-analysis pipeline, run in order:

1. **The operator's shape note.** Before any frame is drawn, the run writes the operator's shape on four dimensions: how the buyer arrives, whether entry is paid before the offering is reached, how the sale is taken, how a place is held. Where the market's comparables mostly differ from the operator on a dimension, the frame draws a second population that shares it, or declares the difference so that every rate measured in the corpus can be marked against it at the cards. The note lives in the survey stage and the card clerk reads it.
2. **Frames from source lists.** A prevalence claim needs a denominator, so a frame is populated from published lists that can be re-opened and counted, standing for the market buyers choose from. Prefer the lists that mirror where this market's buyers actually look: buyer-facing directories and platforms, trade bodies, paid directories. A statutory or accreditation register qualifies where buyers or their intermediaries consult it, and its provenance is part of the finding, since a regulator's list enumerates the market as the regulator sees it. A search hit carries no denominator, so search finds go to the declared convenience list rather than into a frame, and rates are computed on frame rows only. Record every source list used: who runs it, entry count, who is structurally shut out.
3. **Documented selection.** Every step between source list and frame is written in a coverage note. A cell that ends small is declared a census; a sampled cell states its mechanical rule. An undocumented shrink from list size to a convenient frame size is the classic corruption, and a reviewer will find it.
4. **A codebook frozen blind.** The coding manual is drafted by an agent who has not seen any frame, and frozen before collection. Standing instructions: text only; silence is a value, not a fact; no inference in either direction; a rule two coders would apply differently has failed and escalates. Changing a frozen rule takes a version, a date, and re-coding of everything coded.
5. **An adversarial frame review before collection.** An independent agent attacks the frames as enumerations: proxy populations, quota-shaped cells, censuses dressed as samples, forbidden cross-cell comparisons where source-list architecture is confounded with geography. Verdict per cell: go, conditional, no-go. Collection runs only on cells that pass, under their conditions. The review also checks every operator the owner named in his scoping statement, the document of his own knowledge that opens a run, against its cell's frame. An absent one is added as a named row outside the draw: collected and coded under the frozen codebook, excluded from every numerator and denominator, and available to any ranking to place and any card to cite. The coverage note records which source lists should have carried it and did not; that record is a finding about the lists.
6. **Verbatim collection, then coding.** Profiles quote what each operator publishes, with capture records (URLs, dates, what was unreachable). Coding applies the frozen codebook; an inter-coder spot check re-codes a mechanical sample blind and orders corrections before any synthesis.
7. **Numbered findings.** The synthesis emits findings as numbered statements citing coded data, so every later document writes "finding 12" instead of restating a claim it might drift.

Alongside the findings, the corpus yields an activity taxonomy with prevalence figures: what fraction of coded operators run each element. Establish's scarcity claims are measured against exactly this.

**The standing caveat, stated in the findings themselves:** the corpus reads what operators publish, and what is bought is not always printed. A module can be routinely sold by phone and appear in no brochure. Absence in the corpus is absence from publication, never absence from the market; treat a surprising absence as a question for the distributor channel below. The enquiry record has the same limit. Absence from the enquiry record is absence from what the operator invited, never absence from the market: the inbox holds only asks for what the operator already sold, under the name it used, on the surfaces it occupied, in that window, so a low count for anything else describes the current product line. Where the search findings show a large cluster and the inbox is empty, the inbox is the residue.

## 2. The rival register — the incumbents to beat

The local operators that contest the same bookings, profiled one by one and section-numbered, with the demand signal around them, in five kinds: which buyers already go where, what they pay all-in, the live enquiries, the meetings behind those signals, and what buyers type. This is the half that says who has to be displaced, and it feeds the named-buyer boundary test at Adjudicate and the judge scenarios at Game.

A live enquiry is recorded with the date of enquiry, the date wanted, the party, the channel and the outcome; a weekday and a month without a date cannot serve a card's buyer test. Beside any count of enquiries the record states the condition the count was taken under: what was offered in the window, under what name, on what surface. An enquiry for something the operator did not offer is recorded as such and kept apart from the rest. It is the buyer doing the survey's work, and it weighs more than many for what is already on the price list.

**What buyers type** reads the market side only, so it runs before the offering has a name or a page, and its findings are numbered like any other and decide nothing alone. Two instruments read it, both LAMP's: the rank database, against the operator's national market, which returns per term the monthly volume, a difficulty score, a competitive density and an intent label; and a located results-page capture, what one buyer at one place sees on one day, the only instrument that sees a local pack. A single-offering study costs two to four database lookups and three captures. Where the run has no keyword instrument, the survey records that as a named gap rather than standing in for it with judgment. The study runs in order:

1. **Seed the terms** from four sources, the tool last because it amplifies whatever bias the seed carried: the comparables' own words for themselves (titles, navigation labels, the heading a landing page leads with); the buyer's own words from enquiries, booking free-text and meeting transcripts; the operator's internal vocabulary, worth having because the gap between it and the buyer's word is a finding; the rank database's phrase-match and question expansions. Terms come in three kinds and the study measures all three: the category as the trade names it, the category as a buyer names it, and the place itself. A study that asks only the first kind measures the trade's vocabulary and calls it demand. Artifacts: the comparables' self-descriptions, the enquiry phrase list with counts, the internal-term against buyer-term table.
2. **Read the volume** per term, and split the cluster into three layers by wording, reported apart and never as one total: proximity terms (near me, closest), place-qualified terms, bare generic terms. The layers behave differently and one may be unreachable for a fixed location. Artifact: the term table with a layer column.
3. **Strike intent.** Volume asking for something the operator cannot be is struck from the total, with the strike's size stated. Intent is read three ways and two must agree: the rank database's label, the modifiers in the phrase (free, best, spots, cheap, hire, mobile), and what the results page for the term already rewards. Artifact: the term table with a struck column and one reason per strike.
4. **Check the name.** One located capture of the bare candidate name with no place qualifier. Where the first page is held by other industries, states or countries, the name is a collision and the offering would compete for a term it cannot own. Artifact: the capture, with a verdict of collision or clear.
5. **Check the adjacent term.** In the cluster the offering will sit in, the largest term the operator does not serve; capture it. Where venues rather than publishers hold the pack seats, an operator can win it, and the term is a candidate offering rather than a candidate page. Artifact: the row, its capture, and a build-or-not question for the owner.
6. **Read the season**: relative interest over five years and twelve months, national and state, against the school calendar and against the climate that makes the offering pleasant or unpleasant. Where a term returns no data, say so. Artifact: the seasonality table with method, region codes and dates.
7. **Read the ceiling**, which is not a search step. From the operator's own records: the hours the offering can run, the spaces it occupies, the bookings already taken in a comparable window. Demand above the ceiling is not opportunity. Artifact: slots available, slots sold, occupancy, headroom in units.

Where the signal stops is stated in the findings themselves. Search gives volume and never money: no conversion rate turns it into revenue, and cost per click is an advertising price. A cluster's total can be many times its addressable part where most of it asks for a place the buyer can reach without a long drive. Volume on a shared word may be demand for other businesses. A generic national phrase attached to no place inflates a cluster. A local pack counts as one shared position, so a pack-gated offering can hold organic first place and stay invisible. And search says nothing about whether the operator can deliver the thing; that is the capability leg's job.

Buyer profiles of the whole catchment, one file per prospect, live with the operator's outreach campaigns rather than in the run: they pay three times, as demand evidence now, an outreach roster later, and the casting pool for Game's judges.

## 3. Distributor evidence — the transaction view

Intermediaries who transact across many operators hold the knowledge published text cannot; their kinds vary by market, and the run names its own: carriers who move the buyers, platforms that see conversion by price and season, brokers and underwriters who know who really runs what, trade bodies whose staff talk to every member. They answer factual aggregate questions cheaply, because answering is their sales call. Ask what is growing, what died, what buyers complain about on the way home; do not ask what to build, and do not ask for any one client's business.

This channel is the corrective for the publication blind spot in the corpus, and the cheapest evidence in the whole phase. A run that skips it should say why.

## 4. The fence — conditional

A product that existed in half-shape before the run (drafts, plans, a manager's local practice, meeting captures) has pre-decided parameters lying around, and a deriving AI will inherit them. For such a product, Survey builds the fence: an index of every file carrying pre-decided or suggestive parameters. The index generates the clean-room blacklist for Game's arms, and it is the update list for stale dimensions once the owner rules.

A wholly new product has no such debris and skips the fence. The test is honest: if no internal document could anchor a deriving agent, there is nothing to fence.

## Outputs

Frames with their source lists and coverage notes; the frozen codebook; the frame review; the coded corpus with numbered findings and the activity taxonomy; the rival register and demand signals, the search-demand findings among them; distributor evidence notes; conditionally, the fence index. All inside the run folder, survey stage, in pipeline order.
