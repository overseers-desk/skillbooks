# SAGE Game — designs compete blind, buyers judge

Game produces the product's interior by contest rather than by one author's draft. Isolated arms derive designs from the evidence, a reviewer strips what oversteps the rulings, blinded judges cast from real buyer profiles rule as buyers, the winners are absorbed into one sheet, and a held-out panel confirms the crown. The strongest evidence a game produces is independent arms converging on the same correction.

## Scoping ruling

The game opens with a ruling from the owner: what the game is about, and how many games run. The subject follows the nature of the product; the arms compete on that dimension and on nothing else, with every other dimension held by the decisions table. A game whose subject nobody ruled is a game whose arms will invent format.

## The arms

Several model arms derive the design content context-free: behind the fence where one exists, reading only the Survey evidence and the operator's standing capability records, under a whitelist and blacklist written into the brief. Banned channels are listed alongside banned paths, version-control history included, since an arm that can read history can read the fenced drafts. General domain knowledge is welcome; live lookups are not.

Each arm's brief opens with role priming: the arm recites that its job is product development, meeting demand with supply, not growing text from a corpus, and after each substantial read it restates in one sentence what a text generator would conclude against what a product developer would. This is a cheap recalibration against text-generation inheritance, and it works.

Every element an arm keeps carries a demand justification: a finding number, a taxonomy prevalence figure, a named comparable's practice, or stated buyer behaviour. "We have it" alone is supply-anchored text generation and is rejected. Each brief also asks for an excluded-with-reasons list: the high-demand elements deliberately left out, so the absence is a decision rather than an oversight.

## The reviewer gate

Between arms and judges stands the reviewer. It checks every format assertion in every sheet against the decisions table as amended by dated rulings: it reverts changed decided values, strikes format decisions outside the game's ruled subject, and strikes vocabulary from outside the scope. Design interiors pass untouched. Judges receive only the reviewer cut, and the review log records each cut with the ruling behind it.

The gate exists because it was needed: an unreviewed arm wins on smuggled format, not on the quality of its design, and the smuggled format then reads as ruled downstream.

## Blinding, mechanically

Blind copies are built outside the repository: frontmatter stripped, each sheet's title replaced by a neutral label, uniform presentation throughout, because one sheet that looks like a draft loses a round on looks. Labels are assigned by two or more cyclic permutations so no judge's mapping is shared by all; each judge gets one mapping and a shuffled reading order. The blind key records the mappings and the judge table; judges do not see it. Before a round that recreates voided documents, the voided versions are deleted from the working tree so nobody is anchored by a predecessor; version control keeps the record.

## The judges

Judges are cast from the operator's profiled buyer roster: a main panel diversified across the dimensions that segment the market (size, sector, distance, cohort), each judge role-playing one named profile. The orchestrator assigns each judge a concrete scenario (occasion, cohort, season, incumbent supplier, budget pressure) and records it in the blind key; an unrecorded scenario cannot be re-judged, so the record is part of the round.

Judges return booking-grade verdicts: a ranking, what they would actually book, their own all-in cost including the expenses the product does not bill (transport being the classic), dealbreakers, and the one change that would win them. Verdicts are persisted verbatim in the run; scoring is rank points summed across the panel, wins counted as first places. Judge-estimated costs are the judge's own figures, a standing instrument caveat worth restating in every results document.

Judges find defects authors cannot see in their own work; an arithmetic slip in a price floor is a typical catch. That alone pays for the panel.

## Absorption and the crown

One sheet absorbs the winners: for each element where winners differ, the verdict-rewarded choice, recorded element by element in a provenance redline auditable as a diff. Gaps the judges hit are answered only within already-ruled format; a gap whose answer needs an unruled format decision goes on a fold list for the owner, because an absorbed sheet that invents format to answer gaps voids its own game. The absorbed sheet passes the reviewer gate like any other.

The same judges then re-run until the absorbed sheet scores no fewer points and no fewer wins than any parent. Then a fresh panel of profiled buyers who sat in no earlier round tests whether the crown generalises; that held-out check is the only proof the design beats its parents rather than its examiners. Results split into floor fixes (clearly correct, applied) and forks (price or scope changes, the owner rules).

Two instrument caveats to carry in any results document: a judge panel drawn from one model family shares that family's tastes, and an absorbed sheet that answered gaps its parents were never revised for has won as the shipping candidate, not as a controlled ablation.
