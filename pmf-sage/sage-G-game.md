# SAGE Game — designs compete blind, buyers judge

Game produces the product's interior by contest rather than by one author's draft. A base sheet drafted from the rulings sets the mark, isolated arms derive designs from the evidence and vary them against it, a reviewer strips what oversteps the rulings, blinded judges cast from real buyer profiles rule as buyers, the winners are absorbed into one sheet, and a held-out panel confirms the crown. The strongest evidence a game produces is independent arms converging on the same correction, and that independence is bought partly by drawing the arms from different model families.

## Scoping ruling

The game opens with a ruling from the owner: what the game is about, and how many games run. The subject follows the nature of the product; the arms compete on that dimension and on nothing else, with every other dimension held by the decisions table. A game whose subject nobody ruled is a game whose arms will invent format.

## The base sheet

Before the arms run, a base sheet is drafted from the decisions table and the evidence: the product as the rulings and findings alone would write it. It enters the round as a sheet like any other, reviewer-cut, blinded and judged beside the variations. The arms are then measured against what the table already buys, and a game whose arms cannot beat the base says so in scores rather than in opinion.

## The arms

Several model arms derive the design content, drawn from different model families where the roster allows, one family per arm. Arms sharing one family converge on shared temperament rather than on demand, and hand the judges near-identical sheets with nothing to discriminate; a run that cannot diversify families records that in the round and reads convergence accordingly.

Each arm first derives context-free: behind the fence where one exists, reading only the Survey evidence and the operator's standing capability records, under a whitelist and blacklist written into the brief. Banned channels are listed alongside banned paths, version-control history included, since an arm that can read history can read the fenced drafts. General domain knowledge is welcome; live lookups are not.

Derivation is the first of two steps. In the second, the fence lifts on exactly one file, the base sheet, and the arm judges both directions element by element: where its design improves on the base, where the base's element is the one demand actually supports, and where the base can be bettered beyond either document. It then writes its variation, keeping, taking or synthesising, with every kept element carrying a demand justification whichever document said it first; loyalty to either document is text generation. The variation, not the first-step design, is what enters review.

Each arm's brief opens with role priming: the arm recites that its job is product development, meeting demand with supply, not growing text from a corpus, and after each substantial read it restates in one sentence what a text generator would conclude against what a product developer would. This is a cheap recalibration against text-generation inheritance, and it works.

Every line of the frame an arm designs inside carries its provenance on the line: RULED with the card and date, or DEFAULT with the finding it rests on. The two bind differently. A ruling is a wall; a default is the recommended answer, which the arm may depart from with a citation and the reviewer judges like any other choice. An arm reads a frame line with no mark as a default, whatever verb the line uses, and a line that states a property as fact and grants what is left ("within that, it is yours") is a frame line like any other and carries its mark on the premise. The mark is what performs I1's test at authoring time: an author cannot write it without going to the row, and a reader can tell a wall from a recommendation without trusting the author's verb. The case: a brief whose "these are ruled" block held five defaults among eight rulings, the offer's name among the five, and six arms across two rounds proposed the same name and cited the same finding for it.

Every element an arm keeps carries a demand justification: a finding number, a taxonomy prevalence figure, a named comparable's practice, or stated buyer behaviour. "We have it" alone is supply-anchored text generation and is rejected. Each brief also asks for an excluded-with-reasons list: the high-demand elements deliberately left out, so the absence is a decision rather than an oversight.

## The reviewer gate

Between arms and judges stands the reviewer. It checks every format assertion in every sheet against the decisions table as amended by dated rulings: it reverts changed decided values, strikes format decisions outside the game's ruled subject, and strikes vocabulary from outside the scope. Design interiors pass untouched. Judges receive only the reviewer cut, and the review log records each cut with the ruling behind it.

The gate exists because it was needed: an unreviewed arm wins on smuggled format, not on the quality of its design, and the smuggled format then reads as ruled downstream.

## Blinding, mechanically

Blind copies are built outside the repository: frontmatter stripped, each sheet's title replaced by a neutral label, uniform presentation throughout, because one sheet that looks like a draft loses a round on looks. Labels are assigned by two or more cyclic permutations so no judge's mapping is shared by all; each judge gets one mapping and a shuffled reading order. The blind key records the mappings and the judge table; judges do not see it. Before a round that recreates voided documents, the voided versions are deleted from the working tree so nobody is anchored by a predecessor; version control keeps the record.

## The judges

Judges are cast from the operator's profiled buyer roster (where the operator runs SPAR, these are SPAR profiles): a main panel diversified across whatever dimensions segment the market, each judge role-playing one named profile. The orchestrator assigns each judge a concrete scenario (occasion, cohort, season, incumbent supplier, budget pressure) and records it in the blind key; an unrecorded scenario cannot be re-judged, so the record is part of the round.

Judging runs one subagent per judge, a single run each, launched in parallel, with an isolation clause in the launch itself: read only the brief, the profile and the sheets in the given order, write no files, the verdict is the final message. A judge that can browse the repository can find the blind key, so the clause is part of the blinding, not a courtesy.

Judges return booking-grade verdicts: a ranking, what they would actually book, their own all-in cost including the expenses the product does not bill but the buyer pays to use it, dealbreakers, and the one change that would win them. Verdicts are persisted verbatim in the run; scoring is rank points summed across the panel, wins counted as first places. Judge-estimated costs are the judge's own figures, a standing instrument caveat worth restating in every results document.

Judges find defects authors cannot see in their own work; an arithmetic slip in a price floor is a typical catch. That alone pays for the panel.

## Absorption and the crown

One sheet absorbs the winners: for each element where winners differ, the verdict-rewarded choice, recorded element by element in a provenance redline auditable as a diff. Gaps the judges hit are answered only within already-ruled format; a gap whose answer needs an unruled format decision goes on a fold list for the owner, because an absorbed sheet that invents format to answer gaps voids its own game. The absorbed sheet passes the reviewer gate like any other.

The same judges then re-run until the absorbed sheet scores no fewer points and no fewer wins than any parent. Then a fresh panel of profiled buyers who sat in no earlier round tests whether the crown generalises; that held-out check is the only proof the design beats its parents rather than its examiners. Results split into floor fixes (clearly correct, applied) and forks (price or scope changes, the owner rules).

Two instrument caveats to carry in any results document: a judge panel drawn from one model family shares that family's tastes, and an absorbed sheet that answered gaps its parents were never revised for has won as the shipping candidate, not as a controlled ablation.
