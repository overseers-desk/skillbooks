# Two decision pages for one offering: a marketer's review for the owner

Two pages put the same offering's product parameters to its owner for ruling. Page A was written earlier; page B was written on 19 September 2026, and a note beside it (`priors-pass.md`) records what the venue's own documents and the owner's earlier rulings held for each parameter. Your prompt names the files. Read page A in full. Read page B's ruling sheet, order of ruling and counts in full, rate every card of page B from its row and its printed face, and open the card file, the third derivation and the survey record behind it for the cards your prompt lists and for any other row that raises a doubt. You may open any file in the run folder to check a claim, and nothing else is needed.

You are a seasoned marketer reviewing both pages for the owner. He has two worries, in his words. The first: work that "predicts what market needs by meeting notes and owner inner thoughts instead of reality". The second, its mirror: "thinking if owner believe A, A must be incorrect". A third sits between them and is yours to test as well: reading what the venue already sells as what the market wants, when sales made at the only terms on offer cannot show what buyers would have done at other terms. Assess both pages against all three, card by card. Neither page is yours, and the owner wants the blunt reading.

## For every card on each page, one table row

1. The parameter.
2. How many options are put, with evidence shown for each.
3. What the recommendation chiefly rests on: **M** market behaviour (comparable operators, local rivals, what people search for); **B** this venue's own buyers' behaviour (enquiries, refusals, stalls, sales); **D** the venue's documents, opinions, plans or present arrangement; **N** nothing stated.
4. Its relation to what the owner or the venue already held: agrees, departs, or nothing held.
5. Your verdict: **EARNED** (whichever way it points, the evidence shown carries it); **ECHO** (it agrees with the venue chiefly because the venue holds it, sales at the only terms on offer included); **CONTRARIAN** (it departs from the owner's view without evidence that carries the departure); **THIN** (it recommends while saying honestly that the evidence is thin, which is no fault); **UNSUPPORTED**. Page B lets a card withhold its recommendation and name the observation that would carry it. For such a card the verdict is **WITHHELD-SOUND** (the evidence on the card does not separate the options, or the only leg could not have shown the other option winning) or **WITHHELD-EVASIVE** (the card holds evidence that carries one option and declines to say so).
6. One line of reason, quoting the card.

## Then

- The count of each verdict on each page.
- The three clearest cases of each fault on each page, with the quotation.
- Whether page B treats agreement with the owner fairly: is a match with his view ever held against a recommendation, and does a departure from his view ever pass without the proof a match is asked for?
- What the owner has to read on each page in order to rule: the word count, the number of cards, and whether he can see at a glance which cards need his judgment and which only confirm what he ruled. Page B opens with a ruling sheet and an order of ruling; say whether an owner could rule from those two sections alone, opening a card only where a row raises a doubt, and how many words that is.
- Whether page B withholds too much or too little: of the withheld cards, how many are sound and how many evasive; of the cards that recommend, how many should have withheld.
- The three changes that would most improve page B for an owner who will give it one sitting.
- One sentence: is page B an improvement on page A for this owner, and how sure are you.

Write the full review to the output file your prompt names, then report its path and your verdict counts in under 250 words.
