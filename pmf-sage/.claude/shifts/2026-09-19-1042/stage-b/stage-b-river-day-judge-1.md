# Two decision pages for one offering: a marketer's reading for the owner

Reviewer: seasoned marketer, reading blind to whose work either page is.
Page A: `3-decisions/2026-08-10-decision-review.md` (18 decisions, 10–13 August 2026).
Page B: `3-decisions/review.md` (30 cards, 19 September 2026), with `3-decisions/priors-pass.md`.

The three tests applied to every card:

1. **Inner-thoughts prediction** — does the recommendation read the market off meeting notes, the venue's plans, or the owner's held view?
2. **Reflex contradiction** — does it depart from the owner's view because it is the owner's, without evidence carrying the departure?
3. **The only-terms-on-offer trap** — does it read what the venue has already sold as what the market wants, when sales made at the only terms ever offered cannot show what buyers would have done at other terms?

Verdict key: **EARNED** (whichever way it points, the shown evidence carries it) · **ECHO** (agrees with the venue chiefly because the venue holds it, sales at the only terms on offer included) · **CONTRARIAN** (departs from the owner without evidence carrying the departure) · **THIN** (says honestly the evidence ties or is missing — no fault) · **UNSUPPORTED**.

Evidence key: **M** market behaviour (comparables, local rivals, search) · **B** this venue's own buyers (enquiries, refusals, stalls, sales) · **D** the venue's documents, opinions, plans or present arrangement · **N** nothing stated.

### Page A's three clearest cases of each fault

**Inner-thoughts prediction (worry 1) — three clearest**

1. Decision 12, the 100-a-day ceiling: *"A smaller ceiling (60) would be easier to staff but loses the largest live buyer on day one: AIIC's 88 students would not fit."* AIIC enquired and did not book. A ceiling set to fit one unconverted enquiry is the pipeline used as a market.
2. Decision 13, ages: *"forfeits the Year 5–6 science demand that is already in the pipeline unprompted."* Five enquiries, all Prep-to-Year-6, arrived at a page that only ever addressed schools; page B's card 8 names the same silence — "the 175-email mailing of 13 August reached schools and no college".
3. Decision 16's familiarisation visit and decision 17's sibling line: both are added to the offer with no market count, no comparable and no buyer behind them. Decision 17 says so out loud: *"policy follows the segment list, not goodwill."*

**Reflex contradiction (worry 2) — three clearest**

Page A has none worth the name. It is the owner's own record of his own rulings, and every departure from the *discarded* file is argued against evidence. The nearest approach to the fault is decision 7, where the *Further* line rejects the first pass's own $750 as shutting out "the single-class band of 25–30 children, which is the catchment's basic booking unit" — asserted, with no count behind "basic booking unit"; and the whole floor was reversed again a week later (14 August, no minimum at all), which is a parameter that moved three times in five days without an instrument.

**Only-terms-on-offer (worry 3) — three clearest**

1. Decision 6: *"the catchment's booked venues (Currumbin 12 schools, Sea World 8, Lone Pine 6) all sell base-plus-options, and the all-in venues show zero observed bookings in the demand data."* This one is legitimate — it reads *rivals'* bookings, not this venue's. Noted because it is the model the rest of the page does not follow.
2. Decision 2's *Ruled* line rests the whole structure on one comparable's composed price: *"a near-identical composed price ($25 for three hours against $24 here)"* — a price this venue has never sold at.
3. Decision 10: *"not one buyer has ever asked for a horse."* No buyer was ever shown a horse to ask for — the $15 ride was on sale five days. Page B's card 17 catches exactly this and marks it: *"which is five days and not a test."*

### Page B's three clearest cases of each fault

**Inner-thoughts prediction (worry 1) — three clearest**

1. **Card 27.** *"Recommended: The pack goes out with the quote, unasked; margin: 1 booking over Published on the school page."* A margin of one. The venue's current practice supplies both the option and the count that beats 68 publishing venues.
2. **Card 18's second leg.** *"the $6 line standing unsold for 36 days"* is used against the owner's ruling — but the card's own closing sentence admits the symmetric fact: *"no show inside the price has ever been offered here, and the sales say nothing about terms never on sale."* The unsold line is still allowed to carry weight in the recommendation.
3. **Card 14.** The whole departure from a -24 count rests on a single quoted sentence from one teacher in finding 27. It is real demand evidence and it is n=1; the card does not say so.

**Reflex contradiction (worry 2) — three clearest**

1. **Card 23.** The owner ruled "either online or through a human, and neither is required" — the widest, least committal answer available. The card narrows it to "a person from the first word" on a 30-profile lead its own corrections line then calls *"a tie"*. Narrowing a permissive ruling on a dissolved instrument is the clearest contrarian move on the page.
2. **Card 18.** *"No set-piece show"* against a ruling that published one, on 39 of 60 resolved venues where *"124 of 184 pages are silent and coder agreement on this field is 60%"* — and where the blind third derivation, which never saw the ruling, landed nearer the owner than the card did.
3. **The machinery itself.** `card-check.py` *"refuses this set: 12 of 30 recommendations match a prior with no third derivation recorded, above the one-in-ten line"*. Agreement with the owner is the condition that triggers a refusal; disagreement triggers nothing. That is a structural thumb on the scale even though no recommendation was flipped by it.

**Only-terms-on-offer (worry 3) — three clearest**

Page B does not commit this fault; it names and defuses it on card after card, in almost identical words. The three places the defusing is weakest:

1. **Card 21.** Uses the unsold picnic basket and platters as its closing argument — *"the picnic basket and the platters have never been sold, on one booking and five enquiries"* — which is a fair reading, since those lines *were* on offer. But five enquiries and one booking is an n so small that "never sold" is nearly uninformative, and the card presents it as *"the strongest single argument for the recommendation"*.
2. **Card 19.** Same shape: every unsold line is counted against the recommendation the card nonetheless makes. Honest, but the inference runs on the same n=6.
3. **Card 16.** *"The one live buyer who bought anything bought contact and nothing else"* is the closing sentence — one $75 purchase, standing in for a market.

### Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

**Is a match ever held against a recommendation?** Not in the recommendations themselves — no card on page B is weakened, hedged or reversed because it agrees with the owner. Every card that matches a ruling says the same sentence, and means it: *"His ruling stands and this card does not reopen it."* Cards 0, 7, 11, 12, 16, 17, 22 and 24 all agree with the venue or the owner and are among the best-evidenced cards on the page.

**But agreement is taxed and disagreement is not.** A match with a prior triggers a mandatory extra derivation and, at 12 of 30, refuses the whole set back to the clerks. There is no corresponding gate on a departure. So the process makes agreement expensive to keep and disagreement free to publish. The effect is real and measurable at the card level:

- The four cards that **agree** with a ruling (0, 7, 12, 16, 22, 27) each clear a blind re-derivation *and* a count reconciliation.
- Of the four that **disagree** with a ruling, two (21, 28) clear an equivalent bar — 75 to 39 and 202 to 215 — and two (18, 23) do not. Card 18 departs on a field with 60% coder agreement and 124 of 184 pages silent; card 23 departs on a hand read of free text its own corrections line calls a tie.

So: **a match is never held against a recommendation, but a departure has twice passed on an instrument that would not have been allowed to carry a match.** That is the asymmetry to fix, and it is two cards wide, not thirty.

One further point in page B's favour on this test: on card 26 the card departs from the venue's own standing term (the free reschedule) and the blind third derivation lands *on* the venue's term. The page keeps both readings rather than preferring the departure. That is the right behaviour and it is the strongest single piece of evidence that the page is not reflexively contrarian.

### The three changes that would most improve page B for one sitting

1. **Put the ruling map first, not last.** Move "Rule these together" and its five ordering statements to the top, in front of card 0, and add the one table the page lacks: 30 rows — card, one-line recommendation, `open`/`ruled`, and a mark for the nine cards that hold two live readings. He should be able to see in ninety seconds that card 1 is the first ruling, that six cards wait on it, and that nine cards are genuine contests while the rest are near-unanimous (card 17 at 38 of 39, card 28 at 202 of 215).

2. **Take the count-reconciliation apparatus off the cards.** The 9,813 words of `Corrections:` are an audit trail for the clerks, not decision material. Keep on each card one sentence — the blind derivation agreed or diverged, with its figure — and move the rest to a companion file he never has to open. That is a fifth of the page gone with no loss to the ruling.

3. **Collapse the twelve ruled cards to an appendix.** Each becomes its ruling line plus, where the card found something new, one sentence. Four of them (18, 21, 23, 28) argue at length against rulings the page then says it does not reopen — those arguments belong on the record, but not in the path of a man deciding eighteen other things. Between changes 2 and 3 the owner's reading falls from about 50,000 words to roughly 20,000, and every word of it is a decision he has to make.

### The one sentence

Page B is a decisive improvement on page A as evidence and a regression as a decision document — it puts real options with real figures where page A put assertions and pipeline names, and it defuses the only-terms-on-offer trap card after card in a way page A never attempts, but page A can be ruled in one sitting and page B cannot; I am highly confident of the first half and confident of the second.
