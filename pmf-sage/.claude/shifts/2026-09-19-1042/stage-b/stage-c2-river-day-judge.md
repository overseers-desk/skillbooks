# Two decision pages for the River Day: a marketer's reading

Written for the owner, 19 September 2026. Page A is `3-decisions/2026-08-10-decision-review.md` (4,873 words, 18 decisions). Page B is `3-decisions/review.md` (37,217 words, 30 cards) with `priors-pass.md` beside it. I read both in full and opened the card files for cards 18, 21, 22 and 28, which are the four that recommend against a ruling you have already given.

The three tests I applied, in your words: **(1)** does it predict what the market needs from meeting notes and the venue's own opinions instead of reality; **(2)** does it assume that because you believe A, A must be wrong; **(3)** does it read what the venue already sells as what the market wants, when sales made at the only terms on offer cannot show what a buyer would have done at other terms.

Short version. Page B is a much better page than page A and is not a close call. Its characteristic failure is not either of your two worries. It is a **counting rule applied unevenly**, and on three of the four cards that depart from your rulings the unevenness runs against the value you ruled.

## The four departures, read closely in both directions

### Card 22, the smallest booking: the departure is carried, and your ruled value was measured fairly

The card recommends a stated minimum party size against your ruling of 14 August (no headcount minimum, per head for 1 to 100). Three independent legs: 36 venues stating a headcount minimum against 3 stating a no-floor in terms; three of the four catchment school sellers nearest this day's shape publishing a minimum of 10 (Currumbin, Lone Pine, David Fleay); and a fence-blind third clerk landing on the same option at the same number without seeing your ruling or this card.

Your option is measured at 3 of 184, which looks brutal until you see why: 145 of the 148 that "state no minimum" say nothing at all, and finding 11's own caution is that **websites understate real group minimums**, so the silence leans toward floors and not away. The card also prints the fact that hurts it most: "one of the five is a party of 8 to 9 children that a minimum of 10 would refuse outright."

One caution you should carry. This card did not turn on new evidence. It turned because the page changed how it counts silence, and the review says so on its own face: "Nothing about the market changed; what changed is that silence stopped counting as a decision." A ruling you gave on 14 August is being reopened by a bookkeeping rule. The rule is the right one, but you are entitled to know that is what moved it.

### Card 18, the show: carried in direction, but your ruled option is measured in a unit that cannot contain it

The card recommends a set piece inside the day's price against your ruling of a published $6-a-child add-on. The evidence for the direction is real: 38 of the 60 venues whose page resolves the question gather the group for something.

Two problems, and the second is the serious one.

First, the leg is one clerk's coding with no second coder, on a variable outside the frozen codebook, drawn from the stated-absent cell of a field whose own coder agreement is 60 per cent. The card says all of this itself and calls the lead "thin, at 2 venues on the narrower of the two readings". A recommendation against a standing ruling, resting on an un-replicated in-house recode of the weakest-agreed field in the study, is the one place on this page where your first worry has a foothold: the observation that broke the withholding was manufactured in the same building, not found in the market.

Second, and this is the unfairness: the margin against your ruling is stated as **"the corpus holds no instance of a staged display charged separately to a school, 0 of 60"**. But the card's own Corrections line records that the record *does* hold watched items priced on top: "a zoo park's 'School Talks: £45 per session, up to 30 pupils' and a wildlife park's keeper animal talks at '£1 per child'". It says plainly that "the card's zero is true of staged shows and not of watched items in general". Your ruled value is a priced per-head gathered item. It is measured in the one unit that excludes the two real instances of exactly that. The 0-of-60 should not be on the ruling sheet in that form.

On the third test the card is half-right and half-wrong. It notes correctly that "a set piece inside the price has never been on sale here", so the record cannot carry the alternative. But it still puts the unsold $6 line on the scale, "has stood on sale for 36 days through five enquiries and one booking without being taken", against an option that was never on sale to be taken instead. That is your third worry in miniature.

**For what it is worth on the other side: your ruled value is itself weakly founded.** Page A's own case for publishing the show names the opposite as the market's dominant practice: "Including the demonstration free inside the day is the market's dominant show treatment (21 corpus venues run shows as schedule items; Paradise Country's arena show sits inside $13.50 admission)". It rules against it anyway, on discoverability. The $6 and the 25-child minimum are shift-cost arithmetic, not a market figure. So page B is pushing you toward the treatment page A already identified as dominant, by a route page A did not take.

### Card 21, food: the departure is not carried against what you actually ruled

The card recommends nothing sold to the school at all, against your 10 August presentation ruling (café platters promoted, school-packed lunches permitted but unadvertised).

The headline margin of 36 venues, 75 to 39, is against **food packaged into the per-child price**, which is not what you ruled. You ruled the third option, a paid catering line published beside the packed lunch. Against *that* option the card states a lead of 37: "64 of the 91 publish no paid line beside the packed lunch against 27 that do."

Fifty-two of that 64 are silent. The card says so and exempts itself: "the 52 silent venues are counted in that 64 because what divides the two options is whether the line is published at all, which is the one case in which an operator's silence is a position rather than an absence." Strip the silence, as this same page strips it on cards 9, 17, 22 and 24, and the count reads **12 stating a packed-lunch-only posture against 27 publishing a paid line, a lead of 15 for the option you ruled.** The departure survives only under the exception.

The exception is more defensible here than on card 28, because your ruling genuinely is a publication decision. But the card cannot have it both ways: on card 22 it declares that "this card rules the smallest party the day is sold to, not whether the number is printed" and strips 145 silent venues out of your ruled option's figure, and on card 21 it declares the opposite reading of a structurally identical question and puts 52 silent venues into the figure that beats your ruled option.

Finding 24 is also doing less than it appears. The card calls it "the one that decides it for this venue". What finding 24 actually says of this venue's kitchen type is that it **predicts nothing**: "packaging in 34% of cases against a 32% base rate". A null result is not evidence for the recommendation; it is only evidence that the kitchen is not a reason to package.

What *is* legitimately held against your ruling: the basket and the platters were genuinely on offer through the whole period and a buyer could have taken either. "the picnic basket and the platters have never been sold, on one booking and five enquiries, and the one enquiry that named food asked for a spot to picnic." That leg passes the terms-on-offer test cleanly, and it is the real argument on this card. It is worth more than the count it is printed under.

### Card 28, the site visit: the departure is not carried, and your ruled option cannot be seen by the instrument

This is the weakest of the four, and the clearest instance of your second worry on the whole page.

The recommendation is "no visit published, the paperwork goes instead", at a margin of 190 profiles over your famil visit's 12 of 215. **All 202 profiles on the recommendation's side are silence.** The corrections line says it: option 3 stands "at 202 of 215 with thirteen naming a visit firmly". Among venues that say anything at all, the count is 12 to 1 *for* a teacher visit. Under the rule that reset cards 9, 17, 22 and 24, this recommendation has a figure of roughly zero.

The card claims the same publication exception as card 21. But the exception does not reach your ruled value, because you did not rule a published visit. You ruled a free familiarisation visit **into the compliance pack**, which travels with a quote. And the card's own Measured-in line concedes the instrument cannot see that: this is "a whole-text read of the profile bodies... not a coded variable", and "a famil offered only in a returned quote is offered nowhere the collection read". Your ruled value lives precisely where the instrument does not look, and the count of pages that do not mention it is then read as 190 venues voting against it.

The page knows this argument, because it makes it on the very next card. Card 27, on the same corpus and the same problem, says: "the coded variable reads whether a document is reachable from a venue's pages, so it cannot see a pack sent with a quote and a no is silence on a page rather than the absence of a pack". Two further facts sit against the departure. He reads the catchment's 0 of 33 as unoccupied ground rather than as prevalence, the opposite of this card's reading of the same zero. A card that departs from a standing ruling, on an all-silence margin, in a decision it says does not matter, is a card that should have withheld.

**In fairness on the other side:** your ruled famil has no demand evidence behind it either. Page A justified it by inference alone: "The familiarisation visit doubles as the teacher's own site risk inspection, which Queensland approval expects anyway: one visit, two jobs, zero price". ## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Fault 1: predicting the market from the venue's own documents and opinions

**Page A (three clearest):**
1. Decision 16, the famil visit: *"The familiarisation visit doubles as the teacher's own site risk inspection, which Queensland approval expects anyway: one visit, two jobs, zero price."* An internal convenience argument, ruled into the product, and now a standing ruling.
2. Decision 7, the floor: *"a dedicated visit for 12 children at $15 yields $180 against roughly $250 of staffing and setup: the venue would be paying schools to visit."* The number that shapes the smallest booking is a cost sum with no market comparison.
3. Decision 12, group mechanics: chipped **Anchored**, which the page's own glossary defines as *"evidence fitted around a pre-held value"*. The committed numbers survive and one comparable is fetched to stand beside them.

**Page B (three clearest, and they are much milder):**
1. Card 18's finding T2. An in-house recode of the venue's own corpus, one clerk, no second coder, on a variable outside the frozen codebook. It was produced to break a withholding and used at once to depart from a ruling.
2. Cards 9 and 22 on one field: *"Cards 9 and 22 are the same published group-bounds field cut two ways, and the two cards now recommend on opposite sides of it."* The page prints this and then ships both. Two clerks' readings, not two market facts.
3. Card 21's finding 24, presented as decisive when its own figure is a null: *"packaging in 34% of cases against a 32% base rate."*

### Fault 2: if the owner believes A, A must be wrong

**Page A: no instances.** Page A never departs from a ruling; it departs from the discarded file.

**Page B (three clearest):**
1. Card 28. A 190-profile margin composed entirely of silence, against a ruled value the instrument admits it cannot see: *"a famil offered only in a returned quote is offered nowhere the collection read"*. The fence-blind clerk meanwhile lands on your side.
2. Card 21. The silence exception is invoked exactly where it beats the ruled option, on the same page that strips silence out of the ruled option on card 22. Without it: 27 to 12 for what you ruled.
3. Card 18. Your ruled option is scored at "0 of 60" in a unit the card's own corrections show excludes the two real priced instances in the record.

Note what these three are *not*: none of them is malice toward your view. All three are one inconsistently applied counting rule.

### Fault 3: reading sales at the only terms on offer as market demand

**Page A (three clearest):**
1. Decision 6: *"the catchment's booked venues (Currumbin 12 schools, Sea World 8, Lone Pine 6) all sell base-plus-options, and the all-in venues show zero observed bookings."* Those venues' bookings are driven by gate, brand and scale; the structure is not the variable that was varied.
2. Decision 13: *"the Year 5–6 science demand that is already in the pipeline unprompted."* The pipeline arrived under the only published scope.
3. Decision 10: *"not one buyer has ever asked for a horse"*, offered as proof of no demand, at a venue that had never sold a horse element to a school.

**Page B: this is the page's outstanding strength**, and it is worth saying plainly. It disarms this fault on card after card, unprompted and in its own voice:

- Card 4: *"every enquiry and both sales arrived under a per-child unit and no flat group fee has ever been quoted to anyone, so the venue's own record shows buyers accepting the unit on offer and carries nothing against the unit never offered."*
- Card 9: *"the two small parties on this record are evidence that small parties enquire where nothing turns them away and no evidence of what they would do facing a floor."*
- Card 25: *"**no buyer has ever cancelled a booking of this offering**: the four cancellations in the system were created and cancelled within minutes on one morning by staff testing a new product."*

The three residual lapses: card 18 weighing the unsold $6 line against a never-offered alternative; card 1 leaning on 3-of-5 enquiries while conceding the mailing reached schools only; card 16 citing "the only live buyer who bought anything bought contact and nothing else" when contact was the only thing cheaply on offer.

## Does page B treat agreement with you fairly?

**Is a match with your view ever held against a recommendation? No.** Cards 12 and 16 agree with your rulings and are carried on their own market evidence without apology or discount; card 22's third clerk agreeing with the card is explicitly recorded as *"evidence about the market and the chip stands at derived"*, which is the right treatment. Six cards withhold on parameters you have ruled (0, 7, 13, 14, 23, 27) and every one of the six has a stated tie behind it, card 13's "2, 2, 2 and 1 of the seven" and card 23's three incompatible reads of one field. Declining to flatter a ruling is not the same as attacking it, and this page does the former.

**Does a departure ever pass without the proof a match is asked for? Yes, twice, and by one mechanism.** The page's dominant counting rule, that silence carries for no option, is applied to shrink the ruled option on card 22 (148 down to 3) and suspended to inflate the recommendation on cards 21 and 28 (52 and 202 silent profiles counted in). The suspension is declared each time, but it is declared where it helps. Applied evenly, card 28's departure disappears entirely and card 21's reverses.

That is my answer to your second worry. The page is not biased against your beliefs. It has one rule with an exception, and the exception has fallen three times out of four on the side that leaves your ruling. Fix the rule and three of these four cards move.

## Withholding: too much, or too little?

Of the 11 withheld cards, **11 are sound and 0 are evasive.** I looked hardest at card 14, which holds a two-to-one lead on both its instruments and declines to name it; I let it stand because its stated reason (its catchment counter-leg, and the fact that the one demand leg is silent on the grain in question) is real and disclosed. Card 20 is similar and clearly sound: two hand-reads of one field give opposite leads of one operator.

**Of the cards that recommend, three should have withheld:**
- **Card 28**, unambiguously. An all-silence margin, an instrument blind to the ruled form of the value, and a fence-blind clerk landing the other way. The card even says the decision is costless either way.
- **Card 18**, on its own account. One clerk, no second coder, weakest-agreed field, "thin, at 2 venues on the narrower of the two readings", and departing from a standing ruling. Its own reversing condition, "a second coder declining the seven venues T2 reads on its wider rule", is one pass away.
