# Two decision pages for one wedding offering: a marketer's review

Written for the owner. Both pages put product parameters to him and neither has been ruled: every Review and Ruled line on page A is blank and every Ruled line on page B reads "open." So "what he already held" below means the venue's own live documents, its published terms, and the earlier unruled cards — not a decision of his.

Three tests are applied to every card:

- **Fallacy 1, the inward read.** Predicting what the market needs from meeting notes and the owner's own thinking.
- **Fallacy 2, the mirror.** Treating a match with what the owner or the venue holds as evidence against it.
- **Fallacy 3, the sale that proves nothing.** Reading what the venue already sells as what the market wants, when a sale made at the only terms on offer cannot show what a buyer would have done at other terms.

Verdicts: **EARNED** (the evidence shown carries it, whichever way it points); **ECHO** (it agrees with the venue chiefly because the venue holds it, sales at the only terms included); **CONTRARIAN** (it departs from the owner's view without evidence that carries the departure); **THIN** (it says honestly that the evidence ties or is missing, which is no fault); **UNSUPPORTED**.

Leg codes: **M** market behaviour (comparables, local rivals, search), **B** this venue's own buyers, **D** the venue's documents, opinions, plans or present arrangement, **N** nothing stated.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

**In its prose, scrupulously. In its machinery, no.**

What is right. The priors pass states the rule outright: "A match is not a fault and a departure is not a merit." Roughly fifteen cards carry the sentence "neither is preferred for being the venue's or for not being." Card 8's corrections applies the sales-at-offered-terms test in both directions and finds half the venue's own evidence valid. Card 7 refuses to read zero chapel bookings as zero chapel demand: "absence here is absence from what the operator invited." Cards 9, 19, 20, 24 and 25 all land on the value the venue already holds and say so without embarrassment. Page B is far better than page A at naming fallacy 3, and it names it card by card.

**Is a match ever held against a recommendation?** Yes, mechanically. `tools/card-check.py` "refuses the set on that ground alone, since 24 of 33 is above the one-in-ten threshold: the set returns to the derivation clerks for third derivations." The trigger is the *rate of agreement with held values*, nothing else. A set that agreed with the owner on 30 of 33 parameters because the owner is right would be refused harder than one that agreed on 3. That is fallacy 2 written into a tool. The chip rule compounds it: where the two derivations agree the chip is upgraded to *derived*; where they diverge "the stamp is left as the priors pass set it." Agreement with the market earns a promotion; agreement with the owner earns an audit.

**Does a departure ever pass without the proof a match is asked for?** Yes, seven times. Cards 5, 10, 13, 18, 23, 28 and 30 all depart from the terms the offering sells under, all carry a thin or negative margin, and none is struck. Compare the treatment. Card 3 agrees with the venue and is met with "the two are not one denominator" plus a third derivation that finds its middle rung nowhere on earth; it survives as one of two readings. Card 13 departs from the venue and is preferred explicitly "not [on] its count but its shape"; it survives as one of two readings as well. The test fires only where the leg is the venue's own record, so the burden falls on the agreeing side by construction.

**One cost of the symmetry.** The third derivation is made from the market side alone and therefore never sees capability. On card 17 it recommends whole-site exclusive use — which E27 records the venue cannot deliver on a trading day without an approval "rarely granted." The card presents it beside the deliverable option as a co-equal reading "for the owner to rule between." An undeliverable option given equal standing to a deliverable one is not neutrality; it is a defect the neutrality machinery produced.

## The three changes that would most improve page B for one sitting

1. **Put a one-page table at the front, with a column page A already has and page B dropped.** One row per card: the question in six words, the recommendation in one clause, the margin with its sign, whether the two derivations agree or diverge, and whether the card is ruleable now or gated on a named fact. Move the seventeen-contested-cards list and the four-rulings-carry-the-rest ordering up beside it. That is about 900 words at the top of a 54,000-word file and it turns an unreadable document into a two-hour sitting.

2. **Make the Recommended line carry the verdict its own Corrections line reaches.** On seven cards the Recommended line states a confident recommendation and the Corrections line, 200 words below, withdraws its leg — "those enquiries could not have shown the alternative at all," "says nothing about what they would do with one," "the card's one-section lead rests entirely on counting Colliston Farm." An owner skim-reading 33 Recommended lines collects 33 recommendations, seven of which the page itself does not stand behind. Either mark such a card "open, no recommendation carried" on the Recommended line, or restate the leg's limit inside the recommending sentence. This also shortens the page: the seller's-sentence column is roughly a third of every option table and does the same work thirty-three times.

3. **Retire the one-in-ten match threshold and apply the terms-on-offer test to both sides.** The threshold penalises agreement with the owner as such, which is the exact error the owner named. Replace it with a per-card test that fires on any recommendation whose leg is a count taken under terms the alternative was never offered against — that test already exists on page B and is well written; it is simply pointed at one side only. Then a departure on a one-rival lead (card 13) and a match on a 31-booking lead (card 3) get the same question asked of them, and the owner can tell which divergences the market produced from which the process produced.
