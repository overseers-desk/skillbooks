# Two decision pages for the River Day: a marketer's reading

Reviewed 19 September 2026. Page A is `3-decisions/2026-08-10-decision-review.md` (18 decisions, 4,873 words). Page B is `3-decisions/review.md` (30 cards, 32,659 words), with `3-decisions/priors-pass.md` beside it. I read both in full and opened card files where a claim was load-bearing.

The three tests, in the owner's words and mine:

1. **Meeting notes for market.** Does the recommendation rest on what the venue thinks, plans or already runs, rather than on what buyers and comparable operators do?
2. **Contrarianism.** Does it depart from the owner's view without evidence that carries the departure — treating his belief as a reason to doubt?
3. **The terms on offer.** Does it read this venue's own sales and enquiries as demand, when every one of them was made at the only terms ever published, so they cannot show what a buyer would have done at other terms?

Basis codes: **M** market behaviour (comparables, catchment rivals, search), **B** this venue's own buyers, **D** the venue's documents, opinions, plans or present arrangement, **N** nothing stated.

## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Page A

**Meeting notes for market (D where M was needed)**

1. Decision 7, the \$375 floor: "a dedicated visit for 12 children at \$15 yields \$180 against roughly \$250 of staffing and setup: the venue would be paying schools to visit." A cost model is not a demand finding; the owner reversed it on 14 August.
2. Decision 15, online-first booking: "a 67-guest visit existed only as a calendar entry three days out. The failure mode is documented in the venue's own record." One internal incident decides a channel.
3. Decision 1, the name: "throws away the venue's existing recognition (about 37k followers, Best of Queensland credential), the one trust asset a newcomer holds." An asset the venue holds, standing in for evidence about what a teacher types.

**Terms on offer read as demand**

1. Decision 13, ages: "forfeits the Year 5–6 science demand that is already in the pipeline unprompted." The pipeline arrived under a published Prep–Year 6 scope; it cannot show what a Year 8 buyer would have done.
2. Decision 2, structure, buyer line: "AIIC Durack's 88 Year 5s take the base and compose River Scientists with Grooming the Pasos" — a composition predicted under the only structure ever quoted, written as though observed.
3. Decision 6, price: "AIIC's 88 compose at \$24: about \$2,112 plus a short coach, at effective parity with the Currumbin day they know." Parity is asserted; no buyer has been shown the composed figure.

**Contrarianism** — none. Page A never departs from the owner's view; it departs from an earlier file's.

**Echo** (the fault Page A does have in its place)

1. Decision 9: "Kept: **free to 10 working days out; 50% inside**" with one named venue produced at the same number.
2. Decision 13: "Prep to Year 6 core" kept, argued from the venue's own pipeline.
3. Decision 12: "Rotation groups up to **25**... a **100-a-day ceiling**" — one market ratio, then the venue's own station map and deck seating carrying the ceiling.

### Page B

**Contrarianism (the two departures from a standing ruling)**

1. Card 28: "No visit published, the paperwork goes instead; margin: 190 profiles". The margin is a count of profiles that did not use five words on four pages; the card file calls the same instrument "the weakest count in the whole area", and the fence-blind clerk read the same silence in the catchment as open ground, landing on "a free hosted teacher visit... on 0 of 33 catchment rivals publishing one".
2. Card 21: "Children bring lunch and nothing is sold to the school; margin: 36 venues over Food packaged into the per-child price." The owner's ruling is neither of those two; it is the third option, a paid line beside the packed lunch, and the card's own Corrections concede "the market side cannot separate bringing lunch from bringing lunch with a paid line published beside it".
3. Card 8, a weaker third: not a departure from a ruling, but a recommendation against the venue's published scope on a leg the card says may be void — "what that sweep may be measuring is silence rather than a strategy".

**Meeting notes for market** — three near-misses rather than three faults; no Page B card rests chiefly on D.

1. Card 6: the price band is measured in the sub-population that shares the venue's own day length, "the length this band is measured on", while its Joint line concedes the length is unruled. The comparison set is chosen by the venue's present arrangement.
2. Card 11: the same move in the other direction — "every named venue selling a led whole-group farm day with rotations publishes 3 hours or more, 7 of 7" — a sub-population defined by the shape the venue has chosen, with, as the card says, "no denominator under it".
3. Card 26: the recommendation's capability leg is the venue's own cover, and the card states the contradiction rather than hiding it: cover is "the barn at 60 and the red dining area 40 against a stated ceiling of 100 children".

**Terms on offer read as demand** — Page B catches this on every card I checked; these are where the catch is closest to being missed.

1. Card 21 is the one failure. It refuses the argument on one line — "it carries nothing against food inside the price" — and accepts it on the other: "The basket and the platters were on offer through the whole period and a buyer could have taken either, so the unsold record carries against a paid line published beside the packed lunch." Card 19 says the opposite about the same unsold lines: "no line is published anywhere a buyer can read without asking, the product being offline and hidden from the public catalogue, so the unsold menu is absence from what was invited and not a refusal." Both cannot be true. The reading that departs from the owner's ruling is the one that got the benefit.
2. Card 1 leans on the venue's record after discounting it: "3 of 5 enquiries and the only day sold are primary schools", with the discount stated two sentences later, "the college zero in the venue's record is a zero in what the venue invited".
3. Card 18, second half: "the one separately-priced show in this record has sold nothing in 36 days" is used to close half the question, immediately qualified in the card file by "no show inside the price has ever been offered here, and the sales say nothing about terms never on sale."

## Does Page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

**Is a match held against a recommendation?** No. The standard is stated symmetrically — "Keeping and leaving were asked for the same proof, a second derivation by a fresh clerk reading the market alone" — and all 30 cards carry one, which `card-check.txt` confirms. The corrections of 19 September ran in both directions: card 22 withdrew a 112-venue margin that supported the owner's 14 August ruling, and card 18 withdrew an 18-venue margin that ran against his 10 August one. Cards that keep a held value (1, 11, 12, 15, 17, 21, 25) carry their contrary counts openly, including negative margins printed on the sheet.

**Does a departure pass without the proof a match is asked for?** Yes, and this is the page's one systematic asymmetry — but it runs *against* the owner, not for him. The two cards that contradict a standing ruling, 21 and 28, are the two weakest recommendations on the page. Card 28 departs on a count of non-mentions while card 1, which agrees with the venue, is made to print "the sign is against the recommendation on this instrument" in its first clause. Card 21 departs on a margin measured against an option that is not the owner's, while card 11, which agrees with the venue, is made to disclose that its lead is "the same re-measurement made twice from different venue lists". A match is asked to name every leg that fails; the two departures name theirs more quietly and in the card file rather than on the page.

So the owner's second worry — "thinking if owner believe A, A must be incorrect" — is not the page's habit, but it is the page's two failures.

## Does Page B withhold too much or too little?

**Of the 12 withheld: 12 sound, 0 evasive.** I tested each for a leg that carries one option and is declined. Card 22 was the closest candidate — the catchment has "three of the four named publishing a minimum of ten", the third derivation lands on a minimum of 10, and only 3 profiles state no-floor in terms — but the 148-of-184 census stands on the other side, and finding 11's caution cuts both ways. Withholding is right, and the card gets credit for withdrawing a margin that had favoured the owner's ruling.

**Of the 18 that recommend, 3 should have withheld:**

1. **Card 8**, whose single leg it calls a possible count of silence, and whose third derivation differs.
2. **Card 28**, a departure from a ruling carried by non-mentions the card file grades weakest in the area.
3. **Card 21**, whose own Corrections say the instrument cannot separate its recommendation from the runner-up it actually competes with.

**One caution on the other side.** Two of the twelve withheld cards, 0 and 7, wait on the same observation, and that observation is "a re-read of profiles already held rather than a new collection". Withholding on a count nobody has spent an afternoon making is closer to unfinished than to unknowable, and it leaves two already-ruled parameters carrying no recommendation for no reason the owner can act on.

## The three changes that would most improve Page B for one sitting

**1. Fix the "what is held" column. It is wrong on four rows, and it is the column his echo worry depends on.** Cards 4, 9, 16 and 24 are marked "leaves it" when each recommendation reproduces what the venue already holds, word for word in three cases:

| Card | Held (`sot/S2 §5.10`, decision review) | Recommended | Sheet says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | "Base day (per child) \$15" | "A price for each child" | leaves it |
| 9 | "Any group from 1 to 100 children in a day; no minimum group size" | "A ceiling with no floor" | leaves it |
| 16 | "Two base activities: the Feed Shed and the River Ecosystem Walk" (feed and meet the animals) | "Hands-on contact inside the price everyone pays" | leaves it |
| 24 | "Schools pay no deposit; accepting the quote confirms the booking" | "Nothing down: the accepted quote holds the date" | leaves it |

The label is a string match made by `tools/card-check.py`, and the priors pass records the same four as "Matched a prior: No". The true tally is **11 recommendations keep the held value and 7 leave it**, not 7 and 11. The page tells the owner it agrees with him less often than it does. (My verdicts above hold — all four are earned on outside evidence — but he is entitled to the correct count before he decides how much to trust the set.)

**2. Put the contradictions and the negative margins on the row, not in a paragraph.** Add a mark to the "ruled" column for cards 21 and 28 reading *contradicts this ruling*, and a one-line key under the table: a negative margin means the largest count runs against the recommendation. Those two changes are worth more to a one-sitting reader than any card body.

**3. Split the twelve withheld into "commission" and "rule without it".** The order of ruling already names every missing observation; it does not say which are cheap. Cards 0 and 7 need only a re-read of profiles already in the corpus. Cards 3, 10, 18, 22 and 23 need a lookup or a coding pass. Cards 13, 19, 20, 27 and 29 need someone to talk to a buyer. Three lines instead of one paragraph, and he can commission four legs in a week instead of holding twelve parameters open indefinitely.

## Is Page B an improvement on Page A?

Yes, clearly and on every axis I can test — it puts figured options where Page A put assertions, it states the count that runs against each recommendation, it refuses the venue's own sales as proof of what buyers want in all but one place, and it costs the owner about 10,400 words to rule instead of 4,873 words that do not tell him what is open; and I am confident of that, with the two reservations that its only unearned recommendations (cards 21 and 28) both contradict rulings he has already given, and that its ruling sheet misreports four rows in the direction of overstating its own independence.
