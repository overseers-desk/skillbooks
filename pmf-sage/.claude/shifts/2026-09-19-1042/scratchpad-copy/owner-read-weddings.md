# READING

**What the page asks me to do first.** The opening two paragraphs under "The ruling sheet" tell me to work the table below "in the order that follows it" and to open a card only when a row raises a doubt. I initially read "the order that follows it" as the row order printed top to bottom, and started mentally ruling Card 0, then Card 1, then Card 2 in sequence. Only after reaching "The order of ruling" section further down did I realize the actual sequence is the one spelled out there (0+1, then 4 before 2, then 14, 17, 13, 16, 18, 20, then 15, 21, then a separate run through 23, 22, 27, 29, 31, 26, 28, 24, 9) — a different order than the table. That's a real stumble: a first-time reader will start working the wrong list before the correction arrives.

**The five column definitions** (your part / recommended / rests on / what is held / third derivation) are given plainly before the table and I could hold onto them while reading every row — no complaint there. The negative-margin rule ("the largest count runs against the recommendation, which then rests on the other evidence its card names") is the one definition I had to keep re-checking, because two rows actually use it (10 and 12) and both still list "rests on: market" in their own cell — the same category the header just told me gets overridden by "other evidence" when the margin goes negative. I could not tell, from this page, what that other evidence is for either row.

**The confirm rows (1, 3, 15, 16, 18, 22, 23, 24).** These are the eight where the recommendation just restates what I already sell, so my job is to agree, not decide. Rows 15, 18, 22 read cleanly and I would confirm them without hesitation. Rows 1, 3, 16, 23 and 24 all carry a parenthetical after the headline count that explains how the number was derived rather than what I'm deciding — long clauses about "the measured-in line," fields not kept, two instruments that "cannot be differenced," and in Card 16 a pair of raw codebook tags (F39's V17, F22's `V24_ceremony_only_present`). I could still confirm each of these rows on the strength of the headline recommendation matching what I sell, but I could not follow the arithmetic behind the count, and I don't think I'm meant to have to — this reads like process narration that escaped into the decision sheet.

**The rule rows (0, 10, 12, 26, 27, 28, 30, 31).** These are the real decisions — recommendations that move me off something I currently hold. I paused hardest on two:
- **Card 0** (whole day vs. small wedding): the lead is "11 of 34 against 5 of 34" — 18 of the 34 catchment rivals fall into neither count. I can't tell if those 18 undercut the lead or are simply irrelevant, and this is the card the whole ruling order starts on.
- **Card 12** (off-peak discount, negative margin, -1): only 5 of 34 rivals are classified at all, and the "lead" is one rival. That's a very thin base for a ruling, and it's exactly the negative-margin case where the header says the true support is "other evidence" I can't see here.

Cards 26, 27, 30, 31 have comfortable double-digit leads and I'd be willing to rule on the strength of the sheet alone. Card 28's derivation clause is the densest sentence on the page — "one cell that counts both... 32 naming a couple in a caption and 6 publishing a floor plan... one operator coded the other way reads 53 against 15, a lead of thirty-eight" — I read it twice and still can't reconstruct the arithmetic, though the conclusion (a named gallery) is one I'd rule for regardless.

**The seventeen commission-or-rule rows.** I read these as: no clear market winner exists, so either I spend to get one, or I just pick. The page groups them by what the observation would cost (five needing a re-read of records already held, seven needing a fresh lookup, two needing outside asking, three needing a live trial), which is genuinely useful — it tells me where a quick yes gets me evidence and where it doesn't. Card 2's row stopped me: "no unit of the package-total group publishes at this offering's ceiling band, so its six rank nothing" — I could not find what "six" refers to anywhere in the row or the surrounding prose.

**"The order of ruling" section.** The three boxed paragraphs at the top ("Three things are wrong today whichever way you rule...") are the most useful part of the whole page — they tell me, plainly, that my public wedding page currently contradicts my own signed terms on exclusivity, that two secured weddings already exceeded my stated chair capacity, and that my deposit is named two different ways internally. Those are things I can act on today regardless of any card. Good writing, no complaint.

The rest of the section leans on two labels I never saw defined: "Area B" and "Area C" ("the only way into Area B's circle," "In Area C, Card 23 before 22..."). No "Area A" is ever named. Since every card in each group is already listed by number, the labels add a term I can't resolve without adding any information I don't already have from the numbers themselves.

Four more terms stopped me across this section and the next: "Joint line" (cited for cards 2, 7, 16, 29 as if it's a field I'll recognize — I haven't opened a card yet, so I don't know if this is something the card shows me or a separate ledger); "the terms this offering sells under," a six-time recurring phrase that I eventually inferred means "matches the current contract wording," but it's never glossed where it first appears (Card 8); "the dead book" versus "the stalled book of 241 enquiries" (same thing twice, or two things?); and "143 openings" versus "241 enquiries" (is an opening a subset of an enquiry, or a different count entirely?).

**"One fact, several cards."** This paragraph is where the sharpest surprises live. "The 53-character cap on every exported message" is presented as an established constraint behind four cards' worth of missing evidence, with no statement of what system imposes it or whether it's fixable. Two sentences later, "209 events unread" behind "a device-verification wall" — I don't know if "events" here means the same thing as the "519 wedding-type events" in Card 1's row (clearly booking records) or something else (message/notification records), and the word is doing two different jobs on the same page.

**"What passed through."** This closing tally is the one place I could fully check the page's own arithmetic against itself, and it holds up: 8 confirm + 8 rule + 17 commission = 33; and 16 agree + 10 differ + 2 decline + 5 split = 33. That gave me some confidence in the page's bookkeeping even where I couldn't follow individual derivations.

Also worth naming: "catchment rivals," "register sections," and (once, in Card 8) "R1 section" are used as if interchangeable for what looks like the same 34-venue population, while "coded comparables" (72, glossed once in Card 28 as "72 international units") is a separate, larger population. Nothing on the page states outright that these are two different sets and not three or four.
