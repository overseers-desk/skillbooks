## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Page A — the market predicted from meeting notes and the owner's own mind (D)

1. **The compliance pack, decision 16.** "Blue Card details are out at the Director's instruction (`river-day-insurance-and-compliance-INTERNAL.md`, item 3); the commissioned water-safety document is out of scope at the Director's scope ruling of 13 August." A product parameter closed by instruction, then dressed in a rule — "The test for anything proposed into the pack: the venue supplies the inputs only the venue holds" — which no buyer was asked about.
2. **The menu, decision 4.** "The discriminator the earlier passes lacked: a session is 30-plus minutes of children doing; a stop is minutes of watching." A definition written on the page, used to delete two sessions, cited to nothing.
3. **The ceiling, decision 12.** "A smaller ceiling (60) would be easier to staff but loses the largest live buyer on day one: AIIC's 88 students would not fit." The day's capacity set by one enquiry that never converted, and by the dining deck.

### Page A — sales at the only terms on offer read as what the market wants

1. **Decision 7.** "this decision moved when Graceview (15 children, $150 ceiling) was priced through it and failed every prior version." One buyer who bought a different product reshapes the floor for all buyers.
2. **Decision 16.** "two customers asked for the risk assessment unprompted" — two of five enquiries, all arriving through a page that had borne one set of terms, become the demand case for a pack.
3. **Decision 6.** "AIIC's 88 compose at $24: about $2,112 plus a short coach, at effective parity with the Currumbin day they know." A composition nobody bought, at a price nobody was charged, offered as proof the ladder works.

Page A shows **no** clear CONTRARIAN case. Its risk runs the other way: eleven of eighteen decisions were reached against the file's own earlier positions on market counts, and three were reached from the venue's furniture and its own five enquiries.

### Page B — the fault that is actually there

Page B has largely solved the third worry. Almost every card carries an explicit terms-on-offer limit, and they are the best sentences on the page: "no day between \$13.50 and \$16.50 has ever been offered to anyone… so the venue's own sales are evidence at the terms on offer and separate neither band from the other" (card 6); "adults have been free at the school's own ratio throughout this offering's life… so the five enquiries show what buyers did at the terms on offer" (card 20). One ECHO survives (card 1). What has gone wrong is different, and it is the owner's second worry inverted.

1. **Card 22 contradicts itself within one file, and the contradiction resolves in the owner's favour.** Under the options table: "finding 11 records that websites understate group minimums, **which leans it against the no-floor reading rather than with it**." Twelve lines later, on the Recommended line: "the silent cell holds unprinted floors and unprinted no-floors **in unknown proportion**." The same silence is read as directional where the card is careful and as symmetric where the withholding is justified. Only the second reading neutralises a 36-to-3 count against the value the owner ruled on 14 August.
2. **The instrument test is a one-way ratchet.** It says a recommendation that leaves a held value must rest on an instrument that could have shown the held value winning *in the form it is held*. It is never run on the fourteen cards that keep a held value. On the day of 19 September it was run four times — cards 18, 21, 22, 28 — and all four moved off a recommendation that disagreed with the owner. None moved onto one. The priors pass reports the result as an achievement: "no card now recommends against a ruling you have given". On card 22 the ratchet killed a lead that, hours earlier, the same clerks had called a duty: "there is a lead where the withheld line found a tie and **the method requires it to be recommended and its thinness said**."
3. **The ruling sheet tells the owner a card asks nothing of him when the card says the opposite.** Card 18's row reads "nothing: the evidence neither confirms nor overturns your ruling". Card 18's own Recommended line reads: "the margin against the ruling is 5 named venues to 3 on the inside-or-extra leg and **29 to 0** on the coded one; that comparison is untouched by the second coding, and it is the one part of this card that does not move." His \$6-a-child horse show is the one option on that card the corpus rules out, and the sheet row and the order of ruling both file it under things he need not look at.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

**No. The traffic runs one way, and it can be counted.**

Twelve parameters carry a ruling. On none of them does the final line depart from it: five keep it (0, 7, 12, 14, 16) and seven are withheld (13, 18, 21, 22, 23, 27, 28). Every one of the four departures on the set — cards 2, 6, 8 and 26 — sits on a parameter the owner has **not** ruled.

Is a match with his view ever held against a recommendation? No. Cards 0 and 7 spent most of 19 September withheld and turned to agreement only when a blind second coding arrived; nothing was discounted for agreeing with him. Card 14 turned *to* his grain on a count. Agreement is never penalised.

Does a departure pass without the proof a match is asked for? Yes, and here is the pair to put side by side:

- **Card 26 departs** from the term the offering sells under on a whole-text read of an uncoded field, 20 to 11, while the fence-blind clerk carries the held value on its own cut of that same field and the venue's covered space cannot deliver the recommendation at the stated ceiling.
- **Card 28 withholds** on a whole-text read of an uncoded field where the stated postures run 12 to 1 — a wider lead, on the same class of instrument — because the parameter is one the owner ruled.

Same instrument class, same kind of count, opposite treatment; the difference is whether the owner had spoken. That is not the clerks marking his views down. It is the clerks declining to mark them.

One further defect, small to write and large to read. The sheet's legend says "A negative margin means the largest count runs against the recommendation", and prints −38, −40, −16 and −8 elsewhere. Card 1's row prints **"37 observations"** with no sign. Card 1's own Corrections line says: "this card's margin is **−37** observations against the primary band alone." Card 1 is the card the order of ruling calls "the first thing to settle", with five open cards behind it, and on the sheet it reads as the safest confirmation in the set.

Page A is one continuous argument. It has no index, no "your part" column and no ruled/open marking beyond scattered *Ruled:* and *Review:* lines; the only summary is a closing paragraph that mixes rulings, revisions and open items in one block. To find what he must decide he reads all 4,873 words.

Page B's sheet does the job page A never attempts. Thirty rows, four values in the "your part" column, and the split is legible in one pass: **4 rule** (2, 6, 8, 26), **9 confirm** (1, 4, 5, 9, 11, 15, 17, 24, 25), **5 commission or rule blind** (3, 10, 19, 20, 29), **12 nothing** (the ruled set).

Could he rule from the sheet and the order of ruling alone? **Not safely, and not briefly.** Two reasons.

- **Length.** The sheet with its legend is 1,859 words and the order of ruling is 3,434; with the "what passed through" tally that is **5,462 words** — about 600 more than the whole of page A. The order of ruling quotes Joint lines verbatim at length ("Card 22's Joint line states…", "Card 9's Joint line states…") and reads like a transcript. It is not a front page; it is a thirteenth document.
- **Accuracy.** Three rows misdirect him. Card 1's margin carries the wrong sign. Card 18's row says his ruling is neither confirmed nor overturned when the card says 29 to 0 against it. Card 22's row says the same while the card holds 36 to 3 and a fence-blind clerk at 47 to 22 against the ruled value. Every one of the three errs toward leaving a ruling of his alone.

The card faces average 969 words each, which is right for a card he opens on doubt and too long for a card he opens out of duty.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

**Twelve withheld: ten sound, two evasive** (22 and 27).

Of the eighteen cards that recommend, **none should have withheld** — the nine graded THIN all say they are thin, name what would reverse them, and that is no fault. Two are worth a second look for a different reason: card 7 recommends where its own fence-blind clerk lands on the opposite option and the corpus-wide count runs 12 venues against, and card 1's leg is the venue's own invited record.

The real answer is that page B withholds **too little in one direction and too much in the other**. It withholds on two parameters where the evidence does carry an answer against a ruling (22) or carries "do both" (27). And it never once withholds on a parameter the owner has not ruled where a ruled parameter's evidence would have been withheld — cards 6 and 26 are the cases.

## The three changes that would most improve page B for one sitting

1. **Add a fifth value to the "your part" column: *your ruling is contradicted*.** Cards 18, 22 and 27 each hold a count against the owner's ruled value and each currently reads "nothing". Put the count in the row — "your \$6 show: 0 of 60 venues charge a school for a watched display" — and the owner learns in one line what presently costs him a 4,554-word card to find.
2. **Run the instrument test on the cards that keep a held value, and say on the sheet what each keep survived.** As written the test only ever fires where the answer would be a departure, and the sheet that results has no departure on any ruled parameter. Either every held value is tested or none is; a test applied on one side only is not a test.
3. **Cut the order of ruling to one page and fix the sheet's arithmetic.** Drop the verbatim Joint-line quotation — the dependencies are five sentences, not 3,434 words. Correct card 1's margin to −37. Print beside every margin the figure the one-operator test leaves, so the owner can see at a glance that card 6 is 6-to-1 and card 21 is 27-to-26 without opening either.

## One sentence

Page B is a clear improvement on page A for this owner — it puts real options with figures where page A put assertions, it names the terms-on-offer limit on nearly every card where page A read its own five enquiries as the market, and it gives him a sheet he can rule from — but it has acquired the opposite fault, an instrument test and a silence rule that fire only where the answer would contradict him, and I am confident of both halves: the improvement is plain on the face of the two pages, and the one-sidedness is not an impression but a count, four departures on the set and not one of them on a parameter he has ruled.

## LEDGER

**Part 1 — work the project's goal needs that nobody has asked for**

- The one-way ratchet is a methodology bug, not a run bug. `pmf-sage` should state, at the point of temptation, that the instrument test and the one-operator test are run on every card that holds a value, keeping and leaving alike, and that a pass which moves cards off disagreement with the owner and never onto it is a failed pass. The present wording lets a run reach "no card now recommends against a ruling you have given" and report it as quality.
- The card form permits a withholding between two options a card has itself declared non-exclusive (card 27). The form needs a "both" landing, or a rule that options on a card must be mutually exclusive before a withholding is allowed.
- `tools/card-check.py` counts figured options, third derivations and margin units but does not check margin **sign** against the card's own text. Card 1 passes the check with a +37 the card's Corrections line calls −37. A sign check is a few lines and would have caught it.
- The sheet's "your part" column has no value for "a card contradicts your ruling". SAGE should require one; without it the ruled set is invisible to an owner reading only the front matter, which is the reading the page invites.

**Part 2 — things found while doing this work**

- Card 1's ruling-sheet margin reads "37 observations" where the card's Corrections line reads "−37". The order of ruling calls card 1 the first thing to settle and five open cards wait on it.
- Card 22's silent-operator line ("leans it against the no-floor reading") and its Recommended line ("in unknown proportion") read the same caution two incompatible ways in one file.
- Card 18's sheet row and the order of ruling both file the card under "nothing", while the card states 29 to 0 and 5 to 3 against the owner's ruled \$6 horse show. The contradiction reaches him only if he opens the card the sheet tells him not to open.
- Page A's decision 11 justifies a published per-head show with Currumbin's \$30 Koala Connections and Rainforestation's \$23.50 boomerang painting. Page B's card 18 establishes those are contact lines, not spectating: "the lines admission venues do charge apart are contact, not spectating". Page A's horse-show ruling rests on a category error, and the ruling is still live in the product definition.
- Page A's decision 7 ($375 floor) was ruled on cost arithmetic with no market count of any kind; the owner changed it to no-floor on 14 August. Page B's card 22 now holds the count that was missing in August (36 of 184; three of the four nearest catchment sellers at 10) and withholds rather than putting it to him.
- The two second-coding sheets (`second-coding-gathered-session.tsv`, 39 rows; `second-coding-structure.tsv`, 56 rows) are genuine blind recodes with per-venue quotes and notes. They are the best evidence on the run and the mechanism that produced them — code it twice, carry the agreement — is worth making standard rather than incidental.
