# Two decision pages for one wedding offering: a marketer's reading

Written 2026-09-19 for the owner. I have read both pages in full and opened the underlying card files where a claim needed checking. Neither page is mine and neither was written for me. The reading below is blunt on purpose.

**The three tests applied to every card.** (1) Does the recommendation predict what the market needs from meeting notes, staff opinion and the owner's own stated direction rather than from anything a buyer did? (2) Does it treat the owner's view as wrong *because* it is the owner's view? (3) Does it read what this venue already sells as what the market wants, when every sale on record was made at the only terms the venue offered?

Verdict key, as the brief sets it: **EARNED** · **ECHO** · **CONTRARIAN** · **THIN** · **UNSUPPORTED** · **WITHHELD-SOUND** · **WITHHELD-EVASIVE**.
Basis key: **M** market behaviour · **B** this venue's buyers · **D** the venue's documents, opinions, plans or present arrangement · **N** nothing stated.

## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Fault 1: predicting the market from meeting notes, staff opinion and the owner's inner thoughts

Both pages are, on the whole, clean here, and both are explicit about the fence. The three worst residues:

1. **Page A card 8, the October reading.** "S12 offers two readings it declines to choose between, that October demand is lost or that October enquiries are being converted into November dates by the venue" — the second reading is supplied by S15, a templated subject line, and the card then builds a recommendation ("recommend the attention go to October instead") on a mechanism nobody measured.
2. **Page A card 2, the BYO half.** The whole parameter rests on two opinions in direct conflict. A platform account manager calls BYO "one of the most powerful selling points in the wedding market". The venue's own weddings lead describes a shift the other way, to "a one-stop shop". The card is right that "nothing in this run separates them", but it still carries the parameter as a live ruling rather than as a named gap.
3. **Page B card 1.** The buyer of record is chosen on a field the venue's own form populates: "the operator's own type field, 506 Wedding against 8 IntimateWedding and 5 Elopement". A CRM category list is a statement of how the venue sorts enquiries, not of who is enquiring.

Against those, credit where it is due: Page A card 28 refuses the owner's own stated direction to the owner's face ("Recommending it would be exactly the is-to-ought move the run was warned against"), card 21 records the coach's premium recommendation and gives it no weight, and card 25 refuses to build on the owner's own 1-per-cent figure. That is the right instinct in all three places.

### Fault 2: treating the owner's view as wrong because it is his

Rare on both pages, and Page B is close to immune because the priors clerk's own note states the rule: "A match is not a fault and a departure is not a merit." The three residues:

1. **Page A card 8, the day band.** The venue's two-band structure is attacked as "built for a book that does not look like this one" on evidence the structure itself would produce, namely the venue's own midweek-heavy book. That is a departure the evidence does not carry.
2. **Page A card 4.** The floor is to rise partly because "a floor set below every comparator with no derivation is still not a positioning choice". Having no written derivation is a fault in the filing, not a reason the number is wrong.
3. **Page B card 3, in the other direction.** Here the third derivation departs and the card overrules it to keep the venue's own ladder. This is fault 2 inverted and it is the one place on Page B where a match survives evidence that should have unseated it.

### Fault 3: reading what the venue already sells as what the market wants

This is where the two pages separate most sharply, and it is Page B's clearest gain.

1. **Page A card 10, the bundling half.** "the new evidence argues for caution rather than for bundling, because the attach rate is two in ten." A room has never been inside a tier; a two-in-ten attach rate on a separately quoted product cannot say what a bundled one would attach.
2. **Page A card 4.** "the operator's own small bookings bill $2,180 to $4,700" is offered as demand evidence for a higher floor. Those totals were built on a ladder capped at $3,700 at that end. Page B's card 6 rules on the same parameter and states exactly the limit Page A omits.
3. **Page A card 23.** "a confirmed venue tour multiplies conversion eightfold" carries the whole recommendation, while the page's own gap register (card 20, item 9) records that S16 "cannot separate" selection from treatment. The caveat is in the register and not on the card that uses the number.

Page B's answer to this fault is systematic rather than occasional. The "one fact, several cards" section states that on cards 7, 14, 17 and 23 "the corrections pass withdrew it as a leg rather than counted it, because those weddings were sold under a published ceiling, a published ladder and a whole-site claim the alternatives were never offered against", and cards 24 and 25 each say in their own recommending sentence why the venue's book could not have shown the runner-up winning. That is the discipline the owner's third worry asks for, applied as a rule and not as a remark.

## Does Page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

**Is a match ever held against a recommendation?** No. The opposite: the page states the rule and applies it. Card 18's own status line records that "The recommended line was rewritten on this pass to the other option, on-site accommodation stated and not priced, which carries on the settled catchment count once the venue's own unpublished-rate leg is struck" — the correction moved *toward* what the venue already does, because the market count said so once the internal leg was removed. Cards 24 and 25 keep the venue's mechanism and go out of their way to find a margin elsewhere rather than let the match look like the reason. Card 3's corrections note refuses to strike the recommendation merely for matching: "a match is not a fault and a departure is not a merit". Twelve of the fifteen carried recommendations keep or leave a held value and none is penalised for it.

**Does a departure ever pass without the proof a match is asked for?** Once, cleanly, and it is worth the owner's attention. **Card 4** faces an admitted dead tie in the market — "the catchment splits 7 to 7" — and breaks it on the operator's own enquiry record, the all-inclusive asks and the 21.6 per cent food-and-beverage share. That is the same class of internal leg the corrections pass struck on cards 7, 14, 17 and 23. It survives here, and the difference is that card 4's recommendation *departs* from the terms on sale rather than matching them. The leg is a fair one on its merits — enquiry text is buyer behaviour, not venue preference — but it is not held to the test a matching leg is held to, and the page does not say why.

A second, softer instance: **card 26** departs from the group tour on a register column read wider than its own coded variable, and admits "on the coded appointment variable alone the two stand level at 9 sections apiece". A matching recommendation resting on a 9-to-9 tie would have been withheld.

**Could he rule from the sheet and the order of ruling alone, at 4,472 words?**

For the fifteen carried cards, yes. Each row gives the question, the recommendation in a clause, a margin and whether it keeps or leaves what the venue holds; a yes or a no is possible on the row, and the order of ruling tells him which to take first and what each one silently settles.

For the eighteen withheld, no, and this is the sheet's real limit. Those rows print two options and a blank margin. The row carries no evidence at all, and the owner is being asked to choose between, say, "a ceiling between 101 and 149 guests" and "a ceiling of 150 guests or more" with nothing in front of him. The order of ruling's bulleted list of the eighteen (about 800 words inside the 4,472) names the *observation that would carry each* but not the evidence either way. So a realistic sitting is: rule fifteen from the sheet, then either open eighteen cards (roughly 700–1,000 words each, ~16,000 words of the 30,650 in the card body) or commission eighteen observations and rule nothing.

Page A's equivalent sitting is shorter to read and softer in what it produces: the one-page table plus notes is 2,890 words and every row carries a recommendation, but eleven of the twenty-eight rows carry a gate, so the owner rules a recommendation and a condition at once.

## Does Page B withhold too much, or too little?

**Of the eighteen withheld: fourteen sound, four evasive.** The four, cards 7, 13, 20 and 30, share one shape: the card's own text names a count that carries one of its two options, and the card withholds anyway. Card 30 is the sharpest (16 of 72 against 2 of 72, on the card's own words "the only V28 field on double figures"); card 13's is unanimous (three of three catchment sellers of daylight attach a size condition); card 7's is eighteen sections wide; card 20's is 27 to 7. In each case the honest line was a recommendation with the observation named as what would reverse it, which is a form the page already uses elsewhere.

**Of the fifteen that recommend, one should have withheld and two are close.** Card 3 should have withheld: the market count runs to a two-step ladder, the third derivation "finds no instance anywhere in either corpus of a 65-guest rung", and the card keeps four rungs on the venue's own guest distribution. Card 1's margin is a venue-side field and its own Further test argues for ruling both buyers, which is a withholding in all but name. Card 29 recommends presence-and-accuracy while conceding "nothing in either derivation says a reconciled listing wins anything" — the presence half carries, the accuracy half does not.

Net: the page withholds about the right amount and withholds the wrong four. Correcting all seven would leave roughly 15 withheld and 18 carried, which is a better sitting than either page currently offers.

## The three changes that would most improve Page B for one sitting

1. **Put the leg into the sheet.** The margin column today runs "6 catchment rivals", "493 enquiries", "20 bookings", "6,210 searches a month" and "12 register sections" side by side, and the owner cannot tell from the row whether a recommendation rests on rivals, on searchers, or on this venue's own book — which is the exact distinction his two worries turn on. One extra column reading M / B / D per row, or a margin written as "20 bookings (own book)", makes the page answer the question it was written to answer without opening a card.
2. **Close the four evasive withholdings, and put the third derivation's option on the sheet where it beats both.** Cards 7, 13, 20 and 30 name a lead and decline to state it; say it, and name the observation that would reverse it. Cards 28 and 30 withhold between two options while their own third derivation lands on a third — a real-wedding gallery at 54 of 72, a stated deliverable at 33 of 72 — that never reaches the sheet. An owner ruling between the two printed options on those rows is ruling with the best-attested answer out of sight.
3. **Give each withheld row one line of evidence.** Eighteen rows currently carry a blank margin. A clause apiece ("3 rivals to 2, one wide"; "16 of 72 against 2 of 72"; "a population apart, not a count apart") turns the sheet from a list of questions back into a sheet he can rule from, and would let him see at a glance which withholdings are ties and which are refusals.

A fourth, smaller: Page B drops Page A's four documentary fix cards (the security template that has lost its evening-with-alcohol qualifier, the dead pointer, the glamping line still bookable, the spelling), the gap register and the seven capability questions for the Guest Experience Manager and Wedding Coordinator. Those are real, cheap, dated work items, and an owner who reads only Page B never meets them.

## One sentence

Page B is an improvement on Page A for this owner, because it separates what the evidence carries from what it does not, strikes the venue's own book as a leg wherever the terms censored it, and gives every option a figure and a source; and I am fairly but not wholly confident, because it buys that discipline by handing eighteen decisions back with no recommendation, four of which its own text could have answered, and by dropping the documentary fixes and capability questions Page A carries.

**Part 1: work the project's goal needs that nobody has asked for**

- The 33-card set on Page B drops Page A's documentary fix cards (16–19), its gap register (20) and its seven Q-numbered capability questions. Whether that is deliberate scoping or loss needs an owner-visible decision; the card-set note treats the fixes as "corrections to documents, not options between which a market chooses", which is a reason to file them elsewhere, not a reason for them to disappear.
- The ruling sheet has no column distinguishing a market leg from the venue's own book. Since the whole run's credibility rests on that distinction, it belongs in the artefact the owner actually reads.
- No card on either page carries an owner-facing cost or revenue consequence for the ruling. He is being asked to choose between a $7,000–$9,500 band and a $13,200–$18,950 band with no line saying what either does to the book.

**Part 2: things found while doing this work**

- Page B's withheld-card form has a structural hole: where the third derivation lands on an option that is neither of the two the card withholds between (cards 28 and 30), that option never reaches the sheet. The sheet's "third derivation: differs" tells the owner nothing about *what* it differs to.
- Four withheld cards (7, 13, 20, 30) state a count in their own recommending sentence that carries one of their two options. Whatever check produced the 18 withholdings did not test a card's stated counts against its own withholding.
- Page B card 4 breaks an admitted 7-to-7 market tie on the operator's own enquiry record, which is the same class of leg the corrections pass struck on cards 7, 14, 17 and 23. The corrections rule appears to be applied to matching recommendations and not to departing ones; if that asymmetry is intended, it should be stated, and if not, card 4 needs re-reading.
- Page A card 20 item 9 records that S16's eightfold tour multiple "cannot separate" selection from treatment, while Page A card 23 uses that multiple as its whole leg without the caveat. A caveat that lives only in the gap register does not travel to the card that spends the number.
- The priors-pass table is now stale by its own admission, "its Matched column reads against recommendations that no longer all stand", and it sits beside Page B as though it described the current set. An owner reading it as current will mis-read cards 2, 5, 7, 8, 10–15, 17–21, 23, 28, 30 and 32.
