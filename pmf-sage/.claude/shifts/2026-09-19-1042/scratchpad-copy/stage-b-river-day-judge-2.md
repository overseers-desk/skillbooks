# Two decision pages, judged card by card

A marketer's reading for the owner, 19 September 2026. Second independent judge.

Page A: `3-decisions/2026-08-10-decision-review.md`, eighteen decisions.
Page B: `3-decisions/review.md`, thirty cards, with `3-decisions/priors-pass.md` beside it.

**What I was testing.** Three faults, in the owner's own framing. (1) Predicting the market from meeting notes and the owner's inner thoughts instead of from reality. (2) The mirror: treating "the owner believes A" as a reason A is wrong. (3) The one between them: reading what the venue already sells as what the market wants, when a sale made at the only terms ever offered cannot show what a buyer would have done at other terms.

**Verdict key.** EARNED — the evidence shown carries the recommendation, whichever way it points. ECHO — it agrees with the venue chiefly because the venue holds it (sales at the only terms on offer included). CONTRARIAN — it departs from the owner's view without evidence that carries the departure. THIN — it says honestly that the evidence ties or is missing; no fault. UNSUPPORTED — a value asserted with no instrument behind it.

**Rests-on key.** M market behaviour (comparable operators, local rivals, what people search for) · B this venue's own buyers (enquiries, refusals, stalls, sales) · D the venue's documents, opinions, plans or present arrangement · N nothing stated.

### Page A: the three clearest cases of each fault

**Predicting from inner thoughts (fault 1)**

1. Card 1: "those buyers book animals in hand, not the word 'farm'; the demand transfers to any animal-contact day." The only observed standing demand in the catchment for a farm-day format is explained away by a claim about buyer psychology with nothing behind it.
2. Card 1 again: "A pure destination brand with no venue name ('Coomera River School Days') tested better for category ownership" — a test is asserted, and no test exists in the run.
3. Card 7: "the single-class band of 25–30 children, which is the catchment's basic booking unit." A market fact stated without a figure, doing load-bearing work under a dollar floor.

**Reading the venue's own arrangement as the market's answer (fault 3)**

1. Card 4: the base is "the nature tour's full component set along the route" — the venue's existing walking tour, now the compulsory core of the product, with the prevalence table used only to strike things out.
2. Card 12: "the menu's five station sites support four rotating groups with one site always clear for transitions" — the site map fixes a 100-child ceiling that the market-side reading puts at 50–60.
3. Card 13: the Years 7–9 channel exists "because one session (card 4's Pasos) scales up an age band on existing assets" — the age scope follows the asset, and the pipeline that supports it was never invited to include a secondary cohort.

**Treating the owner's view as suspect (fault 2)**

Only one clear case, card 1, and it is mild: the committed farm-and-nature position is dismissed as hedging — "Hedges rather than decides" — before its evidence is weighed. Page A's characteristic failure is the opposite of fault 2: it agrees with the venue too easily on cards 4, 12 and 13, not too reluctantly.

### Page B: the three clearest cases of each fault

2. Card 9's ceiling. "100 children is the dining deck's seating and is above the 55 m clear circle's comfortable rotation." The venue's furniture fixes a number the market-side reading puts at 50 to 60, and the Recommended line spends its argument on the floor instead.

**Departing from the owner without proof (fault 2)**

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

**Is a match ever held against a recommendation?** At the card level, no. Cards 0, 7, 12, 13, 16 and 22 all agree with a ruling and all keep their recommendation; none is weakened for agreeing, and cards 12 and 16 record the blind derivation landing on the owner's ruling as *evidence about the market* — "the fence-blind derivation landing where he ruled is evidence about the market, which is why the chip reads derived" (card 28). That is the correct treatment and it is stated as a principle.

At the process level, yes, once — and the owner should know about it. `priors-pass.md` records that `tools/card-check.py` **refuses the whole set** because "12 of 30 recommendations match a prior with no third derivation recorded, above the one-in-ten line that returns a set to the derivation clerks". Matching a prior is the trigger; departing from one is not. The gate is asymmetric in design. In practice the asymmetry was neutralised, because page B re-derived all thirty blind — "12 recommendations landed on a value an internal document already held, and each was re-derived blind by a fresh clerk; 18 more were re-derived for a reason each card's Corrections line states" — so every card carried the same burden. The rule is unfair; the run did not act on the unfairness. If the one-in-ten line is ever enforced literally, it will push clerks to manufacture disagreement.

**Does a departure ever pass without the proof a match is asked for?** Yes, twice, and they are my two CONTRARIAN rows. Card 23 narrows the owner's ruling on an instrument the card's own re-read calls a tie. Card 18 departs from the owner's ruling on a field with 60% coder agreement and 124 of 184 pages silent, and leans on a line standing unsold at terms the page elsewhere says prove nothing. Compare card 21, which also departs from a ruling and brings 75-against-39, an Australian slice of 16-against-2, an independent blind derivation on the same figure, and a mechanism that predicts this venue specifically. Card 21 is the standard; 18 and 23 fall short of it.

So: page B is fair to agreement and slightly loose with disagreement — the mirror of what one would fear, but a smaller error than page A's, which is loose with agreement.

## The three changes that would most improve page B for one sitting

1. **Move the closing section to the front and make it the ruling sheet.** Everything an owner needs to route his attention is already written in "Beyond the Joint lines" — the nine two-reading cards, the four that contradict a ruling, the ordering constraints, the four rulings that already decide cards he is about to read. Put it first, as a one-page table with a link per card, and the 49,597 words become a reference rather than a reading list.
2. **Put a three-word status flag on every card head.** `## Card 9 · Group bounds — OPEN · two readings` versus `## Card 12 · What the children do — RULED · agrees`. Today the Ruled line is at the bottom of a 1,650-word card and the divergence is buried in a Corrections paragraph. Eighteen cards are open and twelve are not, and the owner should be able to skip twelve without reading them.
3. **Hold cards 18, 23 and 27 to card 21's standard before they go to him.** Card 21 shows what a departure from a ruling should look like: a corpus count, an Australian slice, an independent blind derivation, and a mechanism specific to this venue. Card 18 departs on a 60%-agreement field, card 23 on an instrument its own re-read calls a tie, and card 27 recommends on a figure of one quote and one booking. Either strengthen the instrument or mark the card as not carrying — the owner should not have to discover the weakness in paragraph six of a Corrections line.

A fourth, if there is room: cards 19 and 21 rest on the same food count and cards 0, 7, 13 and 29 on the same structure census. The page says so itself — "four margins, one count" — but says it at the end. An owner reading card by card will bank four confirmations that are one fact.

- Nothing in either page tells the owner which of the 18 open cards cost money to get wrong. A ruling on card 2 (the name) and a ruling on card 20 (adult ratio) arrive at the same weight on the page; one is a brand decision with a search collision under it and the other gates nothing at a free-entry site, as card 20 itself says.
