# Two decision pages for one offering — second judge's reading

Reviewed for the owner, 19 September 2026. Page A = `3-decisions/cards-price-and-composition.md`, `cards-viewing-enquiry-and-presentation.md`, `cards-fixes-and-gaps.md` (29 cards, dated 7 September). Page B = `3-decisions/review.md` (33 cards, dated 19 September) with `priors-pass.md` beside it.

Three tests, applied card by card:

- **T1** — does the card predict what the market wants from meeting notes, staff opinion and the owner's inner thoughts rather than from anything outside the business?
- **T2** — does the card treat the owner's own view as evidence *against* a position (if he believes A, A must be wrong)?
- **T3** — does the card read what the venue already sells as what the market wants, when sales made at the only terms on offer cannot show what buyers would have done at other terms?

Verdicts: **EARNED** (the evidence shown carries the recommendation, whichever way it points) · **ECHO** (it agrees with the venue chiefly because the venue holds it, sales at the only terms on offer included) · **CONTRARIAN** (it departs from the venue's held position without evidence that carries the departure) · **THIN** (it says honestly that the evidence ties or is missing — no fault) · **UNSUPPORTED** (the stated legs do not carry the stated recommendation).

Basis codes: **M** market behaviour · **B** this venue's own buyers · **D** the venue's documents, opinions, plans or present arrangement · **N** nothing stated.

## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Page A

**ECHO — none.** Page A recommends changing what is on sale on 24 of 29 cards and withholds on the rest; there is no card where it recommends the status quo because the status quo exists. The nearest thing is card 27's "leave the home page title alone", and that is carried by a denominator: "69 per cent of the market does what the operator does."

**CONTRARIAN**
1. Card 22 — "rule that a viewing must be available on at least one non-Saturday day", against a written policy with a stated reason, on a leg the card itself calls a prediction: "The card predicts that a couple whose wedding will be midweek is the couple most likely to be free to view midweek."
2. Card 11 — recommends abandoning group-tours-only while conceding the cost the policy exists to avoid is unknown: "no document this run reached prices a one-couple viewing, so the capability leg has a hole in exactly the place the decision needs it."
3. Card 8's day half — "recommend the day band be re-derived rather than kept, because a band that discounts Thursday was built for a book that does not look like this one." The book looks like that partly because Thursday is discounted.

**UNSUPPORTED** — the same three; page A has no fourth.

**T3, reading sales at the only terms as market appetite**
1. Card 1 — "the mean realised venue-hire line of \$4,834 and the three lines above \$6,000 say the top of the published ladder is below the top of the trade."
2. Card 8 — "The operator is already, in practice, a mid-week wedding venue for a majority of what it sells, and its two-band structure prices that majority into the cheaper band."
3. Card 4 — "the operator's own small bookings bill \$2,180 to \$4,700, against a published elopement floor of \$1,700."

On all three, page A stops short of the ruling the contaminated leg would support (card 1 withholds the step; card 4 leaves the step to the owner) — which is why none of them reads as UNSUPPORTED overall.

### Page B

**ECHO**
1. Card 21 — the recommendation is the internal security term word for word ("evening events with alcohol and 60 or more guests, or any event with 100 or more … at \$75 an hour") on a margin of −29, with the second reader declining to recommend at all.
2. Card 3 — the four-step ladder the offering sells, on the book that ladder produced, where the second reader "finds no instance anywhere in either corpus of a 65-guest rung."
3. Card 1 — "margin: 493 enquiries", which is the operator's own Sonas type field, a categorisation the operator wrote, not a behaviour a buyer showed.

Card 20 is a fourth, and the most avoidable: the recommendation restates a clause already in every package while the market-carried delta (publish the condition) is left in a footnote.

**CONTRARIAN** — none available. The priors pass establishes that "No **Ruled:** line carries an owner's decision anywhere in the closed set", so no card can depart from a ruling. The nearest equivalents are departures from the terms on sale that the evidence does not carry: card 30 (2 of 72), card 28 (a margin the card reduces to zero), card 15 (against 8 operators to 0).

**UNSUPPORTED** — twelve; the three sharpest:
1. Card 2 — "\$7,000 to \$9,500 is the band this venue already bills inside and does not publish", at a margin of −1, where the second reader recommends \$13,200–\$18,950 and the billing figures are invoices under a \$5,600 headline.
2. Card 10 — a firm computable total, recommended at a margin of −22 in a market where 23 of 34 publish nothing at all, on a leg the card describes as unable to "say what a firm total would earn."
3. Card 30 — a stated reply period, 2 of 72, recommended over the named contact at 16 of 72 and the named deliverable at 33 of 72, while the operator holds no measurement of its own response time.

**T3**
1. Card 2, as above.
2. Card 8 — "13 of 25 secured weddings are not on a weekend, six of them Thursdays … so the venue already is a mid-week venue in practice."
3. Card 12 — November's 7 of 28 read as a season worth a premium, on the same page that records the venue creating that concentration for free: "a templated 'Jacaranda Season' subject line … including to a couple whose own desired date is 2 May."

**What page B does better than page A on this fault, and where it stops.** Page B is the only one of the two that names the fault. Every negative-margin card carries a paragraph of the form "those sales could not have shown the alternative at all", and the reasoning in them is exact — card 24's is the model: "Thirty-one weddings held on a deposit is evidence of what buyers did at the terms offered and says nothing about a hold that was never offered." Card 8's is the sharpest thing either page contains, because it distinguishes correctly: the day mix *is* evidence, "because both day bands were on sale throughout and buyers chose between them", while the year band's absence is not. That is real craft, and page A has nothing like it.

Where it stops is the place that matters to a reader. The void sits in the **Corrections** line, several hundred words below the **Recommended** line, and the Recommended line is never changed to reflect it. On twelve cards the owner reads a bolded recommendation with a margin, and then, if he keeps reading, a paragraph explaining that the margin's leg proves nothing. Nothing in the page's form obliges the two to be reconciled. Card 32 shows what that costs: the card concludes it "should be ruled open rather than ruled either way" and still prints a recommendation at the top.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

**Stated policy: yes, and it is stated well.** `priors-pass.md` is explicit — "A match is not a fault and a departure is not a merit" — and applies the test mechanically, "inside the prior's stated range, or the prior's own word", without touching any card's wording.

**Is a match ever held against a recommendation? Yes, by the tooling, and structurally.** `tools/card-check.py` "refuses the set on that ground alone, since 24 of 33 is above the one-in-ten threshold". That is a rule that treats agreement with the venue, in aggregate, as a defect requiring remedial work — a bright line at one in ten with no evidential content. If the venue's held values were mostly right, a compliant page would be impossible. In practice the remedy was applied to all 33 cards, so the outcome was symmetric; the trigger was not.

Two milder asymmetries follow from it. On four cards where the derivations disagree (13, 17, 18, 21) the chip is left unstamped — "the stamp is left as the priors pass set it for the owner to read against both" — and all four are cards that agree with the venue. And the negative-margin void, though correctly applied to any card whose leg is the venue's own book (it lands on departing cards 2, 10, 14 and 23 as well as on agreeing cards 19, 20, 21, 24 and 25), strips the leg from the agreeing cards while leaving the departing cards their market counts, however thin, as the stated margin.

**Does a departure pass without the proof a match is asked for? Yes, on five cards.** Card 30 departs to a 2-of-72 practice and the second reader recommends something else at 33 of 72; the departure stands as the recommendation. Card 2 departs upward and the second reader departs further; both readings are kept and neither is tested against the fact that no buyer was offered either. Card 5 departs to a shape "evidenced nowhere in the corpus". Card 12 departs to a season band the only market evidence inverts. Card 28's departure survives a margin the card itself reduces to zero. None of these five is asked to show what a matching card is asked to show — an independent market-side derivation landing on the same place.

**One accounting error worth the owner's minute.** Three rows of the priors table read "Matched: Yes" while the row's own last column records the opposite substantive relation — card 32 ("old 24 explicitly declined to rule a cadence; this card rules one"), card 24 ("old 15 preferred the opposite"), card 13 ("old 6 recommended no ruling at all"). The Matched column is measuring against the *held value* and the last column against the *earlier card*; a reader skimming the Matched column will draw the wrong conclusion on all three. The summary paragraph does distinguish the two counts, 24 of 33 against 20 of 24, but the table does not.

**Verdict on fairness:** the page's own conduct is fair and its reasoning about agreement is better than page A's. The rule sitting behind it is not fair, and the page inherits one visible consequence: the cards that agree with the venue arrive with their legs stripped and their chips unstamped, and the cards that depart do not.

## The three changes that would most improve page B

**1. Move the void up into the recommendation.** Every card whose margin rests on the venue's own book carries, hundreds of words below, a paragraph beginning "those sales could not have shown the alternative at all". That paragraph is the best writing on either page and it is in the wrong place. Either the Recommended line states the margin *and* what the margin cannot show, in the same sentence, or the recommendation drops to "open" as card 32 already concludes it should. Twelve cards change character when that paragraph is read; today a skimming owner will not read it.

**2. One front table, 33 rows, one page.** Card · recommendation in eight words · does it change what is on sale or confirm it · is its leg the market or this venue's own book · did the second reader agree, diverge, or decline. Every one of those five facts is already computed somewhere in the file or in `priors-pass.md`. Assembled, it is the page the owner would actually rule from, and it would surface in one glance the four ECHO cards, the twelve unsupported ones and the seventeen with two live readings.

**3. Cut the option tables to the two contenders.** The Recommended line already names the runner-up on every card, and the owner is choosing between two on almost all of them. Fourteen options on card 1 and six on several others are where the 54,000 words sit. Keep the recommended option and the runner-up in full, list the rest as one line each with their figure, and the page loses perhaps 40 per cent of its length without losing a single decision. The discarded options can stay in the card files that already hold them.

## Is page B an improvement on page A for this owner?

Page B is a better-instrumented and more honest evidence base — every option carries a figure, every recommendation carries a margin and a second independent reading, and it is the only one of the two pages that names the sales-at-the-only-terms problem at all — but it is a worse decision document, because its form obliges a recommendation on every card and so converts four honest withholdings and twelve uncarried positions into bolded advice, and it asks half again as much reading to do it; on balance I would rule from page B's cards with page A's card 6, 19, 21 and 28 discipline imposed on them, and I am confident about the instrumentation and only moderately confident about the net, because a great deal turns on whether the owner reads the Corrections lines.

## LEDGER

**Part 1 — gaps against the goal of this review**

- No third judge exists on the split cards. Page B's eleven, and page A's card 8, carry half an earned recommendation and half an unearned one. Whoever consolidates the two judges' readings should decide whether a card may hold one verdict at all, or whether the verdict belongs to each half of a recommendation.
- Neither page has been read against what it costs to be wrong. Twelve UNSUPPORTED cards on page B are not equally dangerous: card 2 is a price move on the offering's headline figure, card 28 is a brochure insert. Nothing in either page ranks its recommendations by the size of the mistake, and an owner with one sitting should be told which three cards he must not get wrong.
- The verdict counts here are one reader's. A count of EARNED against UNSUPPORTED is a judgment call on roughly eight borderline cards (page B 4, 7, 14, 19, 24, 25, 29; page A 1, 4). Two judges' counts should be compared card by card, not headline to headline.

**Part 2 — found while doing the work**

- **`priors-pass.md`'s Matched column will mislead on at least three rows.** Cards 13, 24 and 32 read "Matched: Yes" while the same row's final column records the earlier card recommending the opposite or declining to rule. Two different comparisons share one column heading. The summary paragraph distinguishes them (24 of 33 against 20 of 24); the table does not, and the table is what gets read.
- **`tools/card-check.py`'s one-in-ten threshold is a rule against agreement as such.** It "refuses the set on that ground alone, since 24 of 33 is above the one-in-ten threshold." A tool that refuses a set for agreeing with the venue too often is the owner's second worry ("thinking if owner believe A, A must be incorrect") written into the build. If the intent is to force independent derivation, the trigger should be per-card and unconditional, not a rate.
- **Card 32 is a form failure, not a judgment failure.** The card reasons its way to "this card should be ruled open rather than ruled either way" and the template still requires a **Recommended:** line, so it prints one at a margin of −66. The page form needs a "recommendation withheld" value, which page A's cards 6, 19 and 21 use to good effect.
- **Page B drops two gates page A had built and does not say why.** Page A's card 15 gated a published response period on "one month of measured response time from the weddings inbox"; page B's card 30 recommends the period with no gate and no measurement in hand. Page A's card 10 warned against bundling accommodation on a "two-in-ten attach rate"; page B's card 18 carries no attach-rate figure at all. Both are regressions in a later document and both should be reconciled before either page is ruled from.
- **The two pages disagree outright on at least four parameters and neither says so.** Season premium (A21 withholds, B12 recommends a band), quoting basis (A3 recommends the venue-hire total, B5 recommends a venue-owned per-head figure), directory spend (A14 defers for want of attribution, B23 rules against the paid channel), response promise (A15 prefers a hold term and gates the period, B30 recommends the period). The owner is holding two documents about one offering; a one-page reconciliation of where they contradict each other is work nobody has been asked for and he cannot rule without.
- **Page A's summary file is 35,952 words for no decisions of its own.** If page B supersedes page A's card set, that file is 36,000 words of reading with no ruling line in it. Somebody should say whether it is still live.
