# Two decision pages for one wedding offering: a marketer's review

Written 2026-09-19 for the owner. I have read page A's one-page table, its five notes and its joint-test
map in full, and its cards 1, 1A, 2 to 15, 16 to 20, 21 to 28. I have read page B's ruling sheet, order of
ruling and counts in full and every card's printed face, and opened the card files behind cards 2, 3, 4, 6,
7, 9, 10, 12, 20, 23, 25 and 28, plus `priors-pass.md`, `card-check.txt` and the eight top-up counts in
`0-comparables/findings-top-up.md`. Neither page is mine. Nothing here is a ruling.

Three tests run through the whole review, the owner's two and the one that sits between them:

- **(i)** does the work predict the market from meeting notes and the owner's own thinking?
- **(ii)** does it treat "the owner believes A" as evidence against A?
- **(iii)** does it read what the venue already sells as what the market wants, when sales taken at the only
  terms on offer cannot show what a buyer would have done at other terms?

Test (iii) is the one that decides most of this review. Both pages know about it. Page B has a name for it and a mechanism. It calls it the censored leg. It uses it well on eight cards and
badly on three. Page A has no such mechanism, and on two cards it walks straight into the fault.

## Part 3. The clearest cases of each fault

### Page A

**ECHO, one case: card 1A.** "The mean realised venue-hire line across the ten Sonas-native Completed
weddings is \$4,834 (card 1), which sits between Classic's own two figures of \$4,200 and \$4,800." The card
then concedes the whole point, in its own words: "the coincidence of figures is a coincidence of figures."
It uses the coincidence anyway, two paragraphs later, as the reason to keep the rung coupled to card 1. Every one of those ten
weddings was sold off this ladder. A mean drawn from a ladder cannot testify for the ladder.

**UNSUPPORTED, one case: card 8.** "recommend the day band be re-derived rather than kept, because a band
that discounts Thursday was built for a book that does not look like this one." What the book shows is that
13 of 25 couples took the cheaper band. That is what a discount does. The card has no observation of anyone
declining Saturday at the Friday-to-Sunday figure, and none of anyone taking Thursday at a Saturday figure.
Its own buyer test half-admits it: B1's \$282 a head is offered as proof the discount was unnecessary, and
B1 bought a Thursday *with* the discount in place.

**THIN, three cases: cards 13, 22 and 23**, each of which announces its own thinness. Card 13: "**C-series leg,
thin by construction.**" Card 22: "Nothing here measures attendance by day, so this remains a real argument."
Card 23: "The card should be ruled on that basis or not at all." No fault in any of the three; they are
listed because an owner counting his minutes should know which three recommendations are carried by less.

**No CONTRARIAN on page A.** Card 28 is the test, and it passes: the owner's own stated AI-content direction
is chipped as a dated statement, recommended in its measurable half and withheld in the other, with the
refusal reasoned against the owner rather than against his standing: "the wedding section already runs
1,449 words on its landing page against a market median of 575... so a recommendation to write more wedding
content would be recommending more of what the operator already has most of." That is disagreement earned.

### Page B

**ECHO, three cases.**

1. **Card 1**, the largest margin on the sheet: "the count lead is the operator's own type field, 506
   Wedding against 8 IntimateWedding and 5 Elopement of 519 events". That field is filled by this venue's
   own staff, against this venue's own tier names, on a ladder whose small rungs are its cheapest. It is a

   prints "11 of 72 coded comparables" as the margin for keeping the venue's own mechanism.

**CONTRARIAN, one case: card 7**, and it is the sharpest single fault on either page. The card recommends

**THIN, three cases: cards 12, 25 and 29**, all self-declared, all no fault. Card 29's is the most useful to

## Part 4. Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

**Is a match ever held against a recommendation?** Once, and it matters: **card 7**. On the priors pass the
card agreed with the earlier card and with the figures the venue already quotes; after three corrections
passes it recommends abolishing the tier. Nothing in the new evidence is new. B10's word count, F22's five
priced rivals and I7's international rarity were all held before. What turned it is a change of counting
rule, not a change of fact. I cannot show the turn was made *because* the venue held the value; the change
of rule is general and applies to cards pointing both ways. But card 7 is where that rule does the most
damage, and the sheet does not tell the owner that on the only instrument that separates postures his
recommendation has no supporter at all.

Otherwise, no. The method's own statement, that "a match is not a fault and a departure is not a merit",
is respected in every mechanic I can audit. All 33 cards took a third derivation from the market side alone
whichever way they had landed. Nine keeping cards and nine leaving cards were put to the same test.

**Does a departure pass without the proof a match is asked for?** Structurally, no: all nine leaving rows
carry the "seen by:" clause naming the instrument that could have shown the held value winning and did not,
and I checked all nine. That is a real and unusual discipline, and it is more than the keeping rows are
asked for.

But there is one asymmetry, and it is arithmetic rather than intent. The counting rule reads "a lead one
operator coded the other way would erase is a tie". It is applied evenly, and its *effect* is not even. It
punishes contested evidence on a wide base and rewards unanimity on a narrow one:

- **Card 12 carries** a recommendation to cut the venue's own prices, on a base of **four** operators at 3
  to 0. One recoded leaves 2 to 1, so the lead survives. "Two operators coded the other way would erase it,
  and the base is four."
- **Card 2 withholds** the venue's headline price on a base of **thirty-four**, at 3 to 2. One recoded
  reverses the sign.
- **Card 4 withholds** on a base of thirty-four at 7 to 7, with a 16-unit international lead sitting beside
  it that the run treats as a carrying leg on cards 24, 27, 28, 30 and 31.

So the thinnest evidence on the sheet produces a ruling and some of the thickest produces a withholding. It
is consistent, and it is the wrong way round.

## Part 6. Does page B withhold too much or too little?

**Too much, and in the wrong places.** Of 15 withheld cards, I read **12 as sound and 3 as evasive** (6, 11,
20). That ratio is defensible. The distribution is not:

| area | cards | withheld |
|---|---|---|
| Price, composition and terms (0–21) | 22 | **14** |
| Presentation and enquiry (22–32) | 11 | **1** |

Every card in Area C carries except card 32. Fourteen of the eighteen cards that touch what a wedding costs,
what it contains and what the terms are, withhold. The owner commissioned a run to decide a wedding offer
and page B hands him a settled gallery, a settled viewing route, a settled enquiry form and a settled
testimonial policy, and withholds the price, the coverage, the ceiling, the day band, the money terms, the
child rate, the length of the day and the exclusivity claim. Those are the cheap questions answered and the
expensive ones deferred. It is honest deferral, and it is still deferral.

**Three withheld cards hold a ruling they decline to make:** 6, 11 and 20, quoted above.

**Four withheld cards hold a partial finding that is rulable today and is buried in the body:**

- **Card 2**: all six rivals in the package-total group who publish a level publish above this venue's
  \$5,600/\$6,000. The level is a tie; the *direction* is not, and page A's card 1 ruled exactly that
  direction. Page B's sheet prints "withheld" and no direction at all. This is the single most costly
  withholding on the page.
- **Card 9**: "what the owner can rule from this card today is the shape a published money term takes, not
  whether to publish one." Those are four counts that survive recoding, stated at the end of a 900-word
  paragraph.
- **Card 15**: both readings land between five and eight hours and neither disputes the 11:00 PM end. The
  length is rulable now; only the shape ties.
- **Card 8**: the season half is not withheld at all and is settled 3 to 0 on both derivations. The row on
  the sheet reads withheld.

**Three cards that recommend should have withheld: 3, 7 and 24.** Card 3 prints a margin between two
denominators it says are not one denominator. Card 7's only separating instrument runs against it. Card 24
prints a difference between two batteries it says cannot be differenced. In each case the honest line is
"withheld, and here is what would carry it", which is a form this page already has and uses well
elsewhere.

**One card should have ruled more than it did: card 1.** Its own Further test "argues for ruling both rather
than one", and it rules one.

## Part 7. The three changes that would most improve page B for one sitting

**1. Put both margins in the sheet, and flag every row whose sign turns on silence.** Add one column: the
count among rivals that state a posture. Today the margin column prints a single number, and on cards 7, 25
and 10 that number is carried by rivals who say nothing about the parameter, while the recommendation has no
supporter among those who do. An owner ruling from the front page cannot see it. Card 7 would look entirely
different to him with "18 with silence counted in; 0 of 11 among those that state a posture" on its row.
This is the change that most changes what he would rule.

**2. Print, on every withheld row, the one thing the evidence does carry.** Fifteen rows currently offer him
nothing to act on. At least seven hold something: a direction (card 2), a shape (card 9), a length (card
15), a season half already settled (card 8), a child rate at 6 to 0 (card 11), a permission both derivations
publish (card 20), and a small-end band with rival figures, search volume and two realised payments behind
it (card 6). A withheld row that says "not ruled, but this much is settled" costs one line and converts a
dead row into a live one.

**3. Lift the delivery contradictions out of the option tables into a short block he reads first.** Three
facts on this page are not product parameters at all; they are live exposures, and page B has dissolved them
into cost lines inside options he may never open. The public page and brochure promise whole-site exclusive
use where the signed terms cover the booked spaces alone (E26). A FAQ claims 200 guests against a total
chair stock of 100 (E14, E17), with two secured weddings already above 100 and a 120-guest Saturday booked
for July 2027. The \$1,000 deposit is named two ways, its receiving entity is internally flagged as needing
the owner's word, and two different default payment schedules stand on record (E70, E71, E73). Page A
carried all three as cards with urgency attached: "two or three secured weddings now sit above the guest
count at which the security table, the fee SOT and the chair inventory all stop." Page B's ruling sheet
names none of them. That is the clearest thing the new page has lost.

## Part 8. The one sentence

Page B is an improvement on page A for this owner, because it puts real alternatives with figures in front
of him, tests every line with a fresh clerk who read the market alone, says on nine departures exactly which
instrument could have vindicated his held value and did not, and lets him rule or commission from a
5,500-word front page where page A could only triage from a 2,500-word one; and I hold that at about
seventy per cent, because it withholds on fourteen of the eighteen cards that decide what a wedding here
costs and contains, because its counting rule lets a base of four carry a price cut while a base of
thirty-four cannot carry a price level, and because on card 7 it recommends abolishing a tier the venue
already sells on the strength of rivals who merely never mention one.
