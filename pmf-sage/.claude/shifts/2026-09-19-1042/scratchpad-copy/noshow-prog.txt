########## aongatete-outdoor-education-centre.md

### school_offer_detail_level
timed_programme

### programme_names
No single named "school programme"; the Educational Groups booking page serves schools, youth groups and adult education groups, who select activities from eight named activity categories: Bush Walks and Tramps; Outdoor Safety & Bush Craft; Aongatete River Activities; Nature & Environmental Studies; Self-Confidence / Personal Performance; Night Time Activities; Team Building Challenges; Gap Fillers & other activities. Within Nature & Environmental Studies is a named sub-programme, "Aongatete Forest Discovery Program".

### activities_verbatim
From the Activities overview page: "we work with you to create customised camp programmes that match your group's goals, age range, and budget. Simply choose the activities you're interested in, and our experienced team will help design a safe, engaging, and memorable outdoor learning experience — with support and activity SOPs provided."

Bush Walks and Tramps:
Nature Trail: A 20 minute walk through easy terrain following a track with signs about the areas Natural Habitat. This track is ideal for any other nature study activities.
Short Loop: A 1 hour walk through intermediate terrain, this track is a B grade and is about 2.2km long.
Long Loop: A 3 hr walk with challenging parts and river crossings. A C grade track about 6.5 km long.
Swimming Hole Track/River Crossing towards North/South Track: a 30 minute one-way walk to the river.
Upland Road Track: an easy C grade. It takes about 2 1/2 - 3 hours to reach AOEC and is about 5km long and includes a river crossing.
Wairere Falls (8 hrs one way): several schools start their AOEC camp by walking in from the Wairere Falls.

Outdoor Safety & Bush Craft:
The Aongatete Survivor Experience (Full Day)*: a full day experience led by a trained and experienced AOEC Instructor, covering Trip Planning, Outdoor Safety & Environmental Care, Navigation with Map & Compass, Outdoor Cooking, Shelter Building, Environmental Care, River Safety.
The Aongatete Survivor Experience (Half Day)*: a half day experience, 3hrs in the bush, led by a trained and experienced AOEC Instructor.
Shelter Building: a simple session designed to learn how to shelter from the elements in different ways.
Fire Lighting: led by a trained and experienced AOEC Instructor.
Outdoor Cooking: children can cook on hobo or hexi stoves; equipment available to hire.
Navigation: Basic Map Orienteering on Lodge Grounds; Map Reading using a Topo50 Map; Compass Confidence Basics; Compass Navigation through the bush led by an AOEC Instructor.
Camp Outs: sleep in dormitories, in a tent, under canvas or tarpaulin bivvy style, or under the stars.

Aongatete River Activities:
Aongatete River Challenge: an adventurous walk up/down the river with experienced AOEC Instructors.
River Studies & Discoveries: find and watch eels, what lives in our rivers, plants that grow in or near the river.
Dam Building.
Rock Sculptures.
Boat Races.
Swimming.

Nature & Environmental Studies:
"Thanks to the generous support of the Wright Family Foundation, all of our Environment Education Activities are fully subsidised for 2025, 2026, and 2027."
Aongatete Forest Discovery Program: an AOEC Instructor led, full day hands on program where students explore the forest, learn about plants and trees, forest colours, pest control and conservation, and identify birds; concludes with a night walk.
Forest Discovery - Get to know a tree.
Forest Discovery - Native Birds in our Forest.
River Studies & Discoveries (as above).
Layers of the Forest: can be extended with a team building lesson to last a whole morning or afternoon.
Pest Control & Conservation.
Nature Trail: 15 minutes to 2 hours depending on detail and design.
Nature Crafts & Forest Art.
Rongoa Maori Medicine: with AOEC Rongoa qualified Instructor.
Waste Management (Mandatory).
Night Time in the Forest.
Solos: can be run day or night, for any length of time.

Self-Confidence / Personal Performance:
Challenge Course: informal (play time, adult supervision and spotting required) or formal (set goals and objectives) sessions.
Archery: "an AOEC trained staff instructor is required to take this session."
Indoor Climbing Wall: "(Currently unavailable)".

Night Time Activities:
Burma Trail.
Night Walk: equipped with torches, magnifying glasses and night creatures identification cards.
Candle Trail.
Camp Concert.
Camp Fire: "*Strict Conditions apply".
Bouldering (on the indoor climbing wall): "this activity can only be run with the presence of our rock climbing instructors."

Team Building Challenges:
Pancake Challenge.
"Below are collection of short challenges commonly used as team development activities... Each activity takes 15-20 minutes depending on the age and capabilities of the students."
Tram Tracks. Lava Flow. Tyre Shift. Stacker. Spot Maze/Swamp Crossing. Balancing Pole. The Wall. Pipe Line. Tarp Flip.

Gap Fillers & other activities:
Fire drill: "a quick 20-30 minute exercise or you can spend a good hour."
Bouldering (on the indoor climbing wall): "this activity can only be run with the presence of our rock climbing instructors."
Camp Journal: printable PDF journal available to download.
Nature Crafts: Nature Weaving and Leaf Creatures.

### duration
No single fixed duration for a school visit as a whole; occupancy is per booked day/night (see Commercial > booking-related occupancy times below). Individual activities carry stated durations as transcribed above, ranging from 15 minutes (some team-building challenges) to 8 hours one way (Wairere Falls track).

### group_size_min_max
Booking form: accommodation "no more than 92 total". Facilities page: "A total of 92 people can be accommodated in dormitory style bunkroom accommodation... Regulations allow for a maximum of 100 people on site." Minimum charge applies below 22 occupants (see participant_thresholds).

### participant_thresholds
Minimum charge of $500 per day/night applies for groups smaller than 22 occupants. Maximum accommodation capacity of 92 people; regulation maximum of 100 people on site.

**activities_verbatim:**

- Nature at Your Place (Yr 1-8): "Nature at Your Place is a free programme for high EQI schools in the greater Wellington region, including two school outreach visits by Zealandia Educators and a sanctuary trip for your students."
- Zealandia at Your Place (Yr 1-8): "Can't quite find the right date to visit the sanctuary or enough adults to accompany you on your trip? No problem; let us come to you! Our educators will visit you to explore nature at your place."
- Experience Zealandia: self-guided (Yr 1-8): "Happy to show yourself around Zealandia Te Māra a Tāne? Self-guided school visits are available — come and explore at your own pace and see what you can discover!"
- The Family of Life: facilitated session: "Zealandia Te Māra a Tāne provides a wonderful context for students to experience the rich biodiversity of Aotearoa New Zealand and consider the impact of people upon it – both positive and negative."
- Tales Through Time: facilitated session: "The Zealandia valley is loaded with history: from hunting ground to goldrush, from waterworks to sanctuary. Students learn stories from past, present and future as they visit the now thriving valley."
- Mini Beasts: facilitated session: "Storytelling is intertwined with a bug hunt in the sanctuary, where students will see what's hidden under leaves and in the ground in a place like Zealandia Te Māra a Tāne."
- Nature & Nurture: facilitated session: "Learn about the importance of acting as kaitiaki of te taiao / nature while exploring the amazing mammalian predator-free valley at Zealandia Te Māra a Tāne."
- A 500-year Journey: facilitated session: "Students learn about the past, present and future of Zealandia as they experience the valley. What can the valley tell us of the past and what does the future hold in its 500-year restoration journey?"
- Pests & Predators: facilitated session: "Students learn about the ecological role and effect of endemic, native and introduced predators in Aotearoa, the strategies of their prey and the methods used by people to manage introduced species."
- The nature of Science: facilitated session: "Students become scientists in the field as they experience the valley and document the many amazing and unique species they can see. Ask questions, make observations and draw conclusions!"
- Special Species: facilitated session: "Students experience and learn about the unique biodiversity of Aotearoa New Zealand's native and endemic species."
- Nature at Your Place (Yr 9-13): same wording as the Yr 1-8 entry above.
- Zealandia at Your Place (Yr 9-13): same wording as the Yr 1-8 entry above.
- Experience Zealandia: self-guided (Yr 9-13): same wording as the Yr 1-8 entry above.
- Don't Feed the Kākā: facilitated session: "Get involved in the solution! This session presents Year 9-10 students with a real-world situation challenging them to devise a campaign to help prevent an undesired public behaviour — feeding kākā."
- A Living World: facilitated session: "Students experience the unique biodiversity of Aotearoa New Zealand at Zealandia Te Māra a Tāne and learn about the dramatic impact of people upon it."
- Citizen Scientists: facilitated session: "Students become scientists in the field as they experience the Zealandia valley and document the amazing and unique species that live there. Ask questions, make observations and draw conclusions!"
- Sanctuary & Society: facilitated session: "Students learn about the Zealandia sanctuary's origins, societal impact and ongoing growth as a successful ecotourism project in the heart of Aotearoa New Zealand's capital."
- Pests & Predators: Managing Mammals: facilitated session: "Learn about the ecological role and effect of endemic, native and introduced predators in Aotearoa New Zealand, the strategies of their prey and the methods used to manage introduced pest species."
- Where Have all the Takahē Gone? (facilitated session, NCEA Biology): "'Where Have all the Takahē Gone?' explores the communities in which takahē currently live and the impact of the presence or absence of introduced mammals within these communities."
- Understanding Plant & Animal Responses: facilitated session (NCEA Biology): "Engage and enthuse your students by letting them see how Aotearoa New Zealand species respond to their external environment."
- Experience Zealandia (Ages 7-18): "Discover some of Aotearoa New Zealand's rare and unique fauna, flora and fungi. Join the Zealandia Educators to explore the sanctuary and witness native wildlife once again thriving on the mainland."

### activities_verbatim

- Exploration du monde animal (atelier TSA et DI): "Partons explorer le monde animal! Que ce soit dans l'eau, sur la terre et même dans le ciel, les animaux sont partout autour de nous. Atelier conçu et adapté pour les classes spécialisées avec des élèves à besoins particuliers (TSA et DI)"
- À la bouffe: "Carnivore, herbivore ou omnivore, mais quelle est la différence? Découvrez de quoi et comment se nourrissent les animaux, qu'ils soient à plumes, à poils ou à écailles!"
- Les animaux sauvent Noël: "Rudolf, le renne, s'est cassé la patte ! Vite, il faut remplacer notre ami au nez rouge, mais qui pourra relever ce défi ?"
- Les métiers du Zoo: "Du vétérinaire au technicien en soins animaliers en passant par les biologistes, le zoo de Granby est une véritable ruche où de nombreuses spécialités se côtoient afin de veiller au bien-être de nos animaux."
- SOS, espèces en péril!: "Les espèces animales disparaissent à un rythme effréné : inquiétant ou normal? Apprenez pourquoi elles sont toutes si importantes à préserver!"
- Naître et grandir: "Découvrez les petits secrets de la séduction, de la naissance et des soins parentaux chez les animaux par une présentation colorée, interactive et amusante!"
- Planète mammifères!: "Partez à la découverte des mammifères dans ce tour du monde endiablé! Découvrez les caractéristiques uniques de ces animaux dont nous faisons partie!"
- Frissons nocturnes: bestioles au grand jour: "Serpents, araignées et chauves-souris hantent peut-être vos cauchemars, mais sous la loupe de nos biologistes, vous les découvrirez sous un tout nouveau jour!"
- Ouache! Un reptile!: "Qu'est-ce qu'un reptile? Nos biologistes passionnés répondent à cette question en démontrant que c'est ni gluant, ni méchant!"
- Opération flocons: guide de survie en hiver: "Brrrr! Il fait froid, les animaux font quoi? Explorez les différentes stratégies des animaux à l'arrivée de l'hiver et découvrez quels indices ils nous laissent de leur passage dans notre cour!"
- Halloween, chauves-souris et cie: "Une fête de l'Halloween sous le thème des chauves-souris! Levons le voile sur ces créatures de la nuit et découvrons ce sympathique petit mammifère volant dans une ambiance ensorcelante!"
- L'univers des insectes: "Pas besoin de travailler comme une fourmi pour piquer la curiosité sur le monde passionnant des insectes et autres bibittes."
- Les sens aux aguets: "Participez à l'aventure rocambolesque d'un animal vedette qui doit utiliser tous ses sens afin de surmonter plusieurs obstacles!"
- Le comportement animal: "Une initiation ludique et interactive à l'étude du comportement animal permettant d'en apprendre davantage sur les besoins des animaux. Découvrez aussi tous les efforts mis en œuvre pour favoriser les comportements naturels chez nos animaux!"
- Reproduction animale sous la loupe: "Notre biologiste vous conduit sur les chemins de la reproduction animale. Découvrez la séduction en passant par la naissance jusqu'aux soins parentaux chez les animaux!"
