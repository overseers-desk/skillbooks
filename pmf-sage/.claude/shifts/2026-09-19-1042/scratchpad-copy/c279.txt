## Third derivation

**What do I have to give my principal?** What paperwork do you give me for our approval process, when does it arrive, and do I have to ask for it?

Three sub-questions: what documents, on what clock, and who initiates. The market answers the first two with counts and the third with published wording.

### The ways the market sells this

**A. Publish nothing about it and let the teacher ask.**
Figure: 138 of 215 venue profiles carry no statement about a risk assessment, the reduction being on whether the venue's own text says anything on the point. Per cell: 65 of 89 in the Australia, New Zealand and Canada census, 38 of 60 UK farm, 18 of 40 UK nature, 12 of 19 Queensland supplementary, 5 of 7 South East Queensland comparators. On the 189-venue register sample that is 121 of 189 (64%). Australia alone: 35 of 56 (63%). Queensland alone: 19 of 28 (68%). Source: `venues/index.tsv`, field `risk_assessment_available`, with the reduction rule recorded in `collection-notes.md`. **This is the market's majority practice, and it is the option a recommendation has to beat.**

**B. Publish it as a document the teacher takes without asking.**
Figure: 77 of 215 profiles record the venue saying something about a risk assessment; of the 74 whose profile text names one, 60 point to a downloadable, linked or otherwise published document. Source: `venues/index.tsv` for the coded 77, and a text sweep of the 215 profile bodies for the 60. The sweep is a text hit and not a coded value, so it is carried as direction. Published wording from named instances: a NSW farm publishes "Risk assessment is available here" with the PDF behind it; a Scottish heritage centre publishes a titled risk assessment listing significant hazards including animal bites and infection; a zoo publishes "Zoo general visit risk assessment 2022" as a linked PDF; a UK farm park offers a risk assessment PDF alongside the government "Avoiding infection on farm visits" leaflet as downloads. In the catchment: the Burleigh wildlife park publishes its risk assessment inside its information pack, the Brisbane koala sanctuary publishes both risk assessment and insurance, and the Mount Samson guided-rotation farm, the closest format comparator that is also fully booked for September and turning schools away, publishes risk assessment and insurance as downloads. Source: rival register, sections 2, 4 and 5.

**C. Send it with the booking confirmation.**
Figure: named instances rather than a count. An Essex farm park publishes "Risk assessments sent with booking confirmation and available on Teachers' Guide page" plus "a visit information document that is sent to you with your booking confirmation"; a Surrey farm gives a safety-talk script "included in your booking confirmation"; a Gloucestershire farm park sends a full-day itinerary in advance. Source: those venue profiles. Note that the first of these does both, which is the shape most consistent with the clock in option F: the document is on the page for the approval paperwork and again in the confirmation for the day.

**D. Refuse to individualise and hand over a generic document instead.**
Figure: one named instance with its own wording. A Devon woodland park's group-visit guidelines state: "Due to the amount of individual risk assessments which cover the park's operation, it is not possible to send them out on an individual basis. However, we have put together some information and a general risk assessment document upon which to base" the school's own. Source: that venue's profile. This is the option that says the teacher writes her own assessment and the venue supplies the raw material, and the market's own phrasing elsewhere agrees: one UK venue describes its document as a risk assessment "which should assist in producing your own risk assessment".

**E. Bundle the paperwork into a named pack.**
Figure: teacher-pack, teacher-guide or information-pack wording appears in 49 of 215 profiles (15 Australian, 11 Queensland); pre-visit or familiarisation wording in 32 of 215 (3 Australian, 3 Queensland). Source: text sweep of the profile bodies, a hit and not a coded value. Named contents from one Australian profile: risk assessment, social story, child-safe policy and pre-visit information pack, all four named as downloadable supporting documents. The pre-visit split is the sharp one: the UK market sells a teacher familiarisation visit, often capped at four adults and priced free, and Australia barely does, 3 of 56.

**F. Pair the paperwork with the curriculum line, because the principal is approving both.**
Figure: 111 of the 189-venue register sample publish curriculum links (52 of 89 in the census cell, 29 of 60 UK farm, 30 of 40 UK nature), 24 of 56 Australian venues (43%) and 12 of 28 Queensland (43%). Source: `venues/index.tsv`, field `curriculum_links_present`. The buyer says why in her own words: "I also really need to match the excursion up to my curriculum to justify the time out of the classroom." Source: corpus finding 27.

### The demand leg: what the buyer's own process does with this document

The Queensland approval order runs up to three gates plus principal sign-off, and consent cannot be sought from parents until after approval is granted, which puts the chain at five to nine weeks. NSW takes one signature and permits a repeat visit to reuse the previous risk assessment, at three to six weeks. Neither department publishes a lead time; policy fixes order and authority rather than timing. Source: corpus finding 22. Two further constraints bear directly: a NSW principal may not sign an indemnity releasing a provider from liability, and NSW requires the venue to verify its own staff's working-with-children clearances. Source: corpus findings, the three paperwork constraints. The catchment shows the second of these being published as a selling fact: the Burleigh wildlife park states its rangers hold blue cards. Source: rival register, section 2.

The consequence for this card is a clock, not a preference. In Queensland the document is a gate item the teacher must hold before she is permitted to ask parents for money, so a document that arrives with the booking confirmation arrives after the point it was needed. The two clocks of the buying cycle say the same: the calendar slot is decided the previous year and the booking itself lands one to three months out. Source: corpus finding 25 and the calendar reading.

### What the evidence carries

**Publish it, so the teacher takes it without asking, and publish the curriculum line beside it, because the principal is approving both in one paper.**

The reasoning has to be stated plainly, because on raw prevalence this recommendation loses. The majority of the market publishes nothing (option A, 138 of 215). What carries the minority option is that the demand leg and the supply count point in opposite directions and the demand leg is the one measured on buyer behaviour: Queensland's approval order makes the document a precondition of the parent consent that funds the trip, and the catchment's two venues that do publish are the departmental-adjacent wildlife park and the Brisbane sanctuary, with the fully-booked format comparator outside the catchment doing the same. A card ruled on prevalence alone would rule for silence; a card ruled on what the buyer has to hold before she can proceed rules for publication.

### The margin

Stated twice, because the two units disagree and a single number here would be misleading.

**Within the minority that says anything at all, in that option's unit of venue profiles:** published without asking leads sent-on-request or sent-with-confirmation **60 to 10**, a count lead of **50 venues**, with 14 unresolved. Publication is overwhelmingly the form this practice takes when it exists.

**Against the largest option overall, in that option's unit of venue profiles:** silence leads publication **138 to 77**, a lead of **61 venues**. Publication is a minority practice and the card carries it on the demand leg rather than on the count. In the Queensland cell the same gap is 19 to 9, a lead of 10 venues.

**On the catchment, in the unit of enumerated rival venues:** of the 33 venues in register sections 2 and 3, one has a published risk assessment recorded (the Burleigh wildlife park, inside its information pack); the other two instances in the register sit in the marginal section and among the out-of-catchment format comparators. So in the contested set, publication leads on a count of 1 and the margin is not statable in that unit; the register was built to enumerate contested bookings rather than to code paperwork, so a blank there is a blank in the register.

**On the "do I have to ask" half, it is close to settled and on the "when does it arrive" half it is not.** Nothing in the corpus codes the arrival clock. The named instances split between a page the teacher reaches before booking and a confirmation email after it, and one venue does both. **What would break it:** a coded variable for the point in the buying sequence at which each named document becomes available, applied to the 77 profiles that carry any paperwork statement, which is one coding pass over an existing population; and, on the demand side, the twenty phone calls the corpus already owes, which would establish at what point in the approval chain a teacher actually needs the document in hand.

### Limits on this derivation

- The 60-versus-10 split is a text sweep of profile bodies, not a coded value. It overstates address wherever a profile mentions a document in passing, and it is reported as direction.
- The `risk_assessment_available` field is a mechanical reduction on whether the text says anything, so a yes means the venue speaks to the point and never that the document is adequate.
- The UK registers require a published programme as a near-condition of listing, which inflates UK publication rates relative to the other cells; the cells may not be compared against each other.
- No corpus rate here may be marked shared or differs on the four dimensions of the operator's shape note, since the codebook reads none of them. This card turns on how the sale is taken, which is one of the four, so its demand leg is drawn from the rival register and the published approval policy rather than from a corpus rate.
- The 26 Australian venues added for local comparison carry no denominator and the Queensland figures above are counts on 28 named venues, not an Australian rate.
- The venue's own enquiry and booking record was not read for this derivation.

## Third derivation

**Card 29. If we come again next year, is it the same day, or can the next class do something different?**

Derived from the market side alone, by a clerk who saw no card, no sales record and nothing the venue holds or sells. The question has two halves that the market answers with different evidence: whether a school comes back, which is demand evidence, and what it gets when it does, which is supply evidence.

### What the demand side already establishes

Rebooking is the default, and the returning party is usually the next year's class rather than the same children. Finding 27: "we book incursions that we already know about because we used them last year… the first booking is worth far more than one booking, because rebooking is the default. The relationship belongs to the year-level team, not a person, and a school runs roughly one excursion per year level per year." Finding B19 puts a count on it in this catchment: 26 of 315 school-venue observations are flagged recurring, the register names six standing buyer-venue relationships, and "two of the six are a state school's annual Prep booking at a departmental farm campus, and one a Year 4 standing programme at the same campus." The register's own list (`catchment-rival-register.md` §1) names a Prep booking repeated annually at a wildlife sanctuary and at a farm venue, a Year 4 standing programme, another school's Prep booking, and a K–2 group that returns to the same venue "alternating years". B19's limit is stated there: the flag fires only where a profile used the word, so 26 understates recurrence.

So the buyer who comes back next year is, in nearly every observed case, a different class of the same age doing the same year level's excursion. That is the frame the three options sit in.

### The ways the market sells it

**Option R1: one fixed programme, and the next class does what the last one did.** This is the market's largest single group. Of 110 venues with usable school pricing, **51 (46%) sell one fixed programme at one price** and never reach a choice question at all (F28). Paradise Country, in catchment, publishes a programme that "cannot be tailored" (`catchment-rival-register.md` §2). The two catchment standing bookings the register names are of exactly this shape: the same Prep day, taken again next year by next year's Preps. The seller's sentence is "it is the same day, and that is why your Preps can hand it to next year's Preps"; the buyer's answer is that this is what she was going to do anyway, and it costs her nothing to approve the second time. Finding 22 gives that its mechanism: NSW permits a repeat visit to reuse the previous risk assessment, so sameness is worth paperwork to the buyer, and Queensland's stricter order (up to three gates, 5 to 9 weeks) makes a known day cheaper to approve than a new one.

**Option R2: a programme per year band, so the next class up gets a different day.** 27 of 215 profiles name their programmes by two or more year bands, and 26 publish curriculum links across three or more. Sea World publishes "about 19 curriculum-tagged programmes by year level" and Currumbin Wildlife Sanctuary "45-min curriculum sessions (v8.4/v9.0 lessons by year level)" (`catchment-rival-register.md` §2). Lone Pine's sessions carry codes "separately for every year level from Prep to Year 12". Manor Wildlife Park splits KS1, KS2 and KS3; Old MacDonald's Farm EYFS, KS1 and KS2; Myuna Farm Foundation, Grade 2, Grade 3, Year 7 and Year 9; Abbey Farm EYFS, Primary and Secondary. Calmsley Hill City Farm, the at-scale benchmark from the site visit, runs "weekly grade rotations" (§5). The seller's sentence is "your Preps did the Prep day, so your Year 2s get the Year 2 day"; the buyer's answer is that this multiplies her school's slots, because a school runs one excursion per year level per year (F27) and each level is a separate booking with a separate teacher.

**Option R3: a menu the school draws a different day from.** 59 of 215 venues sell choice, 39 of them on a compulsory base (F28); the skew is sharper here, at 14 of 17 Australian choice-sellers and all 7 resolved Queensland ones. The menus are large enough to support a second visit that is genuinely different: counting raw published entries the median is 7 and the range runs to 65, "because the biggest publishers list a choose-from menu. Edendale publishes 65 sessions; nobody runs 65 in a day" (F7). Trevena Glen publishes "8-plus half-hour choices" and Highfields Pioneer Village a "core programme plus up to 3 of 15-plus half-hour segments" (§5). The seller's sentence is "you did the river and the yards, so this year take the pond and the bees"; the buyer's answer is that it is the same booking with a different line on the form.

**Option R4: say the words "come back and it will be different".** Essentially nobody. A sweep of the 215 profile bodies for returning-school, repeat-visit or come-back-next-year language returns **six hits, and every resolved one is a testimonial rather than an offer**: a college tutor writing that she has brought students "for several years now". No venue in the corpus publishes a returning-school rate, a second-year programme, a progression ladder sold as one, or any statement addressed to a school that has already been. The counts here are a whole-text sweep of the kind finding B12 labels a hit and not a coded value; the codebook carries no variable for recurrence, so this null is a publication fact and the standing caveat applies in full, that what is bought is not always printed.

### What the evidence carries

**The evidence carries R2, a distinct day per year band, with R3 beside it as the mechanism that makes R2 cheap to publish.**

Against R1 the margin is the one to state carefully, because R1 is the larger count and the weaker fit. On the corpus R1 leads: **51 of 110 priced venues sell one fixed programme, against 27 of 215 profiles publishing year-banded programmes**. What turns the card is that the buyer this card asks about is a returning buyer, and on returning buyers the catchment's evidence is unanimous in the other direction: every one of the six standing relationships B19 names is a **year-level** booking, Prep or Year 4, repeated by the next year's class, which is an R2 purchase even where the venue publishes an R1 product. In the returning buyer's unit, standing relationships in the catchment, the margin for R2 is **6 to 0**, and the corpus's 27 year-banded venues are the supply-side answer to it.

Against R3 the two are not rivals and I do not separate them on counts: R3 is how a venue builds R2 without writing a second programme, and 39 of 59 choice-selling venues already anchor a choice on a compulsory base (F28), which is a spine that stays the same while the chosen half changes. Read as competing answers to this buyer's sentence, R2 leads R3 by **27 profiles to 59** on raw counts, which is a loss; the reason I carry R2 anyway is that a menu answers the teacher who is composing this year's day, and the card's buyer is asking what her *successor* gets, which a year band answers and a menu only permits.

**Where the market side cannot separate two ways:** it cannot say whether a returning school actually wants difference or wants sameness. R1's evidence that sameness is what schools buy (51 of 110 venues, the catchment's standing Prep bookings, NSW's reuse of the previous risk assessment under F22) and R2's evidence that the market publishes difference by year band (27 profiles, 26 curriculum ladders) are both real, and no instrument here measures a school switching venue or programme out of boredom. This is a **tie on buyer preference**, decided above only on the shape of the returning buyer. **What would break it:** asking the six named standing-booking schools whether the year-level team would take a different day if offered one, or asking three year-banded venues what share of their bookings come from a school that has been before. Neither has been asked, and B19's own limit says the recurrence count itself understates the truth.

### Measured-in

The card turns on variables the codebook does not read: recurrence, year-banded programme structure and returning-school offers. No corpus rate here may be marked *shared* or *differs*; every figure reads **not coded** on the four dimensions of `shape-note.md`. F28's choice census and F7's menu counts are coded corpus reads on the collection index's price-and-structure field and stand as rates on their stated denominators (110 priced venues, 215 profiles). The deciding leg is B19 and `catchment-rival-register.md` §1, which read this catchment's own buyers rather than the corpus, as the shape note directs where a card turns on how the buyer arrives.
