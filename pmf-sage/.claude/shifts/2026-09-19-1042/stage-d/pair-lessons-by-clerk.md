# What the pair taught, sorted by the clerk it changes

Source: the shift ledger and the six judges' reviews of River Day and weddings, 19 September 2026. Each line is a lesson and the change it makes to a brief under `briefs/` beside this file. Lessons the method, a form or a tool already carries are listed last, for the record.

## Records clerk

- Four clerks found River Day's corpus out of joint (coded rows for 184 of 215 profiles, the nearest cells never coded; four field syntaxes; file names that do not join the index), and the wedding corpus kept stored pages for one stratum only. The records clerk writes a fifth file, a corpus inventory, so every later clerk knows what a denominator covers.
- The venue's own coded unit sat among the comparables in both runs. The inventory names it and the fence index marks it as a row outside every rate.
- Enquiry counts were lower bounds (no log of telephone or direct email; a form that rejected submissions for a week). The sales record says which channels are logged and which are not, and what the record cannot tell (which tier a booking bought, why an enquiry closed).
- A rival register and a shape note restated the venue's own published terms and leaked them to the market-only clerks. The shape note marks the operator's shape without its values, and a register that restates the venue's terms is re-cut, the market's part open.
- The question list carried card numbers that later disagreed with the card set. Questions carry no numbers.

## Codebook amendment author

- Withheld cards in both runs waited on variables nobody had coded (how the sale is taken, a gathered talk, enquiry-form labels, whether a rival trades publicly beside the offering), and no corpus rate could be marked shared or differs because the codebook held none of the four shape dimensions. The amendment is drafted from the question list as well as the procedure: one variable for whom each operator sells to, one for each shape dimension, and one for each question the codebook cannot already answer.
- The whom-sold-to variable divided one market well and the other hardly at all (one class held nearly every row). The variable carries a second field for the kind of buyer the operator names inside its main class, captured verbatim.
- A frozen codebook's worked example named the commissioning venue. An amendment's examples name no venue.

## Coder, and the orchestrator who cuts the shards

- Shards are cut from the profile folder's listing, not from an earlier coded table, so an uncoded cell is coded. The venue's own unit is left out of every shard.
- Shards coded 0 where their notes said not captured, and screened-out units appeared as rows. The brief says which missing-value code is which.
- A count handed to a clerk is looked at value by value before it is written down (the orchestrator's own error on the wedding coding note).

## Search-demand clerk

- Naming a cluster in the brief chooses the buyer. The brief names none.
- Lookups never made were scattered over findings. The study closes with the unmade lookups as one batch the owner can commission.

## Strike-list clerk

- Buyer findings mixed the market's counts with the operator's own. Two files.

## Deriving clerks

- Two clerks carried different counts of one fact, and a census could not be reconciled because its decision rules were never written. A count a clerk makes is written with its rule.
- Thirty and thirty-three cards were more than an owner rules in a sitting, and the lifted wedding page lost the earlier page's document fixes and its questions for named staff. The card set keeps the sentence test and gains a third list: work that is no market question, filed and not carded.

## Third-derivation clerk

- Two third-derivation counts were wrong on the record. It writes each count with its rule, as the first clerks do.

## Corrections clerk

- A recommending sentence cited a fact its own option table contradicted. The clerk reads the sentence against the table.
- The second pass is for the withheld cards a top-up finding bears on, not for the whole set.

## After the second and third judge rounds (19 September, evening)

- **Codebook amendment author.** Two coders parted where a made-to-order rule was silent (a priced consumable as a component; a base with add-ons against separate whole-visit products; free admission as a published price), not where the text was hard. A decision rule is tried on its borderline cases and says how each falls before it is frozen.
- **Deriving clerks.** Options whose counts are nested (five of seven rivals in both) separate nothing: write options that exclude one another. With thirty-odd units in a cell, a lead of three or fewer is one coding decision wide, so say on the measured-in line where a cell can show which options exist and cannot rank them.
- **Survey top-up clerk.** A count made to order is made twice before it turns a card (in the brief). On its first use the rule returned one card to withheld (17 against 8) and carried two others as a thin lead both coders share.
- **Corrections clerk.** One rule for silence with both margins; a line leaving a held value names the instrument that could have shown that value winning; a lead one operator would erase is a tie, whichever side it favours; boundary tests are re-read on a turn (all in the method, the form, the check and the brief).
- **Integrator.** Pairs of cards that ask one question are reported, settled by a corrections clerk on both Joint lines, and only then assembled (in the brief). The order of ruling leads with the owner's part and groups withheld cards by what their observation costs.
- **Orchestrator.** My own wording swung the clerks twice: an exception to the silence rule was claimed exactly where it beat a ruling, and "withheld is for no lead at all" produced recommendations on leads of one. Each change to the method's words about evidence is followed by a judge before it reaches another run. A record that narrates a retired rule keeps teaching it (a third judge read the retired one-in-ten threshold out of a priors note): rewrite forward-only at the moment of retirement.
- **For the owner (needs the user).** A catchment of thirty-odd rivals can rarely rank options; obtaining rivals' terms through the enquiry route would close the most withheld cards at once and is barred by the runs' own collection rule. Whether to lift that rule is his.
