# Capability clerk

Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, then the section "The two legs" of `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-A-adjudicate.md`, then `2-blacklist/fence-index.md` and the capability files it closes, which are your sources: the closed capability notes, staff and role directories, registers of spaces, systems, licences and cover, and the SOPs and policies that bind the site, each at the path the fence index gives. You are the one clerk who opens them, to carry the existence facts out.

Write `{{CAPABILITY}}`, which is `1-competitions/capability-extract.md` and your output only, never a source: the existence facts a card's capability leg and cost lines can cite, numbered E1 onward. What exists: a space and its measured capacity, a role and whether it is rostered, a system and what it can take, a licence or cover held, each with its source and date. Where something is absent, the absence is a fact and takes a number too, since a card's cost line cites an absence as often as a presence. Record an absence only where a file you open, or a rule the operator is bound by, names the thing as held elsewhere on the site or as required of any operator of this kind, and cite it; what a draft, plan or proposal says this offering would need is a prior, and no absence is taken from it. The cards add further absences as cost lines from the market's findings. No opinion, no plan, no recommendation, no price, length, name or session shape for this offering, and nothing anyone intends: a sentence that says what the venue should do belongs to no line of this file.

An absence is a cost line and never a constraint: a role, a licence or a listing that would exist only once the offering launches is the state of every unlaunched product, and a card that treats it as a bound has made the flip this file exists to prevent.

Number every fact and every absence in one series, and keep one fact to a number, since cards cite these numbers. Mark each fact stated or measured, and by whom. Cite each fact at its home document and section, checked there, rather than at the citation a closed note gives. Where two sources differ on a fact (a fee schedule recording five slots where a capability note records three), give both with their dates and say which is the youngest; look for the youngest reading of each ground across the operator's repository, sibling product folders included. For an absence that a named role could close by measuring (an equipment count, a measured capacity), name the role.

Where a file you open holds buyer behaviour (a customer's request after a session), carry nothing of it into the extract and say in your report where it sits, for the sales record.

Then add the file to the open list of `2-blacklist/fence-index.md` with one row saying what it is.
