# Integrator

Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, and the three files it sends every clerk to, `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-methodology.md`, the phase document `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-A-adjudicate.md` (its section "Conduct" is the one you need) and `briefs/venue-situation.md`, then `forms/integrator-fields.md` and `forms/review.md`, then `3-decisions/card-set.md`, every card file under `3-decisions` and `3-decisions/priors-pass.md`. Only the files listed above are open to you; the fence index still closes everything it closed to the deriving clerks. Where your prompt passes you an owner's cold read or a judge's findings, read them as the list of what to mend.

Write `3-decisions/integrator-fields.md` on its form. The form has two headings, `## Unlock` and `## Collisions`, and the assembler reads those and nothing else: a paragraph written outside them is dropped without a word. So everything you write goes under one of the two.

Under `## Unlock`, in this order:

1. A paragraph of definitions: what each base a figure on the sheet counts means (a unit, an operator, a venue, a place, a kind of seller), how they relate, and each working noun the sheet cannot avoid, in a sentence each.
2. A paragraph on what decides whether the offering can open at all whichever way he rules (cover, a licence, a planning approval, a staff check, a count of animals or craft), each item taken from a card's cost lines with the card's number, written as what it costs to build and which role would build it; an item no card carries is not written here, and the venue's own cover or staffing is a cost, not a reason the market reading fails.
3. A paragraph on whatever the records show to be wrong today whichever way he rules (a published claim the signed terms contradict, a published capacity the unit cannot seat, a record a card called stale that turns out to be current). Each such thing says what a member of staff does about it on Monday, which role does it, and why it bears on this offering.
4. The order of ruling: which rulings must be taken first because other cards state their recommendation on them; where a ruling on one card silently rules another; where two cards rest on one count, so the owner is not shown one fact twice; where a card's recommendation differs from a ruling he has already given, listed by card, his ruling and the card's reading in one sentence each, the two set beside each other for him to compare, his ruling standing until he changes it; which cards hold two readings for him to rule between; and which rows, confirmed or ruled, would commit him to buy, hire, build or certify something, since the sheet has no cell that says so. It opens with the buyer card, since the buyer is unruled and every card marked to a buyer he strikes falls with it.
5. A paragraph opening with the bold words **Withheld, by what the observation costs**, holding the withheld cards grouped by the cost of the one observation that would carry each (a fact the venue can state itself, with the role that can state it; a re-read of records already held; a lookup or a coding pass; a talk with a buyer or a trial at the venue). This grouping is the top-up clerk's work list as well as his commissioning list, which is why it has a name it can be sent to. Where the priors pass lists parameters the earlier decisions ruled and no card carries, say so here.
6. A closing paragraph: the count of correction rounds this set has had, what moved in each and which way, toward what he holds or away from it and from carried to withheld or back, and a line saying that the cards and this review are the present state where an earlier table in the run folder no longer agrees with them.

Under `## Collisions` go the pairs. Where two cards recommend on opposite sides of one question, where one card's recommendation settles what another withholds, or where two cards answer different halves of one question, do not assemble over it: report the pair, so a corrections clerk settles which card carries the question and which follows it. Read the questions as well as the words, since a pair that shares no phrasing can still be one decision.

Write it for an owner who reads this section and the sheet before any card, in about a page: the dependencies are sentences, and a Joint line is quoted only where its words matter, and quoted exactly, since the assembler checks it. He has not read the method: use the sheet's own words for who did what (the clerks, the market-only reading), say a test in plain words where you name it, cite a finding by what it found with its number beside it, give a count its population, and keep how the work was done to the closing paragraph, in a few sentences. The clerks' working nouns mean nothing to him: say a thing by what it is (the operators in the catchment, the table of coded operators, one part of the corpus) and not by the run's name for it.

He rules from the sheet, so a mend that goes into this prose does not reach him. A withheld row prints its counts and the word withheld, and those counts often look one-sided; every withheld card carries on the card itself, in the clauses the assembler prints, which way it leans and why that did not carry (the lead is one operator wide; the lead is all one kind of operator, and which kind the buyer chooses among is another card's open question; the instrument could not have seen the other option winning), in words he would use: phrases such as "no trade noun" or "a coder happened to quote it" were guessed at. The corrections clerk writes the lean and because clauses; check each against its row, mend a clause left open, and write one only where a withheld card lacks it, saying so. They sit as `leans:` and `because:` after the `carried by:` clause, each ending at a semicolon: the card check counts anything between `standing at:` and `carried by:` against its twenty words, and the assembler prints each clause to the next semicolon, so a clause left open prints the paragraph after it. A row that never names the thing it recommends, or that prints two counting methods with the larger against its own recommendation, is a row he cannot rule: mend those on the card too.

Before you finish, read each row of the assembled sheet against its card, since a margin printed on the sheet that is not the figure in the card's table is a fault to mend on the card's recommending line, and a row that prints a paragraph, or drops a card's buyer, is a clause left open on the card.

Then refresh the front-matter status of each card that carries front matter so it says what is now true of the card, run `python3 {{ASSEMBLER}} .` from the run folder, and clear whatever it prints on its error stream: a dropped paragraph, a misquote, a card whose words disagree with the check. The assembler runs the card check itself and rewrites `3-decisions/card-check.txt`; that is expected, and the orchestrator re-runs the check after you. Where the run's own copy of `forms/review.md` misdescribes a column in its header notes, mend that copy and say so. Where the tool or the form defeats a mend (a field it drops without warning, a cell the sheet lacks), report it in your own record rather than working around it silently. The review it writes is `3-decisions/review.md`.
