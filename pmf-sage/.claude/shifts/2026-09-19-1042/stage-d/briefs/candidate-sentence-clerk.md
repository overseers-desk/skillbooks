# Candidate-sentence clerk

Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, and the three files it sends every clerk to, `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-methodology.md`, the phase document `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-S-survey.md` and `briefs/venue-situation.md`, then the sections of `{{CODEBOOK}}` on scope and text units, then in `{{AMENDMENT}}` the lists of the variables your prompt names as they stand after every amendment, with the matching rule, then the corpus index under `{{CORPUS}}`, `{{CORPUS}}/shards/units-with-text.txt` and `{{CODED}}.tsv`. A unit's pages are read by running bin/corpus-unit {{PAGES}} <unit> (as its line of the units list names it) and reading the folder it prints; search across units with bin/corpus-grep. Beyond those files nothing in the repository is open to you: no card, no finding, nothing of this venue.

Where a variable's test is a phrase list plus a judgement, the two halves are different work. Whether a page prints a listed phrase, under the amendment's matching rule, is a mechanical fact; whether the sentence that prints it states what the variable means is a judgement, which two readers blind of each other will make after you. Coders asked to do both at once in one run read the lists two ways and recorded only the sentences they chose, and agreed well while counting words. Do the mechanical half by script, over every page of every unit the prompt names, so that the judgement is made on the same sentences by both readers and can reach units the first coders coded as silent.

Your script reads `{{CORPUS}}/shards/units-with-text.txt` as the orchestrator measured it and writes no list of its own; before you search, compare it with the corpus index and the coded table, and report each unit found in one and not another.

Write a script, kept at `{{CORPUS}}/candidates/find-candidates.py`, that reads every page of each listed unit, extracted via bin/corpus-unit {{PAGES}} <unit>, leaves out what the codebook and the amendment put outside every scope where a file's name or first lines show it (a third party's or directory's listing, an error page, a log, a booking widget where the rules bar it, a page not in the codebook's language), removes a sentence repeated between a captured text and a text derived from markup, splits the text into sentences or, where it has no sentence punctuation, into lines, and finds every occurrence of every listed phrase of the named variables, case-insensitively, under the matching rule as the amendment states it. Write `{{CORPUS}}/candidates/candidates.tsv`, tab-separated, no quoting, tabs and line breaks inside a cell replaced by spaces, one row an occurrence. The table carries these kinds of column, under names your script chooses and prints once in its header: a running id; the unit's key as the coded table spells it; the variable, and the class, kind or closed value the phrase belongs to; the phrase matched; the whole sentence (to 600 characters) and the neighbouring sentence each side (to 300); whether a word of the codebook's own list for the offering stands in the sentence or a neighbour, which is what lets the orchestrator choose, on the record, how far from the offering's mention a sentence is read; whether the row looks like a navigation label (short and unpunctuated); where the sentence was found (the stored file, and the page's address where the capture records one); and how many times the sentence recurs across the unit's pages, keeping the first. Where the run copies an earlier run's script, keep its column names: the readers' brief and the merge script are filled from this header, so a table made by a copied script matches them by construction.

Then test the script against the first coding, which is the one check that it finds what a careful reader found: for every unit and variable the coded table codes stated, every sentence recorded in that variable's quote field should be among your candidates for that unit and variable. Write `{{CORPUS}}/candidates/recall-check.md`: the recorded sentences, how many your candidates hold, and every miss with its reason (a page not stored, a scope the rules bar, a phrase the lists do not hold, a sentence split differently, a table, a citation by address that matches no stored file), mending the script where the miss is the script's and saying which misses were mended. Give the counts a reader needs before the reading is sized: candidates in all, by variable and class, units with at least one candidate by variable, how many rows stand near the offering's word and how many look like navigation labels, and the units with none. Name the words that carry most of the candidates in more than one sense (common words such as "parking", "camp" or "free", or a place name holding a listed word), with their counts, so the reading is sized honestly and the readers are warned. Say which files the script left out and by which rule, with counts; what a script cannot see (a quoted customer, an offer that is not the offering, a same-named other business); Name the units that share pages or quoted text with another, in the recall note and as a list the operator-index clerk is handed as evidence; the readers' shards keep one operator's units together by `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv`, not by this list.

Where you had to choose how the vocabulary works (narrowing a sweep to sentences that also carry another word, how a preposition or "our" against "your" matches), make the choice, and write it in the recall note with the rows it adds or suppresses, marked for ratification by the rule review or the orchestrator. Say where the script's inputs live and whether the candidate table can be rebuilt from a fresh clone of the repository; whether to commit a derived table of that size is the orchestrator's call.

Judge no sentence. Write nothing about which candidates state what their variable means. Report in under 200 words: the counts, the recall figure, what the script cannot see, the choices awaiting ratification, and the size of the reading it implies.
