# Records clerk

Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, then `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-S-survey.md` and `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-A-adjudicate.md`, then this run folder `.` and the product folder `..` in full, then `{{OP}}/sot/S2-fees-and-service-hours.md` and whatever booking, accounting and enquiry records `{{OP}}` holds for this offering. You are the one clerk in this run to whom nothing is closed, because your job is to sort.

Write six files.

1. `2-blacklist/fence-index.md`. Every file in this run folder, the product folder and the wider repository that carries an opinion about this offering's parameters is closed: one row each, with the reason. This run's own earlier decisions, definition, game, absorption and claims are closed, and so is every sibling run. Then list the records open to the deriving clerks, and every file a later brief of this run names, open or closed, so no clerk meets a file the index is silent on. Where one file mixes behaviour and opinion (a meeting note that also records a refusal at the gate), close the file and carry the behaviour into the sales record with its source. Staff dossiers and delivery reports are among the files to sweep for behaviour, since a customer's request made after a session sits there and nowhere else. Where a file is open but some of its sections quote closed files, carry a buyer prior from an earlier making, or name private people, open it in part by section and say which sections are closed and why. A file that is open but carries the venue's own naming or terms in part (a search study holding the venue's own query residue and page slugs) is listed open with that part marked, so the orchestrator can weigh it before handing it to a clerk the venue is closed to.
2. `1-competitions/sales-record.md`. What this offering's buyers did, at the terms it has been sold under. The terms first (name, unit, price, length, surface, the dates each held, and whatever else was on sale beside them in each period, since a sale shows a preference only between things that were both on offer), then bookings, cancellations, refusals and enquiries that did not convert, each with its date, party, channel and outcome, split by who the buyer was. Every count states its source and its date range, and every total is re-added against the table it summarises before you return the file. No opinion, no plan, and no reason anyone at the venue gave for a term. Where the record is thin, say how thin; say which enquiry channels are logged and which are not, which mailboxes and accounts were searched, which could not be read and since when, so a count is read as a lower bound where it is one; a count that equals a tool's return cap is a floor, since the tool may have stopped there without saying so. Name what the record cannot tell (which terms a booking was bought under, why an enquiry closed). Where the record holds several populations of one thing (orders taken, orders confirmed, orders still standing), name each and say which a later count should quote. Where products typed otherwise (an activity rather than a lesson, say) may be this offering or its forerunner under another name, list them in one table, each product with its own count, and give each total with and without each product, rather than choosing. Read each system itself; where an earlier file says what a system holds, check it against the system before carrying it. Where the offering has never been on sale, the record is the enquiries alone, each count beside the condition it was taken under: what was offered, under what name, on what surface. Where two held sources contradict each other on a fact of the site or the record, give both with their dates and name the contradiction. Where a meeting record holds a dated observation of the venue's own buyers at terms other than those on sale (a voucher campaign's uptake, a free first session, a claim tested on booking clicks, a buyer who asked for another price or format), carry it into a section of its own as an item to verify, dated, marked as from a meeting and not a system, without a figure the record does not hold; a judge found such items the only observations of buyers at other terms. A booking system's internal notes are closed by name, since they hold participants' health histories; carry nothing from them.
3. `0-comparables/shape-note.md`. The operator's shape on the four dimensions of step 1 of `sage-S-survey.md`, from how the operator sells today: where this offering has been on sale, from how it is sold; where it has not, from how the operator's other offerings are sold, naming them, and nothing of any shape a file intends for this one. Mark the shape without the operator's values, since this note is handed to clerks the operator's terms are closed to.
4. `3-decisions/questions.md`. The parameters a buyer meets, as questions in a buyer's words, drawn from the method's named cards and from the parameter names of this run's earlier decisions, each question marked with which of the two it came from. Questions only, unnumbered: no value, no option, no recommendation, and nothing that says what the venue does today. No question presumes who the buyer is or who uses the offering: the buyer is unruled until the owner rules the buyer card, and the strike list only says which buyers the frame reaches, and a list written in one buyer's voice has chosen the buyer for every card written from it. Say "I" for the person asking and a neutral word for whoever takes part, and where a question arises for one kind of buyer only, keep it and say for whom.
5. `0-comparables/corpus-inventory.md`. What the coded corpus covers, as a list, each line a count with how it was counted:
    - how many profiles are held and in which cells, how many carry coded rows, and which cells carry none; which files in the profiles folder are not profiles (escalation logs and the like), so a glob of the folder is not read as the corpus;
    - which profiles keep a stored copy of the page, read from `{{CORPUS}}/shards/units-with-text.txt` as the orchestrator measured it (report any unit you would place otherwise rather than making a second list), with how many hold markup only; where that copy lives, whether it survives the run or sits outside version control; and in which cells the profiles without stored text fall, since a gap that is all one kind of operator, or the local set, biases every count taken from pages;
    - which file names do not join the index, and what field syntaxes the profiles use;
    - what keys a row at each level the corpus codes at, said in a form a script can split on, since a key that is a bare name drops out of any pass that reads strata from the path;
    - for each unit, the body that sets its terms, or a plain statement that the corpus has no such column and that every rate taken from it is a rate of units;
    - which units the survey recorded as unreachable, under the reachability column's name as this corpus spells it, and what a coder writes for them, since coders otherwise settle that three ways;
    - which addresses the capture record holds, counting its document rows and not its page list alone, and which profiles hold no address at all, where a re-fetch of pages is foreseeable;
    - where the operator's own unit sits. It is a row outside every rate; mark it so in the fence index.
6. `0-comparables/findings-market-side.md`. The survey's findings cut to the market's side, for the clerks who derive from the market alone: every section of the survey's findings that reports what other operators do, with the survey's own numbering kept so that a gap shows, and every paragraph naming this operator, its prices, its site or its pages left out. Cut it by script over the findings file and say in the head which sections were dropped and why, so a reader knows the file is a cut and not the whole. Where a dropped paragraph also carried a market fact that no kept section holds, name it in the head, so the orchestrator can have it cleaned and restored rather than lost.
