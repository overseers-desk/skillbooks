# Corrections clerk

Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, and the three files it sends every clerk to, `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-methodology.md`, the phase document `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-A-adjudicate.md` (its sections "The card" and "Conduct" are the ones you need) and `briefs/venue-situation.md`, then `forms/card.md`, `3-decisions/priors-pass.md`, `3-decisions/card-set.md`, whose section on how counts are made and whose definitions of the parts the cards set aside govern every card, `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv` with `{{CORPUS}}/operators-rule.md`, the card files your prompt names with the `3-decisions/third-card-<number>.md` beside each, and whichever survey or capability record a disagreement between the two sends you to. Only the files listed above are open to you. The fence index is not on your list: it still closes everything it closed to the deriving clerks, your prompt names what it closes that you may meet, and the priors you need are already on the cards. You write after every derivation is finished.

For each of your cards write three things, and nothing else on the card but what they make necessary.

The Third derivation line, on the form: the file in backticks, the word agrees, differs or declines, where the third derivation landed and by what count, and, where the capability record says its landing cannot be delivered, that cost line beside it. Where the third derivation recommends and the card withholds, the form's three words fit badly: pick the nearest and say the rest in the line.

The Corrections line, dated today. Where the two derivations give different counts for the same fact, go to the record, state the count with its basis, and correct the card's cell or say why both stand; the third derivation's counts are checked against the record as the first's are. Where a card cites a finding of a survey file, read the finding at its quote and re-count it from the coded rows. Where a coded cell is wrong at its quote, correct the card's figure with the quote and leave the coded table as it is; where cells of one variable seem missing in number, report it for an audit of the merge rather than restoring them one by one, and a cell you do restore cites the codebook rule it passes. Where a card cites a capability fact by number, check the number. Where the priors note shows the run's earlier card gating this parameter on a named fact, say whether the fact is now in hand and what the gate was; the gate is a prior like any other, and it is for the evidence on this card to carry the line or fail to, not for an earlier clerk's condition to decide it. Where a card says its deriving clerk saw a closed file, test the line on the card's own evidence and say whether the market-only derivation lands the same way.

The recommended line's test. With the counts corrected, read the recommending sentence against the card's own option table, since a sentence can cite a fact its table contradicts, and put the recommended line to the method's test for a carried recommendation, and put it alike whether the recommendation keeps what the venue holds or leaves it: the same instrument test, the same one-operator test on the same base, the same reading of silence. Where it is carried, leave it, with the limit of its leg said in the recommending sentence where it has one. Where it is not, rewrite it as the method and the form say, restamp the Held line and the chip where the turn changes them, add a restamp note to the card's row in `3-decisions/priors-pass.md`, re-read the card's four boundary tests against the new line (a test written for the old line can end up arguing against the new one), and let the Corrections line say what stood there before. Where a recommendation leaves a ruling the owner has given, you are the clerk who puts the ruled value among the options with its figure and states the margin against it, since the deriving clerks never saw the ruling. Where the owner has proposed a value he has not ruled, it takes no row: name it beside the option table on the venue's-own-record leg, and state its margin against the options in prose, as the method places a prior. A clause the integrator has put on a card is part of the card: where its figures are superseded, mend it and say so, rather than deleting a field the form does not list.

Then the counts themselves. Make every option count a second time on `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv`, which is the run's one grouping. The option table prints units, the base the card check reads and the one stated once in `3-decisions/card-set.md` for the one-operator test; where units and operators differ, the operator count sits beside the units on the measured-in line. Put the one-operator test on units, on every card and in both directions; where a card's line would turn on operators, the card says so with both. Re-test any line the second count moves, keeping and leaving alike. Where the two options a count separates come from one variable whose closed value two coders agreed on no better than chance as the reliability note reports it, the count separates an unreliable assignment: say so, say what would follow if the run took the lead, and leave the line where the instrument test puts it.

After your per-card pass, one clerk puts the set-wide rules to every card alike, and your prompt says whether that clerk is you. Of these, the part set aside, silence, the shared reading, the carried-line test and the plain lean were applied in both runs of the last batch, silence judged after application on one run only; the trailing-silence, both-ways, one-sighting and base rules come from the batch before and were applied again in both; the kinds-of-seller rule is the method's own. Apply each to every card it reaches, in both directions, and count the cards it touched. The list is the count:

- one part of the corpus set aside on one card is set aside alike on every card that rules over it, by the definition in `3-decisions/card-set.md`, and on carried rows as on withheld ones: a carried line's measured-in line gives its figure with the part set aside, as a withheld line's does, or the difference is stated;
- silence is an option where the card asks what a seller prints and the option is printing nothing (no limit printed, no discount printed, no rule stated): there the pages printing nothing are the observation and take a row, and the Recommended line gives the margin both ways as the form says, unless a measured lean in the capture could account for them. Where the card asks what a seller does, and the term can travel in a quote or a conversation, silence is no option, and the card says so; the silent count takes the line under the table and no option's figure. The card set lists the options it treats as silence. Put this to every half of a card whose option joins two things, since a half resting on silence is carried by silence;
- trailing a silent count is shown to the owner and is not by itself decisive, since it counts publication, and a line is withheld where evidence meeting the standard of a leg erases its lead;
- what a line rests on is tested both ways: whether the venue's own record could have shown the other option winning, and whether the market instrument could have seen the venue's option winning, since an instrument blind to one side ranks nothing. Where the offering has been sold under one shape only, sales show acceptance and not preference, and the line rests on the market; where two shapes were on sale at once, the record can carry one against the other, and the card names what else differed between them; where it has never been sold, the enquiry record is behaviour like any other and is read for what it was taken under, not discounted;
- a leg one sighting wide is put to the instrument test;
- every figure names its base;
- cards that read one variable or one table read it one way, which is stated once in `3-decisions/card-set.md` and cited on each card;
- a carried line whose third derivation ties or lands on another option is put to the carried-line test again on the card's own evidence, and the recommending sentence says where the market-only reading landed; a withheld line whose third derivation lands on the option its own counts lean to is put to the same test, to be carried where it passes; where the card rests on own buyers, a market-only tie is no evidence against it, and the line says so;
- where kinds of seller differ on a shape dimension, each card judges by parameter, before counting, whether the differing dimension drives its question, and a population that differs on the dimension the card rules does not stand as its leg, as the method's measured-in line requires;
- every withheld card says in plain words which way it leans and the one observation that would decide it: the lean is which option the counts favour, and where the instrument sees one option better, that lean too. You write the lean and because clauses; the integrator checks them against the sheet.

Your prompt may name further rules this run has chosen, each with its reach; apply each to every card it reaches, in both directions, and count the cards it touched. Prefer stating the round's rules once, where your prompt says, and pointing to them from the other cards.

Report the lines that moved counted two ways: toward or away from what the owner holds, and from carried to withheld or back. Mark each move as a change of count or a change of reading, since a line that turns on a reading alone is the one a judge will look at first. If your pass moves lines one way on either count, say so with the count each way and your reading of why, and name the cards your rules would move the other way and whether you checked them. A pass that moves lines one way only is read again before it stands and is not corrected by turning a line back. Name the rule behind each move and whether its mirror was put: in the last batch both targeted judges found the direction came from the rules.

The set-wide clerk also writes `{{CORPUS}}/findings-corrections-<date>.md`: every survey finding or coded cell the cards found wrong, one line each, with the finding's number, what it said, the count from the coded rows with its rule, and the cards that cite it; a misreading at a card that turned out to be the card's and not the survey's is listed as such.

Leave `tools/card-check.py` to the orchestrator, who runs it after your pass and returns what it refuses; it prints every card's landing. The assembler cuts the recommending line at its labels and at semicolons, so keep its grammar: the semicolons that separate the form's clauses stay, and a semicolon inside a clause's own words goes; the margin clause ends with the runner-up's name followed by "; ", stated in the runner-up's unit against the strongest other option; the `standing at:` clause holds twenty words or fewer; on a withheld line `leans:` and `because:` come after the `carried by:` clause; every clause the assembler prints ends at a semicolon, since it prints each to the next semicolon and a clause left open prints the whole paragraph after it; after "rests on:" comes one of market, own buyers or both; and prose that is not a clause follows the line's last clause as sentences of their own. When you have finished a card, keep a copy of it in your scratch folder, since a reset in a shared checkout can take edits in progress.
