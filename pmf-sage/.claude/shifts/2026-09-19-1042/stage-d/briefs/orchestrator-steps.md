# Orchestrator's steps: what is made and checked around each clerk

This file stays beside the templates and is not copied into a run's `briefs/` folder, where the launch check would read it as a brief. The templates say what a clerk does; this says what has to exist before one launches and what is read when it returns.

## Filling a template

Copy the templates into the run's `briefs/`, and the files under `run-forms/` into the run's own `forms/` folder beside the method's, since a clerk is sent to `forms/third-derivation.md` by that name.

Replace `{{OP}}` (the operator repository's root), `{{CORPUS}}` (the coded corpus folder), `{{CODEBOOK}}`, `{{AMENDMENT}}` (the amendment file the author writes), `{{CODED}}` (the folder the coders write into), `{{PROFILES}}`, `{{FINDINGS}}` (the market-side cut, never the whole findings file), `{{PAGES}}` (the folder holding page text, whether kept at capture or re-fetched), `{{CAPABILITY}}`, `{{CLUSTER}}`, `{{MARKET_MAP}}`, `{{COMPETITION_NOTE}}`, `{{SEARCH_DATA}}`, `{{START}}`, `{{ASSEMBLER}}` (the review assembler the integrator runs: the copy beside `method-improvements.md` where the shift keeps one, since the method's own prints no lean or because clause, else `tools/assemble-review.py`), `{{READING}}` (the date of the method's reading this run actually runs under, which moves when a change to the words about evidence is judged), and `{{ON_SALE}}`, which takes whichever of four wordings is true: "This offering has been on sale since <date>; the sales record holds the terms it sold under"; "This offering has never been on sale; there are no terms it sells under, and the record is the enquiries alone"; "This offering is not on sale. A forerunner, <what it was>, was sold from <date> to <date>; the sales record holds the terms it sold under, which show that buyers accepted those terms and not that they preferred them"; "This offering has never been on sale. A related offering, <what>, is on sale now, and some of this offering's buyers may have bought it; the sales record lists those bookings apart"; or, where buyers paid for the offering under other products while a listing of its own stands unbought, "This offering has been sold by invoice since <date> under several products; a listed <product> has stood in the booking system since <date> and has <its bookings>; the sales record holds the terms sold under and the terms listed." Where the offering has been sold under several names, the first wording names them. A listing no buyer has bought under is not "on sale" in the first wording's sense.

A run opened before a template rewrite has its `forms/` and `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` re-copied from the rewritten set and re-filled before its Adjudicate stage, and its Adjudicate briefs filled from the rewritten templates.

Where `{{PAGES}}` holds pages kept at capture rather than re-fetched, say so in the coders' prompts, since the page-text brief branches on when the text was taken.

Then run `python3 tools/launch-check.py <run-dir>` and clear it: every brief needs its read-order paragraph first, and a line that fixes a buyer or a price shape needs its mark. It writes `briefs/venue-situation.md`, which the standing block sends every clerk to.

Two rules of your own hold throughout. Anything several clerks must apply alike is made as a file, a table or a script, and the clerks are sent to it. Nothing launches on a file another clerk still has open; a record written before a coding is refreshed before the next clerk reads it.

## Survey

Before step 1, measure by script which profiles have a stored pages folder and which folders hold text files rather than markup alone, counting in and beneath each folder. Where profiles lack pages, a re-fetch clerk fetches what their capture records address, into a dated folder under the corpus's data folder, and logs the profiles that hold no address; where folders hold markup only, a clerk derives text beside the markup, additively and with a log. Write the measured list to `{{CORPUS}}/shards/units-with-text.txt`, which every later clerk reads and none re-makes. Nothing is piloted or sharded before this.

1. Records clerk. Returns the fence index, the sales record, the shape note, the questions, the corpus inventory and the market-side cut of the findings. Check the cut by grepping it for the venue's name, for its own site and for its prices, and read its head for dropped paragraphs that carried a market fact; check the inventory names the key each level is coded on, says whether the corpus has an operator column, and gives coverage of stored text by cell; check the fence index opens a mixed file in part by section rather than whole.
2. Number the questions once, by script from `3-decisions/questions.md` into `3-decisions/questions-numbered.md`, each question carrying over the source mark the list gives it (the method's named cards, or a parameter of the earlier decisions), since the amendment author and the cards read the mark there, before any card is written, and re-run the script after any edit to the list, since the two files otherwise drift. Move earlier card sets into `superseded/` so that one numbering is in play.
3. Search-demand clerk and capability clerk, in parallel with the amendment. Say in the search-demand prompt which of step 1's seed sources you open (the enquiry record's phrases; the comparables' own self-descriptions; the operator's own names for what it sells), and whether a new database pull is allowed, which the owner has not ruled (until he does, none is made and the unmade lookups close the study as one batch), as a choice with its cost: opened, the venue's wording enters a file market-only clerks will be handed and has to stay in its marked section; closed, step 1 is a named gap for that source. Grep the returned study for the venue's name, site and product names outside its marked section, front matter included. Check the capability extract numbers every fact and every absence in one series.
4. Codebook amendment author. Name in its prompt the columns the coded tables already join on. Read the returned amendment for a closed list with a second prose test beside it, for a membership test that would count words rather than what the variable means, for the row keys at each level, and for a closed value spelt more than once; send the rules whose trial the author could not run to a second clerk, on passages drawn by seed from the stored pages. Settle which variables the first pass codes and which over the whole corpus or a seeded draw, and write the choice in the journal: both runs of the third batch took the buyer variable and the four shape dimensions at unit level and left the rest until a card asks, which cost some later cards a count (they counted from profiles as floors).
5. Record-form clerk. Cut the headers and the header notes, and, where you ask for them, the units lists, the shards and the reliability sample, naming the seed and the strata. Check each header against the amendment's field lists column by column, and check that the names the coding briefs use are the names on disk. From its split of the units with text by the survey's offer column, choose which parts the pass covers and write the choice with what it leaves uncounted (one run held back 52 units that name the offering and state nothing of it).
6. Pilot, where the amendment is large or its levels are new: two coders on the same seeded sample, of the size the amendment asks. A smaller pilot is a choice to state with what it cannot see, since a matching rule written from a few units meets forms it never saw. Read what could not be joined before what disagreed.
7. Reliability sample drawn by seed within strata, coded blind by two coders from profiles and by one from page text. Run `agreement.py`, stripping stray quotation marks first and comparing several-valued variables as sets. A variable is coded from pages over the whole corpus where the page-text coder finds a value stated, in units both profile coders called not stated, more often than the two profile coders part from each other on that variable; short of that, the profile counts are floors and every later brief says so. Set that comparison out variable by variable, in figures, before deciding. Where the run keeps stored text for most units, write in the journal the share with text that decides you to code from pages, and this comparison becomes the coverage check above. The amended rules can be tested by a second pilot or inside the full pass, on a fresh sample drawn by a new seed and coded twice blind; the second saves a round of coders and keeps the test off the review's own worked cases, and costs a variable that fails there a pass already made. Either is a choice to write in the journal.
8. Top the sample up, by seed, from units the first coding found stated, until each variable a finding will cite has been seen stated by both coders: agreement on silence says nothing.
9. Shards cut from the profile folder's listing, not from an earlier coded table, with the venue's own unit left out, and each coder writing into its own folder. Say in every coder's prompt that the sibling folders are closed, when the page texts were taken, and whether later-fetched pages may be coded under this amendment, in agreement with the amendment's own capture rule, which a prompt does not override. On every returned file check the row count against the shard and the column count against the header.
10. Merge the returned shard files by script into `{{CODED}}`'s one table, only once every coder whose file the merge reads has returned, checking the row count against the shards and the column count against the header, and running the script that sets the amendment-version and operator fields . Write the reliability note beside it: every disagreeing cell with each coder's value and words, the variables the sample never saw stated, the page-text comparison of step 7, and a validity figure where the coders' `validity` notes or a seeded sample give one. Gather the wave's notes by script, in the coder block's one grammar (`<label>: <section number> <text>`), for one review a run rather than one a coder.
11. Rule-review clerk on the disputed cells and the gathered notes, with what each rule measures as a question of its own. Then cut `header-recode.tsv` from the amendment's changed variables, and draw a fresh sample by a new seed from units the review never saw, for the agreement read; the old sample measures recall of the review's worked cases. Record in the journal, for each variable the change list names, whether it will be re-coded and by which instrument, or will carry counts under the earlier version.
12. Re-coding, by the instrument the review names for each variable.
    - a. A rule a coder applies: re-coders on the variables the change list names; set by script the cells the review says a script sets. Merge and re-write the reliability note as in step 10.
    - b. A phrase list plus a judgement: the candidate-sentence clerk, on the variables that take it. Read its recall note before anything else launches: the recall against the first coders' recorded sentences, each miss and its reason, the words carrying the candidates in more than one sense, and the vocabulary choices it made, which you ratify or send back on the record. Decide whether the candidate table is committed, since it may be derived from a folder outside version control and then cannot be rebuilt from a fresh clone.
    - c. Choose the reading and cut the shards. The readers may judge every candidate, or the sentences the candidate table flags as near the offering's own word, with named additions, as kayak hire did (3,535 sentences of about 5,500). The narrow reading keeps to what operators say about the offering and roughly halves the reading, which in that run was mostly homographs; it hides what a venue selling many things says on pages away from the offering (there 27 to 33 of 73 destination venues named a buyer, against 94 to 102 of 134 specialists), and a card then needs a top-up to read those pages. Before choosing, measure on the recall note how many candidate rows of each buyer class the flag keeps: where the offering's own word is on nearly every sentence the flag buys nothing, and where a buyer is named on a page apart from the offering (a funding page, a groups page) the narrow reading hides exactly that buyer. Write the choice in the journal and name the reading in every later prompt that carries its figures. Cut shards of a few hundred candidates by script, balanced by unit, keeping one operator's units together by `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv`; cut `header-adjudication.tsv` once, and beside each shard `<shard>-pairings.tsv`, the one list of pairings both its readers judge.
    - d. Candidate adjudicators (`candidate-adjudicator.md`, its prompt stating the key column's name from the candidate table's header), two a shard and blind of each other, in waves the usage window can carry. A reader cut by a timeout or a usage limit is resumed from its own transcript, where its reading of the brief and its judgements so far sit, rather than relaunched.
    - e. Merge the readers' files by script once every reader of the run has returned, taking every figure from the files and not from the readers' reports: agreement by pairing, and at unit level both readers to either reader, by class or value. Before the merge sets a class from its kinds, list every row where a kind is stated under a class coded 0, by reader, for the strike-list clerk; then state the merge rule for classes and kinds (a class stated where any of its kinds is). Gather the readers' `rule point` notes for the next amendment. The ranges replace the first pass's counts, which become upper bounds and are not quoted.
13. Operator-index clerk, then a blind second making of a seeded sample of the table by another clerk closed to the first. It reads no coding, so run it beside the coding from step 9 on and before step 12c, whose shards it serves; hand it the candidate clerk's list of units sharing pages as evidence where that comes first. Name a private scratch folder in its prompt. The clerk makes both readings of an operator (owner first; terms-setter), and nobody has ruled which a run cites: state in the prompt which this run cites, before it launches, with the cost the template sets out for each. Until the owner rules, the strike list and every card print both counts wherever they differ, so no reading is taken silently. For each pair the table marks as drawn twice, decide which row stands where the two differ, and write it. The table is the run's one grouping from here on.
14. Records-refresh clerk, once the coding lands, before the strike list. Read its list of coded rows that break the amendment's own rules, of units without a row, and of findings that no longer reproduce, and settle each before the strike list launches.
15. Strike-list clerk. State in its prompt the run's citing threshold for a rate, and the reading each two-step variable was judged under. Re-add its totals yourself against the source, and close any open file it reports carrying a buyer prior or naming private people. Two things here are the owner's to rule and he has not, so state each on the record before the clerk launches. Whether he sees the list: put it to him where he can be asked; where he cannot, the run goes on with every row unstruck, which costs a buyer he would have struck carried through every card. And whether a list made after the coding is accepted for a re-run: the method has the strike list on day one, before the frame is drawn, and these re-runs make it from the coded buyer variable over a corpus drawn earlier, which lets the list rest on the corpus's evidence and costs the reason for the order, that a codebook frozen around one population is not un-frozen by a later striking. Until he rules, re-runs make the list after the coding and say so. The clerk leaves the owner's fields blank: you fill the Returned line with what happened, opening with the date: the date he returned it, or his words declining it; where he could not be asked, say so, and say it in every Adjudicate prompt, since `tools/launch-check.py` reads any line opening with a digit as returned and tells every clerk the frame reaches those buyers as if he had returned the list. Then re-run the launch check: until it is re-run, `briefs/venue-situation.md` tells every clerk that no strike list exists and the frame reaches no buyer.

## Adjudicate

16. Before the lead deriving clerk, and after step 15's re-run of the launch check, read `2-blacklist/fence-index.md` against the Adjudicate briefs as filled. Every file a brief opens is open in the fence and every file a brief closes is closed there. A file open in part names its closed sections by heading: batch three met a register four of whose sections quote closed files, a search study whose residue section, two findings and front matter name the venue, a catchment file one section of which holds the owner's own figure and another reuses postcodes from a closed file, and a regulatory note whose head and two sections a market-only clerk may not see. Each closure says which clerks it binds, since "not for a clerk the venue is closed to" was read as barring the area clerks from the sales record their own brief lists. Where the card set will name a part of the corpus drawn by an existing column, check the column against each unit's own description by script first. Write into `3-decisions/card-set.md`, under how counts are made, that the one-operator test runs on units (step 20). Then the lead deriving clerk; settle the number of area clerks from the areas the card set returns, then launch them. Check the card numbers against the numbered questions, that the third list names a role against each item, that each card is marked with every standing buyer whose seller's sentence it answers to, and that no card or sentence names a private buyer.
17. Market-only clerks: before launch, grep every file on the read list for the venue's name, its site, its locality, its prices, its products' and forerunner's names and the words that name its own unit, and cut or close by name what hits; name in each prompt the sections open of every file open in part. The findings file, the folder holding the venue's own coded unit, and a search study's findings and front matter have all leaked this way. `briefs/venue-situation.md` and the shape note are not on a market-only list at all: the note is generated by the launch check and cannot be cut, and on both batch-four runs it carries the venue's own bookings and listed terms.
18. Priors clerk, in one pass after the cards. The template ranks held values: the owner's ruling; terms a buyer paid for (a sale, an invoice); terms listed and never bought; a draft, a definition or a plan, a definition entered into the booking system the day it was written and never bought ranking as a draft. State in the prompt where the tiers the template does not place go (a standard procedure, an owner's stated position that is not a ruling); nobody has ruled those, and the riding academy's round put a standard procedure above a draft and the owner's stated position last. Check the returned pass: a bounds column; every bound on its own Bound line with the Ruled line reading "open" alone; no definition's values on a Ruled line; no card still carrying the form's placeholder chip; dollar signs escaped.
19. Before the third-derivation clerks launch, a middle-tier clerk cuts `2-blacklist/venue-situation-market.md` from the venue-situation note: the operator's shape mark on each dimension (shares or differs, against each kind of seller in the corpus, written as "kind of seller"), and no count, price, deposit, booking or product name of the venue's. Grep it as step 17 greps. It lives under `2-blacklist/`, so the launch check does not read it as a brief, and is written once the shape note is final. Then the third-derivation clerks, by range, on market files only, each writing on the copied form, their read lists opening that copy, the card set's part-definitions and the cut tables, and nothing else of the venue's. Name in each prompt the variables its questions rest on that no unit was coded on, so the clerk labels its counts on them as one reader's.
20. Run `tools/card-check.py` yourself after every clerk that writes cards returns, and hand back what it refuses: it prints every card's landing, so no deriving or corrections clerk runs it. Then the corrections clerks, by range, then one clerk over the whole set for the set-wide rules. Give a clerk the rule and never the verdict. State in every corrections prompt the survey findings and coded cells already known wrong, that the populations the cards set aside are defined in `3-decisions/card-set.md` and nowhere else, and which of the choices under step 22 this run takes. Where per-card clerks restore coded cells by hand, audit the merged table against its shards and the profiles on those variables before the set-wide pass.

    The one-operator test runs on units, stated once in `3-decisions/card-set.md` before the first corrections clerk, with the operator count beside the units on the measured-in line wherever they differ. The card check reads units and does not know "operators" as a count unit, so an operators base would cost refusals on every value card until it does. Units count a double-drawn operator twice and, in a corpus of chains, one decision many times, which is why the operator count sits beside them and a card whose line would turn on operators says so with both.
21. Integrator. Commit the page the moment the integrator reports, before any other git act. Its paragraph headed "Withheld, by what the observation costs" is the top-up clerk's work list, so it runs before the top-up, then the top-up, then a corrections re-test of the cards the top-up touches, then the integrator again. Whether the top-up runs before the page reaches the owner is a choice to state in the top-up's prompt: batch three deferred both runs' top-ups, which sent 13 and 20 withheld rows to him, and the kayak judges counted about six rows that a few hours of lookups and calls would have carried.
22. The owner's cold read of the front, before the judges, its faults going straight to the integrator: a page he cannot rule from is the first failure and the cheapest to find. Then two judges blind of each other. Before any judge reads the front, assemble the page from the cards as they now stand. Turn the judges' findings into rules, never verdicts, for one fresh corrections clerk, then have the integrator refresh the page.

    The rules a judges' round applies are in the corrections template, the kinds-of-seller rule among them, since the method's measured-in line already requires it. The owner's own proposal is placed as the method places a prior, beside the table on the venue's-own-record leg with its margin in prose, and takes no row. These were one run's, and each is a choice to state in the prompt with its cost:

    - the venue's observations of its own buyers at other terms: where a record holds one (an enquiry for something not offered, a group refused a space), it is already a leg by the method and no choice arises; where only a meeting holds it (a voucher campaign's uptake, a free first lesson, a click test on a claim), it is placed on its card as a dated item to verify, without a figure (riding academy): the only observations of buyers at terms not on sale, from meetings and not from systems;
    - the gate leading the because-clause (kayak hire), for a rule that binds every seller (a regulation, a peak body's scheme): the owner meets the rule first, and the market reading loses its place at the head of the clause. The venue's own cover conditions are a capability fact and go on the cost lines, not at the head of the clause;
    - a priced trial at the venue named as the deciding observation where price, turns, payment or walk-up need real buyers (kayak hire): the one observation that sees buyers at terms not yet on sale; it needs cover and the owner's consent, and on a funded scheme the scheme's price rules and the participants' consent arrangements;
    - a single reader's count on a variable no coder coded may limit a line, meaning it is stated beside the line and moves it neither way (kayak hire), or may move a line either way: the first keeps an unreplicated count from turning a card; the second treats both directions alike and lets one reader decide;
    - the round's rules stated once on card 0's Corrections entry and the other cards pointing there (kayak hire): one statement to read, and a card read alone sends its reader elsewhere.

    A round that moves lines one way goes to a targeted judge before the page refreshes, and the judge's rules go to one more round. Count the direction two ways, toward or away from what the owner holds and from carried to withheld or back. The riding academy put its targeted judge's rules and the two judges' findings into one resume, so the cards changed once; kayak hire ran them as two rounds, which keeps each round's direction legible and costs a round.
23. Where a change to the words clerks read about evidence was made during the run, a judge reads its result before it reaches another run, and `{{READING}}` moves.
24. Run-record clerk last. Name in its prompt where the owner's recorded words sit, so it quotes them from their source.

## Figures, facts and choices you write yourself

A count you put into a brief or a prompt is read value by value against its source first. A population the cards set aside is defined once, in `3-decisions/card-set.md`, and a prompt points there rather than restating it. A fact about a folder is read against a listing of the folder before it goes into a prompt. A cause you state in a prompt is offered as a hypothesis for the clerk to test. A figure a clerk returns is taken from its file by script, not from its report. The on-sale sentence filled before the records clerk returns is a guess: the records clerk's prompt asks it to test the sentence against the booking system, and the standing block is re-filled from its return before any other clerk launches. Read each prompt against the brief it carries before launch.

A choice made for one run is written into the journal when it is made, with what it costs, and every clerk whose figures it shapes is told in its prompt: which variables the first pass codes, which parts of the corpus wait uncoded, the pilot's size, whether amended rules are tested by a second pilot or inside the full pass, the reading a two-step variable is judged under, which operator reading the run cites, the citing threshold for a rate, where the tiers the priors template does not place go, whether the top-up runs before the page reaches the owner, and which of the judges' round choices the run takes. The steps above offer these as choices because nobody has ruled them; a later run may choose otherwise and say why. Five of them, and the gates around them, are the owner's to rule and wait for him: whether he sees each strike list, whether the buyer card is put to him before the card set, whether a new search pull is allowed, which operator reading is cited, and whether a strike list made after the coding is accepted for a re-run. Steps 3, 13 and 15 give the default each run takes meanwhile; for the buyer card the default is that every standing buyer is carried and each card is marked with its buyers.

Every clerk's prompt names a private scratch folder. A clerk that writes under the operator repository's path is told in its prompt to write with the file tools, since a shell command carrying code is refused there; where the harness refuses the Write tool on a path whose name holds "findings" or "report", the clerk writes in its scratch folder and copies the file into place. A clerk cut by a timeout or a usage limit is resumed from its own transcript rather than relaunched. In a checkout shared with another session, nothing runs that discards or moves uncommitted changes (a reset, a checkout of a path, a stash pop); a rebase runs as `git rebase --autostash` after a fetch and stops and reports on a conflict.
