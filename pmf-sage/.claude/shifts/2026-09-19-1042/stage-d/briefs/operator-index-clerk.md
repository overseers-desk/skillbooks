# Operator index clerk

Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, then `0-comparables/corpus-inventory.md`, the corpus index under `{{CORPUS}}`, and the comparable profiles under `{{PROFILES}}` as your rule sends you to them, with its pages: run bin/corpus-unit {{PAGES}} <unit> (the profile's name without .md) and read every file in the folder it prints; search across units with bin/corpus-grep, where a profile's addresses are not enough. Beyond those and the standing block's own files, nothing in the repository is open to you: no card, no finding, nothing the venue holds.

Several units of a corpus of this kind belong to one body that publishes one decision across many sites, so a count of units can count one decision many times; and a frame drawn from several registers can draw one operator twice as two units. Write the one grouping the run will use.

Write `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv`, tab-separated, one row for every unit of the index that is not marked a duplicate, keyed by the column the coded tables are keyed on so that the two join: the key, the operator under the reading the run cites (the body that sets this unit's terms, named as the unit's own profile names it), the operator under the other reading, the basis (named in profile, shared domain, shared document, drawn twice, or own), the evidence (the profile's words or the address that shows it, short; for a unit drawn twice, the other unit's key), and a doubt column, empty or holding what a second reader might say instead.

Two readings of "operator" stand, and nobody has ruled between them. Owner first: an owning body the profile itself names as owning or operating the site groups its sites, which counts one decision per owner and merges sites that set their own terms. Terms-setter: units are grouped only where one page or document sets the terms they sell under, which keeps such sites apart and counts one owner's policy several times wherever its brand page was missed. In one corpus the two gave 24 operators and six for the same 24 sites. Make both columns, give both counts, and cite the one your prompt names; the orchestrator states that choice on the record before you launch.

Under either reading, and after the owning body where the reading uses it: the registrable domain the unit's own pages sit on, where two or more units share it and a page on it sets their terms; a schedule, terms document or booking calendar at one address cited by two or more units; failing those, the unit is its own operator. A marketplace, directory, register or booking platform's domain is not a unit's own pages, so listings on one platform are not one operator. An association's, federation's or franchisor's domain that hosts pages for members who set their own terms counts as a platform's for this rule: a shared domain groups units only where a page on it sets the terms they sell under, and the rule document says how many units that decided. Say so in the rule document, since that is the likeliest point where a second clerk parts from you. General knowledge of who owns whom is not evidence here: where the profile does not say it and the addresses do not show it, the unit stands alone and the doubt column may say what you suspect. Where one body's two brands publish opposite postures, the collapse has no single answer: group them and say so in the doubt column.

A unit drawn twice is one operator appearing as two units, not two sites of one body: mark it with the basis "drawn twice". Find such pairs by identical quoted text across profiles and by the domains in the capture record and the page texts, since the frame's own website column may hold the register's address rather than the unit's. Take as evidence the list of units sharing pages or quoted text that the candidate-sentence clerk hands you, where one exists. Where a pair's two profiles or coded rows disagree (an offer status, a value), name the pair and both values in the doubt column and settle neither: which row stands is the orchestrator's call.

Work by script where a rule can be run by script (the domains, the shared addresses, identical quoted text, a search of every profile for each owning body you find, whole words only), and write the rule and the commands into `{{CORPUS}}/operators-rule.md` so that another clerk can run them again, with the count of operators under each reading and under any other a second clerk might take, the groups of two or more with their sizes, the pairs drawn twice, and the units whose operator you doubted. Keep your working files in the private scratch folder your prompt names.
