# Strike-list clerk

Read `briefs/standing-brief-block.md` first, then `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-S-survey.md`, the section "The card set" of `/usr/local/src/aesop/pmf-sage/sage-A-adjudicate.md`, and `forms/strike-list.md`; then `2-blacklist/fence-index.md` and, of this run's files, only the records its open list names, among them `{{AMENDMENT}}`, `{{CODED}}.tsv`, `{{CORPUS}}/coded-reliability.md`, `{{CORPUS}}/rule-review.md`, `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv`, `{{CORPUS}}/candidates/recall-check.md` and `{{CORPUS}}/candidates/adjudicated-merged.tsv` where the buyer variable was made by the two-step instrument, `{{FINDINGS}}`, `0-comparables/corpus-inventory.md`, `0-comparables/shape-note.md`, `1-competitions/search-demand-findings.md` and `1-competitions/sales-record.md`. Everything the fence index closes is closed to you.

Write three files.

1. `{{CORPUS}}/findings-buyers-market.md`: the market's buyer findings, numbered B1 onward, from the coding (each class's count over the units whose buyer variable could be read, by cell where the cells differ, with the agreement figure that bears on it), what the catchment's operators publish, and what buyers type. Each finding states its measured population and its limit. Say plainly beside each class count that it counts whom operators publish for, not whom buyers choose, and that a class no page names is absent from print and not from the market. Nothing of this operator's own record enters this file, since it is the one a clerk the venue is closed to will be handed, and the operator's own coded unit is a row outside every rate.
2. `{{CORPUS}}/findings-buyers-operator.md`: what the operator's own record says of its buyers, numbered O1 onward, from the sales record split by buyer, each count beside the terms it was taken under and the channels the record does and does not log. Where the offering is already on sale, say plainly that a sale shows a buyer accepted the terms on offer and does not show that those terms were preferred to others; where it is not yet on sale, say what that leaves the record unable to show. Where the sales record lists products typed otherwise that may be this offering, give each total with and without each of them, as the record's table does.
3. `3-decisions/strike-list.md` on `forms/strike-list.md`: every buyer this offering could have is a row, the market's rows and the venue's own alike, each cell filled from a named source or reading "not searched" or "not coded". The form has no recommendation field and the list makes none. Leave the Returned line and every owner's field at the head of the form blank for the orchestrator, who holds the closed run record and carries the owner's standing words onto the form.

The limits that bind every count you take from the coding, since a later clerk will quote your words and not the reliability note:

- A count made from profiles rather than pages is a floor, by about the margin the note measures: write "not quoted in the profile" where you would have written "not stated".
- A variable the note marks untested, or short of the run's citing threshold, carries its counts and no rate, with the note's figure beside it; so does a figure judged by one reader alone. Say which threshold you applied and where it is written; where your prompt and the run name none, say so and say which you took.
- A count from two readers blind of each other is a range (both readers to either reader), by unit and by operator, taken from the merge's output rather than re-made, with the pairings the readers agreed on beside it.
- Every figure names the reading it was measured under where the orchestrator chose one for the run (a buyer addressed within a sentence of the offering's own word, say), and the list says which rows that reading can hide: in one run such a reading found far fewer buyers among destination venues, whose group pages sit away from the offering's page.
- Where a rule review moved a value from one variable to another, say so in both findings, since a reader comparing the two otherwise reads a fall in one as a fall in the market.

Where the run keeps no separate register of local rivals, the rivals column reports the catchment part of the coded corpus and says so, rather than reading empty. Where it keeps one, state the register's inclusion test and mark a row the test cannot see as outside it, not as nought. A finding carried from a hand-made register rather than from the coded table says so, and one a card is likely to cite is re-read at the rival's own page.

Every total on the list is re-added against its source before you return it. Where parts do not add up to the whole (an operator counted in two kinds), say why. An inconsistency between a class and its kinds, either way (a class stated while its kind reads not present, a kind stated under a class coded 0), is a coding fault to report and not a count to publish; the merge lists the rows where a kind was stated under a class coded 0, by reader, and you report them from that list. Contradictions you meet are listed, not resolved silently: those between market findings (two findings on one count) at the foot of the market file, and those that touch the operator's record (a sales record against its own tables) at the foot of the operator file, which no market-only clerk is handed; the operator file's list also names any open file you find carrying a buyer prior from an earlier making or naming private people, for the orchestrator to close. Close each buyer file with what is unmade and owed on its side: the readings not made and the lookups not run in the market file, the channels not read in the operator file.
