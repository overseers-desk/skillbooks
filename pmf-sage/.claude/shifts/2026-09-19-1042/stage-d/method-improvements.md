# Improvements to the method, tried in the runs and not yet written into it

The user's instruction of 20 September 2026: "do not believe pmf-sage is perfect, improve method as you see problems, and we can review what improvements should be permanentalised by the end of the run by you create an artifact to me. improvement suggestions are given to each subagent run without coding into aespo but collect learning along the way."

So the method's own files (`sage-*.md`, `INVARIANTS.md`, `forms/`, `tools/`) stay as they stand at commit 441bb98. An improvement reaches a run through the run's briefs, its shard and header files, or a script kept beside this file. Each entry below says what went wrong and where, what was given to the clerks in its place, what happened when it was tried, and what the permanent change would be. The end of the run turns this file into the artifact the user reviews.

## 1. The coders' header is a file, not a sentence

- Seen: chapel hire, 19 September. Sixteen coders read one brief ("every field the amendment's own record form lists, in its order, one column each") and wrote two layouts: six wrote 81 columns, ten wrote 17 with the closed values folded into the phrases column. In the narrow layout 16 of 163 positive codes lost their closed value (the sale-taken variable lost 7 of 12), and one shard dropped a unit unnoticed.
- Given to the run: `shards/header.tsv`, cut by script from the amendment's Fields lines, and a brief that says to copy it as it stands. The script's header matched the six wide shards column for column.
- Tried on: the two reliability coders, the patch coder and the two page-text coders. Both reliability files came back with the fixed header and a full row count.
- Permanent change, if kept: the coder brief template names a header file; the orchestrator cuts it from the amendment before any shard launches and checks each returned file's row and column count before committing it.

## 2. A profile is an earlier reader's selection, and a zero coded from it may be that reader's silence

- Seen: chapel hire and party packages, 19 September. Neither run holds a stored copy of the pages it read (the capture folder is outside git and is absent from this disk). The amendment's new variables are coded from profiles, which keep the quotations the first codebook's variables called for. The amendment defines a zero as "the applicable scope text was retrieved and read in full and says nothing", which a coder reading a profile cannot attest. Two chapel variables came back with no positive code in 188 units.
- Given to the run: the pages of the reliability sample re-fetched into the git-ignored data folder (14 of 18 units with addresses fetched; four behind a gateway that refuses the fetch), and a third coding of those units from the full page text under `briefs/whom-sold-to-coder-from-pages.md`. The two profile codings give the noise between coders; what the page-text coding adds beyond that noise is the profile's miss rate, variable by variable.
- Tried on: chapel hire, 20 September. Over fourteen units and fourteen variables (196 cells) the two profile coders part on 9 cells; the page-text coder parts from them on 16 and 13. Of 100 cells both profile coders called not stated, the page-text coder found 8 stated, 6 of them on words the profile does not hold, one each on six variables, all in units of three pages or more. Of 19 cells both called stated, the page-text coder did not find 2. The five rarely stated variables stayed at zero on the full page. So the fear was half right: a profile's zero is the page's zero about nineteen times in twenty, profile counts are floors, and a lead that a six per cent shift would erase is not a lead. The corpus was not re-fetched. The record is the run's `coded-whom-sold-to-reliability.md`. Party packages' pilot repeats the check on pages five weeks old.
- Tried on: party packages, 20 September, with the opposite result. On the 13 sampled units with page text, the pages address a parent or guardian in 6 and the profiles in 2; five of the eleven values the page added were the buyer variable. The profile there was written to record whom the celebration is for, and keeps the sentence that names who books only by accident. The shape dimensions moved within the noise (six added, four lost, against twelve partings between the profile coders). So the same check that cleared one corpus condemned the other's headline variable: the buyer variable is being coded from page text over the whole corpus. Without the check, a strike list would have gone to the owner with the parent buyer at a third of its published weight.
- Permanent change, in the form the two trials support: every amendment coded from profiles takes this check on its reliability sample before the full pass, variable by variable, and a variable whose page-text coding finds materially more than the profile coders do is coded from page text; the measured miss rate is stated beside every count taken from profiles; Survey keeps its stored copies where a later run can read them; an amendment's variables are coded from stored pages or the run re-fetches; a zero coded from a profile is reported as "not quoted", a value apart from "not stated".
- Result (entry 2, continued): the third pair kept the pages it read, as text beside the markup, under the operator repository's git-ignored data folder (kayak hire 505 folders, 705 MB, for 560 profiles; the riding academy 217 folders, 553 MB, for 270), so the profile-miss check became a coverage check. The gaps clustered: twelve of the riding academy's nineteen missing units in one Canadian census stratum, and 14 of kayak hire's 15 local river units. A re-fetch recovered text for 22 of 35 and 21 of 36 profiles without pages; 9 and 13 held no address, and the fetch script made an empty folder and no log line for each of those. 85 of 214 riding folders and 122 of 504 kayak folders held markup and no text, and text was derived beside the markup for 118 and 119 of them; a split of units by folder existence had overstated the units with text by that much. Re-fetched pages were stored as numbered files while coders cited addresses, so no citation matched a file for those units, and kayak hire's candidate table, derived from the data folder, was committed and cannot be rebuilt from a fresh clone. The two corpora spell their reachability column differently. Added to the permanent change: stored text lives where a clone can reach it, or a committed manifest names each file with its hash and address; re-fetched files are named by address; the fetch script logs a profile with no address; one spelling of the reachability column across corpora.

## 4. A threshold on nineteen units is one coding decision wide

- Seen: chapel hire, 20 September. The codebook holds each variable to an alpha of 0.80 on a ten per cent sample, which is nineteen units. One disagreement moves alpha by about 0.08, so the buyer variable at 0.76 (16 of 19) and the arrival variable at 0.82 (17 of 19) differ by a single decision. Six variables were stated by no sampled unit, and their alpha of 1.00 says only that the coders agree on which units were never captured. A second, selected look (twelve profiles a shard coder had coded without naming the closed value, re-coded by another coder) agreed on 7 of 12 for the buyer variable and 8 of 12 for the offering's name.
- Given to the run: the reliability note prints every disagreeing cell with each coder's value and words, says which variables the sample could not test, and sends the whole list to a rule-review clerk instead of gating on the coefficient.
- Tried on: chapel hire. The rule-review clerk read the 39 disputed cells and found one fault behind most of them: a rule declared a list closed and then stated its inclusion tests in words of their own, so one coder applied the list and the other the test. Its amendment restates nine variables' tests as lexical ones (an anchor word, a slot, a text unit), tried on the disputed passages, and leaves five variables citable as they stand. It also found two units coded that an earlier ruling excluded, and confirmed by its own check that six of the page-text differences were words absent from the profile, which no rule reaches. A pass-or-fail on the coefficient would have sent two variables to review; reading the passages sent nine. Re-coding running.
- Permanent change, if kept: Survey's reliability step reports the count, the coefficient and the disputed passages together; names as untested any variable the sample never saw stated; and tops the sample up, by seed, from units the first coding found stated, until each variable a finding will cite has been seen stated by both coders at least a handful of times.

## 6. An amendment delivers its header files, and has a size

- Seen: party packages. The amendment author was asked for a variable for each question the codebook could not answer and returned eighteen variables at three levels (unit, package, element): 232, 113 and 17 columns. No coder can be asked to lay that out from prose, and the author's own spelling of one variable's fields cannot survive into a flat header.
- Given to the run: a record-form clerk who read the amendment whole and cut one header file a level, with a notes file of the doubts; a pilot of three variables before any full pass.
- Tried on: party packages. Headers cut. The pilot failed on all three flagged variables, and under the rules the record form itself was undecided: each coder wrote 52 package rows and only 27 join, since nothing keys a package the profile leaves unnamed; one coder put cancellation terms on package rows and the other on unit rows, so that variable could not be compared at all; element assurance agreed on 147 of 175 rows with alpha 0.27, nearly every row being not stated. The run's first pass was cut to the buyer variable and the four shape dimensions at unit level, keyed by file and unit id; the package and element variables wait for the card set to show which a card cites, then take rule review before coding.
- Permanent change, if kept: the amendment author's brief asks for the header files and a doubt list as part of the amendment, and asks the author to say which variables a card is likely to cite, so that the first pass codes those and leaves the rest until a card asks; and the amendment says what keys a row at each level, tried on a profile that leaves a package unnamed and on one that holds several listings.

## 7. Small things the fixed header taught

- Two coders said the header's shared phrases column is never explained and repeats the per-variable words fields; one opened other coders' files to settle the layout. The brief should say what each trailing column is for, or the header should drop the column where every variable has a words field.
- One coder keyed its rows by the profile's name without the extension, because the brief used that form for a folder. The agreement script now keys without extensions, and the pilot briefs say "as your shard file gives it".
- Three of about forty coders opened other coders' files lying in the folder they write into, to settle a layout or a convention the brief left unsaid. None saw a second coding of its own sampled units. A fence of words around files beside the output path does not hold: give each coder its own output folder and write the conventions into the brief.
- The same happened to a market-only clerk on the strongest tier, who read sixty lines of the closed card set looking for a house format and declared it. The third-derivation brief now shows the file's shape in a sentence. The rule that generalises: a brief that names an output names its shape, since the nearest example of the shape is usually a file the clerk is closed to.
- A coder that must name the closed value codes fewer doubtful positives: on the twelve profiles coded twice, the wide-header coder turned eleven stated codes to not stated and three the other way.

## 8. A brief that tests its own list against its own words

- Permanent change, if kept: the standing block's read order applies to deriving clerks and their successors; coders get a shorter block (evidence is what the page prints, write only your file, return your ledger).
- Result (entry 10, continued): the contradiction reached clerks who are not coders, three times in the third pair's Survey stage. Given to the next runs: every Survey template whose clerk reads the standing block names its three files in the read-order paragraph and says the closure does not reach them. Added to the permanent change: the standing block says its files are open whatever a brief's closing sentence says. There is also a question for the owner beneath it: whether a clerk that drafts blind (the amendment author) should read the venue-situation note at all, since that note carries the operator's shape, without values.
- Result (entry 10, continued, the Adjudicate stage): the standing block's read order put the fence index after the method's documents and the run folder listing, and a README at the run root carries no closure warning, so the riding academy's first area clerk opened the closed README before the fence, and three cards carried the earlier values it held, each saying so on its Corrections line; the block was changed mid-run (25 September 09:05) to put the fence straight after its three files and name the README as closed. Given to the next runs: every Adjudicate template names the block's three files in its read order and puts the fence before any other run file; the block says its three files are open whatever a brief's closing sentence says; a market-only brief says the fence index is not on its list, and its prompt names the open sections of any file opened in part. Added to the permanent change: `forms/brief-read-order.md` puts the fence index straight after the standing files, and the launch check fails a brief that names a run file before it.

## 13. A withheld row says in plain words which way it leans and why that did not carry

- Seen: chapel hire's owner's cold read, 20 September. Of 25 rows on the ruling sheet the reader could rule nine from the row alone. The sixteen it could not were nearly all withheld rows: the sheet prints the two counts and the word withheld, and several counts look one-sided (16 to 8; 43 to 2), so the reader met a verdict that seemed to contradict its own figures and went hunting through the order of ruling for the reason. It also guessed at five words the run's clerks use freely and the sheet never defines (the band, the register, the coded table, the measured-in line, a stratum), and could not act on one "wrong today" item because the page did not say why another room's capacity bears on this one.
- Given to the run: the integrator is resumed with the cold read, to give each withheld row its reason in a sentence a stranger can follow (the lead is one venue wide; the lead is all one kind of venue, which is the buyer card's open question; the instrument could not have seen the other option), to define or replace the five words, and to mend the unclear item.
- Tried on: chapel hire. The integrator's first mend put a plain sentence for each withheld card into the order of ruling; a second cold read then ruled 7 rows of 25 from the row alone, fewer than the first read's 9, because an owner rules from the sheet and the sheet's rows still printed a signed margin beside a stated lead (a "-10" next to "leads 9 to 6"), cut a long clause short with "the card has the rest", and gave a withheld row four or five counts without saying which two decide. The sheet is the assembler's, so the cure is a copy of the assembler kept beside this file (`assemble-review.py`): it says a margin in words with the figures for and against and what the line rests on instead, prints the derived-on clause whole, shows a misquote whole, reads the run's own copy of the review form, and prints two new clauses from a withheld card's recommending line, `leans:` and `because:`. The integrator moved its thirteen sentences onto the cards as those clauses. Result: a third cold read ruled 21 of 25 rows from the row alone, against 7 and 9 before. The four it could not: one row whose recommendation never names the thing recommended (the name card says a form of name and not the name), one where the largest count runs against the recommendation and two counting methods are both printed, and two it would act on by asking for more because the row says honestly that its evidence is thin. The front is about 4,500 words and every reader has called that too long for one sitting; the columns no reader has cited as deciding a row (what it rests on, what is held, the market-only reading) are the remaining lever.
- Tried on: party packages. The same assembler copy and the two clauses, from the integrator's first pass. A second cold read marked 10 of 24 rows yes and 14 no. Twelve of the fourteen are withheld rows whose lean and reason the reader restated correctly in his own words ("reverses hard in the catchment"; "the count is admitted to be incomplete"): he understood each row and marked it no because he could not rule it, which is what a withheld row says of itself. Chapel hire's third reader had counted such a row as yes when it told him what he would be waiting for. The two figures are therefore two measures (entry 18). Two rows were faults of the page: a recommendation and its support worded as two different questions, and a population cut that decides four cards (whether a place charges for each child at the gate) never explained on the front. The integrator is resumed on those two under a bound that the front may not grow.
- Permanent change, if kept: the card form's withheld line carries a plain reason of a dozen words that the assembler prints on the sheet; the review form lists the run's own nouns beside the method's; and the owner's cold read runs before the judges, its faults going to the integrator, since a page he cannot rule from is the first failure and the cheapest to find.

## 14. A count of units is made a second time on distinct operators

- Seen: chapel hire's two judges, 20 September, blind of each other. Both called the same withheld card evasive: by units the lead was 10 to 8, a tie under the check's rule, and by distinct operators 10 to 3, because one corporate group prints one sentence across several of its sites. The corpus holds several such groups (memorial-park chains with a shared fee schedule, a church-conservation charity with shared hire pages), and every card counted units. A unit count treats one published decision as several, and it can hide a lead as easily as make one.
- Given to the run: the three corrections clerks resumed to make every option count a second time on distinct operators, put both figures on the card, and re-test any line the second count moves, keeping and leaving alike.
- Tried on: both runs. Party packages' frame had already drawn one unit an operator (a chain with three venues is one text), so the second count equalled the first on every card of the seven checked so far, and the clerk points out the opposite cost: a shortlist holds more places than the count holds operators. Chapel hire's frame drew sites, and there the second count moves figures (parking 47 units to 40 texts, eight memorial-park sites sharing one sentence) though no line of the eight cards checked so far. The corpus has no operator column, so the clerks grouped by identical text, which a template with small variations escapes, and a silence cannot be grouped at all.
- Result on chapel hire: three lines turned under the second count, all toward what the venue holds. Two withheld cards became thin recommendations (the unit of sale, 9 operators to 6 where units had read 9 to 19; when the clock starts, 6 to 3 where units read 10 to 8), one of them the card both judges had called evasive, turned here by a rule given to the clerk and not by the judges' verdict; one recommendation became withheld (extras inside the fee, 16 units to 12 but 9 operators to 11). The clerks' account of the one-way direction is mechanical: the groups that print one text are the funeral trade's, so a unit count inflates the options that lie away from what this venue holds. It is put to a judge before the rule reaches another run.
- Judged: a fresh judge re-ran the recount and found the one-way direction to be the corpus and not the clerks, and found that three groupings had run in one day, because the rule went to three clerks as a sentence and each made its own groups (seven groups over 25 units, four over 14, identical text within an option). One of the three turns exists under one grouping only. A rule handed over as prose is as many rules as it has readers, the same fault as the coders' header in entry 1. Cure in hand: one clerk writes an operator table by a stated, scriptable rule (an owning body the profile itself names, then a shared domain, then a shared document, else the unit alone; general knowledge of who owns whom is not evidence), and the affected cards are re-run on it.
- Result on the one table: 189 venues are 112 businesses, twelve bodies holding 89 of the venues; a blind second making of thirty seeded venues placed all thirty alike. Re-made on it, the morning's two turns toward what the venue holds stood, and two further lines turned the other way (access, from withheld to a recommendation, 26 businesses to 21, where one charity's template had been counted nineteen times; the booking route, from a recommendation that kept what is held to withheld, 32 businesses each). One card the table separates was kept withheld by its clerk because both options come from one coded variable whose closed value two coders agreed on no better than chance. The round ends at four lines toward what the venue holds and two away, which answers the one-way worry better than any argument did.
- Permanent change, if kept: the frame says whether a unit is a site or an operator and carries an operator column either way; the card form's measured-in line states both counts where they differ; the corrections brief asks for the second count as it asks for the silence margin.
- Result (entry 14, continued): in the third pair one clerk per corpus made the operator table under the template, before any card was written. Riding academy: 250 units are 245 operators, with no chain, four pairs being one operator drawn twice from two registers; other readings a second clerk might take give 246, 243 or 240. Kayak hire: 538 units are 410 operators, the 245 coded units 213 and the 216 in English 184; the largest bodies a canal trust with 28 units and five conservation authorities; one directory alone would have made 104 units one body had its domain counted as their own pages. The orchestrator had brand-name holiday parks stand alone unless a brand-level page set their terms, against the template's owner-first ladder, and the rule note carries both readings (24 parks, or six operators by ownership); that choice is the orchestrator's and is offered as such in the template now. Both strike lists give every count by unit and by operator from the one table, and no clerk made a grouping of its own.
- Result (entry 14, continued, the test's base): both targeted judges of batch three found the one-operator test sound as a test of independence and its base unfixed. On the riding academy the buyer card was tested on units while every sibling was tested on operators, so the base was chosen per card after the result; the judges' round fixed the base at units for the whole set in `card-set.md` (a lead of 2 units is a tie where the operator moved holds one unit, a lead of 3 survives unless a double-drawn operator sits in the leading option), moved ten tables and margins from operators to units, and left the buyer card a tie on units and a carry on operators, said on the card, against the judge's advice to fix operators. On kayak hire the judge found the grouping uneven across kinds of seller (24 holiday parks of six companies split unless a brand's terms page was cited, while conservation authorities grouped by domain), which inflates in operators every lead the parks carry, and proposed stating that operators decide robustness and units measure exposure, printing units, operators and ownership together wherever a margin is within two operators. Given to the next runs: the base is the orchestrator's to state once for the set before any corrections clerk launches, with the cost of each, and a card whose line would turn on the other base says so. Added to the permanent change: the method names the base for the one-operator test and says which question each count answers; the operator rule groups by shared terms text alike for every kind of seller.
