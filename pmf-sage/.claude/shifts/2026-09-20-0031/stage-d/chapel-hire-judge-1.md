## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Page A

**Predicting the market from internal material.** Card 10: "the venue confirms a funeral or memorial date at **72 hours' notice**, written because nobody in the corpus publishes one and the trade books in days" — the sentence concedes there is no observation on either side and states the figure anyway, alongside a four-business-hour reply promise with no source at all. Card 8: "A 30-minute interval is scheduled before and after each block... on the Australian funeral trade's own sentence" — the trade's own sentence, quoted elsewhere in the same run, is fifteen minutes, before only. Card 15: "a second staff member for any hire expected above 60 attendees, that being the threshold E50 §1.1 already uses for a security guard" — an internal number for a different job, moved across.

**Contrariness toward the owner.** Page A is barely guilty of this; it agrees with the venue far more often than it departs. The two departures that lack the proof a departure needs are card 1's name, "One parent page called **Chapel Hire at Historic Rivermill**", chosen against the venue's own "Wedding Chapel" with no instrument that ranks names anywhere on the page, and card 7's "\$450 is 75 per cent of that card", which moves off the venue's held \$600 on a percentage nothing derives.

**The venue's own arrangement read as the market's answer.** Card 0 excludes five hirer kinds partly because "no enquiry of the kind exists in any system the venue runs (IN-7)" — the systems are a wedding funnel with no chapel option on any form, so they could not have caught a concert or a retreat enquiry if one existed. Card 6 takes the venue's held per-block unit while its own count reads "per hour in 8, per event in 5". Card 12 lands on no commission, which is what the venue's own unused outreach guide already holds, from a silence on hire pages that no fee arrangement would ever have appeared on.

### Page B

**Predicting the market from internal material.** Close to absent, and where an internal fact carries weight the card labels it. Card 14 is the one place a meeting-note fact does load-bearing work, and it is stamped: "heat in this room, second-hand and undated (O7)". Card 23's second leg is a single enquiry, declared as one. Nothing else in the set derives a parameter from an opinion.

**Contrariness toward the owner.** None found. No card departs from a held value without a count whose lean has been tested; the two clearest departures, cards 2 and 9, each carry a measured instrument and each state what would reverse them. The one asymmetry is in the corrections paragraph, below.

**The venue's own arrangement read as the market's answer.** The page guards against this explicitly and well — "This offering has never been on sale, so no price, length, name or session shape for it exists outside the fence, and a clerk meeting one has been leaked to" — and it names the funnel problem in card 1 rather than letting the nine-to-one approach count stand. Where a reader might still trip: card 19's capability line, "the telephone is the only channel in its own record that produced a sustained exchange", is a statement about the only channel the venue has ever offered. It sits in the capability column and does not carry the recommendation, which rests on 48 of 107. That is the correct placement and it should stay there.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

In its recommendations, yes. Six recommendations keep a held value and five leave one, and the evidential bar is not visibly different: of the six keeps, five are EARNED and one THIN; of the five leaves, three are EARNED and two THIN. No departure passes on less than a match is asked for — card 9 tests its lean twice, card 18 restates its lead at the corrected figure of four units, card 14 prints a negative margin, and card 2 names the blindness in its own instrument. That is the direct answer to the owner's second worry: agreement is not penalised and departure is not waved through.

Two qualifications, in order of weight.

**The reticence is asymmetric even though the recommendations are not.** Both evasive withholdings sit on cards where the option that would have won is the one the venue already holds. Card 3's own page, and card 5's access clock, are the two readings the venue's own documents carry, and both are declined against evidence that on the page's own numbers separates them. A set that is scrupulous about not contradicting the owner without proof can still fail him by declining to confirm him with proof in hand. That is where the second worry actually surfaces in page B.

**One extra read was applied to agreement and not to departure.** The corrections paragraph says of the one line that turned: "The one line that turned moved toward what the venue already holds, which is why it was read again before it stood." No departure in the set is recorded as receiving a second read for being a departure. It is declared, it is small, and the line survived — but the rule as written is "check harder when we agree with him", and stated that way the owner will not like it. The honest form of the rule is that a turn made of silences is re-read whichever way it points.

## What the owner has to read to rule

**Page A.** 27,558 words, 21 cards. The front matter and the three summary tables run 1,150 words and the three forks a further 518. He can see at a glance which cards are ruleable now (10), ruleable in part (2) and gated (9), which is a genuine service. What he cannot see anywhere in the summary is a margin, a count, or what any recommendation rests on: the tables carry a "Recommended ruling, in ten words" and a gate, and nothing else. To check a single recommendation he must open the card and read 700 to 1,400 words of prose legs. There is no ruling sheet, no per-card evidence line, and the joint ledger — 1,793 words of dependency prose — sits between the summary and the cards. Realistic minimum to rule anything with your eyes open: about 1,700 words of summary plus roughly 1,000 words per card he wants to check, and he has no way to tell in advance which ones need checking.

**Page B.** 27,454 words, 25 cards. The ruling sheet is 1,837 words with the header, and the order of ruling is 1,924 — **3,761 words to the end of the two sections, and 3,934 to the end of "What passed through"**. Within that he gets, per card, what the row asks of him, the margin or the counts it stands at, whether it rests on the market or on his own buyers, whether it keeps or leaves a held value, where an independent clerk landed, and whether he has ruled it.

**Can he rule from those two sections alone?** For most of the set, yes, and that is a real advance. The nine rows marked confirm or rule carry their margins in the row, and a reader who trusts the method can sign six of them off the sheet. The fourteen withheld rows tell him plainly that they ask a commission, not a decision. Four things break the reading and they are all fixable.

The margin column is not one kind of thing. Card 14's cell is a forty-word paragraph ending "the card has the rest"; card 23's is "-95 units"; card 1's is "funeral director 3 of 118 stated and 11 of 17 transacting parties". A column that has to be read three ways is not a column.

Card 0's row says confirm, and the card says its lead is "a thin lead" that becomes "a tie" when church units drop. If he confirms from the row he confirms something the card would not have him confirm.

The one thing that blocks every ruling below it — nobody has asked the broker whether the liability policy responds to a third-party hire — is the opening clause of a 330-word paragraph that also carries the flyer, the toilets, the seating contradiction, the pricing and the unreadable diary. It reads as context. It is a stop sign.

The "ruled" column is twenty-five rows of "open" and tells him nothing.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

Of the fourteen withheld cards, twelve are sound and two are evasive (cards 3 and 5). Both evasive cards should have recommended: card 3 for the venue's own page answered by a person, with the intermediary named as an addition to it rather than an alternative, which is what its own third clerk says; and card 5 for the booked time being access, on ten distinct operators against about three once one corporate group's repeated sentence is set aside.

Of the eleven cards that recommend, one should have withheld and it is card 0, whose own text takes its 14-unit lead down to a tie inside the population that matters. Card 14 and card 18 recommend on thin evidence and say so, which is the right handling; they should stay as they are.

Net: page B withholds about right in volume and slightly wrong in direction. Fourteen of twenty-five is defensible for an offering that has never been on sale, where the buyer question itself is unruled. The fault is not the count, it is that two of the withholdings protect the owner's own position from being confirmed while the one over-confident card sits on a "confirm" row.

## The three changes that would most improve page B for one sitting

**1. Tell him how few acts close the fourteen.** The page groups the missing observations by kind of cost, which is good, and then leaves him to work out the arithmetic. Four acts close twelve of the fourteen: one eight-seed rank-database batch closes cards 1 and 6 and feeds card 10; one round of calls to the four in-band funeral incumbents closes cards 17, 20, 21 and 22; one question to the funeral platform closes cards 11 and 24 and helps 12; one walk of the site with a tape measure closes 12 and 13. Add card 3's single results-page capture and card 5's re-read of twenty pages and the whole fourteen are six acts, four of them a morning's work. Put that as six lines with an owner's name against each, above the sheet. It converts fourteen open cards from a stalled set into a short worklist.

**2. Lift the two stop signs out of the prose and above the sheet.** The broker letter and the site walk block everything beneath them. Two lines, each naming who writes or walks and by when, before the first table. Everything else in the "wrong today" paragraph is context and can stay where it is.

**3. Make the sheet's columns honest and one kind each.** Split the margin column into a count and a direction, put card 14's paragraph in the card where it belongs, change card 0's part from "confirm" to "rule, and read the card: the lead ties inside the matched population", and drop the "ruled" column of twenty-five "open" for a column that says what it costs to rule this one wrong. The sheet is the page's best idea and four small edits make it readable in one pass.

## Verdict

Page B is a clear improvement on page A for this owner — it invents no figure where page A invented five, it tests every lean against the population that matches this venue, it runs an independent market-only clerk on all twenty-five cards and prints the eight disagreements, and it killed the one published precedent page A leaned on twice — and I am confident about that on the evidence in front of me; the costs he should weigh are that page B leaves him with no price, no name ruled for an unruled buyer, and fourteen commissions where page A gave him rulings he could not have checked.
