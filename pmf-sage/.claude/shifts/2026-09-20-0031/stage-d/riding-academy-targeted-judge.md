# The set-wide pass on the riding academy: evidence or clerks?

Run folder: `/usr/local/src/rivermill/product-development/equestrian-me-academy/2026-09-07-how-the-riding-academy-was-decided`. The round under review is the second correction round, the set-wide corrections clerk's entries dated 25 September 2026 on each card's Corrections line, which followed the per-card corrections clerk's entries of the same day.

The short answer: the direction is mostly the rules, not the evidence, and the rules were not put alike. However, the clerks were not hunting for moves away from the venue. Applied evenly, the same rules would have moved more cards away from what the venue holds, not fewer (cards 10 and 23). One rule, the silence rule, was misapplied to card 3. Card 1's test was run on the wrong base. Card 29's move is earned. Card 22's move is a correct restamp and nothing more.

## Rules the round used

The round applied six rules. Card by card they are quoted where they bite.

- **No pooling across country cells.** A rate is read inside the AU cell only.
- **One shared reading of the commitment on Cards 22, 23, 29 and 32.** A buyer's commitment is read from V8, V10 and V28 together. A term that is stated and priced per session counts as a term.
- **The silence rule, from the corrections brief.** "trailing a silent count is shown to the owner and is not by itself decisive, since it counts publication, and a line is withheld where some evidence puts an option against it."
- **The one-operator test.** A line is carried only if it survives one operator coded the other way. The brief requires "the same one-operator test" whichever way a line runs.
- **The priors rule.** A Held stamp is judged first against the terms on sale, and only then against a draft.
- **The instrument test for a leg one sighting wide.** Cards 4 and 15 use it.

## Card by card: the four moved cards

### Card 3, rider weight

1. **Before and after.** Before: "no limit printed, given when asked, margin 7 units over a limit printed, rests on market". The Held stamp was keeps, because the terms on sale publish no limit (S2). After: withheld, leaning to unprinted. Held is restamped from keeps to held. **The move runs away from what the venue holds.**
2. **Counts.** They did not change. Before and after, the card stands at "8 printed against 15 silent units, 8 against 14 operators", and the register at 2 of 12 printing. The per-card clerk had already corrected 6 against 17 to 8 against 15 and let the line stand. The set-wide clerk moved the line using the silence rule and nothing else: "the line's lead was the silent count alone, which counts publication and is not by itself decisive, while the searcher's weight question puts the printed option against it."
3. **Applied alike? No, in two ways.**
   - The card's own question is a publication question: "Is the heaviest rider the horses can carry printed where the buyer books, or given when the buyer asks?" On this question the silent page *is* the observation, so "it counts publication" is no reason to discount it here. The count the card leans on instead, "among pages that say anything a printed limit leads 8 to 0", cannot come out any other way, because no page prints "we have no limit".
   - The "evidence on the other side" is one unsized related-search suggestion. The card's own Measured-in line demotes that same item: "The search question is incidental capture from a pull made for other products (SD-1) and is provenance for what searchers ask, not a leg."
   - Card 10 had stronger counter-evidence and was held to a harder standard. Its riding-time half also rests on silence (13 against 2 operators). Its nearest rival, Gold Coast Equestrian Centre, ten minutes away, prints "15 min of horsemanship + 45 min riding lesson". That line was kept because "No evidence on this card puts a stated riding time ahead". The rule says "puts an option against it", not "ahead".
   - So the same rule withheld card 3 on a weak item and kept card 10 despite a stronger one. **Card 10 is the card this rule should have moved and did not.**
4. **Verdict: WITHHELD-EVASIVE.**
5. **Reason.** A sound reason to withhold was available and is on the card: the coders missed two printed limits ("their companion cells were left empty at the coder stage"), and one "Weight Policy" document was never opened. That is a measured lean of the instrument towards silence. The card did not withhold for that reason. It withheld because "that lead is the pages that say nothing", on a question about whether to say anything.

In the register, Arundel Park moved to a term alone.

Avoca: "PCA Group Sessions must be booked as a term commitment" beside casual rates. Equestrian Spirit: "Weekly - \$90 per lesson. Casual - \$105 per lesson". ### Card 22, single session, block or term

Under the rule that restamped card 22, terms once on sale rank above a draft, so **card 23 should read leaves, not keeps.** This is a second card the rules should have moved and did not, again away from the venue.

### Card 1, who it is for

The clerk dismissed the operator figure because it "differs only because the two Gooromon Park units sit in household alone as one operator". ## Is the one-way direction the evidence?

**My judgement: mostly not. It is the rules meeting a venue that publishes little. It is not clerks leaning against the owner.**

**The strongest reason for.** The two lines that moved did so on different grounds.
- Card 29 moved because the round resolved a real contradiction: two cards read the same price pages two ways. The pages behind the moved units say "booked on a term basis" and "must be booked as a term commitment".
- The same pass moved five units *towards* the venue on card 22 and left that line standing.
- Where the rules were put unevenly (cards 10, 18 and 23), putting them evenly would move *more* cards away from the venue. A clerk biased against the owner would have taken those moves. These clerks left them.

**The strongest reason against.** Almost every posture the venue holds is a posture of saying or selling the minimum:
- no weight limit printed;
- no riding time stated;
- single sessions only;
- no block on sale.

A rule that discounts silence, or a reading that finds more commitments by reading more columns, runs away from those postures by construction, whatever the market says. Card 3 shows this in its purest form. No count moved, yet the line turned, and the only new evidence was an unsized search suggestion that the card itself calls "not a leg". **Card 3's move is the clerks, not the evidence.**

## Counting each operator once

**Here the rule is sound, and it should be the default base.** In this corpus it does less than its name suggests. `operators.tsv` finds "No body in the corpus runs two or more distinct riding sites". Its four groups are all "the same operator drawn twice from two registers". Counting operators here simply removes double draws. The fault this round shows is not the rule. It is choosing a base per card after the result is known (card 1).

It carries four risks in the next corpus:
- **Collapsing a chain.** Where a real chain or franchise prints one text across several sites, one decision counts once. Yet a buyer's choice set is sites. For "what does the buyer see" questions the unit is the right base; for "what do sellers decide" the operator is. The run should name which question it is asking.
- **Grouping by shared domain** can merge tenants of one landlord. The rule escapes that only by judgement (Centennial Parklands, 243 against 245 operators).
- **The union rule** ("an operator taking the union of its units' structures") can create a posture no single site offers.
- **Where groups hold opposite postures across brands,** one-operator-once hides the split. None were found here, but the rule has no safeguard for it.

## Three changes before this rule is used on another run

1. **Fix the test base in the method, before any card is written.** Run the one-operator test on operators, on every card, with units printed beside. Re-run card 1 on that base, where it becomes a THIN recommendation of the household with a second buyer.
2. **Scope the silence rule, and apply it to every silent half.**
   - Where a card asks whether something is printed, silence is the observation and is not discounted. Only a *measured* capture lean may discount it, such as card 3's two limits missed at the coder stage.
   - "Evidence on the other side" must meet the same leg standard as the card's own Measured-in line.
   - The test is "puts an option against it", applied alike. Put it to every compound option's silent half: card 10's riding time, card 18's "no games", and cards 11, 24 and 35.
3. **One priors ranking, restamped across all 28 cards in a single pass, with a direction audit.**
   - The ranking: terms on sale today, then terms once on sale (the forerunner of 2025), then SOP, then draft, then owner-stated.
   - Card 23 becomes "leaves" under it.
   - Any pass whose moves all run one way must list the cards its rules would move the other way, and show that it checked them.
   - Each move must be marked as either a count change or a reading change, so the owner can see card 3 moved on reading alone.
