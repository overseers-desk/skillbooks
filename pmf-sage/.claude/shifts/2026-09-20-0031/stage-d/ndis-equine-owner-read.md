# An owner's cold read of the front of "Decision review" (NDIS equine)

**Page read:** `product-development/ndis-equine-therapy/2026-08-14-how-ndis-equine-was-decided/3-decisions/review.md`
**What I read:** the title through the section "What passed through": everything above the "Open cards" heading, the ruling sheet, "The order of ruling" and its sub-parts, and "What passed through." I read it once, top to bottom, in the order it's printed.

**A disclosure before I start.** When the file was first pulled up for me, the fetch ran past my stopping point and put cards 0, 1 and the start of card 2 in front of me as well, before I could stop it. I did not go looking for them, and I have not used anything from them below. Every mark, quote and guess that follows comes only from the ruling sheet and the prose that follows it, down to "What passed through." I did not otherwise open any card; nothing on any row left me needing to.

---

## 1. What I believe I'm being asked to decide

I'm being asked to rule 28 separate questions that together fix exactly what this horse session for scheme participants is, who it's for, what happens in it, and how it's booked and paid, each one scored against roughly 197 other sellers' own pages plus my own handful of bookings. Most of that work isn't ready for me: eighteen of the twenty-eight are marked "withheld" because the count itself doesn't settle cleanly, and what's actually being asked of me there is permission to go and fix the count, not a choice between two options. Only ten rows are put to me as things I could settle today: seven where I'd be told to actively change what we currently do, three where I'd just be confirming we keep doing what we do. Underneath all of it sits a live problem that doesn't wait on any of my rulings: the outside instructor isn't showing as an active registered provider, there's no written contract with her, and several of our own pages contradict each other on length, price, cancellation and whether riding happens. My very first ruling, on who the session is actually addressed to (card 0), reshapes most of the rest of the sheet, so it isn't optional to defer.

## 3. Words, phrases and figures I had to guess at

The ruling sheet (rows) comes before its own glossary ("How to read the figures"), so on a first, in-order read I hit most of this jargon before it's explained. By the time I reached "What passed through" I had it, but the guessing was real in the meantime.

- **"units"**: guessed one seller's web listing. Confirmed later: "one seller's entry in one of the published registers... 197 units... fetched on 25 and 26 September 2026." Counted over: web pages, three kinds of seller (89 practitioners, 79 RDA charities, 29 Canadian CANTRA centres = 197).
- **"operators"**: guessed the business behind a listing. Confirmed: the same 197 units regrouped two ways, by owner (154) or by whoever's name is on the page's terms (177), "counted two ways because you have not ruled which to cite." On the sheet itself the two figures are often given unlabelled: row 1's "operators 24 to 26 and 24 to 21" doesn't say which pair is which. I had to pattern-match against rows that do label them (e.g. row 14: "14 to 11 terms-setters, 14 to 6 owner-first") to guess the order holds everywhere.
- **"terms-setters" / "owner-first"**: the two operator counts above; confirmed by the glossary, not obvious before it.
- **"both-readers" / "either-reader(s)"**: guessed the two ends of an inter-rater agreement range. Confirmed by the glossary sentence, but see the card 0/21 note above: I could not confirm the direction convention actually holds row to row.
- **"catchment rivals"**: guessed local competitors. Confirmed: "the 18 sellers in this venue's own area... read by one reader on 14 August 2026." Not stated: whether these 18 sit inside the 197-unit count or are counted separately from it. I could not tell, and several rows (5, 6, 8, 10b, 11, 17a, 23, 25, 29) lean on this figure without saying.
- **"silent"**: guessed pages that say nothing on the question. Confirmed, plus a rule I would have missed without the glossary: "where a card lists printing nothing as an option, the silent count is that option's figure," meaning on cards like 6, 10b, 17a, 23, 25, 27, 29 the market "lead" I'm ruling on is itself a count of sellers saying nothing.
- **"market-only reading" / "third derivation"**: guessed an independent second opinion. Confirmed by the sheet's own header. Its three values, "agrees, differs, declines," I would not have read correctly on my own: "declines" specifically means the second clerk found nothing in the market to recommend on, not that it voted the other way.
- **"withheld" / "carried"**: "withheld" is defined ("no option is carried, and the row names the one observation that would carry either"); "carried" itself is not defined as a verb anywhere in the front. I inferred it from context and from "a lead counts only if one unit coded the other way would not erase it; anything less is a tie."
- **"the day-one strike list of buyers"**: not defined anywhere in the front. I guessed it's an earlier screening list of 22 candidate buyer-types, some struck out as implausible, made before this run, but that's a guess, not a reading.
- **"the priors pass"**: used at least twice with what look like different meanings, once tied to "the earlier decision review of 5 September" (twelve questions, all blank), and once with its own count ("4, 6 and 18") that doesn't match either the 5 September review or the 26/27 September correction rounds described just before it. I could not tell whether this is one process or two under one name.
- **"the card check," "the corrections clerks," "the set-wide pass"**: three separately named review passes, two of them both dated 26–27 September. Individually clear, but I had to hold four named processes in mind for a page I'm meant to rule in one sitting.
- **"August profiles"**: defined as "an earlier coding of the same market," but its relation to the 197-unit count (same list, different list, subset) is not said.

**Overall:** the three headline counts (units/pages, operators/businesses, catchment rivals/local businesses) are each nameable once you have the glossary, and the venue's own buyer figures (8 bookings, 4 buyers) read clearly as a separate, much smaller kind of evidence rather than something pooled into the market counts. What I could not settle, even with the glossary, is exactly how the catchment-rival and August-profile figures relate to the main 197-unit count, and which end of a "both to either" range is which on a given row.

## 5. Three changes that would most help me rule this in one sitting

1. **Put the glossary before the table, not after it.** "How to read the figures" defines every term the ruling sheet uses, but it sits after the 28-row table it's needed to read. I hit "units," "operators," "both/either reader" and "catchment rivals" cold, thirty rows before anyone tells me what they mean.
2. **Fix the range and labelling conventions so a figure reads the same way every time.** Some rows give operator figures unlabelled (I have to guess which of two numbers is terms-setters and which is owner-first by matching other rows); the "both to either" range runs low-to-high on some options and high-to-low on others in the very same row (card 0). If the convention is fixed, say so once and hold to it everywhere; if it isn't, tell me which end of each range I can trust.
3. **Tell me up front how many of the 28 I can actually touch today.** Only ten rows (7 rule, 3 confirm) are mine to settle without new work; the other eighteen are really asking me to fund a survey top-up. A one-line count at the top of the page, stated before 6,600 words of table and prose, would tell me whether this sitting can close anything at all, and save me reading rows I can't act on until I see it.

---

## 6. Length

The front, title through "What passed through" and not counting the cards themselves, runs to roughly 6,600 words counting the table's own pipe marks, about 6,300 words of running text and figures once those are stripped out. That is too long for one sitting: it is longer than most reports I'd read start to finish, it carries 28 separate rulings inside it, and eighteen of those turn out not to be decisions at all but requests to fund more counting. A "front" that's meant to be read in one go should say, in the first paragraph, how many of its rows are actually mine to rule today.
