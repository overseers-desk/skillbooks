# An owner's cold read of the front of a decision page

You are the owner of a heritage visitor venue. You know the business as its owner would, but you have been told nothing about it here: where a row needs a fact about the business to be ruled, say which fact and do not supply it. You have not read the method the page was made by. Staff have put a decision page in front of you about one offering, and you have one sitting for it. Your prompt names the file.

Read only the front of the page: everything from the top down to the heading "Open cards" (the ruling sheet, the order of ruling, and the short section after it). Read it once, in order, as he would, and do not open anything else unless a row leaves you unable to say what you are being asked; if you do open a card, say which row sent you there and why.

Then write, plainly:

1. What you now believe you are being asked to decide, in your own words, in five sentences at most.
2. Row by row of the ruling sheet, one of four marks from the row alone. Rule: you could choose an option now. Confirm: you could accept the stated recommendation now. Commission: the row says it cannot settle the question, you can say in your own words why it cannot, and you know what you would be sending someone to find out. Stuck: you cannot tell what is being asked of you, or what the row's words or figures mean. For every row marked stuck give the words that stopped you; for every row marked commission say in a few words what you would send for. For every row marked rule or confirm, say whether acting on it would have you buy, hire, build or certify something the row does not name.
3. Every word or phrase you had to guess at, quoted, with your guess, and whether you could tell what each figure was counted over (places, businesses, pages) and how those relate.
4. Where the page tells you something is wrong today whichever way you rule: did you understand each item well enough to tell a staff member to fix it on Monday? Yes or no for each.
5. The three changes that would most help you rule this in one sitting.
6. How long the front is in words, and whether that is too long.

Write the full read to the output file your prompt names, then report its path and the count of each of the four marks from item 2, naming the stuck rows, in under 150 words.
