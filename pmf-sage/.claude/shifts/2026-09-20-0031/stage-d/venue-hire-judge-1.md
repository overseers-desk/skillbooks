# Venue hire: judge 1's review

## The short answer

Page B is the better evidence for the owner, and the main reason is a factual one. Page A rests nearly every recommendation on its F1: "bare hire has one comped booking ever against 95 catering-bundled ones", and it calls the 10 October evening 40th "the only non-wedding space price ever agreed with a customer". The sales record behind page B (`1-competitions/sales-record.md` §2, §4) shows 93 live invoices to 68 contacts for plain Barn and Red Table hire from October 2023, at \$249 to \$349. Page A never saw them. Its radical cards (retire bare hire, publish a minimum spend, go in-house catered, move to an evening-only block) predict the market from one unpaid invoice, one case study in the February draft, and the club-and-tavern shape of the catchment. That is the owner's first worry in its plainest form.

Page B makes the opposite trade. It is careful, it counts sellers against what they share with this venue, and it will not recommend where the counts tie. On keeping and leaving it is mostly fair to the owner. It has three faults of its own. It withholds 16 of 24 cards, and on three of them its own buyers' asks carry an option that the card declines to name. It sets aside, on some cards and not on others, the comparators that carry a line. And it is too long to rule from in one sitting: the ruling sheet and the order of ruling come to about 4,900 words of dense code before a single card opens.

## The three clearest cases of each fault

**Page A, CONTRARIAN.** A1: "keep the capability, retire the bare-hire listing", resting on a listing that "a buyer reading the site cannot reach" (A7's own words). A6: "F1 says consumption is the only thing that has ever sold here", a claim 93 space-only invoices contradict. A10: "rule the offer in-house-catered", on the rules of "Club Helensvale ... North Burleigh ... Nerang Community Bowls Club ... The Glades ... Hinterland Hotel", every one a seller that lives off its own bar and kitchen.

**Page A, UNSUPPORTED.** A4: "the \$1,000 the market has actually paid", a single invoice still unpaid at the cutoff. A7: "four hours fits neither", said of two buyers while the daytime hires are ignored. A18: "list on Tagvenue and Partystar", resting on the platforms' inventory and not on buyers searching them.

**Page A, WITHHELD-EVASIVE.** A12 is the only one: "No variable in the codebook captures an age restriction", while the same page records 87 children's party bookings on the Barn.

**Page A, the owner's second worry.** The page opens by teaching distrust of what the owner holds: "A card wearing *inherited* or *anchored* is the card to spend the most minutes on. Thirteen of the eighteen cards here wear one of the two". Thirteen of its sixteen recommendations depart from a held value.

**Page B, WITHHELD-EVASIVE.** Card 17, quoted above: the card carries the rejection of the floor of 25 in its own words and still withholds. Card 15: "family food asks 3 and 3" beside "the own record cannot separate the two". Card 12b: asks for an evening are counted as "only terms on offer".

**Page B, THIN.** Card 7 is the only one, and it is the one carried card that leaves a held value.

**Page B, ECHO.** None is clear. The nearest is card 0's sheet margin: "leads by 199 confirmed bookings". Those are sales under products "named and priced for a household party". The card's real proof is the neutral-name invoices, 68 households against no employer, and the sheet should print that figure.

**Page B, CONTRARIAN and UNSUPPORTED.** None.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

Mostly, yes. The correction round moved two leaves (cards 2 and 17) and two keeps (cards 5 and 12a) from carried to withheld, and nothing the other way. Both keeping and leaving were asked for the same market-only reading. A match is never held against a recommendation: card 18 keeps against a market-only tie and says plainly that "a market-only tie is no evidence against a line that also rests on own buyers".

There is one departure that passes on less proof than a match was asked for. Card 7 leaves the invoiced "both" on the reference ring, 9 against 0, while its Gold Coast count outside the bar-and-kitchen part is 3 against 1, "which one unit coded the other way would erase". Card 12a, a keep, was withheld on exactly that kind of one-unit lead. Card 6 refuses to be carried by the same kind of comparator: "the 4 are homesteads and listings beyond the Gold Coast". The ring carries a leave on card 7 and cannot carry a price on card 6. The problem is inconsistency more than a bias against the owner, but the one place it bites, it bites in favour of the departure.

A second, smaller distortion: the sheet reports the market-only reading as "differs" on 13 cards. On five of them (5, 6, 11, 19a, 19b) the fresh clerk landed on the silent count, which the set's own rule treats as no option. Those five should read "declines". As printed, the column overstates how often a fresh reader disagreed with the venue.

## What the owner has to read

**Page A.** 12,793 words and 18 cards. There is no ruling sheet. The owner can skim the 18 "Recommended ruling" bullets, about 2,000 words, but he cannot tell at a glance which cards only confirm. The chips flag where a held value came from, not what the evidence does. Every card hangs on card A1, so a wrong A1 moves the rest.

**Page B.** 27,408 words and 24 cards. The ruling sheet is about 3,300 words and the order of ruling about 1,600, so about 4,900 words before any card. The "your part" column does show at a glance which rows only confirm (7) and which need a ruling or a commission (17), and the order of ruling rightly puts card 0 first. The owner cannot rule the withheld rows from those two sections alone. The margin cells are paragraphs written in the run's own code ("`r1`", "the ring", "units", "outside the part", "one reader's"). The rows never name the held value, only "held; neither kept nor left", so he cannot see from the sheet that the held birthday age is 25 or that the held booking window is eight weeks. For the 7 confirm rows and card 7 the two sections are enough. For the other 16 he has to open the card or accept the commission list. A plain-words sheet of one short line a card would do the same job in about 1,200 words.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

Of the 16 withheld cards, 13 are sound and 3 evasive (12b, 15, 17). Of the 8 cards that recommend, one should have withheld or counted first: card 7, whose deciding count, the venue's own months with both shapes on sale, is in records the venue already holds.

Five withheld cards are sound only because the silence rule makes a "no" option impossible to carry: no seasonal price (5), no minimum spend (11), no bond (13b), no horizon (19a), no minimum notice (19b). A marketer would recommend the absence as the default on each, since nothing on the card carries adding the term, and would name the observation that would add it. Page B instead proposes season-long priced trials for a bond and a minimum spend. That is a heavy spend of the owner's attention on terms nobody has asked for.

Cards that count what sellers print where a regulation, an insurer's condition or a peak body's scheme settles the parameter, or a band of it:

- Card 16: the Liquor Act and the licensed area (a change 21 days ahead each time), and the public liability policy's exclusion of a licensed bar (E67). Until a broker's quote is in hand, the insurer decides whether "venue's bar" can be offered at all. The card counts sellers first.
- Card 19b: for any hire with a bar outside the café, the 21-day licence change fixes the minimum notice. The card files this under cost lines instead of splitting the parameter.
- Card 9: the ceiling is the room's certified occupancy, which has never been measured (E3, E4, E7). Market ceilings cannot set it.
- Card 17: guards or refusals for 18ths and 21sts are often liquor-licence or insurer conditions. The card should check the venue's licence conditions and policy before counting what sellers print.
- Cards 12b and 15, in part: an evening needs a music licence from the rights bodies' scheme (E60), and the venue's catering needs a food business licence that has lapsed (E61). Both are listed as commitments, which is right, but both options are still ranked on seller print.

## The three changes that would most improve page B for a one-sitting owner

1. **Rewrite the ruling sheet in plain words, one short line a card.** Each line gives the question, the held value by name and figure ("held: minimum age 25 in policy; never applied in 191 sales"), the recommendation, and one clause of why. Group the rows as confirm (7), rule now (the three evasive cards, where the card already has the evidence), and commission (the rest, cheapest first). Move all counts and codes into the cards. Aim at about 1,200 words.
2. **Recommend the null option by default** on cards 5, 11, 13b, 19a and 19b, naming the observation that would add the term, and put the law, licence, insurer and occupancy bounds ahead of the market counts on cards 9, 16, 17 and 19b. Together these turn about eight withheld rows into rows the owner can rule, and cut the season-long trials.
3. **Count what the venue's records already hold before handing the owner a commission.** That means the months when space hire and packages were on sale together (cards 1 and 7), the 13:00 against 14:00 finishes (12a), and the 143 unread enquiries (9). Also put the wedding-priority question from page A's A13 back as a card, with the displacement record.

## Rules for the corrections round

These are rules, not verdicts on single cards.

1. An enquiry that asks for a term not on offer is a leg. "Acceptance only" may be said of sales and never of asks. Where the venue's own buyers asked across options, the card says which option serves every ask.
2. Never join two halves that can be decided apart into one option. Where one half is carried, recommend it and withhold only the other.
3. Where an option is the absence of a term (no bond, no minimum, no seasonal price, no horizon, no notice) and nothing on the card carries adding the term, the card recommends the absence and names the observation that would add it. The silence rule may not turn a default into a withhold.
4. Where a law, a licence condition, an insurer's condition, an occupancy certificate or a peak body's scheme fixes the parameter or a band of it, the card states that bound first and counts sellers only on what remains.
5. The comparator cell that may carry a line is the same on every card that turns on the same dimension, and the bar-and-kitchen part is set aside on every card where sale shape can move the count or on none. The card says which. If a cell can carry a departure, it can carry a keep, and the reverse.
6. The margin printed on the sheet is computed on the offering's own rows. Another offering's sales may be shown as context but never make the headline figure.
7. Where the venue's records can count the observation that decides a card, the round counts it before closing rather than listing it as a commission.
8. A market-only reading that lands on the silent count is stamped "declines", not "differs".
9. Every sheet row names its held value in words and figure, and names any held value the evidence contradicts, so that withholding cannot quietly leave a contradicted rule in force.
10. Where a value is held, the card's runner-up is that value. A margin taken against an option nobody holds is not a margin the owner can rule on.

## One sentence

Page B is an improvement on page A for this owner, because it stands on the venue's real sales and enquiries where page A stood on one unpaid invoice and the clubs' habits, but it needs its sheet rewritten and its three evasive withholds carried before he can rule it in one sitting; I am about 80 per cent sure of that verdict.
