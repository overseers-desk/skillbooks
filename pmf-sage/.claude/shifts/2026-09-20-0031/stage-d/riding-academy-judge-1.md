# Riding academy: judge 1 on the two decision pages

The context behind every verdict below: nothing in this run has ever been ruled. The forerunner, Equestrian Me, sold for three months in 2025 and sold almost nothing. Every "held" value is either the terms now on sale (single private lessons of 30 and 60 minutes, `S2` section 7), an earlier draft (page A itself), a proposal, or something the owner said on 25 August or 7 September 2026. So on both pages, "agrees with the owner" usually means agreeing with a draft or a meeting remark, not with a ruling.

## The clearest cases of each fault

### Page A

**Echo:**

- Card 12: "The anchored chip is honest: \$95, \$135 and \$175 are the venue's own listed numbers, kept deliberately." The visitor list is kept because it is the list. The fence-blind draft of the same card refuses to do this ("that record is of single sessions sold into a visitor-shaped stream").
- Card 18: "the one measurable thing this channel has produced so far is 14 school-topic enquiries in eight months with no lesson ask among them". The card then recommends the owner's school channel at the owner's pace.
- Card 6: "Rejected as the first slot on capacity, not on demand." Thursday is the afternoon the present arrangement leaves free. Card 2 is a near fourth: "where the family already comes" restates the segment matrix.

**Sales read as preference (the owner's third worry):**

- Card 1: "152 of 164 genuine sessions start between 9am and 1pm ... so the family is on site in the morning, at the weekend, already." These are morning single sessions, sold at the only terms on offer, read as the buyer's occasion.
- Card 16: "two blocks, one weekday afternoon and one Saturday morning, carry the buyers already on this record into a programme".
- Card 20: "a 9am to 3pm camp day sits inside the window this venue's own buyers already book".

Page A's own card 5 names the flaw ("a record of single sessions sold into a visitor-shaped search stream") and three other cards commit it anyway.

**Contrarian:**

- Card 22 is the only clear case: it publishes the weight limit although, by its own reading, "the Australian cell is against it".
- Card 0 carries a contrarian line, "It is rejected because it is the category the venue is already in and the run exists to move past it", although its evidence does not need it.
- Card 5's market leg against the owner's evening overreads K1 and K2. The daylight, not the market, carries that departure.

Page A's other departures from the owner are evidenced: the name (card 3), the direction of the catchment (cards 1 and 17), the free trial (card 13), games as method rather than product (card 14), and adults (card 25).

**Unsupported:**

- Card 16's four slots, contradicted on the same page by cards 5 and 6.
- Card 21 by design.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

Mostly yes, and far better than page A. The page asks keeping and leaving for the same proof, a market-only reading. The sheet states this, and the correction note is candid: "Every second-round move that stood ran away from what you hold, which the method asks to be read again." I checked the moves it names, and they stand on their counts:

- Card 3 went to withheld because stated postures run 8 to 0 against the held silence.
- Card 29 left the terms on sale on 15 against 8.
- Card 8 went to withheld on a one-operator lead.

No card turns a recommendation because it matches the owner.

There are two asymmetries, and they run in opposite directions:

- **Against the owner.** Card 18's departure from his games proposal passes on an instrument the page elsewhere distrusts. On cards 3, 11, 24 and 35 the page withholds because "X needs no sentence", and a games-as-method school needs no sentence either. Card 14 keeps his small group off the pair altogether. In both cases a departure passes without the instrument test that a match is put through.
- **Towards what is held.** Card 10 keeps `S2`'s lengths with the catchment leaning the other way, while card 34 is withheld for the same kind of reversal. The difference is defensible: card 34's local reversal is 7 to 2 and card 10's is 4 to 2, which one rival ties. But the page does not say so.

Page B also never directly tests three of the owner's own evidence-bearing claims:

- the two-markets geography;
- the keyword naming strategy;
- the credential click test.

Page A tested two of them against evidence and corrected them. Neither page holds the owner's view against him because it is his.

## What the owner has to read

**Page A.** 43,001 words and 26 cards. The front matter, from the summary to the end of the two-markets section, is about 5,700 words; the summary table alone is about 700. The skeleton decisions table adds about 2,000. The table says Ready or Blocked, and the chips say where a value came from. But no column says "this only confirms what you hold" or "this departs from you". "Owner-stated" appears both on cards that correct him (1, 17) and on cards that keep him (18). He cannot tell at a glance which rows need his judgment. Every row is put as "rule", and in practice he would have to read most cards.

**Page B.** 34,971 words and 28 cards. The ruling sheet is about 2,470 words and the order of ruling about 1,165, so about 3,630 words, or 3,710 with the opening paragraph and "What passed through". The "your part" column splits the rows into 11 to confirm, 3 to rule and 14 to commission or rule, so he can see at a glance where his judgment is asked.

Could he rule from those two sections alone, opening a card only where a row raises a doubt? For the confirm rows, largely yes. For the rest, with difficulty, for four reasons:

1. The margin and lean cells are written in the method's language: "one business read the other way", units against operators, "silent". For an owner giving it one sitting this is heavy going; the glossary helps but does not cure it.
2. Two rows mislead as they stand. Card 28 says "confirm" on a lead the card itself calls thin and edge-dependent. Cards 16 and 24 point opposite ways on the first session: confirm a set first session, then withheld with a lean to "no separate first session".
3. Several confirmed recommendations are not values he can put on sale. "From 4 to 6", "\$70 to \$99", and "half an hour and an hour" with the entry-lesson format left open all need a second decision.
4. Card 22 and card 29 are the same question, and both are "rule".

He would open cards 14, 16/24, 18, 23 and 28 at least. Realistically that is the 3,700 words plus about 6,000 words of card text, which is still well under page A.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

Of the 14 withheld cards, all 14 are sound by the brief's test, and none is evasive: each names why the evidence on it cannot separate the options, and names an observation that could.

Two are over-strict, withholding answers that common sense and the card's own figures carry:

- Card 4: beginners are taken; 0 of 40 print an entry gate and the market-only reading lands 14 to 1.
- Card 31: accept the voucher; no page anywhere prints a refusal.

Card 14 is withheld between the wrong pair.

Of the 14 cards that recommend, two should have withheld or split:

- Card 28: a thin, edge-dependent band that the card concedes; a withheld line with a price observation would be more honest than "confirm".
- Card 18's "no games" half: split off and withhold that half, keeping horse care.

Card 10's riding-time half and card 23 are borderline. On balance the page withholds about the right number, with the two errors cancelling in count but not in kind.

## Three changes that would most improve page B for a one-sitting owner

1. **Make the sheet tell the truth about each row.** Mark card 28 as rule (thin), not confirm. Resolve cards 16 and 24 into one first-session question before it reaches him. Fold card 29 into card 22. Put the owner's two or three riders on card 14 as a named option. Add one column: "what ruling this commits you to buy or build" (horses for any group above two, instructor verification for cards 15 and 16, lighting for an evening). Today that sits scattered across "Wrong today" and the consequence clauses.
2. **Restore the venue's own non-sales evidence that page A held and page B dropped, and test the owner's claims against it.** That means:
    - the credential click test, on card 15;
    - the on-site games observation, on card 18;
    - the postcode correction of the two-markets direction and the adult relaunch buyer, on cards 1 and 38.

    These are observations, not sales at the only terms. Without them the page cannot show the owner where he was right or wrong on his own evidence, which is what he asked for.
3. **Turn recommendations into values he can sell, in plain words, and cut the sheet and order to about 1,500 words.** For example: "6, as now" rather than "from 4 to 6"; a price per format and length rather than a band; lengths tied to formats. Keep the method's counts on the cards and give the sheet one plain sentence per row. Put the one dated commission, card 11's check on the rivals before the holidays end on 5 October, at the top.

## Verdict

Page B is an improvement on page A for this owner on the three faults he named: no echoes and no sales read as preference, against page A's five echoes and three sales readings, and one contrarian card on each page. I am about 70 per cent sure, and the reservation is real: page B is the harder page to act on. It hands him market defaults and fourteen commissions where page A handed him prices, block sizes and a timetable he could argue with.
