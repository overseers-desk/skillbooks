# One owner's cold read: party packages decision page (front, through "What passed through")

## 1. What I now believe I'm being asked to decide

I'm being asked to settle, card by card, what our children's and family party product actually is: who books it, what's inside the fee, how long it runs, what it costs and how that moves with headcount, what happens with rain and cancellations, and where and how it's sold. Staff surveyed the market and drafted a recommended answer for each of 24 questions, but eleven came back too evenly split to recommend, and those need me to either choose blind or pay for one more piece of evidence first. Before I rule anything, six things are already wrong in our own paperwork no matter which way I rule, and those get fixed Monday regardless of my decision. I'm also told which cards silently decide each other, so I don't rule one and then find I've already answered three more without meaning to. Finally I'm given an order to rule them in, so I don't lock in the fee before I've settled what's inside it.

## 3. Words or phrases I had to guess at

- "units" (used throughout the margin column, e.g. "6 units (9 of 15 units against 3 of 15 units)") — I guessed this means one counted operator page or business, not a booking or a dollar. The sheet never says.
- "the catchment" (cards 0, 16) — I guessed this means the set of nearby, comparable local operators, as opposed to the whole national/international pool of 45.
- "farms abroad and interstate" vs "local" — I guessed "local" means Gold Coast/South-East Queensland comparable operators and "abroad and interstate" means everything else in the 45, but the boundary line is never drawn on the page.
- The citation codes — F68, O14, E4, E5, F65, O9, E79, O4, F57 — I guessed these point to specific record or observation files behind the scenes; I could not tell from the front which document each one is, only that one exists.
- "coded so" (card 7: "13 of 28 coded so and 2 uncoded") — I guessed this means the market survey tagged those 13 pages as having no extras list, as opposed to the analyst simply not being able to tell.
- "the reliability check" (card 5) — I guessed this is a separate QA pass over the market coding that flagged this particular question as unreliable, but I never see what it found beyond "a known rule gap."
- "the third derivation" (defined in the legend as the market-only reading) — this one is actually explained, so not really a guess, but the term itself reads like jargon I'd never repeat back to a parent or a booking clerk.

## 4. The six "wrong today" items — could I tell a staff member to fix each on Monday?

- Jumping castle named on one page, absent from another, barred by the fee schedule — **Yes.** Whoever owns the website copy reconciles the two pages against §4.3.
- Barn/Red Tables capacity stated as 20 on one page and 60 on the fee schedule — **Yes.** Whoever owns the fee schedule picks one number per space and the other documents point at it.
- Party hours given as 9am–1pm in one clause and implying an 11am end in another, same document — **Yes.** Same document owner reconciles the two clauses.
- Nobody has ever watched what our own checkout shows after payment — **Yes.** One person books a test party and reports what comes back.
- Face painting was dropped from the packages in March but is still taking live orders — **Yes.** Whoever holds the booking catalogue either retires it properly or puts it back on the record as current.
- One of five priced party products has never taken a booking, and two internal records disagree on when it was even created — **Yes, in outline** — I understand the action ("open the booking system's audit trail and settle which record is right"), though I don't know from this page which two records are in conflict or who normally reconciles them.

All six: yes.

## 5. Three changes that would most help me rule this in one sitting

- Put the margin as a plain percentage or "X of Y" next to the recommendation in the table itself, not spread across a sentence I have to do arithmetic on mid-meeting (cards 3, 6, 10, 12, 13 all needed me to stop and divide).
- For the eleven withheld cards, put what would settle each one — re-read, ask the market, or "press one button" — into the ruling sheet as its own column, not three pages later in prose. I have to flip back and forth right now to know which withheld cards are cheap to unstick before I sit down.
- Where the market-only reading is "agrees / declines" or "differs / agrees" (cards 7, 10, 13, 17), say which half of the two-part question each word belongs to. As printed, I can't tell from the row which part of the question the market backed and which part it had nothing to say about.

## 6. Length

The front — the ruling sheet, the order of ruling, and "What passed through" — runs to just under 5,000 words (4,981 by count). That is too long for one sitting against a 24-row decision. I can read a table, but the order-of-ruling prose beneath it (six wrong-today items, the order itself, four silent-rule notes, eleven withheld-card explanations, three near-collisions, and a round-by-round audit trail) is a second document's worth of reading before I reach a single card. I would want the sheet and the six wrong-today items in the room with me, and the rest — the withheld-card reasoning, the near-collision notes, the correction-round history — as a reference I dip into card by card, not read cover to cover first.
