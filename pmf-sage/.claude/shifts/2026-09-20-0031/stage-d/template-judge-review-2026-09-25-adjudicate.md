## 0. The corrections template's rules, and the step-22 choices: rule or choice, and which way each leans

### 0.1 The three rules said to have held on both runs

**Silence scoped.** `corrections-clerk.md`: "silence is an option where the option is the absence of something a seller prints to sell (no limit printed, no discount offered, no rule stated): there the pages printing nothing are the observation, they take a row, and they are not discounted for being silent, unless a measured lean in the capture could account for them."

The evidence is weaker than "held on both runs" suggests. Kayak hire's set-wide pass applied it (cards 19, 28, 33, 35, and 20 cards touched), and both kayak judges and the kayak targeted judge read the page afterwards without faulting it. The riding academy applied it only in its last resume, on ten cards, one line moving toward what is held (rider weight, 15 to 8) and one away (more riders). No judge read the riding page after that resume (ledger, "Riding corrections round on the judges' rules"; the targeted judge had read the round before). So the rule was applied twice and judged once. Its moves ran both ways. It earns the rule as a scope for the method's own sentence ("where saying nothing is itself one of the options"), which the method never scopes, but not in its present wording, for three reasons.

First, it drops the method's margin both ways. `sage-A-adjudicate.md` and `forms/card.md` both require that where silence is an option, "the Recommended line gives the margin both ways: among operators that state a posture, and with the silent counted in". The template says only that the silent pages "take a row" and "are not discounted", which a clerk in a hurry reads as the silent-counted-in margin alone.

Second, it tilts toward whatever a venue holds when what it holds is to print nothing. The riding targeted judge said so of the riding venue ("the venue's held positions are mostly 'say or sell the minimum'"). The same holds on venue hire, whose listed product sits on no public page. The rule is fair only where the card asks what to print. Its own example "no discount offered" is a practice, not a print. On venue hire the terms that matter (a minimum spend, a deposit, a bond, a discount for a weekday) are the method's "term that travels in a quote", and "cannot be seen from public pages". The venue hire pilot coding already found "a minimum spend has no value in the sale variable" (commit 489a55e4f). A clerk given this rule will count every page silent on a minimum spend as the option "no minimum spend", and the lean clause cannot rescue it, because no "measured lean in the capture" exists for quote-borne terms.

Third, it is handed to several per-card clerks as a sentence ("the same reading of silence", in the per-card paragraph). Which options are silence options is a classification, and it belongs in a file (question 4 below).

**A withheld card's lean and deciding observation.** Earned as a rule, on more evidence than any other line in the list. Batch two's cold reads moved from 9 rows of 25 to 21 once the clauses were printed (entry 13). Riding put it on 3 cards, kayak hire on 11. The method already asks that a withheld line name "the one observation that would carry either". It leans neither way. Two faults remain. The word "leans" means two things. In `sage-A-adjudicate.md` it is the instrument's lean ("Where the instrument sees one option better than the other, the line says which way that leans"). In `corrections-clerk.md` and `integrator.md` it is which option the counts favour. A hurried clerk writes the count's lean and never states the instrument's, which is the lean that tells the owner whether the counts can be trusted. And the clauses have two owners: the set-wide corrections clerk ("every withheld card says in plain words which way it leans") and the integrator ("for every withheld card, put on the card itself ... which way it leans and why that did not carry"). Batch two had one clerk delete another's clauses (batch-two lessons, corrections).

**The carried-line test read with the third derivation's landing.** "a carried line whose third derivation ties or lands on another option is put to the carried-line test again on the card's own evidence, and the recommending sentence says where the market-only reading landed". The ledger shows it applied to eight kayak cards and one riding card. It moved one line (kayak's quiet or energetic, carried to withheld), which moved back within the same round under the single-reader rule and "stands carried". Its net effect over both runs is no move. The method already asks it ("where they differ the card holds two readings, and its recommended line is tested as any other"), so it earns the rule, as the method's own test stated for this case. But it only fires one way. The kayak targeted judge found "eight of its nine rules able only to take confidence away" and "no mirror test that could carry a withheld line" (improvements entry 37). This rule is one of the eight, and its mirror is missing: a withheld card whose market-only reading lands on an option, while the first derivation's counts lean the same way, is two readings from different evidence agreeing. By the method ("where they agree ... the agreement is evidence about the market") that card should be put to the carried-line test too.

It also leans on the venue's own buyers in a way a hurried clerk will not see. The third clerk "reads the market side alone, without the operator's own sales record", so on a card that rests on own buyers or both, a market-only tie is expected and says nothing against the line. The equine programme's 8 bookings and venue hire's asks and refusals are exactly such legs. Read in a hurry ("the third derivation tied, so test again, so withhold"), the rule strips own-buyer legs, and on these runs own-buyer legs were taken at the venue's terms, so the strip runs away from what the venue holds.

### 0.2 The other rules in the list

The template says of all nine: "Both runs of the last batch applied each of these and they held; the list is the count". The batch-three lessons file records batch-three application for the part set aside (lines 65 and 67), silence, one reading of a shared variable, the carried-line test and the lean. The trailing-silence, tested-both-ways, one-sighting and figure-names-its-base rules come from batch two (party packages, chapel hire), and the ledger shows the riding set-wide pass touched them on 2, 2, 2 and 8 cards and kayak's on 7, 1 and 3. That is application, not a test, and the sentence should not claim more. "On carried rows as on withheld ones" comes from one kayak judge. It still earns a rule, because it mirrors a rule already in force, which is the right reason to take a one-run lesson as a rule.

The trailing-silence rule carries a clause the method does not: "a line is withheld where some evidence puts an option against it, that evidence meeting the standard of a leg on the card's own measured-in line". The method withholds for "no lead at all, a tie or a leg that could not have shown the other option". The template adds a fourth trigger, which can only withhold. It is question 5's item 5.4.

### 0.3 The step-22 choices, one by one

1. **The owner's own proposal as a row of the option table** (riding). One run, found by both its judges. It only earns a choice, and even that goes beyond the method, since `sage-A-adjudicate.md` says: "A value that only an internal document, a sibling run or an unsold present practice holds is not an option; it is a prior". The method makes one exception, a ruling the card leaves. The stated cost ("a row that may be no way the market sells the thing") is half the cost. The other half is that the row enters the option table the card check counts, the margin and one-operator test are then taken against a row with no market figure, and on the equine run the obvious "owner's proposal" is the PonyPals definition of 27 July, a draft. It leans toward what the venue holds. If taken, it should be a change of reading judged under step 23. The alternative is to put the proposal's value and its margin in the priors pass and the Held line, where the method already sends it.
2. **The venue's observations of its own buyers at other terms, as dated items to verify without figures** (riding). Only a choice where the observation comes from a meeting. Where it comes from a record (an enquiry for a space not offered, a group of 45 refused the Dining Deck), the method already makes it a leg: "an unsolicited enquiry for something not offered is a leg on its own", and "what the record can show is what buyers did for want of it". Offered as a choice, it lets a run downgrade record-borne own-buyer legs to unfigured items, and that leans away from the venue's buyers. The step should split the two.
3. **The gate leading the because-clause** (kayak). A choice, fairly costed, but it runs together two things the method keeps apart. A regulation or a peak body's scheme binds every seller and is a row of the market. The venue's own cover conditions are a capability fact, which "enters the card as the cost line of building it, or as the card's open question" and is "never a standing caution over a review" ("The two legs"). The kayak targeted judge found card 2 "withheld-sound on the insurance gate ... and not on the reason it gives". On the equine run, "Rivermill is not the provider of record" is a held position of the definition (its note sends the reader to "why PonyPals, not Rivermill, is the NDIS provider of record"). A gate-led because-clause would make that held structure the reason every venue-delivered option fails, which leans toward what the venue holds.
4. **A priced trial as the deciding observation** (kayak). Earns a choice, not a rule, and its cost is written for kayak hire. On venue hire the cheapest observation at terms already on sale is linking the listed product from a public page, which is no trial and needs no cover. On the equine run a priced trial meets the scheme's price rules and a cohort who need consent arranged through someone else, which "needs cover and the owner's consent" does not cover. Neutral as to the venue.
5. **A single reader's count limits a line and never moves it, or moves it either way** (kayak). Rightly offered as a choice, since the kayak targeted judge asked for "one rule for a single reader's count either way" and the round chose the first. But "limit" is undefined. In the kayak round it meant a stated caveat that moved the line neither way. A hurried clerk reads it as "may withdraw", which is one-way toward withheld. The standing block has already chosen it for every clerk (question 5, item 5.6).
6. **Kinds of seller judged by parameter** (kayak). This is not a choice, since the method already requires it: "Where the population differs on the dimension this card rules, the rate may not stand as the leg" (the measured-in line). Offering it as a choice lets a run go back to reach-by-sentence, which the kayak targeted judge found left five cards untested, "a miss that favours the owner". It earns the rule, from the method, whatever the count of runs.
7. **The round's rules stated once on card 0** (kayak). The operator repository's own invariant ("every piece of information normally has just one home") already makes this a rule, and `corrections-clerk.md` already prefers it ("Prefer stating the round's rules once, where your prompt says"). Neutral.

Steps 18, 20 and 21 offer three further choices (the held-value ranking, the base for the one-operator test, the top-up's timing). All three are rightly choices, since the two runs differed or nobody ruled. Step 18's cost is written for the riding academy and misfires on venue hire (3.1 below). Step 20's cost leaves out something the card check shows: `COUNT` in `tools/card-check.py` knows "units", "walks", "places", "programmes", "searches", "asks", "enquiries" and "bookings", but not "operators". A value card whose margin is a count lead in operators is refused for a mismatched unit, so choosing operators costs refusals on every price and length card until the check learns the word.

## 1. Tilt

### 1.1 Toward what the venue holds: the third derivation reads the venue's own buyers (`third-derivation-clerk.md`, step 17)

The market-only clerk reads "`briefs/venue-situation.md`" (the standing block makes it open to every clerk), and the brief then says "Nothing else in the repository is open to you: no card, no sales record, no strike list, no fence index, nothing the venue holds or sells." On both batch-four runs, venue-situation is the shape note, and the shape notes carry what the venue sells and what its buyers did. The equine note: "all eight buyer bookings were made ahead of the session. Four of the eight were made by the buyer online, three by staff on a buyer's behalf after an enquiry, and one by the provider for her own client", plus the related product's name, its booking codes and "the buyer pays in full at checkout, per guest". The venue hire note: "Since that date the product has one booking, entered by staff", and "The listed product's own text also asks for a non-refundable deposit that comes off the fee, and a refundable bond."

The third derivation exists so that "the card shows what the market says with the venue's own buyers and without them" (method, Conduct). A market-only clerk who knows the venue's product asks for a deposit and a bond, and that four of eight buyers booked online, is no longer market-only on the commitment and channel cards. The leak runs toward what the venue holds, since the venue's terms are the ones the clerk has seen.

### 1.2 Toward what the venue holds: a draft in a booking system is "the terms on sale" (`lead-deriving-clerk.md`, `area-deriving-clerk.md`, `priors-clerk.md`, step 18)

Lead and area: "The terms this offering sells under are one of the options where the sales record gives them a figure, with no advantage for being the venue's and no handicap". Step 18's ranking puts "terms on sale today" first and "a draft" fourth, with the reason "it stamps against what buyers were offered before what staff wrote".

On venue hire the terms "on sale today" are the definition of 26 February, entered into the booking system the same day and linked from nowhere. No buyer was offered them. The sales record gives them a figure: one free booking a staff member entered. By the template's test that is an option with a figure. By the method it is not one: "A value that only ... an unsold present practice holds is not an option; it is a prior", and "A count for an offering not offered, or not named as buyers name it, is provenance and not a leg". The priors ranking then turns the draft into the top tier of held value, above the invoiced hire the venue has actually sold since 2023. Every Held stamp is judged against the draft, and the draft appears on the card as a market option. Both run toward what the venue holds, and neither is visible, because the sentence reads as neutral.

### 1.3 Toward what the venue holds: a definition read as rulings (`priors-clerk.md`)

"Where the owner has already ruled the parameter (the earlier definition's decisions table marks ruled apart from recommended), write his ruling on the Ruled line with its date and his words, since a ruling stands until he changes it; the card tests it and does not reopen it."

Neither batch-four definition has a decisions table. Both open: "This document was created during product development to capture decisions". A priors clerk in a hurry reads "capture decisions" as rulings. The price, the four hours, the 80 guests, the provider of record would then go on the Ruled line, where "the card tests it and does not reopen it" and the card check reads anything after "Ruled:" but "open" as a ruling. The sentence that follows ("His words are quoted from their source ... a paraphrase ... is not his words") guards against a paraphrase. It does not guard against a draft whose values are not his words at all.

### 1.4 Away from the venue's buyers: own-buyer legs read as acceptance only (`corrections-clerk.md`, fourth set-wide rule)

"Where the offering has been sold under one shape only, sales show acceptance and not preference, and the line rests on the market". On the equine run the offering has not been sold under one shape only. Two shapes have been on sale at once since July: provider-delivered with the scheme invoiced after the session (8 buyer bookings), and venue-delivered with payment at checkout (0). The method's case for a sales figure carrying one option against another is exactly this: "the sales figure carries the on-sale option against another only where both were on sale at once". The rule as written sends a hurried clerk to "rests on the market" without looking. The comparison is confounded (the two differ in who delivers and when money moves), and the card should say so, but it is an own-buyer observation, and the template has no sentence that finds it.

### 1.5 Away from the venue's own conduct under test: "a fact about the corpus until someone shows it is a fact about you" (`corrections-clerk.md`)

"A pass that moves lines one way only is read again before it stands and is not corrected by turning a line back; a one-sided count is a fact about the corpus until someone shows it is a fact about you." This was batch two's finding, where both targeted judges found the corpus behind the direction. In batch three both targeted judges found the opposite: the riding direction "comes from the rules, not from new evidence or the clerks", and on kayak hire "the rule set explains it better than the evidence". The sentence is fair as written. A clerk in a hurry reads it as a presumption that a one-way pass is the corpus, which is what batch three disproved. Nothing in it points a direction relative to the venue, but it takes away the one check that catches drift either way.

### 1.6 Fair as written, read unfairly in a hurry: the judge's third worry (`judge-brief.md`)

"A third sits between them and is yours to test as well: reading what the venue already sells as what the market wants, when sales made at the only terms on offer cannot show what buyers would have done at other terms." It has no mirror. The method counts, as a leg, what buyers did for want of an option: "asked for it, stalled, refused, or left". A page that discounts those observations because they are the venue's own is the owner's second worry in its commonest form, and the ECHO verdict ("sales at the only terms on offer included") gives the judge a word only for the first. Venue hire has exactly such observations (a buyer who asked for the deck, a group of 45 refused).

### 1.7 Fair as written, read unfairly: the cost-to-build list (`integrator.md` item 2 and item 4, `owner-cold-read-brief.md`)

Integrator item 2 asks for "what decides whether the offering can open at all whichever way he rules (cover, a licence, a planning approval, a staff check, a count of animals or craft)", and item 4 asks for the rows that "would commit him to buy, hire, build or certify something". Both are true costs. The integrator's read list holds no capability record, so any such item it did not take from a card's cost lines comes from its own general knowledge. On the equine run that is the whole field of disability-scheme registration. And a front that opens with what stops the offering opening reads, to an owner, as the method's warned flip, "we do not yet have what the product needs" turning into "the product is constrained by not having it". It leans toward options that need nothing new, which are the ones the venue holds.

### 1.8 The top-up's second count is not blind to the card (`survey-top-up-clerk.md`)

"A count of yours that reads pages or profiles by judgment is made a second time by a second clerk who has not seen yours". The method asks more: the count "turns that card only once a second coder has made it blind". The first top-up clerk reads the withheld cards, which after the priors pass carry the Priors and Held lines, so it knows which option the venue holds. The template does not say the second clerk is kept from the card. A second coder who knows which option breaks the tie toward the venue is not blind in the sense the method means, whichever way the clerk is inclined.

### 1.9 Watched and found sound

The standing block's evidence paragraph, the corrections template's "put it alike whether the recommendation keeps what the venue holds or leaves it", the direction report on two axes, and the targeted judge's mirror question are all balanced and should stay word for word. The priors template's "a match is not a fault in the card any more than a departure is a merit" is the method's own.

## 2. Hardening

### 2.1 `lead-deriving-clerk.md`: "The owner has not ruled the buyer and is not asked to mid-run"

The method has the buyer card ruled at the second gate, and "Only then is the seller's sentence written, one per ruled buyer". The templates write every standing buyer's sentences before any gate. That may be the right adaptation for a re-run, but "is not asked to mid-run" states a design of the programme as fixed. Only the owner can rule it, since it is his gate. The closing section's list of what "the owner's to rule" leaves it out. The same gap was found this morning for the strike list's place (morning review 5.1).

### 2.2 `standing-brief-block.md`: the single-reader rule chosen for every clerk

"say so beside it, since a later clerk may let it limit a line and not turn one". Step 22 offers "limit and never move" and "move either way" as a choice nobody has ruled. The standing block, which every clerk reads, has already taken the first. The orchestrator can rule it per run; the block should not.

### 2.3 `corrections-clerk.md`: "Both runs of the last batch applied each of these and they held; the list is the count"

This states nine rules as settled on a record that shows six of them applied, one judged on one run, and several carried from batch two (0.2 above). Step 23 is what rules them: a judge reads the changed words before they reach a run. This review is that judge for these words, and the runs' standing blocks already say "runs under the method's reading dated 25 September 2026" (question 5, item 5.10).

### 2.4 `survey-top-up-clerk.md`: "Work the list cheapest first, since the observations that take hours ... can carry rows before the page reaches the owner"

Step 21 offers "Whether the top-up runs before the page reaches the owner" as the orchestrator's choice. The template presumes one answer. The orchestrator rules it.

### 2.5 `lead-deriving-clerk.md`: "with that rule as the floor row"

"Where a regulation, the insurer's conditions or a peak body's scheme sets a parameter, say so here: prefer carding it with that rule as the floor row". On the equine run the scheme's rule on price is a ceiling, not a floor, and whether it binds a given participant depends on how the plan is managed. The two plan types the definition names (plan-managed, self-managed) may be bound differently. A clerk given "floor row" and a scheme document will set the scheme's figure as a fixed parameter of the offering. Who it binds, and whether the venue or its provider is the party bound, is for the owner or the provider of record to state, and until then the card's third list files it with that role. On venue hire a licence or an approved capacity is also a ceiling.

### 2.6 `third-derivation-clerk.md` and step 22's choice 6

Discussed in 0.3: the method's per-parameter reach is presented as a choice. That is hardening in reverse: a rule softened into an option.

### 2.7 Smaller

`integrator.md`: "in about a page" is a threshold one run happened to aim at. The riding prose ran to 1,850 words because the four parts and ten collision pairs would not fit. Leave it, but as a target, not a limit. `corrections-clerk.md`'s "two coders agreed on no better than chance" is a threshold only the reliability note can set. It should say "as the reliability note reports it", as `third-derivation-clerk.md` does for the floor margin.

## 3. Carry-over to runs unlike the ones the templates were learnt on

### 3.1 "The terms on sale", "the sales record", "own buyers" on venue hire

This is 1.2. The phrases assume that the offering on sale is the one buyers bought. On venue hire the listed product has no paid buyer, and the hires buyers paid for were sold under the party products (sibling runs' offerings) and by invoice. The templates have no sentence for bookings of this offering's substance made under another offering's name. The fourth `{{ON_SALE}}` wording ("the sales record lists those bookings apart") is the nearest, and the run took the first. Step 18's ranking rationale ("what buyers were offered before what staff wrote") inverts here, since what staff wrote is what sits in the booking system.

### 3.2 "The terms it was taken under" on the equine run

The standing block: "what this venue's own buyers did ... each count beside the terms it was taken under". The equine sales record "leaves out the listing's price, length and days under the run's rule" (ledger), while the run's filled on-sale sentence says "the sales record holds the terms it sold under". A deriving clerk can neither follow the block nor state the condition line the method requires ("what was offered, under what name, on what surface"). Separately, the listing's figure is not what the buyer paid, since nothing is paid at checkout and the scheme settles later at a price the scheme's rules shape.

### 3.3 Buyers and bookings, and the payer

The one-operator test protects a lead from one seller counted twice. The own-buyer record has the same fault and no test: 8 bookings are 4 buyers, and one of the 8 was made by the provider for her own client. A card resting on own buyers that reads "8 bookings" has the base problem step 20 solves for the corpus. The templates also have one "buyer" throughout. On the equine run the one who books (a participant, a support worker, the provider), the one who pays (the plan manager, the scheme) and the one who takes part differ. Lead item 3's seller's sentence "said to a named real enquirer from the sales record" meets this directly.

### 3.4 Named enquirers who are scheme participants

Lead item 3 has the seller's sentence "said to a named real enquirer from the sales record where one exists". On the equine run the named enquirers are participants of a disability scheme. A card is copied into the judges' reviews, the cold read and the run record. Their names do not need to travel. A role ("a participant booking through her plan manager") serves the sentence test as well.

### 3.5 "A venue's whole groups page", "the payment variable", "four cards on commitment"

Trade nouns and cases of the earlier runs left in instructions. `lead-deriving-clerk.md` item 1: "a reading kept to sentences beside the offering's own word can miss a venue's whole groups page". On venue hire the missed pages are functions, events and parties, and buyers "name the occasion, not the hire" (venue hire search-demand clerk, SD-3). `area-deriving-clerk.md`: "(one payment variable marked deposits and instalments on ten priced units, seven of them for camps, parties or rides and not lessons ...)". `corrections-clerk.md`: "(four cards on commitment read one structure variable; four price cards read the operator table)". The lesson in each is general, and the example narrows it to a trade.

The area template's instruction behind that example misfires on venue hire in a way that matters: "count on the offering's own rows". On venue hire, whether a hire inside a package is the offering's row is card 0's question (one offer or several), and the lead offer in the venue's own business description is the packaged experience and not bare space. An area clerk that strips package rows as "other products" has ruled card 0 on its way to another card.

### 3.6 "Page A" in `judge-brief.md`

"Two pages put the same offering's product parameters to its owner for ruling. Page A was written earlier". Neither batch-four run has an earlier card page. Each has a product definition. If the orchestrator names the definition as page A, the judge's column "parameters page A ruled that page B puts to no card" reads a draft's values as rulings, which is 1.3 again, from the judge's side, and faults page B for not carding what nobody ruled.

### 3.7 `targeted-judge-brief.md`: chapel hire's question

"whether counting each operator once, where a group prints one text across several sites, is a sound rule for this kind of corpus and what it risks". That was the question of chapel hire's round (entry 14). A targeted judge of batch four is sent to a round whose rules may be the silence scope or the carried-line test. The brief asks it about a rule the round may not have used and does not ask about the ones it did.

### 3.8 `owner-cold-read-brief.md`: "You know the business thoroughly"

The cold reader is a model given one file. It knows nothing of the business, and the sentence invites it to supply the owner's knowledge from imagination. A reader who "knows" the venue's horses are few, or its barn seats 80, will mark rows confirm on facts it invented. That is the owner's first worry arriving in the test meant to measure legibility.

## 4. A rule handed to several clerks as a sentence

1. Which options are silence options. `corrections-clerk.md` gives per-card clerks, "by range", "the same reading of silence", defined in a bullet of the set-wide list. Each clerk classifies each option as print or practice. That list belongs in `3-decisions/card-set.md`, one row an option the set treats as silence, written by the lead clerk and checked by the set-wide clerk.
2. The offering's own rows. `area-deriving-clerk.md`: "count on the offering's own rows, say so with the rule". Each area clerk draws that boundary for itself. On venue hire the boundary is card 0's ruling (3.5). The card set should state it once per variable, as it already states "the one reading" for a shared variable.
3. The population a market-only clerk chooses. `third-derivation-clerk.md`: "where you choose a population because it shares this operator's shape ... say so". Third-derivation clerks work by range, and their read list does not include `3-decisions/card-set.md` or the `{{CORPUS}}/cut-<dimension>.tsv` the lead clerk writes "because area clerks who each draw such a part in words draw different parts". Each market-only clerk draws its own part in words. The corrections clerk then compares a card cut by the table with a third derivation cut by a sentence, and reads the difference as the market disagreeing. The cut table is market-side and can be opened to them by name. On venue hire there is a further choice no sentence can settle, since the shape note gives two operator shapes ("the listed product" and "the hires actually sold"). Which one a population is compared against has to be stated once, in the card set.
4. The base of the one-operator test. Step 20 has the orchestrator "State in every corrections prompt the base". The template reads it from "the base `3-decisions/card-set.md` states, or your prompt where the card set states none". One home is the card set, written once by the orchestrator before the first corrections clerk, as step 20 already requires for populations.

## The three mends that matter most

**1. Close the venue's own buyers and terms off the market-only reading** (`third-derivation-clerk.md`, step 17). On the equine run the third clerk would read which way four of eight bookings were made. On venue hire it would read that the product asks a deposit and a bond. The third derivation is the method's one test that tells a first derivation that matched with the files open from one that matched because the market does. Replace venue-situation on its list with a market-side copy the orchestrator writes and greps, and open the card set's population sections and the cut tables to it.

**2. A draft in a booking system is not the terms on sale, and a definition is not a ruling** (`lead-deriving-clerk.md`, `area-deriving-clerk.md`, `priors-clerk.md`, step 18). Venue hire's listed product is its definition's values entered the same day and linked from nowhere, with one free booking. As written it enters the cards as a figured option, tops the held-value ranking, and, through the priors clerk's "decisions table" parenthesis, may reach the Ruled line, where no card reopens it. Take the lead's and priors' replacement sentences above.

**3. Scope the silence rule to what is printed, keep the method's margin both ways, and give the carried-line test its mirror** (`corrections-clerk.md`). On venue hire the terms that decide a sale travel in quotes. A silence rule that counts every page silent on a minimum spend as "no minimum spend" will carry the options a venue with no public page already holds, and a carried-line test with no mirror can only take confidence away.

## Was one of the lessons the wrong lesson?

**The riding targeted judge's diagnosis of silence.** The judge found the set-wide round's direction came from "a rule that discounts pages saying nothing", which "runs away from" the venue's held positions "whatever the market shows". The lesson took the direction as a sign the rule was wrong, and the scoped rule turned rider weight back toward what was held. A rule is not wrong because it moves lines away from what the owner holds, which is his second worry stated as a method. The scope may be right for its own reason (a card that asks whether to print is answered by what is printed), and the template should stand on that reason alone. What would show whether the lesson was right: telephone or email ten of the riding corpus's units that print no weight limit and ask whether they apply one. If most apply one, the silent pages measured print and not practice, and the lesson holds only for cards that ask what to print, which is the scope proposed above. If most apply none, the silence was practice too, and the lesson holds wider.

**The gate leading the because-clause** (kayak, step 22 choice 3). The kayak targeted judge found card 2 "withheld-sound on the insurance gate ... and not on the reason it gives". The lesson may have taught that a gate explains a withholding the market reading should explain. What would show it: re-read kayak cards 2, 23, 25 and 28 with the gate moved to the cost lines. If the market reading alone withholds each, the gate was a harmless heading. If it carries any of them, the gate displaced a market finding, and the choice should be limited to rules that bind every seller.

**The owner's proposal as an option row** (riding, step 22 choice 1). Both riding judges asked for it, and the round gave it. The method already had an answer: the ruled value only, and a proposal on the Priors line with its margin in the pass. What would show which is right: the owner's rulings on riding cards 14 and 18 when he makes them. If he rules on the proposal's row where it had no market figure, the row anchored him, which is his first worry. If he rules the market's option with the proposal's margin in view, the row did what the priors line was already able to do.
