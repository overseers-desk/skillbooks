# The Survey templates as rewritten on 25 September, read for the owner before venue hire and the equine programme

Judged: the seventeen Survey-stage files rewritten today under `.claude/shifts/2026-09-19-1042/stage-d/briefs/` (records-clerk, records-refresh-clerk, codebook-amendment-author, amendment-record-form-clerk, coder-brief-block, amendment-coder-from-pages, pilot-coder, rule-review-clerk, recode-coder, operator-index-clerk, strike-list-clerk, search-demand-clerk, capability-clerk, whom-sold-to-coder, candidate-sentence-clerk, candidate-adjudicator) and the Survey and closing sections of `orchestrator-steps.md`. Read against `batch-three-lessons-by-clerk.md`, `batch-two-lessons-by-clerk.md` and `pair-lessons-by-clerk.md`, the 20 September review (`template-judge-review.md`), `sage-methodology.md`, `sage-S-survey.md`, `sage-A-adjudicate.md` (the card, the card set, the two legs), `forms/strike-list.md`, `forms/brief-read-order.md` and `tools/launch-check.py`. The standing block, `lead-deriving-clerk.md`, `priors-clerk.md`, `corrections-clerk.md`, `integrator.md` and `third-derivation-clerk.md` were read only where question 5 needed them.

The next two runs differ in the way that matters most here. Venue hire has been sold, under many product names, several of them the offerings of sibling runs (chapel hire, party packages). The equine programme for disability-funded participants has never been sold, is likely to have a proposal draft lying about, and its comparables include centres that belong to associations. Every finding below is weighed against those two cases.

The rewrite is good work. It carries nearly every batch-three lesson as a sentence that changes what a clerk does, and it keeps every mend the 20 September review asked for in these templates (the table below). The anti-tilt lines are intact and, in the new templates, well made: `rule-review-clerk.md` closes with "The rule serves whichever way the market falls; you do not know what this run will conclude", and `candidate-sentence-clerk.md` forbids its clerk to judge a sentence at all. What follows are the places where today's sentences open a channel of their own, most of them only on an offering never sold.

## 1. Tilt

### 1.1 Toward what the venue holds: `capability-clerk.md`, the source of an absence

"Where something a new offering would need is absent, the absence is a fact and takes a number too, since a card's cost line cites an absence as often as a presence."

The clerk's read list is the standing block, "The two legs", the fence index and the closed capability files, and nothing of the market. So the only places it can learn what the offering "would need" are the closed files (proposals, drafts, a manager's notes) and its own general knowledge. On the equine programme, a proposal's list of needs becomes numbered absences (a mounting ramp, a trained coach, a registration), and every card then cites that list as its cost lines. An option that needs what the draft needs carries a cost. An option the draft never imagined carries none, because no absence was recorded for it. The draft's design reaches every card, and it does so through the file written to keep opinion out. This is the owner's first worry, and the batch-two mend beside it ("never a constraint") does not touch it, because a cost line is exactly what that mend lets through.

### 1.2 Toward what the venue holds: `records-clerk.md` item 3, the shape note of an offering never sold

"The operator's shape on the four dimensions of step 1 of `sage-S-survey.md`, from how the offering is sold today".

`sage-S-survey.md` asks for "the operator's shape", not the offering's. Where the offering is not sold today there is no "how", and a clerk to whom nothing is closed will find one in the drafts: the proposal's intended booking route, the plan's paid entry. The shape note is not a private file. `tools/launch-check.py` copies it into `briefs/venue-situation.md`, which every clerk of the run reads. So an intended shape would reach every clerk as the venue's situation.

### 1.3 Toward the venue's parameters: `codebook-amendment-author.md`, draft-raised questions

"one variable for each question of the numbered list that no existing variable can answer".

Today's read order carries the batch-two mark ("the second being a question a draft raised and not one the market did"), and then the instruction treats both kinds alike. That is fair to values. It is not fair to parameters: the set of questions the corpus can answer, and so the set of cards that can be ruled with a count, is drawn partly from the venue's drafts. The market has no route onto the list, since the author is closed to the corpus. The mark changes nothing unless the amendment carries it forward, so that a card resting on a draft-raised variable can be read as answering a draft's question.

### 1.4 Away from what the venue holds, fair as written, read unfairly in a hurry: `strike-list-clerk.md` item 2 against item 1

Item 2, the operator's file: "Where the offering is already on sale, say plainly that a sale shows a buyer accepted the terms on offer and does not show that those terms were preferred to others". Item 1, the market's file: "Each finding states its measured population and its limit."

The operator's evidence gets a named discount, in words a clerk is told to write "plainly". The market's evidence gets a general limit. The method has the matching sentence for the market ("Absence in the corpus is absence from publication, never absence from the market"; operators "mostly publish for nobody"), and the brief does not ask for it. Venue hire has been sold, so its strike list will carry the discount on every venue row and nothing like it on the market rows beside them. That is the batch-two review's 1.2 again, in a Survey template. The third `{{ON_SALE}}` wording in `orchestrator-steps.md` repeats it in the standing block ("which show that buyers accepted those terms and not that they preferred them"). The first wording, the one venue hire will get, carries no such clause, so the same fact is discounted for one run's clerks and not for another's.

### 1.5 Fair as written, read unfairly: `coder-brief-block.md`

"Nothing else in the repository is open to you beyond what your own brief lists, the other coders' output files among them".

"Among them" attaches to the nearest plural, "what your own brief lists". Read at speed, the sentence opens the neighbours' files. That is the exact behaviour batch three recorded ("A kayak full-pass coder opened a neighbour's coded file") and that the sentence exists to stop.

### 1.6 Watched and found sound

`codebook-amendment-author.md` now sends the blind author to `briefs/venue-situation.md`, which holds the operator's shape (without values) before any strike list exists. The shape variables need values that can express the operator's shape, so this is defensible. See the last section for the evidence that would show it was the wrong lesson.

## 2. Hardening

### 2.1 `orchestrator-steps.md` step 15: the owner's gate, answered by a model sentence

"you fill the Returned line with what happened in fact, opening with the date, in the manner of \"2026-09-25: not put to the owner, who is away for this run; every row stands unstruck\""

The strike-list clerk now leaves the line blank, as the 20 September review asked. The orchestrator, though, is handed a worked sentence in which the owner is away and the list never reaches him. The launch check reads any line opening with a digit as returned, and then writes into `briefs/venue-situation.md` "The frame reaches: ..." with no word that the owner never saw the list. Every later clerk reads that as the owner's scoping. The step is honest about what happened, but its model is one batch's circumstance, and the tool turns it into a ruling. Whether the owner sees the strike list for venue hire or for the equine programme is his to rule for each run. For the equine programme the buyer (the participant, whoever manages the funding, whoever books) is less settled than in any run so far.

### 2.2 `operator-index-clerk.md`: owner-first as the default reading

"The rule, applied in this order: an owning body the profile itself names as owning or operating the site; failing that, the registrable domain ... Your prompt may depart from the ladder for a class of units, and says so as the orchestrator's choice."

The closing section of `orchestrator-steps.md` says the choices it lists are offered "because nobody has ruled them", and "any departure from the operator ladder" is among them. The ladder itself is not. Batch three measured the two readings far apart (24 sites, 24 operators one way and six the other), and the template makes one of them the default and the other a departure. Who rules: the study lead for the run, and until then both readings are equal and both counts are given. For the equine run the choice decides the count; see 3.4.

### 2.3 `search-demand-clerk.md`: no new pull

"Use the data already pulled and make no new database pull".

`sage-S-survey.md` budgets "two to four database lookups and three captures" for a single-offering study, and its step 4 (the name) is a capture. The template turns one batch's position (a lapsed trial, the owner away) into a standing bar. For the equine programme, with no sales record, what buyers type may be the only buyer-side evidence there is, and data pulled for other clusters will not cover how funding-scheme buyers phrase a search. Who rules: the owner, since a pull costs money. The template should say "unless your prompt allows one".

### 2.4 `records-clerk.md` item 4: when the buyer becomes ruled

"the buyer is unruled until the strike list exists".

Under `sage-A-adjudicate.md`, the strike list only strikes. The buyer is ruled at the second gate on the buyer card, and `lead-deriving-clerk.md` says so ("The owner has not ruled the buyer and is not asked to mid-run"). A clerk who reads today's sentence, and then finds a strike list in the folder, reads the buyer as ruled. Who rules: the owner, at the second gate.

### 2.5 Smaller

`codebook-amendment-author.md` and `whom-sold-to-coder.md` carry one run's codes for profile-only units: "(one run used 1 on a quote and 9 otherwise, never 0...)". The author drafts blind and has no other example, so it will copy these. The amendment author should set the codes and the rule review should try them. `orchestrator-steps.md` step 7 says "Where the run keeps stored text for nearly every unit, the pass is made from pages", which leaves an adverb deciding the instrument; the orchestrator should write a figure in the journal. `rule-review-clerk.md` says "You are the study lead the codebook names for rule review", which asserts what the run's codebook may say otherwise. The prompt should confirm it.

## 3. Carry-over to runs that differ from batch three

### 3.1 The shape note on the never-sold equine programme

This is 1.2: "from how the offering is sold today".

### 3.2 `{{ON_SALE}}` has no wording for a related offering on sale now

The three wordings are "on sale since", "never on sale" and "not on sale; a forerunner ... was sold from <date> to <date>". The equine programme is likely to fit none of them. Ordinary riding is on sale now, and some funded participants may already have booked it, under a product that is not this one and is not a finished forerunner. Venue hire has the opposite problem: it has been "on sale since", but under many names, some of them sibling runs' offerings. The third wording also sits in the closing section of `orchestrator-steps.md`, while "Filling a template" lists two. An orchestrator who fills from the first section never sees it.

### 3.3 "products typed otherwise ... carry both counts, with and without them" (`records-clerk.md`, `strike-list-clerk.md`)

The rule was learnt on one forerunner class (74 records of on-horse activities), and two counts serve one class. Venue hire's candidates are several: weddings, functions, chapel hire, party packages, each possibly this offering under another name. "Both counts" either becomes every combination of them or turns quietly into a choice by the clerk. The rule should be one table, each product with its own count, so the strike list and the cards can include or leave out each one on the record.

### 3.4 The operator ladder on associations' domains (`operator-index-clerk.md`)

"failing that, the registrable domain the unit's own pages sit on, where two or more units share it". The platform exclusion covers "a marketplace, directory, register or booking platform". It does not cover a federation or association that hosts its member centres' pages. Disability riding centres often sit under a state association's site while each sets its own terms. On the ladder as written, the equine corpus could lose dozens of operators to one "body", and every operator-basis count would shrink with them. Kayak hire's directory case (104 units) was the same fault, and it was caught only because the template named directories.

### 3.5 The narrow reading's cost is described in one trade's terms (`orchestrator-steps.md` 12c)

The narrow reading, a sentence near the offering's own word, is priced by kayak's destination venues. On venue hire the offering's word ("hire", "venue", "function", "space") is on nearly every sentence of a venue's site, so the near-offering flag is near-universal and the choice buys nothing. On the equine programme it is worse than kayak. Riding schools print the craft word everywhere, but they name the funded buyer on a separate funding page that need not carry it. So the narrow reading hides exactly the buyer the run is about. The step should ask the orchestrator to measure, on the recall note, how many candidate rows of each buyer class the flag keeps, before choosing.

### 3.6 Trade nouns left in

In `capability-clerk.md`, "(a craft count, a measured capacity)", and in `orchestrator-steps.md` step 5, "52 units that name the craft". "Craft" is kayak's word for a boat. On venue hire it is noise, or it reads as handicraft. Use "an equipment count" and "name the offering". The other trade nouns ("helmet", "car park", "young riders", "state voucher", "parking, camp, guides, free") are labelled as one run's case and do no harm.

### 3.7 Payer and participant (`codebook-amendment-author.md`)

"whom each operator sells to ... a second field for the kind of buyer". In every run so far the buyer paid and either took part or booked for a group. For disability-funded participants, the one who pays (a scheme, a plan manager), the one who books (a relative, a support coordinator) and the one who takes part are three people. A whom-sold-to variable that does not say which of them it codes will code all three as the same thing. A mend must avoid the launch check's buyer nouns ("parent", "family" and "child" fail it): "a funding scheme, an employer, a relative" passes.

### 3.8 Search step 4 against the fence (`search-demand-clerk.md`)

The method's step 4 checks "the bare candidate name". On venue hire that name is the venue's product name, which the template bars from every finding outside the marked section. So the collision verdict is either a finding that breaks the rule or a finding that lives in the section the orchestrator cuts. On the equine programme there is no candidate name at all. The template should say that step 4's verdict sits in the marked section, and that where no name exists, step 4 is a named gap.

## 4. A rule handed to several clerks as a sentence

1. **Which units hold page text.** The count is made separately in five places: the orchestrator's pre-step ("measure by script which profiles have a stored pages folder ... counting in and beneath each folder"), `records-clerk.md` item 5 ("counted as units whose folder holds text files (in the folder or beneath it)"), `amendment-record-form-clerk.md` ("The list of units with page text counts text files in and beneath each unit's folder"), `candidate-sentence-clerk.md` (reconcile before searching) and `records-refresh-clerk.md` (reconcile after). Batch three's own lesson is a case of this fault: the candidate clerk counted 190 against 189. What counts as a text file, and whether derived text counts, is decided anew each time. Make it one file, `{{CORPUS}}/shards/units-with-text.txt`, cut by the orchestrator's pre-step, and have every later clerk read it and report a disagreement instead of writing its own list.
2. **The adjudicators' pairings.** `candidate-adjudicator.md` says "Build the list of pairings by script from your shard before you judge anything ... one pairing is a unit with a variable and a class or value that appears among its matches." Two readers of one shard, blind of each other, each write their own script from that sentence. Agreement "by pairing" (12e) then joins two lists that were never shown to be the same list. Cut `<shard>-pairings.tsv` once, beside the shard, as `header-adjudication.tsv` already is.
3. **The note line a script gathers.** `coder-brief-block.md` asks for "one line opening with the amendment's section number" and, for validity, the sentence "after the label `validity:`". `candidate-adjudicator.md`, which reads that block first, asks for "`rule point:`, the amendment's section number and the question". That is three grammars for one gathering script (steps 10 and 12e), and it leaves open whether the line opens with the number or with the label.
4. **One operator's units kept together.** `candidate-sentence-clerk.md` reports "the units that share pages or quoted text with another, so the readers' shards can keep one operator's units together", and step 13 says `operators.tsv` "is the run's one grouping from here on" and "serves the reading shards of step 12c". Two groupings serve one purpose, which is the batch-two fault (three groupings in a day). The candidate clerk's pairs should be evidence handed to the operator-index clerk, not a grouping.
5. **Ranges by operator.** `strike-list-clerk.md` asks the clerk to give ranges "by unit and by operator from `operators.tsv`", and step 12e has the merge script compute the same. The strike-list clerk should read the merge's output, not re-make it.

## 5. Contradictions

### 5.1 The strike list's place: the templates against the method

`orchestrator-steps.md` puts the strike list at step 15, the last Survey step, after the coding. `sage-S-survey.md` step 1: "After the owner has returned the day-one strike list and before any frame is drawn". `sage-A-adjudicate.md`: "Day one produces a strike list". `sage-methodology.md`'s artefact table: "Day-one strike list | A (orchestrator), returned by the owner". `forms/strike-list.md`: "Written by the orchestrator from the owner's words and four market-side pulls; returned to the owner the same day."

These runs re-make studies over corpora already drawn, and building the list from the coded buyer variable may be the right adaptation. But no template or step says that it is an adaptation, or what it costs: the corpus was drawn before any buyer was struck, and `sage-A-adjudicate.md` gives the reason the order exists ("a codebook frozen around that population cannot be un-frozen by a later ruling"). The owner should rule it once for re-runs, and `orchestrator-steps.md` should say so in a sentence.

### 5.2 The merge absorbs what the strike-list clerk is told to report

`orchestrator-steps.md` 12e: "State the merge rule for classes and kinds (a class stated where any of its kinds is)". `strike-list-clerk.md`: "An inconsistency between a class and its kinds, either way (a class stated while its kind reads not present, a kind stated under a class coded 0), is a coding fault to report and not a count to publish." Batch three's lesson says the merge's maximum rule "absorbed" three readers' faults. A merge run to this rule sets the class to 1 and erases the evidence before the strike-list clerk reads the merged table. The adjudicator's own pre-return check lowers the rate but does not close the gap. The merge should list each such row, by reader, before it applies the rule.

### 5.3 `records-clerk.md` against `lead-deriving-clerk.md` and the method, on when the buyer is ruled

This is 2.4, quoted there.

### 5.4 `amendment-coder-from-pages.md` against `orchestrator-steps.md` step 9, on who governs capture

The coder's brief: "Where the amendment's own capture rule bars such pages **and the prompt does not say otherwise**, code nothing from them". Step 9: "whether later-fetched pages may be coded under this amendment, **in agreement with** the amendment's own capture rule". `sage-S-survey.md`: "Changing a frozen rule takes a version, a date, and re-coding of everything coded." The brief lets a prompt override the frozen amendment, and the orchestrator step says the prompt may not. Batch three's nine barred units came from a brief that governed "in fact".

### 5.5 `candidate-adjudicator.md` against `candidate-sentence-clerk.md` and step 12d, on the key column

The adjudicator's header is literal: "`profile_file`; `variable`; `class_or_value`; ...". The candidate clerk writes "the unit's key as the coded table spells it" under "names your script chooses". Step 12d says the adjudicator is filled "its column names filled from the candidate table's header", but the template holds no placeholder to fill. Where the run's key column is not `profile_file`, the reader writes one name and the merge reads another.

### 5.6 `whom-sold-to-coder.md` against `amendment-record-form-clerk.md`, on the phrases column

The record-form clerk: "Where every variable already carries its own words field, leave out the shared phrases column". The coder: "The phrases column holds the verbatim words behind each coded value". Where the header has no such column, the coder is told to fill a column that does not exist.

### 5.7 Four clerks each told they alone see the closed files

`records-clerk.md`: "You are the one clerk in this run to whom nothing is closed". `records-refresh-clerk.md`: "Nothing is closed to you". `capability-clerk.md`: "You are the one clerk who opens them". `priors-clerk.md` (Adjudicate): "You are the one clerk who opens the closed files". They behave well enough, but each is told that no other clerk can see what it sees, and none is told that its own output is held to the fence. The refresh clerk writes the market-side cut and the shape note, which go to clerks the venue is closed to. A sentence would fix it: "the files you write onto the open list are held to the fence as the records clerk's were".

### 5.8 `codebook-amendment-author.md` against itself

The fourth paragraph asks for "a second field for the kind of buyer an operator names inside its **main class**". The next paragraph offers the form both batch-three runs reached, which "barred a coder from ranking a main class". The author is told to build a field on a notion the next paragraph advises it to drop.

### 5.9 `search-demand-clerk.md` and step 3 against the method's four seed sources

The template: "Step 1 draws seeds from four sources, two of which (the enquiry record's phrases and the comparables' own self-descriptions) sit in files a fence may close to you." The method's four sources are the comparables' words, the buyer's words, "the operator's internal vocabulary", and the database. The table the riding clerk could not build ("the operator's words against buyers' words") needs the third, which the template and step 3 never name as a source to open. Opening the two named sources, as the lesson prescribes, still leaves that table unbuildable.

### 5.10 `strike-list-clerk.md`: the contradictions list goes to one side

"Contradictions you meet between records (a sales record against its own tables, two findings on one count) go into a named list at the foot of the operator file, which no market-only clerk is handed". The lesson said the market file. The template's change is right for contradictions that touch the operator's record. But "two findings on one count" is a market contradiction, and putting it in the operator file hides it from the market-only clerks who will cite those findings.

## Was one of the lessons the wrong lesson?

**The codebook author sent to the venue-situation note (entry 10).** The riding author "opened neither the methodology nor the venue-situation note", and the lesson made the read order name both so the closure cannot cover them. For the methodology that is plainly right. For the venue-situation note, the lesson may have run the wrong way. The author is meant to draft "blind", and the note is the one file in its read list that describes this venue. The evidence that would show it: set the two batch-three amendments side by side on the four shape variables. If kayak's, drafted with the note, has value lists that mirror the venue's own shape note more closely than the riding one's, drafted without it (a value that exists only because the venue's shape needs it, or a split fine exactly where the venue sits and coarse elsewhere), the note anchored the author, and the author should be closed to it and given the four dimensions from `sage-S-survey.md` alone. If the two are alike, the lesson is harmless.

**"Not put to the owner" as the Returned line (entry 20).** The lesson fixed the tool's reading of the line (open with a date) and not the question behind it (was the list put to the owner?). The evidence: the owner's reading of the two batch-three strike lists now that he can see them. If he strikes no row, the default cost nothing. If he strikes a row that a batch-three card built on, or would have narrowed a buyer the corpus coding was sized for, the lesson should have been to ask him, not to make the tool accept his absence.
