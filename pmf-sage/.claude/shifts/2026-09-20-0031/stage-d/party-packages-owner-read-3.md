# Owner's cold read — party packages decision review, front section

Read once, in order, from the top down to the heading "Open cards." Nothing else was opened.

## 1. What I believe I am being asked to decide

Staff want me to rule how our party offering is built and sold — how many packages we offer, who it's for, what's in the fee, how the price works, how the date is booked and protected, and how the party is found and named online. Twenty-four separate questions sit on one sheet, each backed by a count of what other operators do, and I'm told a fixed order to go through them in rather than the table order. Eleven of the twenty-four can't be settled from what's in front of me and are asking me either to accept that the market doesn't decide it, or to send someone to go get more evidence. Alongside all that there are six things in our own paperwork that are wrong regardless of how I rule any of the above, and those need fixing Monday no matter what. There's also a ruling I already made in August about staff credentials that still binds four of these cards.

## 3. Every word or phrase I had to guess at

- **"The priors pass missed it."** (under the staff-credential bound) — I read this as "an earlier round of this same review should have caught this and didn't," but the review never says what "the priors pass" was or who ran it.
- **"the gate"** (order of ruling, the re-measurement paragraph) — used before it's explained. I worked out from the next clause ("22 charge the public to come in, 22 do not") that "the gate" means whether the site charges admission to get in the door, but the term itself is never defined before it's used.
- **"Southlands charges from 26 September"** — no idea who or what Southlands is. I guessed it's one of the 45 surveyed operators whose free entry is ending, since that's the only thing that sentence could be about, but the name is dropped in with no introduction.
- **"under a floor fixed before any count was seen"** — I read "floor" as a rule for which pages count, agreed before anyone looked at the results, to stop the count being shaped to fit an answer. Not spelled out.
- **"The measured-in lines mark a measured population as differing from this venue's shape 97 times."** — I could not work out what is being counted 97 times: individual card disagreements, or something finer inside them. I've left this one as a genuine unresolved guess rather than a confident reading.
- **The citation codes (F#, E#, O#, M#, B#, SD-#)** scattered through the "wrong today" list and elsewhere — never keyed anywhere in the front. I guessed F = a market finding, E = one of our own capability records, O = one of our own operational/booking records, on pattern alone, not because the page says so.
- **"thin, labelled thin"** (the rounds paragraph) — I took this as shorthand for "a weak margin that the report itself flags as weak," inferred from context rather than stated outright.

1. Put a short key at the top translating the citation codes (F/E/O/M/B/SD) into plain words — right now every "wrong today" item and half the withheld rows send me to guess what kind of source is backing the claim.
2. Turn "The order of ruling" into a numbered checklist of card numbers with one line each, instead of a paragraph of cross-references (Joint lines, the cheap-half note, the withheld-row costs) — I had to reread it twice to extract the actual sequence I'm meant to follow.
3. For the eleven withheld cards, put the one-sentence reason it can't be ruled at the very front of the cell, before the count breakdown — right now I have to read the full count first and only then find out, buried at the end, why it doesn't decide anything.
