# Venue hire: two decision pages, reviewed for the owner

## The blunt reading first

Page B is the better page. It stands on a much truer record of the venue's own buyers. Page A thought bare hire had "one booking ever" against 95 bundles, and that no one had asked for the Pavilion or the Deck. Page B found 93 live invoices to 68 household contacts for Barn and Red Table hire (October 2023 to December 2025), and the Pavilion and Deck asks in sales record §7.3. Page B also sets aside the clubs, pubs and breweries that live off their own bar, and that removes the two biggest errors on page A: the minimum spend (card 6) and the in-house catering rule (card 10). Both were club habits that page A read as the market.

Page B has overcorrected. It carries 8 cards and withholds 16. In my reading, 7 of those 16 hold evidence that carries one option and decline to say so. The mechanism is the same each time. The set's rule that silence is never an option is right as a counting rule, but it has been applied as a reason to withhold. Beside it, the venue's own buyers who asked for something, were refused, or walked away (sales record §5b, §5c, §6) are left out of the cards they bear on. So page B mostly avoids the owner's first two worries and fails the third worry on its second half: it discounts what the venue's buyers did for want of an option.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

A match with the held value is never held against a recommendation outright. Page B says "Keeping and leaving were asked for the same proof", and cards 4, 13a, 14 and 22 keep on the market alone without apology. The bar is not level, though, in two places. It leans in both directions.

The first is against keeping. Card 12a, a keep, was withheld pending a count the venue's records hold: "the split of family bookings between the 13:00 and the 14:00 finish". Card 7, a leave, carried although its own reversing observation is the same kind of records count: "the bookings of that window are not counted on this card". Card 7 also carried on a catchment lead of 3 to 1, which the set's own rule calls a tie. So a departure passed without the proof a match was asked for. Card 5, a keep, went to withheld on the silence rule even though the printable alternative lost 2 to 29.

The second is toward matches, in presentation. On the ruling sheet every keep is labelled "confirm", whatever its strength. Card 0 is the weakest line on the page (market reversed, market-only reading differs) and it opens the order of ruling as "confirm". The label should follow the line's strength, not its agreement. Card 17 runs the other way: both options it compares leave the held 25-year floor, yet the sheet says "neither kept nor left" and the priors pass calls the move "toward what the venue holds". That hides a departure the card's evidence carries.

## What the owner has to read

Page A runs to 12,793 words over 18 cards and a gap card. It has no summary table and no order of ruling. The chips (inherited, anchored, derived) tell him where to spend time, but not which cards only confirm what he holds. He has to read every card to rule.

Page B runs to 27,408 words over 24 cards. The ruling sheet is 3,321 words and the order of ruling 1,593, so about 4,900 words before any card. The "your part" column does let him see at a glance which cards ask for what: 7 confirm, 1 rule, 16 commission or rule. That is a real gain over page A. The margin cells, though, are paragraphs of cell names (`r1`, `r2c`, "outside the part"), which he cannot parse without the "Words on the sheet" glossary. The order of ruling mixes three things: launch blockers, faults that are wrong today, and the dependency order.

Could he rule from the sheet and the order alone? For the 8 carried cards, yes, and he would open card 7. For the 16 withheld cards, he cannot rule the parameter. He can only choose which observations to commission, and the "Withheld, by what the observation costs" paragraph serves that choice well. At one sitting, he would come away with 8 lines and a shopping list.

## What page A carried that page B does not

Three parameters page A put to cards are gone from page B, plus one observation page B holds but does not use.

- **Published response promise (A card 16).** Page B lists it as a gap. Leaving it out as a *recommendation* was right, since page A had no buyer evidence. Leaving it out as a *card* was wrong, because page B's own record now holds buyer evidence: an enquirer who "will find another venue" after 20 days without a reply, "two follow-ups on unanswered emails", a returning 60th customer with "no reply for 4 days", and party bookers who "said booking was not obvious".
- **The wedding-substitution controls (A cards 7, 13, 14 as one set).** Page B dropped the wedding conflict, the no-weddings line, the wet-to-dry refusal and the 7-day change cutoff. Its card 18 asks only whether Saturday is offered, which nobody disputes. This was wrong. Sales record §6 holds four hires displaced by a wedding: two households, the Pavilion bookers, and an employer's party of about 300 accepted and then cancelled. Those are the venue's own buyers lost for want of a rule, and the owner's held rule ("Saturday only if no wedding booked") is exactly the parameter they bear on.
- **Hire page and navigation label (A card 15).** This is folded into card 3 only as the question of which site. Leaving it out is defensible, because it is website design governed by the site's own change triage, not a product parameter.
- **Observations A used that B holds but leaves off the card they bear on.** The 5pm 40th case study and the full run of evening asks are missing from card 12b. The 21-week booking against the held 8-week window is missing from card 19a, where it is reduced to "up to 183 days". The complaint of poor pre-event communication appears nowhere. All three omissions were wrong.
- **Where page B was right to drop page A's material.** It dropped the "95 bundles against one bare hire" framing, which was false. It dropped the club-sourced minimum spend and in-house rule. It moved the Barn 60 against 80 conflict to "the Estate Manager measures both", which is correct: capacity is a fact to measure, not a card.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

Too much. Of 16 withheld cards, 9 are sound and 7 evasive (5, 9, 11, 12b, 15, 17, 19a). Of the 8 that recommend, one should have withheld: card 7, whose reversing count sits in the venue's own records and whose own buyer prediction favours "both".

Some cards count what sellers print where a rule outside the market settles or bounds the parameter:

- **Card 9** bands rival room sizes. A certified occupancy for the Barn and Pavilion bounds the ceiling, and the market cannot set it.
- **Card 17** counts sellers' age rules without asking where the held 25 came from: an insurer's condition, a liquor or security condition, or a preference. Page A named that check. Page B's own launch section records that public liability "excludes a licensed bar and event planning (E67, E68)", so the insurer's terms are live on this offering.
- **Card 15** carries the food business licence only as a cost line. Whether hirers' own food is allowed under the licence conditions is a regulatory question, not a question for the market.
- **Card 16** does it right: it reads the Liquor Act first and counts sellers only inside it.

## Rules for the corrections round

These rules come from the findings above. None of them is a verdict on a single card.

1. **Silence counts as no option, but not as a reason to withhold.** Where one option is the unmarked default and the other is printable, and some sellers do print it, a withheld line must say whether the print could have shown the printable option winning. If it could, and it did not, the evidence carries the default.
2. **Count what buyers did for want of an option.** Every ask, refusal, walk-away, complaint and wedding displacement in the sales record (§5b, §5c, §6) is placed on each card it bears on, as a leg of its own. Sales at the only terms on offer stay what they are: acceptance.
3. **Apply the dominance test before withholding.** Where one option refuses no buyer observed and the other refuses some, the card carries the first, with its capability cost lines stated for the owner. Otherwise it says why the costs outweigh the lost buyers.
4. **The held value is always on the table.** Either it is one of the two options compared, or the card and the sheet say "every option compared leaves the held value". A sheet row never reads "neither kept nor left" when both options leave it.
5. **Keeping and leaving get the same proof.** If a keep withholds pending a count the venue's records hold, a leave awaiting an equivalent count withholds too. A lead the set calls a tie in the catchment does not carry a departure through another cell.
6. **Choose the population per parameter, before counting.** Decide once, in the card set, whether the bar-and-kitchen part is set aside for each parameter, with a reason. Do not decide it card by card after the counts are in.
7. **One option, one parameter.** An option joining two parameters, such as "no floor and no milestone rule", is split, and each half is carried or withheld on its own.
8. **Regulation first.** Where a licence, a regulation, the insurer's conditions or a building certification settles or bounds a parameter, the card reads that first and counts sellers only within the bound.
9. **Label a row by its strength, not its agreement.** "Confirm" is kept for a line with at least two legs agreeing and the market-only reading agreeing. Anything else is "rule".
10. **Fix "own buyers" once for the whole set.** Whether the children's packages count as this offering's buyers is decided in the card set and applied everywhere; today card 0 counts them and card 17 excludes them.
11. **Merge each pair of cards that answers one question.** The review names five: cards 7 and 15, 7 and 14, 6 and 10, 10 and 12a, 10 and 11. The corrections round merges them, or marks one card of each pair as carrying the question.
12. **Put the wedding conflict on a card of its own.** Restore it as a parameter, using the displacements in the sales record.

## The three changes that would most improve page B

1. **Apply rules 2 and 3 across the withheld set.** On my reading, cards 9, 12b, 15, 17 and 19a would then carry lines, each with its cost lines, and the owner would rule instead of shopping.
2. **Add a card on the wedding conflict, and one on the reply to an enquiry.** Both have buyer evidence in the record and appear on no card.
3. **Cut the ruling sheet to one plain-words line per card** (about 40 words each, under 1,000 in all): the question, the line, the one fact it stands on, and what it keeps or leaves. Move the cell arithmetic onto the cards, and relabel "confirm" by rule 9.

## Verdict

Page B is an improvement on page A for this owner. It corrects page A's picture of his own buyers, and it stops importing the clubs' terms. But it hands him 16 withheld cards where about 9 should be, and on each of the other 7 its own evidence already carries a line. About 75 per cent sure.
