# Riding academy: two decision pages, read for the owner

The offering is a children's riding academy the venue may run. A forerunner, "Equestrian Me", was on sale for three months in 2025. The owner has ruled nothing in this run: every Ruled line on both pages is blank. So "held" below means the terms once on sale (the fee schedule, the booking system), a draft, a standing procedure (G65), or a proposal. It never means a ruling.

I have tested both pages against the owner's three worries. The first is echo: predicting the market from meeting notes and the owner's own thinking. The second is contrarian: assuming that if the owner believes A, A must be wrong. The third is sales-as-preference: reading sales made at the only terms on offer as evidence of what buyers want. Where a card's count mattered, I checked it against the run folder: `capability-note.md`, `capability-extract.md`, `sales-record.md`, `source-meetings.md`, the third-derivation files and the card files.

Column 3 codes what the recommendation chiefly rests on: M is market behaviour, B is this venue's buyers, D is the venue's documents, plans or present arrangement, and N is nothing stated. Where a capacity or daylight fact carries the choice, I code it D, since it is the venue's present arrangement.

## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Page A

**Echo.**

- Card 18: "worked at about ten schools a week from the profiled excursion roster". This is the owner's cadence, adopted although the card's own evidence finds "no submission in a full keyword sweep is a genuine lesson enquiry".
- Card 12: "\$95, \$135 and \$175 are the venue's own listed numbers, kept deliberately".
- Card 2: the positioning is the segment matrix's line, carried by "The venue's own composition has no in-catchment rival". That is a gap in supply, which cannot show demand. Card 8's "two riders", resting on the proposing contractor's opinion, is a close fourth.

**Contrarian.**

- Card 1 reverses the owner's hinterland buyer on bookings of a morning visitor product, and states the flaw itself: "the coastal skew may be the product's shape rather than the market's".
- Card 22: "The Director should rule this half knowing the Australian corpus leans the other way".
- The page-level driver is the "radical rate" section: "Zero movement would have been backfill; eight breaks out of twenty-six is the rate". A departure counts as a measure of success, which is exactly the owner's second worry built into the page. Only two cards fail on it, but the incentive is there.

**Sales-as-preference.**

- Card 1: "152 of 164 genuine sessions start between 9am and 1pm ... so the family is on site in the morning". Those were the hours on sale.
- Card 12: "the record predicts only that \$175 is payable by a visitor", used to hold the visitor list.
- Card 0: the single lesson is kept because of "170 lesson bookings since April 2025". Cards 16 and 20 repeat the move in their Buyer lines: "a 9am to 3pm camp day sits inside the window this venue's own buyers already book".

### Page B

**Echo.** One clear case and one borderline, no third.

- Card 10's riding-time half: "No evidence on this card puts a stated riding time ahead, so the half is not withheld". Silence defaults to the held posture, a pass card 3 refused.
- Card 23 is marked "confirm", keeping the clerk draft's ten. The market-only reading ties ten and five, and the partition rule chosen keeps five off the runner-up line.

**Contrarian.**

- Card 18: "horse care, no games", with "Card 18 sets aside your games proposal", on a count of what pages print.
- Card 14's frame puts two sizes, both away from the owner's two or three. Yet "on the smallest cap ... three or four ... leads five or six".
- Card 29 is borderline. It leaves the terms on sale although the market-only reading's "own split ... runs the other way from this card's line". The card's fuller instrument largely answers that.

**Sales-as-preference.** None found. Every card reads the forerunner's record in the same disciplined form, for example card 22: "a count of what buyers took inside this option and not a count against either other option"; card 9: "a Saturday session was never offered under that name".

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

Mostly yes. The proof is symmetric in design and largely in practice. Every card with a held value took a market-only third derivation "regardless of which way the recommendation landed". Two cards recommend against a differing third derivation, and one keeps (card 23) while the other leaves (card 29), so neither direction got a softer test. The page itself flags that "Every second-round move that stood ran away from what you hold". I checked those moves. Card 3 moved from keep to withheld on the silence test, and card 29 from withheld to leave on a fuller instrument. Each rests on a rule stated on its card.

I found no case where a match with the owner is held against a recommendation.

There are two small asymmetries, in opposite directions:

- **Towards the held value.** Card 10's riding-time half keeps the held silence under a test that withheld card 3.
- **Against the owner.** The partition rules on card 14 (largest cap) and card 18 (the "no games" label) move his proposals out of the two-way contest, and nothing proves they belong out. On card 14 the card's own count says the smallest-cap reading would put his side nearer the lead.

These are framing faults rather than counts, and they are the place his second worry still bites on page B.

## What the owner has to read

**Page A** runs to 43,001 words on 26 cards. The one-page summary table is about 740 words and gives each card's recommendation, chip and "Ready" or "Blocked". The chips (Owner-stated, Anchored, Radical) show where his view is in play, but no column says what is asked of him. Twenty-one cards read "Ready" alike, and the summary, three essays and radical-rate section add about 4,700 words before card 0. He cannot see at a glance which cards need judgment. Nothing distinguishes a confirmation from a contested call except card 9's two drafts.

**Page B** runs to 34,971 words on 28 cards. The ruling sheet is 2,543 words and the order of ruling 1,165, about 3,700 together. The "your part" column sorts the cards:

- **Confirm (10 cards):** 2, 5, 9, 10, 13, 16, 18, 23, 28 and 38.
- **Rule (4 cards):** 0, 15, 22 and 29.
- **Commission or rule (14 cards):** the rest.

The order of ruling carries the Monday list of things wrong whichever way he rules, the dependency order and the proposals set aside. An owner could rule from those two sections alone. He would need to open only three cards where a row should raise a doubt the sheet does not voice:

- **Card 18**, where "confirm" silently sets aside his games proposal. That is said only in the order's prose.
- **Card 23**, marked "confirm" with the market-only reading "differs".
- **Card 28**, a band that holds on one edge.

That is about 3,700 + 771 + 1,034 + 1,203 = roughly 6,700 words, against page A's 43,000 for a full read.

The margin column is the weak point. Rows such as "AU cell 11–16 against 9–10 of 55 units, 11–16 against 8–9 operators" are dense even with the glossary, and a one-line plain reason per row would halve his effort.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

About right, and slightly too much. Of the 14 withheld cards, 11 are sound and 3 are evasive:

- **Card 4:** beginners taken, with no gate at entry anywhere.
- **Card 6:** "lessons" in the title, with the brand phrase's own search volume already held in the run.
- **Card 31:** accepting the voucher, where both outcomes of the named observation point the same way.

Of the 14 cards that recommend, two should have withheld:

- **Card 23:** ten against five, where the market-only reading ties.
- **Card 28:** the band holds only at the \$70 edge. Its median of \$88 (middle half \$70 to \$105) is the honest statement.

Two cards should have withheld one half: card 10's riding time, and card 18's "no games".

## Three changes that would most improve page B in one sitting

1. **Put the owner's own proposal, or the live rival draft, on each card as a named option,** and choose partition rules that do not define it out. On card 14 the runner-up should be three or four (the smallest cap); on card 18, horse care with games as teaching method; on card 23, five lessons against ten. Correct the "your part" column to "rule" wherever the market-only reading differs (card 23) or the card sets aside a proposal of his (cards 14 and 18). This removes the page's last contrarian risk.
2. **Release the three evasive withholds and apply the silence test once.** Card 4 should recommend beginners taken. Card 6 should recommend "lessons" in the page title, having read the held Trends figures for the brand phrase. Card 31 should recommend registering, since no answer to its observation turns it. Card 10's riding-time half should be withheld as card 3 was, and card 28 stated as the \$88 median and its range rather than a band with a chosen edge.
3. **Bring back the venue's few observations made at other terms, as dated, meeting-sourced items to verify rather than as evidence.** These are:
    - roughly 1,000 half-price vouchers yielding four bookings, and the free-first-lesson "Mini and Me" of April 2026, both bearing on card 24;
    - the award-claim test that moved no clicks, bearing on card 15;
    - the adult relaunch buyer at \$120 for five lessons, bearing on cards 1 and 28.

    Page B's sales record excludes them because they come from meetings, not systems. But they are the only observations in the run that could show buyers acting at terms other than those on sale, which is the very gap the third worry names. Each belongs on its card as an observation to confirm.

## Verdict

Page B is an improvement on page A for this owner. It all but removes sales-as-preference and echo, the faults page A commits most (5 echo and 3 sales-as-preference cases against B's one half-card). It turns contrarian risk into a visible, fixable framing fault on two cards. And it lets him rule from about 3,700 words plus three cards. It does this at the cost of giving him no price, ladder, capacity or instructor model to open a product with, which page A did. I am fairly sure of this judgment, at about 75 per cent.
