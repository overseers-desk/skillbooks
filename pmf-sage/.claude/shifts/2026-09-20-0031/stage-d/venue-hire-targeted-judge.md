## Is the one-way direction the evidence or the clerks?

My judgement: about half is evidence, and the rest comes from how the rules are built rather than from clerks favouring the owner.

The strongest reason for: the held values after ranking are what buyers actually bought, not what the owner intended. On five of the seven toward-moves (12a, 12b, 15, 17, 19a), the round departs from the owner's written position in the draft or the policy: a four-hour slot, evening only, "not a catering service", a minimum age of 25, an eight-week window. It sides with sold practice and buyers' asks. On the owner's first worry, the round chose reality over intentions. On the second, it did not reject a held value because the owner holds it. Cards 12a, 12b and 15 carry on bookings and asks that were real.

The strongest reason against: three structural devices push the same way, and none of them is evidence.

- J3's first clause carries an absence with no lead (cards 5 and 11, margin -1, labelled "market"), but on card 16, where the absence would leave the held value, the card required a lead that survives one unit. Applied there on the same standard, card 16 carries "either" and the tally becomes seven toward and two away.
- J8 splits put the stated counter-evidence into unruled halves (card 5 closed dates; card 17 milestones, 7 to 0).
- Three held values were re-read in the same entry that moved their line, one of them settling a conflict the page had parked for a director.

The one away-move (13a) comes from a comparison group that fits its parameter worst. The round is therefore not a clerk echo, but its rules lean toward the held value wherever the held value is an absence or the widest option, and at this venue it usually is.

## The rules that moved lines

J1 (silence is no reason to withhold) is sound, and it is the mirror of round 2's silence rule, which withheld cards 5 and 17. Its risk is that it hands every silent card to J3. It was put on one base to every card.

J2 (asks, refusals, walk-aways and displacements count) is sound. It risks counting sales of a product's standard as "refusals" of a narrower option. The four 12a sessions pass this test; the "households that bought café platters" on card 15 are acceptances. It was applied on one side on card 19a (displacements not counted) and not at all on card 17's booker reading.

J3, first clause (recommend the absence of a term nothing supports), is sound as parsimony for restrictions the buyer meets. In a corpus where pages rarely print terms, and where the venue's sold record is mostly absences, it brings silence back as evidence for the absence. Its "support" test looks at sellers' print, buyers' asks and the law, but never at the venue's own record of the harm a term prevents (displacements, unpaid balances, damage). It was not put on one base: cards 5 and 11 were carried at -1, while card 16 was held to a lead.

J3, second clause (the option refusing no observed buyer wins), is sound on the demand side and was applied alike through the one-unit test (cards 9, 7 and 16 tie; 12a, 12b and 15 carry). By construction it always picks the widest option and ignores cost, so the ruling sheet must show what the wider option commits the venue to.

J6 (the part set aside is decided once per parameter) is sound for consistency. It was decided by class ("every term of the sale"), not by the driver of each parameter. On card 11 it removes every seller that carries the term. On card 13a it removes the sellers whose shape the venue shares and leaves one operator on each side. It was put to cards 5, 11, 13a and 13b alike.

J7 (the children's-package buyers are another offering) is sound. It does not say whether a children's-birthday enquiry that names no package counts as this offering's buyer, yet cards 0, 12a and 17 count those enquirers.

J8 (one parameter per option) is sound. Its risk is the one seen here: the half split off is left unruled while the other half carries. It was applied to cards 3, 5 and 17.

The ranking of held values is not a judges' rule but decided 3 of the 8 Held stamps. It is sound as written. It has no clause for a written standard dated after the sales (the SOT row dated 23 September), and it should never settle a conflict parked for a director.

## Three changes before these rules are used on another run

1. Make J3's first clause symmetric and label it honestly. The same standard for carrying an absence (no lead required, or a lead required) applies whether the held value is the absence or the term. A line it carries says "rests on: the absence rule", never "market", and the ruling sheet marks it as a default. "Support for adding a term" includes the venue's own record of the harm the term prevents and any harm the policy names.
2. A half split off under J8 is ruled in the same round on the same rules. Where the held value exists for the split-off half, the carried half is stamped conditional on it.
3. A held value is re-read only in an entry of its own, made before the round counts its moves. Both readings go on the ruling sheet, the move tally is given on both, and a conflict the page has parked for a director is never settled by the ranking.
