# The brief templates, read for the owner before another run fills them

Judged: the twenty-four files under `.claude/shifts/2026-09-19-1042/stage-d/briefs/`, against `batch-two-lessons-by-clerk.md` and `pair-lessons-by-clerk.md` beside them, and against `sage-methodology.md`, `sage-S-survey.md`, `sage-A-adjudicate.md`, `INVARIANTS.md`, the five files under `forms/` and the three scripts under `tools/`. The two offerings ahead have never been on sale, so every sentence that assumes a sale is treated as a sentence that will misfire, not as one that will simply idle.

The set is, on the whole, in better order than the owner's two worries would predict. The anti-tilt machinery is real and it is symmetrical in the places that matter most: `standing-brief-block.md` closes with "No brief in this run asks you to find the venue right, and none asks you to find it wrong"; `priors-clerk.md` says "a match is not a fault in the card any more than a departure is a merit"; `corrections-clerk.md` puts the recommending line to the test "alike whether the recommendation keeps what the venue holds or leaves it: the same instrument test, the same one-operator test, the same reading of silence". What follows is what sits beside those lines.

---

## 1. Tilt

### 1.1 Toward what the venue holds

**`corrections-clerk.md`.** "Where the priors note shows the run's earlier card gating this parameter on a named fact, say whether the fact is now in hand, and carry the gate where it is not."

This is the one clear pull toward the held value in the set, and it is in the template with the power to rewrite a recommending line. An earlier card of this run is, by `sage-A-adjudicate.md`, one prior among several — `priors-clerk.md` says so itself: "what the run's earlier card on the parameter recommended or gated (a separate column, since an earlier card is one held value among several)". A prior neither supports a value nor marks against it; but a *gate* carried forward is a prior with veto power. The corrections clerk is instructed to keep an earlier clerk's condition alive over a later clerk's evidence.

**`strike-list-clerk.md`.** "The owner asked that nothing be put to him before the finished page, so the Returned line reads the date you return it and the word unmarked, which under the method means every row is in."

A remark the owner made during earlier runs is turned into this run's default, by a clerk, on the form that is the owner's first gate. See §2.1: this is also the sharpest hardening in the set.

### 1.2 Away from what the venue holds

**`corrections-clerk.md`.** "What a line rests on is tested by whether the venue's own record could have shown the other option winning; where only one shape has ever been on sale, sales show acceptance and not preference, and the line rests on the market."

Fair as written, and faithful to `sage-A-adjudicate.md` ("a sale taken at the only terms on offer shows that buyers accept those terms, not that they prefer them"). Two faults all the same. First, the test is asymmetric in effect: it bites only lines resting on the venue's own record, which are overwhelmingly the lines that keep what the venue holds, and there is no paired sentence asking whether the *market* instrument could have seen the venue's option winning — though `forms/card.md` demands exactly that of every line ("the instrument, which must have been able to see both options it ranks"). Second, on an offering never sold, "only one shape has ever been on sale" is vacuous, and a clerk reaching for something to apply will apply it to the enquiry record instead, discounting the only behavioural evidence the venue has.

**`integrator.md`.** "Open with whatever the records show to be wrong today whichever way he rules (a published claim the signed terms contradict, a published capacity the stock cannot seat)."

The instruction is right; both worked examples are the venue in error, and they sit at the top of the only page the owner reads. The register of the whole front is set by its first paragraph. A third example of the other kind (a record that is correct and that a card wrongly assumed stale) costs six words and removes the lean.

### 1.3 Fair as written, read unfairly in a hurry

- **`corrections-clerk.md`**: "If your pass moves lines only toward what the owner holds, or only away from it, say so in your report with the count each way and your reading of why, since a judge will be asked whether the direction is the evidence or the clerks." Read at speed this is a scoring rule, not a reporting rule, and the cheapest way to avoid an awkward report is to turn one line back. The method's own words are stricter and safer — `sage-A-adjudicate.md`: "A round that moves lines only toward what the owner holds, or only away from it, is read again before it stands" — *read again*, not balanced. The template should say in terms that a one-sided count is not itself a fault and is never corrected by turning a line.
- **`capability-clerk.md`**: "Where something a new offering would need is absent, the absence is a fact and takes a number too." True, and the clerk is sent to "The two legs", which carries the guard. But the clerk's own file will be a numbered list dominated by absences, and every card cites it. `sage-A-adjudicate.md` names the exact failure: "The flip to watch for is 'we do not yet have what the product needs' turning into 'the product is constrained by not having it'." That sentence belongs in the template, not only in the section it points at.
- **`area-deriving-clerk.md`** and **`lead-deriving-clerk.md`**: "The terms this offering sells under are one of the options where the sales record gives them a figure, with no advantage for being the venue's and no handicap." Balanced, and the clause order puts "no advantage" first. On an offering never sold it is worse than unbalanced — see §3.1.
- **`integrator.md`**: "where a card's recommendation differs from a ruling the owner has already given, listed by card with his ruling and the card's reading in one sentence each, his ruling standing". Correct as method; in a hurry, "his ruling standing" invites a front page that presents every departure as a clerk's error rather than as the comparison `sage-A-adjudicate.md` asks for.

## 2. Hardening

### 2.1 `strike-list-clerk.md` — the day-one gate, closed by a clerk

"The owner asked that nothing be put to him before the finished page, so the Returned line reads the date you return it and the word unmarked, which under the method means every row is in."

`sage-A-adjudicate.md`: "Day one produces a strike list, not a card ... The owner strikes the buyers the frame need not reach; an unstruck buyer is in." `forms/strike-list.md`: "**Returned:** <date the owner sent it back; or the date and 'unmarked' where the owner declined to read it, which under the method means every row is in>". The form's "unmarked" records a declining owner; the template has a clerk write it *before* he is asked. `sage-methodology.md` lists silent scoping among the named failure modes and counters it with "the day-one strike list the owner edits before any frame". Whether this run's strike list goes to the owner is his to rule, on this offering, and a clerk who cannot see his standing words is the last party who should record his answer.

It also contradicts `orchestrator-steps.md` step 14 — see §5.2.

### 2.2 `lead-deriving-clerk.md` — seller's sentences on an unruled buyer

"`3-decisions/seller-sentences.md`: for each buyer the buyer card **recommends**, what the person who takes the booking says to that buyer".

`sage-A-adjudicate.md`: "The owner rules one buyer or several. Only then is the seller's sentence written, one per ruled buyer ... The parameters those sentences contain get cards; a parameter absent from them is design freedom by construction." The template writes the sentences off a recommendation, and then builds the card set from them — and `area-deriving-clerk.md` reads `3-decisions/seller-sentences.md` as an input. The whole card set is therefore scoped by a buyer no one has ruled. Who must rule it: the owner, at the second gate.

### 2.3 Thresholds and shapes one run happened to use

- **`lead-deriving-clerk.md`**: "shared out into three areas of near-equal weight for three clerks". Three areas is the shape of a thirty-card set. Who rules it: the orchestrator, per run, once the card set's size is known — not the template.
- **`orchestrator-steps.md`** step 7: "Where the page-text coding finds **materially more** than the two profile coders part from each other on a variable, that variable is coded from pages over the whole corpus". The decision to re-code a corpus of several hundred operators turns on an undefined word. Who rules it: the study lead, before the reliability read, and it should be a figure in `agreement.py`'s output, not an adverb.
- **`integrator.md`**: "a withheld card costs about a thousand words". A measurement of two earlier fronts, written where a clerk will read it as a budget.
- **`standing-brief-block.md`**: "runs under the method's reading dated 2026-09-19" — a literal date in a template. The lessons themselves require a judge after every change to the words about evidence, which moves the date (`sage-methodology.md`, Versioning: "A change that a finished run's record would fail is what moves the date"). Every future brief will assert a reading it is not running under. Make it `{{READING}}`.
- **`search-demand-clerk.md`**: `{{OP}}/historicrivermill.au/research/seo/2026-09-13-market-map.md`, `.../2026-09-17-competition-and-comparables-by-market.md`, `.../serp-gold-coast`. Dated artefacts and one operator's paths and one city, fixed in a reusable template. `INVARIANTS.md` I5 — "The methodology speaks in kinds ... a run speaks in instances" — is about the method folder, but these templates are the method's in every practical sense: they are filled, not rewritten. Who rules which map a run reads: the orchestrator, at fill time, from a placeholder.

### 2.4 A count that has already drifted

**`corrections-clerk.md`**: "After your per-card pass, one clerk puts **five rules** to the whole set". Six follow: the corpus set aside alike; silence put to every card; trailing a silent count shown and not decisive; what a line rests on; the instrument test on a leg one sighting wide; every figure naming its base. `batch-two-lessons-by-clerk.md` lists five and records the sixth as a separate lesson ("A clerk had to write the rule the method lacks, that trailing a silent count is shown to the owner"). A clerk counting to five drops one, and which one it drops is unpredictable.

## 3. Carry-over: sentences that fit two sold offerings and misfire on one never sold

### 3.1 "the sales record", "the terms this offering sells under"

`records-clerk.md` handles it properly: "Where the offering has never been on sale, the record is the enquiries alone, each count beside the condition it was taken under." So does `strike-list-clerk.md`: "where it is not yet on sale, say what that leaves the record unable to show."

The two deriving templates do not. `area-deriving-clerk.md` and `lead-deriving-clerk.md` both carry, unbranched: "The terms this offering sells under are one of the options where the sales record gives them a figure, with no advantage for being the venue's and no handicap." Where nothing has been sold there are no such terms, and `sage-S-survey.md` is explicit that for such an offering "the capability record and the demand-signal records carry no price, length, name or session shape for it". A clerk holding this sentence and finding no terms in the record has two bad exits: treat present practice or a draft figure as "the terms" (the inheritance failure), or read the sentence as licence to hunt for the venue's intended shape. Both are the first worry, arriving through a sentence written to prevent it.

`lead-deriving-clerk.md` gets it right eight lines earlier — "said to a named real enquirer from the sales record **where one exists**" — which shows the branch was available.

### 3.2 `{{ON_SALE}}` — the one lever, unspecified

`standing-brief-block.md` ends its second paragraph with the bare placeholder `{{ON_SALE}}`, and `orchestrator-steps.md` lists it among ten placeholders to replace with no word about what it must say. This is the single sentence every clerk in the run reads about whether the thing has ever been sold, and no file says what it contains. By the lessons' own rule — a thing several clerks must apply alike is a file or a table — the two alternative sentences should be written out once, in `orchestrator-steps.md`, and copied.

### 3.3 "venue profiles"

Sixteen occurrences across `amendment-record-form-clerk.md`, `amendment-coder-from-pages.md`, `whom-sold-to-coder.md`, `recode-coder.md`, `operator-index-clerk.md`, `area-deriving-clerk.md`, `third-derivation-clerk.md`, `rule-review-clerk.md`, `codebook-amendment-author.md`. In the same templates "the venue" means the commissioning operator: `coder-brief-block.md`, "What an earlier reader thought, **what the venue sells**, and what this study expects to find are none of your business"; `standing-brief-block.md`, "what **this venue's** own buyers did". A coder reads both in the same two minutes: the thing it must not look at, and the folder it must read in full, share a noun. For an offering whose comparables are not venues at all the noun is simply wrong. Use "comparable profiles" or "operator profiles" throughout; "the venue" can keep its meaning.

### 3.4 The earlier trade's nouns

- `integrator.md`: "a published capacity **the stock** cannot seat"; "(the venues within **about an hour's drive**, the list of local rivals, the table of coded venues, one part of the corpus)". Livestock and a catchment radius, in the file that tells a clerk to say a thing by what it is.
- `area-deriving-clerk.md`: "carried a sentence about **one room** as a sentence about another".
- `recode-coder.md`: "which of its **rooms** or listings the coding is about".

Each should be a kind, not an instance: the unit's capacity; the operators in the catchment; one part of the unit.

### 3.5 Literal file names from the earlier runs' variable

`amendment-whom-sold-to.md`, `coded-whom-sold-to.tsv`, `coded-whom-sold-to/`, `coded-whom-sold-to-reliability.md`, `findings-buyers-market.md`, `header.tsv`, `header-pages.tsv`, `header-recode.tsv` are hard-coded across nine templates. The amendment `codebook-amendment-author.md` is asked to write is already much wider than its name (whom-sold-to, plus four shape dimensions, plus one variable per unanswered question). Add `{{AMENDMENT}}` and `{{CODED}}` to the placeholder list in `orchestrator-steps.md` and fill them.

### 3.6 Named counts of the earlier runs

"sixteen coders", "three clerks ... three ways in a day", "three cards cited one number", "two variables ... and ... nine", "Four things a card of an earlier run got wrong", "Three faults of the second pair", "about a thousand words", "five rules". See §6 for what they cost.

## 4. A rule handed to several clerks as a sentence

The templates enforce this rule on the run and break it on themselves four times.

1. **`third-derivation-clerk.md`** — a whole file layout in prose, to a bank of clerks launched "by range" (`orchestrator-steps.md` step 18): "Each file has this shape and no other file need be opened to learn it: the heading `## Third derivation` and the card's number; the question as the numbered questions file prints it; the ways the market sells this, one line each with its figure, its population and its source; a line `Carried:` naming the way the evidence carries, or `tie`; a line `Margin:` in that way's unit; and, on a tie, a line `Would break it:`." This is exactly the survey-side fault the templates were built to fix — `batch-two-lessons-by-clerk.md`: "any layout a clerk must reproduce is cut once by the orchestrator and copied as it stands", after "sixteen coders reading one sentence about columns wrote two layouts". Cut `forms/third-derivation.md` once and point the clerks at it.
2. **`corrections-clerk.md`**, the set-wide paragraph — six counting and grouping rules in one sentence-pile, miscounted as five (§2.4). At least it goes to one clerk. The per-card paragraph does not: "Make every option count a second time on `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv` ... put both figures on the card: where units and operators differ, the measured-in line carries both and the option table says which it prints." That is a layout rule, given as prose, to corrections clerks launched by range. `batch-two-lessons-by-clerk.md` already records the tool gap — "a check that reads a card's operator figures, which today live only in prose (entry 14)".
3. **`area-deriving-clerk.md`** against **`lead-deriving-clerk.md`**, the same rule written twice and differently — see §5.3.
4. **`orchestrator-steps.md`** step 7's "materially more" — a corpus-wide re-coding rule, as an adverb (§2.3).

The counter-example worth copying is `operator-index-clerk.md`: the rule is stated in order, the clerk is told to "Work by script where a rule can be run by script", and the rule and the commands go into `{{CORPUS}}/operators-rule.md` "so that another clerk can run them again". That is the shape the other three should take.

## 5. Contradictions

### 5.1 `integrator.md` against `forms/integrator-fields.md` and the assembler

`integrator.md`: "Write `3-decisions/integrator-fields.md` on its form. Its prose enters the owner's review only through the form's two sections ... It says what a row of the sheet and a card's own Joint line do not: which rulings must be taken first ...; where a ruling on one card silently rules another; where two cards rest on one count ...; which cards are withheld, each with the one observation that would carry it, grouped by what the observation costs ...; and which cards hold two readings for him to rule between." Then: "**Open with** whatever the records show to be wrong today" and "**Close with** the count of correction rounds this set has had".

`forms/integrator-fields.md` has two headings, `## Unlock` and `## Collisions`, and says "read by `tools/assemble-review.py` under these two headings and nowhere else". `assemble-review.py` confirms it: `order = [section(fields, title) for title in ("Unlock", "Collisions")]`. An opening paragraph and a closing paragraph written outside those two headings are dropped **without warning** — which is the very failure the same template tells the integrator to report: "Where the tool or the form defeats a mend (a field it drops without warning ...), report it in your own record rather than working around it silently."

Worse, the withheld-by-cost grouping has no heading of its own, and `survey-top-up-clerk.md` is sent to it as a work list: "then `3-decisions/integrator-fields.md`, whose grouping of the withheld cards by what their observation costs is your work list". It survives in the file for the top-up clerk and never reaches the owner. The form needs a third and fourth heading, or the template must say under which of the two each item goes.

### 5.2 `strike-list-clerk.md` against `orchestrator-steps.md`

`strike-list-clerk.md`: "The owner asked that nothing be put to him before the finished page, so the Returned line reads the date you return it and the word unmarked ... Leave the owner's fields at the head of the form for the orchestrator, who holds the closed run record."

`orchestrator-steps.md` step 14: "Strike-list clerk. Re-add its totals yourself against the source; **carry the owner's standing words onto the form, which the clerk cannot see**."

One says the clerk cannot see the owner's standing words and the orchestrator carries them; the other tells the clerk what the owner asked and has it fill the Returned line, which is one of the owner's fields it has just been told to leave alone.

### 5.3 `area-deriving-clerk.md` against `lead-deriving-clerk.md`

Area: "Where a count of units would count one body's published decision several times, make it on `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv` as well, give both figures, **and say which the option table prints**."

Lead: "Where a count of units would count one body's published decision several times, make it on `{{CORPUS}}/operators.tsv` as well and give both figures, since a chain that prints one sentence across its sites can make a lead or hide one."

Four clerks, one rule, two versions. The lead clerk writes the buyer card and Card 0 — the two cards everything else hangs on — under the version that does not say which figure the table prints, which is precisely the fault `batch-two-lessons-by-clerk.md` records: "Option tables printed unit figures while operator figures sat in the corrections prose, so the sheet showed units."

### 5.4 `corrections-clerk.md` and `integrator.md` against `standing-brief-block.md`

`standing-brief-block.md`: "Nothing else in the repository is open to you beyond what your own brief lists."

`corrections-clerk.md`: "**Nothing is closed to you**, since the priors are already on the cards."

The corrections clerk is the only clerk that rewrites a recommending line, and it is handed a sentence that reads as a licence to open the fenced files — the earlier definition, the sibling runs, the fee schedule — after a whole run has been spent keeping them away from the clerks who wrote those lines. The reasoning offered ("the priors are already on the cards") justifies opening the *priors*, not the fence. `priors-clerk.md` shows how to say it safely: "You are the one clerk who opens the closed files, in a single pass ... the venue's document store is open to you for the files the fence index names." `integrator.md` carries the same sentence, and is harmless only because its read list is cards; `records-refresh-clerk.md` and `run-record-clerk.md` likewise.

### 5.5 `amendment-record-form-clerk.md` against the four coder templates

The record-form clerk is told: "Write one header file **for each level the amendment codes at**, under `{{CORPUS}}/shards/`". The coders ask for files by source, not by level: `whom-sold-to-coder.md` → `{{CORPUS}}/shards/header.tsv`; `amendment-coder-from-pages.md` → `header-pages.tsv`; `recode-coder.md` → `header-recode.tsv`; `pilot-coder.md` → "the matching header line from `{{CORPUS}}/shards/`". Nothing tells the record-form clerk those three names, or that the page header carries quote and source columns where the profile header may not, or who cuts `header-recode.tsv` after the rule review (no template and no orchestrator step assigns it). A coder will open the folder and find no file of the name its brief gives.

### 5.6 The findings file handed to fence-blind clerks

`records-clerk.md` writes "`0-comparables/findings-market-side.md`. The survey's findings cut to the market's side, for the clerks who derive from the market alone". `third-derivation-clerk.md` names that cut file. But `area-deriving-clerk.md`, `lead-deriving-clerk.md` and `strike-list-clerk.md` all name `{{FINDINGS}}` — an unresolved placeholder that `orchestrator-steps.md` glosses only as `{{FINDINGS}}` in its replacement list. `batch-two-lessons-by-clerk.md` records what happens when it is filled with the whole file: "A brief filled from the template opened a findings file that opens with where the operator's prices sit and closes with a long addendum on its own site". The greps at step 16 are the backstop, not the design; the design should name the cut.

### 5.7 `codebook-amendment-author.md` and the provenance of the question list

The author is told `3-decisions/questions.md` "holds the parameters a buyer meets as questions and nothing of what this venue does", and that it must draft "blind, as the codebook was drafted: from what operators of this kind could publish, and from nothing this venue sells or wants". But `records-clerk.md` builds that file "from the method's named cards and **from the parameter names of this run's earlier decisions**". The earlier decisions are fenced; their parameter *set* travels through the question list into the codebook amendment, which then decides what the corpus can be counted for. `sage-S-survey.md` names the channel: "the buyer and the parameters of another product travel in copied sentences." The records clerk should say, per question, whether it came from the method's cards or from an earlier decision, so the amendment author can weigh a question that exists only because a draft raised it.

A separate observation on weight of a different kind: `records-clerk.md` item 5 is a single 200-word sentence carrying nine distinct requirements for the corpus inventory. Nothing in it is narration and nothing should go, but a clerk will drop one. It wants to be a list.

## The three mends that matter most

**1. `lead-deriving-clerk.md` — do not write seller's sentences off a recommendation.** Replace "for each buyer the buyer card recommends" with: "for each buyer the **owner rules** at the second gate; where the gate has not yet been held, write no sentences and say on the buyer card that the set waits on his ruling." This is the silent-scoping failure `sage-methodology.md` names, in the one template whose output scopes everything downstream — and, because `area-deriving-clerk.md` reads the sentences, it is the failure that would reach every card in the set.

**2. `strike-list-clerk.md` — return the day-one gate to the owner.** Replace the whole sentence with: "Leave the Returned line and every owner's field at the head of the form blank for the orchestrator, who holds the closed run record and carries the owner's standing words onto the form." Whether the owner reads a strike list for an offering that has never been on sale is a ruling he has not given about this run.

**3. `corrections-clerk.md` — bound the read list, and make the direction rule a reporting rule.** Replace "Nothing is closed to you, since the priors are already on the cards" with: "Only the files listed above are open to you; the fence index still closes everything it closed to the deriving clerks, and the priors you need are already on the cards." ## Was one of the lessons the wrong lesson?

One, I think: the lesson behind `strike-list-clerk.md`'s unmarked line. `pair-lessons-by-clerk.md` and `batch-two-lessons-by-clerk.md` both record the owner's preference to be shown only the finished page, and the template turns a preference expressed about two runs into a standing default that dissolves the method's first gate. `sage-A-adjudicate.md` builds the whole ordering on it — "the buyer named in a frame-builder brief shapes the population the whole corpus is drawn from, and a codebook frozen around that population cannot be un-frozen by a later ruling" — and for the two offerings ahead, neither ever sold, the buyer is *less* settled than it was in the runs that taught the lesson, not more.

What would show it either way: the two completed runs' strike lists and their run records. If the owner, when the list did reach him, struck no row and the unstruck set matched the buyer card's eventual ruling, the gate cost him minutes and bought nothing, and defaulting to unmarked is right. If he struck rows, or if the buyer card later recommended a buyer he would have struck, or if any card's options were narrowed by a buyer the frame never reached, the gate paid for itself and the default is the second worry in reverse — a clerk deciding, on the owner's behalf, that his judgement was not needed. A second piece of evidence sits in the same place: whether either run's corpus was drawn against a buyer set the owner later moved.

A smaller one I would not call wrong but would watch: "the front is written to the sheet, the prose kept to what a row cannot carry", which is well evidenced (nine rows ruled, then twenty-one) but is now pulling against `integrator.md`'s growing list of things the prose must carry. The form, not the word budget, is where that tension should be resolved.
