## 3. Words I had to guess at

- "units": my guess is the comparable venues counted in the survey.
- "band" (Card 17, "0 in band"; also "funeral incumbents inside the band" in the order-of-ruling text): my guess is a peer group of venues priced or positioned similarly to this chapel.
- "the register" ("the register holds only as a third party's word"): my guess is a master record of what's been found about each comparable venue.
- "the coded table": my guess is a spreadsheet behind the cards that the corrections were checked against.
- "the Survey" / "the Survey's own tables": my guess is the underlying market research document the cards were drawn from.
- "finding 11", "findings 20 and 21", "finding 25", "finding 26": my guess is numbered findings in a companion research document I haven't seen.
- "the card files": my guess is a longer file behind each card with its sources.
- "funeral strata" (Card 1/6 Joint line): my guess is the subset of surveyed venues that serve funeral buyers specifically.
- "the platform" (Card 11's note): my guess is an existing online funeral-directory or booking site that already lists this chapel.
- "as the form asks" (Card 14's margin note): my guess is that some standard template for writing these cards required the margin to be shown both ways here.
- "measured-in lines" ("mark a measured population as differing from this venue's shape 51 times"): I could not form a confident guess at all. I don't know what population or what shape is being compared.

## 4. The four "wrong today" items: could I send a staff member to fix each on Monday?

1. Insurance doesn't cover this kind of hire, and nobody has asked the broker. **Yes**, I understand this and could hand it to someone: get the broker's answer in writing before we run a single hire.
2. The funeral flyer promises ramp, parking and a senior menu that don't exist. **Yes**, clear enough to hand over: pull the flyer's claims or make them true.
3. The Main Dining Deck's own capacity records disagree with each other (180 vs about 100), so the venue supposedly can't safely state any room bigger than the Barn's 80, including the chapel's 120. **No**. I don't follow why a dispute about the Dining Deck's numbers stops me stating the chapel's own seat count. This one would need someone to explain it to me before I could give an instruction.
4. No web page, no routed enquiry address, and no system entry for the chapel, so a funeral enquiry got answered by the wedding autoresponder. **Yes**, clear: build the page, fix the routing, get it into whatever system holds enquiries.
5. The booking diary returns no rows for this room, so nobody can say if it's free on a Saturday. **Yes**, clear: this is a system fault, get it fixed and tell me when the diary is trustworthy again.

## 5. The three changes that would most help me rule this in one sitting

1. Every "withheld" row should say the plain-English leading option and why it didn't carry, in the row itself. Don't leave me to work it out from raw fractions, several of which (Cards 3, 10) look one-sided but are marked withheld anyway with no stated reason.
2. Define "band," "in band," "the register," "the coded table" and "measured-in lines" once, near the top, the way "margin," "held" and "Joint line" already are. I can follow the sheet's own definitions when they're given; I can't invent the ones that are missing.
3. Pull the Main Dining Deck capacity aside (item 3 above) out of the "wrong today" paragraph, or explain in one sentence why it constrains the chapel. As written it reads like a different room's problem wandering into this one, and it's the one thing on the whole front page I could not act on.

## 6. Length

The front, the ruling sheet, the order of ruling, and the short "Open cards" section before the cards start, is about 3,900 words. For one sitting, yes, that is too long. I could read the table and rule nine rows on the spot, but the sixteen "withheld" rows all send me hunting through dense prose for a verdict the row should have given me directly, and that's most of the page.
