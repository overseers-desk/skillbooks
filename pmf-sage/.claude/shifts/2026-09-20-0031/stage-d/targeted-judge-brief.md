# A correction round on a decision page: a marketer's review for the owner

A decision page puts one offering's product parameters to its owner for ruling, a card to a parameter. Each card sets out options with figures, recommends one or withholds with the counts both ways, and carries a second derivation made from the market's record alone by a clerk who could not see the card. After the cards were written, a correction round changed some of them. Your prompt names the run folder and the cards to read.

You are a seasoned marketer reviewing that round for the owner. He has two worries, in his words. The first: work that "predicts what market needs by meeting notes and owner inner thoughts instead of reality". The second, its mirror: "thinking if owner believe A, A must be incorrect". A correction round can fail either way: it can drift toward whatever the venue already holds, or away from it, without the evidence moving. Your prompt gives the round's moves counted two ways, toward or away from what the venue holds and from carried to withheld or back; the round went one way on at least one of them. He wants the blunt reading of whether that is the evidence or the clerks.

For each card your prompt names, read the card file whole (its option table, its recommending or withheld line, its Corrections lines in date and pass order, its third-derivation line and the file that line names) and answer:

1. What the line said before the round, what it says now, which way the change runs relative to what the venue holds, and whether it turned on a changed count or on a changed reading of the same count.
2. The counts on each side before and after, quoted from the card, and the rule that changed them.
3. Whether the rule that moved the line was applied alike to the options on both sides of it, and to the cards in the same set that it did not move. Name a card it should have moved and did not, if there is one, in either direction. Where a test could take a line from carried to withheld, say whether its mirror, which would carry a withheld line on the same kind of evidence, was put anywhere.
4. Whether the new line is carried by the evidence on the card: EARNED, THIN (recommends and says honestly that it is thin), WITHHELD-SOUND, WITHHELD-EVASIVE, ECHO (agrees with the venue chiefly because the venue holds it), CONTRARIAN, or UNSUPPORTED.
5. One line of reason, quoting the card.

Then: whether the round's one-way direction is explained by the evidence, in your judgement, with the strongest reason for and the strongest against; whether each rule that moved a line is sound for this kind of corpus, what it risks, and whether it was put on one base and to every card it reaches; and the three things you would change before this rule is used on another run. Then read the front of `3-decisions/review.md`, down to the heading "Open cards", as the owner would in one sitting; where it was assembled before the round's last card edit (compare the file times), say it is stale and which rows no longer match their cards, and say in five sentences whether he could rule from it.

Write the full review to the output file your prompt names, then report its path and your verdicts in under 250 words.
