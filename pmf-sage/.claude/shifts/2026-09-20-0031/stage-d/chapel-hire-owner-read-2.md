# Owner's cold read: chapel hire decision page (second read)

Read once, in order, from the top to the heading "Open cards." I opened one card, Card 14, because its own row tells me to. The row's evidence column ends "and no ...; the card has the rest," which is an instruction to go and read it, not a typo. Nothing else was opened.

## 1. What I believe I am being asked to decide

I am being asked to rule, in one sitting, on twenty-five open questions about how the chapel is offered. That covers what it is called, who it is for, what is included, how long a hire runs, what it costs, and a dozen practical details besides, because none of them has been settled yet. For most of these the researchers could not find a clear market winner, so they are marked "withheld" and want me to pick between two named options myself. A handful carry a thin recommendation they want me to confirm or rule on outright. Underneath all of that sit five things that are wrong today regardless of how I rule: an insurance gap, a false flyer claim, a contradiction between two capacity records, no enquiry page or system for this room, and a booking diary the system cannot query. Those need fixing Monday on their own timetable. Several of the twenty-five questions are wired together, so one ruling settles another automatically, which the page explains after the table.

**Card 14, opened.** Row 14 sent me there because its own evidence cell is cut off mid-sentence with "the card has the rest." The card confirms my reading of the row is sound rather than broken: cooling leads 13 to 0 among operators who state a position at all, but trails 13 to 42 once the silent majority is counted in. That is the same negative-margin pattern as Cards 4 and 5. It also tells me the recommendation is the only one on the whole page that costs money to deliver (air conditioning), and that no page can safely say the room is cooled until it is actually measured in summer. I would not have known either of those two facts from the row alone.

## 3. Words or phrases I had to guess at

- "unit": used throughout the ruling sheet before it is defined. I took it to mean one comparable venue in the survey (confirmed later, in "Five words the cards use").
- "in band" / "the band": same problem. It appears in Card 17's row ("0 in band") before its definition three paragraphs later. I guessed it meant venues inside the real competing radius, as opposed to the wider survey.
- "operator" versus "unit"/"text": the sheet counts some things "on distinct operators" and others as raw units or texts. I guessed this distinguishes one business, which may repeat the same boilerplate across several listed sites, from each individual published page, so that one company's stock sentence isn't counted six times. Confirmed later but not on first pass.
- "commission the observation" (a "your part" value): I guessed this means order a specific further piece of research named on the card, rather than rule today.
- "held; neither kept nor left" (the "what is held" column on withheld rows): I guessed this means simply that nothing has been decided yet, so no existing value has been kept or changed.
- "margin, or the counts it stands at": I guessed the column changes meaning row to row, a signed net number when there is a recommendation, raw head-to-head counts when the row is withheld.

## 4. The five "wrong today" items: could I send a staff member to fix each on Monday?

- Insurance gap (no hire type covered, wedding/event planning excluded outright): Yes. Get the broker's answer in writing before taking any booking.
- Flyer claims (accessible facilities, parking, senior menu) unsupported: Yes. Strike the three claims, or measure and build until they hold.
- Two conflicting capacity records for the after-room (180-plus vs about 100): Yes. Get the two records reconciled, and until then quote no larger than the Barn's 80.
- No chapel enquiry page, no address, no system entry: Yes. Build the page, name the address, add it to the enquiry system.
- Booking system returns no rows for this room, so Saturday availability cannot be confirmed: Yes, but softer than the other four. "Raise it as a system fault and say when the diary can be trusted" tells me to escalate, but not to whom or by when, so the Monday instruction is less concrete than the other four.

## 5. Three changes that would most help me rule this in one sitting

1. Stop mixing sign conventions in the margin column. "-10 units" next to a stated lead of "9 to 6" for the same option reads as a contradiction until you learn the key by heart. Say "for" and "against" in words on every row, not a signed number whose sign flips meaning card to card.
2. Do not leave a row's own evidence cut short with "the card has the rest" (Card 14). A row that cannot stand on its own is the reason I had to stop reading the sheet and open a card, which is the one interruption the brief allows me and the one the page itself should be trying to avoid. If some rows genuinely can't be self-contained, say up front which ones require opening their card before ruling, rather than leaving me to discover it mid-sentence.
3. Put the two competing options in a withheld row on the same base, or at minimum say plainly which of the several counts offered is the one that decides between the two named options. Rows like Cards 11, 13 and 20 give me four or five numbers and leave me to work out which two are actually being compared.

## 6. Length

The front, meaning the intro, ruling sheet and the order-of-ruling section through "What passed through," is 4,311 words. That is too long to rule in one sitting. It is longer than this read itself, dense with a table whose columns each carry their own private conventions, and it puts the explanation for a hard row, such as Card 14's negative margins or the Cards 4/5 pattern, in prose that comes after the table rather than beside the row that needs it.
