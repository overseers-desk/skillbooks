# Kayak hire: two decision pages, card by card

The offering has never been on sale, so no card on either page can rest on this venue's own buyers of the paddle. Every Held stamp rests on a draft: the 2025 canoe proposal, the Rezdy setup, the grant application, the SOP outline, the P&L forecast. The owner's scoping of 3 September (retail family day visitors first, trade later) is a bound on the buyer card and not a ruling. No card on either page has been ruled.

Codes follow the brief. For what a recommendation rests on: **M** market, **B** this venue's own buyers, **D** the venue's documents, opinions, plans or present arrangement (regulation and policy wording are counted here), **N** nothing. For the verdict: EARNED, ECHO, CONTRARIAN, THIN, UNSUPPORTED, and on page B also WITHHELD-SOUND and WITHHELD-EVASIVE.

A note on page A's use of B. The venue has never sold a paddle. What page A cites as buyer behaviour is the sale of other products, the 8-minute ride at \$20, feed bags and weekend till orders, all sold at the only terms on offer. Where a card leans on that, the rests-on column reads "B (other products)" and the verdict treats it as the third fault.

## The clearest cases of each fault

### Page A

**Echo: meeting notes and the owner's own thoughts treated as market.**

- Card 6, price. "The anchored chip is honest: the double's number is the venue's ride price." The fence-blind draft, offered as independent confirmation, starts its own case the same way: "the block price is the venue's own walk-up unit: \$20 is what the same family already pays for an 8-minute ride." When two drafts meet on a figure both were handed, they have not confirmed it.
- Card 2, positioning. "The words 'peaceful', 'slow', 'shallow' and 'in sight of the bank' carry it." These are the Director's words of 3 September. The market leg is one sentence about a composition with "no rival" and one international interviewee who was never put a paddle price.
- Card 7, block. The market's modal block is an hour, and the card overrides it with arithmetic on a reach that is "stated twice, measured by nobody": "one kilometre of paddling, twenty to thirty minutes at a family's pace (the clerk's arithmetic on a stated figure)". Page B's Card 10 reads the same market and lands on the hour.

**Contrarian: departing from the owner's view without proof.** There is only one clear case.

- Card 10, age. It drops the drafts' floor of 3 for an accompaniment-only rule, on A6's headline ("the rule is more often an accompaniment threshold than a floor"). The finding's own figures print 45 minimum ages against 33 accompaniment rules, so the headline contradicts them. No second case exists. Page A's departures on Cards 0, 5, 16 and 17 are carried by evidence.

**Sales read as preference: products sold only at the terms offered, read as what buyers want.**

- Card 15, channel. "Walk-up at the till is the first channel, the 8-minute ride's model." The market figure on the same card is 76 online against 6 walk-up. The ride is walk-up because the venue sells it that way.
- Card 13, days. "the 8-minute-ride days (S2 §2.3), on walk-up". The paddle's days are copied from the days another product is offered.
- Card 1, buyer. "a parent who has just paid \$20 for an 8-minute ride buys a \$20 double for half an hour at the same till without asking what a kayak is for". This is a prediction about a paddle, drawn from the queue for a different product.

### Page B

**Echo.** There is no clear case. The page applies one test in both directions ("Keeping and leaving were asked for the same proof"), and its correction rounds moved cards both ways: Card 19 towards the drafts, Cards 4, 5 and 35 away from them. The nearest thing to an echo is Card 19, which a correction moved from withheld to carried "toward what the 2025 drafts hold" by switching its leg to the provider family, a population every other card refuses as a leg. The switch follows a stated rule ("the method bars a differing population as the leg only on the dimension the card rules"), and the hire-only count of 45 to 4 carries it. Second nearest is Card 3, marked "confirm" on "a thin lead" that reverses outside the parks. What really carries that card is capability, since the water is slow, and the sheet does not say so.

**Contrarian.** There is no case. Both departures (Cards 2 and 8) carry their evidence. The one to watch is Card 2: its 23-unit lead is "carried by the overnight parks, which hold 24 of the 32 self-hire units: outside them self-hire alone leads guided alone by 2 units, a tie". The catchment's 7 of 10 rivals are what carry it.

**Sales read as preference.** There is no case, and the page guards against it by name. Card 14: the venue's 10-day booking median "is not split by buyer and not taken for this offering, so it stands in for nothing here". Card 15: "the counter's weekend precedent (E70) is a capability, not a preference". Cards 0, 2 and 33: the enquiry record, "taken while nothing was on sale ... could have held an ask for either and shows neither".

**Evasive withholding.**

- Card 23. "for a school's organiser everyone wears one whatever the market prints", the peak body recommends all wear at all times, and "'provided' does not say who must wear one". This is a safety rule that the regulator, the peak body and the insurer settle. The card withholds it pending a market read of FAQ pages.
- Card 6. "the one bare hire among them carries a craft-and-mode name, so the moment names follow the mode". Once the guided titles are discounted, what is left on the card points to "Kayak Hire", and the card does not say so.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

Mostly yes. The owner has ruled nothing, so every Held stamp is a draft, and the page never holds a match against a recommendation. Card 1 leans to the owner's scoping, and the order of ruling says "nothing on the card overturns it, and it stands until you change it". Every card, keep or leave, gets the same market-only reading by a fresh clerk. The carried cards divide 6 keeps and 2 leaves, which is about what drafts would score after a year of the owner's thinking.

There are two asymmetries, neither disqualifying:

- **The sheet shows a withheld row's weakness and hides a carried row's.** Card 1's row prints "the lead is the overnight parks'". Card 2's row prints "leads by 23 units" and Card 0's "leads by 26 units", yet both leads also collapse outside the parks (Card 2 to "a tie" by units; Card 0 to "11 units to 10" with the paddle businesses aside). An owner reading the sheet alone gets a confident departure on Card 2 and a hedged view on Card 1, when both stand on the same kind of lead.
- **Card 19 relaxes the page's population rule, and the relaxation keeps a draft value.** The rule is written and applied openly, so this is a standard applied unevenly. It is not bias.

Page B never lets a departure pass without the proof it asks of a match. Card 8's departure is the best-evidenced card on the page. Card 2's is adequate, and its thinness is buried in the card text rather than shown on the sheet.

## What the owner has to read

**Page A:** 37,921 words, 26 cards. The one-page summary and its table run to about 1,700 words, with the table itself about 800. It gives a recommendation for every card and marks 21 "Ready". He can rule from the table in one sitting. But the table does not show which recommendations rest on his own statements or on the ride queue. "Owner-stated" and "Anchored" appear only as chips, never as warnings, so he would be confirming his own views back to himself without seeing it. He has ruled nothing, so no card only confirms a past ruling. Every card needs his judgement, and the table invites a rubber stamp.

**Page B:** 35,926 words, 28 cards. The ruling sheet is about 3,130 words and the order of ruling about 1,300, so about 4,430 words together. The "your part" column shows at a glance what each row asks: confirm (6), rule (2), or commission an observation (20). "Wrong today" tells him bluntly that all eight carried rows are unsellable until cover is written and the landing is settled.

**Can he rule from those two sections alone?** Yes. He can confirm or rule the 8 carried rows, let the scoping stand on Card 1, and commission the rest. That comes to about 4,400 words, plus a card opened where a row raises a doubt. Rows 0 and 2 ought to raise one and do not, because the sheet hides their parks dependence. Three things make this harder than it should be:

- The rows are written in the survey's own vocabulary ("derived on 39 one-offer venue units less 13 launch-only units", "set aside", "coded blind", "V", "AU-P2"). The glossary paragraph is necessary reading, and dense.
- The sheet says whether a row "keeps" or "leaves" a held value but never says what that value was. To learn what he would be confirming (the drafts' \$25, the grant's guided hour) he has to open `priors-pass.md`, which the review does not link from the sheet.
- "Confirm" on a card he never ruled means "agree with a recommendation that keeps a 2025 draft value". The word suggests he decided it once already.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

It withholds 20 cards: 18 sound and 2 evasive (Cards 6 and 23). Of the 8 that recommend, one should arguably have withheld or at least said "thin" on the sheet: Card 2, whose lead outside the parks is a tie that the catchment rivals alone carry, and which "rests on Card 1" for the household side. Card 3 does recommend on a thin lead, but says so, and capability backs it.

The larger problem is less the number withheld than where the withheld cards are sent. Seven of the twenty (Cards 21, 23, 24, 25, 28, 31, 32) are safety and cancellation rules that the broker's conditions, the peak body and the state rules decide. They are framed as market questions and sent for another reading of comparables' FAQ pages. A marketer would rule the regulatory default now and let the insurer's letter adjust it.

Three others need no survey at all:

- Card 16 turns on how many seats the four kayaks have, which one question to the Guest Experience Manager answers.
- Cards 6 and 12 turn on search volume for the paddle terms, which is a lookup of a few hours that was never made.
- Card 7 turns on phoning the reception desks of about 23 parks.

The page hands the owner the job of commissioning work that the run should have done before the page reached him.

The page also marks Cards 6, 31 and 32 as answering "to the group organiser only", so households-first "sets those six aside for launch". A retail family still meets a product name, still meets a weather call at the landing, and still needs to know what happens if the boats are pulled. As written, the retail launch has no name card, no weather rule and no remedy.

## Three changes that would most improve page B for a one-sitting owner

1. **Make the sheet honest about strength and plain about content.** Put the held value in words in every row ("drafts held \$25 a person for 25 minutes"). Print the parks-aside figure on carried rows the way it is printed on withheld ones: Card 2 is "23 units overall; a tie outside the holiday parks; 7 of 10 local rivals". Replace the survey vocabulary with one plain sentence per row, so that 4,400 words become about 2,000 an owner can read without a glossary.
2. **Take the safety and compliance cards out of the market frame.** For Cards 21, 23, 24, 25, 28, 31 and 32, recommend the regulatory and insurer default (everyone wears a lifejacket; a staffed launch; a weather-permitting call with named triggers), mark each "subject to the broker's conditions", and stop commissioning market reads of FAQ pages for them. Re-mark Cards 6, 31 and 32 as answering to the household too.
3. **Do the cheap observations before the page reaches him.** These are the paddle-term search volumes (Cards 6, 12 and the whole buyer picture), the seats and kind of the four kayaks (Cards 5 and 16), and the parks' and rivals' reception prices and hours by phone (Cards 7 and 15). Each is hours, not weeks. Together they would turn about six withheld rows into recommendations, and he would sit with 14 recommendations instead of 8.

## Verdict

Page B is an improvement on page A for this owner. It removes page A's echoes of his own words and of the ride queue, and it does not replace them with contrarian turns. It pays for that with density and with twenty withheld rows, several of which could have been answered before it reached him. I am fairly sure of this, about 75 per cent. The main doubt is whether he will find it easier to act on a page that asks him to commission twenty observations than to correct one that answered every question, some of them with his own opinions.
