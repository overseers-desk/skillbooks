# Two decision pages for one offering: a marketer's review for the owner

Chapel hire at Historic Rivermill. Page A is `superseded/2026-09-12-decision-review.md`, twenty-one cards of 12 September. Page B is `3-decisions/review.md`, twenty-five cards of 20 September, with the priors note beside it. The offering is not on sale, and the owner has ruled nothing on either page.

One thing has to be said before the tables, because it changes how the third column reads. **Neither page departs from a ruling, because there is no ruling anywhere in this run.** Page B says so in as many words: "all twenty-five Ruled lines read open, and so do the twenty-one of the 12 September review." So the fourth column below reads a card against what the *venue* held: the fee schedule, the enquiry template, the flyer, the policies, the staff captures, and the earlier round's own cards. That is the right comparison anyway. A price sitting in a live SOP since February is a held value whether or not the owner ever signed it, and a card that moves it is departing from something.

The three faults I was asked to test for:

1. Predicting what the market wants from meeting notes and the owner's inner thoughts.
2. The mirror: treating a thing as wrong because the owner believes it.
3. Reading what the venue already sells, or who has already asked, as what the market wants, when sales and enquiries made at the only terms on offer, through the only door built, cannot show what buyers would have done at other terms.

## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Page B

**ECHO.** None. The nearest thing on the page is card 15, where the market lead of 23 units happens to lean toward the shape the venue already has, and the card names the coincidence instead of banking it: "it leans toward in-house in the Australian and New Zealand funeral strata, where the operator owns a cafe on the same site, and that lean is this operator's own shape". Card 7 and card 19 also keep held values, and both show the lead surviving a re-cut. I could not find a card on page B that agrees with the venue chiefly because the venue holds it.

**CONTRARIAN.** None. Five cards leave a held value and three of them (2, 9, 16) carry the departure on a market count that survives its own lean test. The other two are the page's two thinnest recommendations, which is a real asymmetry and is dealt with under fairness below.

**UNSUPPORTED.** None outright. The three weakest recommendations, in order:

1. Card 0, which recommends on a lead it then shows to be a tie: "dropping the church units leaves 11 to 9, which is a tie".
2. Card 14, which reads a market posture about *publishing* cooling as a case for *buying* it: "cooling leads 13 to 0 over Heating named, and no cooling", against "the recommendation is the only option on this card that costs plant".
3. Card 18, whose sheet row prints a margin of 19 units and whose card prints "a lead of 4".

**WITHHELD-EVASIVE.** One, card 5, and it is worth the owner's eye because of where it points. The counts are access 10, interval 8, of which "six of the eight carry one corporate group's sentence published at several sites, so as distinct operators the count is nearer three"; the independent clerk who read the market alone "lands on the access reading"; and access is what the venue's own template already says. A page that recommends on a 10-to-8 lead elsewhere (card 19 recommends on 48 to 35 and card 16 on 16 to 6) withholds on this one.

### Page A

**ECHO.** Card 19, quoted above. Card 4's chapel-and-garden half is second: the ruling is the policy's own sentence, "A hire covers a space within the site, not the whole site", read back as a recommendation. Card 8's two hours is third: the block is REF-CHAPEL's, the chip says *anchored*, and the corpus's own finding for this venue kind is 1.5 to 2.5 hours, which is a range the held value happens to sit inside.

**CONTRARIAN.** Card 7 is the case, and it is a serious one. The venue has held \$600 and \$750 since February; the card moves the non-wedding rate to \$450 on "75 per cent of that card", and the wedding rate to \$900 and \$1,200 on the argument that \$600 "undercuts the venue's own next rung by a margin no evidence explains". Second is card 14, which takes days off the week the venue never restricted. Third is card 1's withdrawal of the room's own published name, which is well carried on search evidence for the page but is asserted rather than measured for the room ("The word 'Wedding' comes out of the room's name in every non-wedding context").

**UNSUPPORTED.** Card 13's build-before-booking; card 14's restricted week; and card 11's deposit ladder written straight into a blind spot the same paragraph names.

**Fault 3, reading the venue's own record as the market**, is page A's characteristic weakness and deserves its own line. Card 0 builds the served-kind list from six approaches without once asking what the door could have shown: the enquiries arrived through a contact form that "creates a record in the wedding event system and fires the wedding autoresponder whatever the enquiry says", and a "Host an Event" form "with no chapel option". Nine ceremony enquiries against one funeral call is therefore a fact about the funnel before it is a fact about the market. Page A treats the nine as demand. Page B's card 1 is the correction, in one sentence: "the venue's own record was taken through a funnel built and staffed to sell weddings, under the names that funnel used, with no chapel option on any form, so it could not have shown a funeral buyer winning."

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

Mostly yes, and the page has a stated guard that works in the right direction. When the one correction of 20 September turned a card toward the venue's own position, the page says it re-read it before letting it stand: "The one line that turned moved toward what the venue already holds, which is why it was read again before it stood." That is scepticism aimed at a match, which is the correct aim; a page that re-read only the departures would be doing the owner's second worry for him.

Is a match ever held against a recommendation? Once, softly, at card 5, which is the evasive withholding above. The option the evidence favours is also the option the venue's template already holds, and the card declines to say so. I do not think this is systematic, but it is the one place where it looks like it.

Does a departure pass without the proof a match is asked for? Not on the three departures that matter. Cards 2, 9 and 16 each survive a re-cut the card performs on itself. But the two thinnest recommendations on the page, card 14 at a headline margin of −29 and card 18 at a corrected lead of 4, both leave a held value, while the six cards that keep one include four of the seven EARNED verdicts. So the asymmetry, such as it is, runs the other way from the owner's worry: keeping is slightly better proved than leaving. On the page's own claim, "Keeping and leaving were asked for the same proof", I would say: asked for it, yes; and on two cards, leaving got away with less.

## What the owner has to read to rule

**Page A.** 27,558 words in all: 21 cards at 23,459 words, a joint ledger of 1,793, and a front matter of 2,306. The front matter is the best-designed thing on page A. Three tables sort the cards into ten ruleable now, two ruleable in part and nine gated on a named missing fact, each with a ten-word recommended ruling and a named gate, and they run to 1,150 words. An owner could genuinely rule ten cards from that table alone in about five minutes. What he could not do is check anything: the tables carry no counts, no figures and no statement of what a recommendation rests on, so any doubt costs him a card of about 1,100 words with the evidence spread through five prose legs.

**Page B.** 27,454 words in all: 25 cards at 23,520 words, and a front matter of 3,934. The ruling sheet is 1,837 words and the order of ruling 2,065.

Could he rule from those two sections alone, opening a card only where a row raises a doubt? For most of the sheet, yes, and this is the page's real achievement. The sheet's second column tells him his part on every row. Six rows say confirm. Five say rule. Fourteen say commission an observation or rule between two readings. The "Wrong today, whichever way you rule" paragraph puts the broker letter, the flyer's false promises, the unmeasured access and the unreadable diary in front of him before any card does. About 3,900 words, fifteen or sixteen minutes, and he has ruled or commissioned twenty-five parameters.

Four things stop it being clean. The margin column mixes units, so he reads "14 units", "150 a month", "44 of 133 against 39" and "−29 units" down one column and has to re-learn the scale each row. Card 14's margin cell is a forty-word parenthetical and is the only cell in the table that cannot be read at a glance. A negative margin needs the header's footnote to parse at all. And the fourteen "commission the observation, or rule between the two" rows are one instruction covering two quite different situations, which the order of ruling separates and the sheet does not.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

Fourteen of twenty-five is a lot, and it is close to right. Thirteen of the fourteen are sound and one, card 5, is evasive. Of the eleven that recommend, one, card 0, should have withheld on its own arithmetic; card 18 is thin but keeps its direction under correction and is fairly held as a recommendation.

The real problem is not the number but that two different things wear the same label. Nine withholdings are genuine: the instrument cannot separate the options (cards 1, 4, 6, 11, 17, 20, 21, 22, 24). Four are not withholdings at all but work orders (cards 12, 13, and behind them 3 and 10), where the parameter cannot be ruled until somebody walks the site with a tape measure, captures a results page or asks a funeral director. Cards 12 and 13 are the clearest: both carry a gate that survives the ruling either way ("the gate the earlier round put on this parameter… is carried whichever option is ruled"), so the owner is being asked to rule between two ways of publishing a fact nobody has measured. Putting those rows to him as a ruling wastes part of his sitting.

## The three changes that would most improve page B

**1. One unit in the margin column, and a plain three-state verdict beside it.** Replace the mixed counts with "carries A", "carries B" or "cannot separate", and move the raw figures into the card. Card 14's forty-word cell goes into the card and its row reads "carries; thin". That alone makes the sheet scannable in one pass instead of two.

**2. Split the fourteen withheld rows into two blocks: "the evidence cannot separate these" and "nobody can rule this until X is done".** The second block is a work order with an owner and a cost, and the order of ruling has already done four fifths of the sorting ("A re-read of pages already held", "A lookup nobody has run", "A talk with the trade or a buyer", "A walk or a trial at the venue"). Lift that structure into the sheet and the owner sees at a glance which rows are his and which are somebody's next task.

**3. Fix the three cards where the page's own evidence is ahead of its own line.** Card 5: say that access leads 10 to 8, and 10 to 3 on distinct operators, that the independent clerk landed there too, and recommend it. Card 0: withhold, on the card's own admission that dropping the church units leaves a tie. Card 14: split it in two, because "name the cooling you have" is a page sentence the market carries 13 to 0 and "buy the plant" is a capital decision it does not carry at all, and the owner should not have to rule both with one word.

One more thing that is not a change to the page but a defect to be corrected before a ruling: **card 18's recommendation settles card 6's withheld question.** Card 6 withholds between a fitted room and the room as it stands; card 18 recommends the live-stream and the music "Inside the hire fee", which can only be true of a fitted room. The page's cross-reference section names cards 6 and 18 as two cuts of one tally but does not say that ruling 18 decides 6. The page's claim that "No pair of cards in this set recommends on opposite sides of one question, and none rules one thing two ways without naming the other" does not survive this pair.

## The verdict, in one sentence

Page B is a clear improvement for this owner, because it puts figures and options where page A put prose, it names what each recommendation rests on and where its instrument is blind, it can be ruled from four thousand words instead of twenty-seven thousand, and it removes page A's two worst faults, a price invented from one comparable and a week restricted to suit the wedding calendar; and I hold that at about eighty-five per cent, the doubt being that fourteen withholdings may read to an owner as a page that declines to advise, and one of them, card 5, deserves that reading.
