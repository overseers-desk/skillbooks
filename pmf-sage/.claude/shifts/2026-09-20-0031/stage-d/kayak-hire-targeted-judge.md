## The finding in one paragraph

The round did not run one way relative to the venue. Card 3 went from a line that kept the venue's holding to withheld. Card 2 went from a line that *left* the venue's holding to withheld: the self-hire recommendation stood against the grant application, the SOP outline and the P&L forecast, which all hold a guided paddle (`priors-pass.md`, card 2: "leaves to held"). What ran one way is carried to withheld, and the rule set explains that direction better than the evidence does. Eight of the nine rules can only take confidence away from a carried line. The ninth could move a line either way. It turned card 2 away from its carried line. On cards 5 and 21, where it would have carried a line toward the drafts, it was stopped by tests that were not put to card 2. It was never put to card 0, where its own logic would have withheld a line that keeps the venue's holding. So the rules were applied unevenly, but the unevenness does not consistently favour or oppose the owner. What it consistently favours is withholding. Both moved lines rest on readings that the same day's method refused elsewhere. Card 2's outcome is right for a reason the card does not lead with. Card 3's outcome is not right.

## Card 3 · Is this a quiet thing or an energetic one?

1. **Before and after, and direction.** Before, the card recommended "A quiet paddle on calm water by a margin of 5 units over An energetic paddle on moving water, resting on the market". It is now "withheld; between A quiet paddle on calm water and An energetic paddle on moving water … leans: a quiet paddle". Held went from keeps to held. The change runs away from what the venue holds: the Director's "very slow moving and safe" and the drafts' "serene" and "tranquil".

2. **Counts, and the rule that changed them.** The counts are the same before and after: "calm 9 units and 9 operators, moving 4 and 4, both 3 and 3, tidal alone 5". What changed is the weight given to the market-only derivation's "outing tempo words quiet 7, energetic 7". Rule one of the round moved it: "where that derivation ties the card's options the line carries only if it survives the test on a stated base". The clerk found that it did not survive, arguing that "the flow list is blind to both outings alike and sees calm water printed as scenery more readily than a quiet outing sold, a lean toward the leading option 5 units wide that could account for the whole lead of 5." The ninth rule was then put and moved nothing: the providers' water words run 32 to 6 for calm, and their unjudged outing words run 41 to 44.

3. **Applied alike? No, on two counts.**
   - *The count that turned it is one the method refuses elsewhere.* The tempo tie is one reader's count on V25, a variable "coded on no unit". The first clerk of the same day ruled on this card that "the method lets a count on a variable outside the frozen codebook turn a card only once a second coder has made it blind … so the tie enters the recommending sentence as the leg's limit and not as a turn." The ninth-rule entry on Card 5 refused a provider count toward the drafts for exactly that reason: "a count made to order … the method lets such a count turn a card only once a second coder has made it blind". On card 3 the same kind of count took a carried line away, dressed as an argument about lean rather than as a turn.
   - *The lean argument redefines the option.* The card's own option A is "A quiet paddle on calm water", and V2 counts calm water printed beside the offer. That is the first clerk's point: "V2 could see both of the options as the card defines them." The tempo reading got its gap between 12 calm-water units and 7 quiet-outing units by hand-dropping hits where the word described "the water itself ('the tranquil waters')". A venue printing "tranquil waters" next to its kayak hire is selling a quiet paddle, so the lean the clerk found is partly built into how the tempo count was made. The active list also takes generic copy words ("adventure", "expedition") that a family hire prints as readily as a whitewater run. The third derivation's own example is "a kayak or stand-up paddleboard adventure".
   - *Should-have-moved cards.* None in the direction opposite to card 3. The tie test had nothing else to catch: no other carried card's market-only derivation declined.

4. **Verdict: WITHHELD-EVASIVE.**

5. **Reason.** The card's evidence all points one way: V2 gives 9 to 4 "a lead … one venue coded the other way leaves at 8 to 5"; providers give 32 to 6; the capability line says "Nothing in the record states moving water on the reach (E10)"; and the card's own buyer prediction for the energetic offer is "Not with the baby along". The one thing on the other side is a one-reader tie that the method says cannot turn a card. The honest line is quiet, THIN on the market and bounded by the water's capability. It should not be a choice put to the owner between quiet and something the site cannot sell. The round's rule that "a held fact of the venue on the card's parameter stands in the table" should have bounded the energetic row with E10.

## Card 2 · Do I take it out myself, or does someone come along?

1. **Before and after, and direction.** Before, the card recommended "On your own: self-hire alone by a margin of 23 units over Both side by side, resting on the market", with Held at leaves. It is now "withheld; between On your own: self-hire alone and Both side by side … leans: self-hire alone", with Held at held. Relative to what the venue holds, this runs *toward* the venue's most developed drafts (the grant, the SOP outline and the P&L are guided; the proposal is "Guided or self-guided"). It runs away only from the Rezdy setup. The brief's framing, "away from the draft", holds only if the Rezdy setup is taken as the draft.

2. **Counts, and the rule that changed them.** The venue counts are unchanged, "self-hire 32, both 9, guided 7". The providers were promoted from context to a leg: "providers both 54, guided 30, self-hire 14". The ninth rule did this: "a card ruling none of the four dimensions reads both families alike, the providers as a leg as Card 19 reads them, or says in a clause why they cannot be one … Two legs ranking opposite ways leave no lead across them." The reversal is not an AU-P2 artefact. Pooled over every provider cell in A4, both leads self-hire alone 75 to 26.

3. **Applied alike? No.**
   - *The confounder used to stop card 21 was not put to card 2.* Card 21's ninth-rule entry held the line back because "The providers' lead is the guided tour's, and which product this venue sells is Card 2's." On card 2 the providers' lead *is* the guided tour's. The providers are paddle businesses whose buyer "arrives for the craft", and the market-only clerk said why that bears on this parameter: "the P family's buyer arrives for the craft and more often books a guide, so its reversal is context." The card's own set-aside points the same way. Taking out the seven venue units that describe themselves as paddle businesses cuts "both" from 9 to 5 units while self-hire barely moves (32 to 31). Selling both is a paddle-business trait. The rule asks whether the card *rules* a dimension, when the real question is whether a differing dimension *drives* the parameter. On this card it plainly does.
   - *Card 0 should have moved with it and did not.* Cards 0 and 2 "count one fact" (review, collisions). Card 0 carries one offer over several on V, 39 to 9, while AU-P2 ranks several first, 54 to 44. That is the same opposite-way ranking that withheld card 2. Card 0 escaped because its measured-in line lacks the literal sentence "The card rules none of the four dimensions" and still reads "P differs, so P's lean to several offers … is context and not a leg". That is the very reasoning the ninth rule struck from card 2. Card 0's Held stamp is keeps, so this miss runs in the owner's favour.
   - *The owner's bound was not used.* The owner has scoped retail families first. Among venues naming a household, self-hire alone leads 10 to 12 against both at 3 to 4. The ninth rule was put to the whole family and not to the buyer the owner has already ruled first.

4. **Verdict: WITHHELD-SOUND, on the gate and not on the reason the card gives.**

5. **Reason.** The card's own sentence carries the withholding: "the venue's policy excludes paddle craft (E89), no quote has been opened (E93) and the peak body's scheme covers a guided paddle only (E103) … the broker's letter is the gate". By the round's fourth rule that is a bound, and it decides. The card instead says E103 is not "published text [that] sets a bound", and it leads its because-clause with the provider reversal, which is confounded. Put the gate first, drop the providers back to context with the confounder named, and the line reads: self-hire for households if the broker writes cover for unguided hire, and guided otherwise.

**Summary of evenness.** The ninth rule reached cards by a sentence in their measured-in lines, not by what they measure. ## Is the one-way direction the evidence or the clerks?

My judgement: it is the clerks' rule set, not the evidence, and not an anti-owner bias.

**Strongest reason for "the evidence".** Both lines were genuinely exposed. Card 3's lead is 5 units, almost all from overnight parks on unlike water, and it reverses outside them ("moving water leads calm by 4 units to 2"). Card 2's two judged-code families rank in opposite orders in every provider cell, and a real insurance gate (E89, E93, E103) stands over the self-hire option. A careful marketer would not have signed either line unqualified.

**Strongest reason against.** Card 2's turn relied on a reading that the market-only clerk rejected with a stated cause and that the round itself used to stop card 21. Card 3's turn relied on a count of a kind that the round refused to let move card 5 and that card 3's own first clerk refused that morning. The one card where the new rule would have taken a line away from the venue's holding, card 0, was not tested. Weaker readings moved lines toward withheld, and equal or stronger readings were not allowed to move lines toward carried. That asymmetry is in the rules. The pages did not supply it. In the owner's terms, this is not "if owner believes A, A must be incorrect". It is a third failure next to his two, a method that retreats to "withheld" whenever a rule can be found, and it leaves him with 22 of 28 cards to rule himself.

## Counting each operator once

It is sound as a test of independence. Twenty-eight Canal and River Trust pages are one decision, not 28 votes, and the "survives one operator coded the other way" test only means something over operators. It carries four risks in this corpus.

- **Grouping is uneven across kinds of seller.** Conservation authorities are grouped by shared domain even where a site may set its own terms. The 24 holiday parks of six park companies are split unless a brand-level terms page is cited, and none was. The parks carry most of the self-hire, the one-offer and the hour counts. The Ontario authorities carry the launch count on card 0 (Rideau Valley alone holds 4 of 13 launch units). So the rule is lenient to the options the parks favour and strict to the options the authorities favour. Every lead that is "the overnight parks'" is somewhat inflated in operators. The cards give the ownership reading only "where it moves a figure", and for the 24 parks that reading would make six operators.
- **It swaps an exposure measure for an independence measure without saying which question it answers.** A format a chain repeats across many sites is a revealed preference: the chain would change it if it did not sell. A buyer also meets it many times. For demand-side questions the unit count is evidence in its own right, not just noise to collapse.
- **The rule is the one place where a second clerk will disagree** (its own note: registers and platforms excluded, and domain versus text), yet it decides margins close to the one-operator line. Cardigan Bay Active and Adventure Beyond are two operators by the rule and one price list by the findings, and both readings have been kept on four cards.
- **"One text across several sites" is the right trigger, but the rule mostly groups by domain.** One domain is not one text. Sites under one authority can print different hire terms, and grouping them hides real variation.

## Three changes before this rule is used on another run

1. **Replace "the card rules none of the four dimensions" with a stated judgement per card, made before counting, of whether a differing dimension drives the parameter.** The market-only clerk already does this (card 2: arrival drives the guide; card 5: "the craft a buyer sits in does not turn on the other three dimensions"; card 8: the price unit "turns on what is sold … more than on how the buyer arrives"). Put that judgement to every card, cards 0, 1 and 10 included. Apply the hire-seller split and any similar confounder check on every card where the rule fires, whichever way it would move the line. Where the owner has already bounded the buyer (retail families first), run the test on that buyer.
2. **Make the tests symmetric and log direction.** A one-reader or script count either can move a line or cannot, in both directions: it cannot take a carried line away if it cannot carry a withheld one. Every test that can withhold a carried line needs a mirror test that can carry a withheld line: a line where every leg leans one way and the lean survives the instrument test carries as THIN. A capability bound, such as card 3's E10, takes an option out of the pair instead of leaving it for the owner to rule. Each round should end with a tally of Held stamps before and after (keeps, leaves, held), so drift in either direction shows up without a judge having to find it.
3. **Fix one grouping principle and say which count decides.** Group by shared terms text, whatever the kind of seller: brand, authority or park company alike. Print the unit count, the operator count and the ownership count together on every card whose margin is within two operators. Before counting, state that operators decide robustness and units measure buyer exposure.
