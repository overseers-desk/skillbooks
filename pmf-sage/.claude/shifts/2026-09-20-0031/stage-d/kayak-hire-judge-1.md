# Kayak hire: page A against page B

The offering has never been on sale. Every value the venue "holds" is a draft: the June 2025 canoe proposal, the Rezdy setup, the lost grant application, the SOP outline, the P&L forecast, plus the Director's remarks of 3 September 2026 ("family-centric", "slow moving and safe", "not the same league"). The one standing bound is the scoping of 3 September: retail family day visitors first, trade packaging after a priced retail product exists. No card on either page has been ruled.

Because nothing was ever sold, the third fault cannot come from paddle sales. It comes in by the side door, when the venue's sales of *other* products at their only terms (the \$20 8-minute ride, walk-up at the till, weekend Rezdy bookings) are read as what a paddle buyer wants. Page A does this repeatedly. Page B refuses to, in so many words.

I checked three claims against the run folder. The findings' A6 headline ("more often an accompaniment threshold than a floor") does not reproduce against its own counts: 45 minimum age against 33 accompaniment (`findings-market-side.md` A6; `findings-corrections-2026-09-25.md` row 21). The fence-blind price draft was not blind to the venue's ride price: its extract prints "an 8-minute horse or pony ride is 20 dollars" (`superseded/capability-extract-for-price-draft.md` line 18). And the operational questions page B leaves off its cards (landing, reach, cover, permit, waiver, rescue) are filed in `card-set.md` as "work the records show that is no market question".

## The three clearest cases of each fault

### Page A

- **Echo of the owner.**
  - Card 2's Opposite: "the Director's statement excludes it". The recommendation's words "peaceful", "slow" and "in sight of the bank" come from his 3 September remark. They are tested only against Jenni and Cecilia, two international adults.
  - Card 6: "the double at the ride's \$20 ... the ride is the price that family already pays for a timed experience at the water's edge". The page then calls the fence-blind draft's agreement "the strongest thing on the card", although that draft read the same \$20 ride price in its extract.
  - Card 1's second half ("at the till ... beside the feed bag and the ride") goes beyond the scoping bound into the venue's present arrangement. Card 7 cuts the block to the Director's unmeasured 500 m. It is a capability input and is flagged, so it is thin rather than echo.
- **Contrarian.** Card 10 is the only clear case. It departs from the Rezdy draft's floor at 3 on "the rule is more often an accompaniment threshold than a floor", a headline that fails against its own counts. The card presents "no floor" as the market's answer when the market's commonest form is the draft's. There are no second or third cases: page A leans towards the owner, not against him.
- **Sales at the only terms read as preference.**
  - Card 13: "Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays and every day of the Queensland school holidays, the 8-minute-ride days", on "Rezdy weekends carry 500 of 739 bookings".
  - Card 15: "the venue's family arrives unbooked (Clover's weekend) ... a booked-only product with zero recorded asks opens with an empty calendar". The ride was only ever sold walk-up, so its buyers could not have shown they would book.
  - Card 1 (also Cards 0, 5, 6, 20, 25 in their Buyer lines): "a parent who has just paid \$20 for an 8-minute ride buys a \$20 double for half an hour at the same till".

### Page B

- **Echo.** No clear case. The nearest is Card 3. It keeps the drafts' and the Director's "quiet" on a 5-unit lead that reverses outside the overnight parks, and its market-only reader tied. It does not cite the owner, and it states that its instrument leans *against* the recommendation. Card 0's lead over the launch outside the parks (11 to 10) is a weaker second.
- **Contrarian.** None. Its two departures, Card 2 (self-hire, against the guided drafts) and Card 8 (per craft, against three per-person drafts), each carry a multi-leg lead and an agreeing market-only reading.
- **Sales read as preference.** None; the page names the fault and refuses it. Card 15: the venue's weekend and winter booking split "says when a household is on the grounds, not when it would paddle, and the counter's weekend precedent (E70) is a capability, not a preference". Card 14: the 10-day median lead "stands in for nothing here". On the own-buyer record, every card says it "could have held an ask either way and holds none".
- **The fault page B has instead: withholding what it holds.**
  - Card 23: lifejacket wearing withheld on a count of "provided", which the card itself says answers another question.
  - Card 6: the name withheld on a method gate, though its own evidence carries "kayak hire" for the self-hire Card 2 carries.
  - Card 7: "about twenty dollars" is buried in the card body while the sheet shows a withheld band. That is sound by the rubric but costly to the owner.

## Does page B treat agreement with the owner fairly?

Yes, with one small tilt towards the match, not against it. Page B makes the same demand of keeps and leaves: each needs a market-only reading made by a clerk who saw neither the venue's records nor the first clerk's work. The two leaves (Cards 2 and 8) both passed it. Five of the six keeps passed; Card 3 was carried with its market-only reader tied. No card counts a match with the drafts against a recommendation. Card 19 moved towards the drafts at correction and Card 33 stayed with them. Card 5 lost a keep and Card 4 a leave when their leads failed, so the corrections ran both ways, as the page itself records ("it fired both ways").

The real test page B applies is the instrument test: does the instrument's lean account for the whole lead? It applies that test evenly. The only visible unevenness is the parks. Cards 0, 2 and 3 carry leads that thin or reverse outside the overnight parks, while Cards 1, 13 and 16 withhold on similar patterns. The difference is explained on each card by which way the instrument leans, and it favours keeps and leaves alike: Card 0 keeps, Card 2 leaves. No departure passes on less proof than a match.

The Director's own words appear nowhere on page B's cards. The scoping bound is stated and honoured on Cards 1 and 12 and in the order of ruling. His "slow and safe", "family-centric" and "not the same league" are recorded only in the priors note. That is neutral treatment, not concealment: page B neither leans on them nor argues against them.

## What the owner has to read

**Page A** runs to about 37,900 words over 26 cards. Its one-page summary (about 2,000 words, the table itself about 800) gives each card's recommendation, provenance chip and whether it is ready or blocked. A second decisions table sits at the end. The chips "Owner-stated" and "Anchored" show where the card leans on him. Nothing tells him which of the 21 "ready" cards merely confirm his view and which ask him to change it, so the table cannot tell him where to spend his minutes. To rule responsibly he has to read the cards, about 29,400 words.

**Page B** runs to about 35,900 words over 28 cards. It opens with a ruling sheet (about 3,060 words) and the order of ruling (about 1,300 words), about 4,350 together. The "your part" column shows at a glance what each card asks: confirm 6 (Cards 0, 3, 9, 10, 19, 33), rule 2 (Cards 2, 8), commission or rule between two 20. The order of ruling lets him set aside six organiser-only cards for launch under his own scoping (6, 12, 14, 28, 31, 32). It also prices each withheld card's observation from cheapest up.

He could rule from those two sections alone, opening a card only where a row raises a doubt. In practice he would open Card 2 and Card 8 (the two departures), Card 3 (the thin keep) and probably Card 23 (lifejacket), about 5,000 more words. Two things make the sheet harder than it should be. The margin column is arithmetic in the sheet's own terms ("parks set aside 4–5, 4–6, 5–6"), readable only after the definitions paragraph. And the header says *rests on* is "blank on a withheld row", yet every withheld row reads "market".

Neither page shows the owner, at the point of ruling, every decision he must make before a boat goes on the water. Page A carries route bounds, launch point, waiver, rescue plan, staffing, cover and approvals as cards with gates. Page B keeps only cover and landing, in "Wrong today", and files the rest in `card-set.md`, which the owner is not directed to read.

## Does page B withhold too much or too little?

Of the 20 withheld cards, 18 are sound and 2 evasive (Cards 6 and 23). Of the 8 that recommend, one should have withheld (Card 3), and Card 0 is on the edge against its launch runner-up.

The larger problem is a matter of method, not count. Six withheld cards are safety rules: 21 age, 23 lifejacket, 24 swimming, 25 watch, 28 training, 31 weather. Their answers will be set by the broker's conditions, the regulation and the peak body's awards, not by what venues print. Page B sends them for more census coding, which costs money and cannot decide them. It also lists "A talk with a buyer or a trial at the venue: none" among its observations. Yet price (7), turns (16), payment (11) and walk-up against booking (13) all turn on counter behaviour the census says it cannot see. For a never-sold product, the one observation of real buyers is a priced trial at the venue once cover is written.

## The three changes that would most improve page B for a one-sitting owner

1. **Take the safety rows off the market census.** Mark Cards 21, 23, 24, 25 and 28 "set by the broker's conditions and the regulation", tie them to the broker letter already on Monday's list, and spend no Survey money coding venue print for them. Card 23 should carry "everyone wears one" now, on the under-12 rule, the peak body and every draft.
2. **Let his scoping shorten the sheet.** Move the six organiser-only cards (6, 12, 14, 28, 31, 32) below a line headed "not needed for a retail launch". Recast Card 6 as the household's till-board name, where its own evidence carries "kayak hire". The sheet falls from 28 rows to 22, and the owner sees that eight rows are ready and about fourteen wait.
3. **Put the sentence he needs in the sheet, and name a trial as the observation.** Replace the margin arithmetic with one plain line: what the market shows and why it was not carried (Card 7: "the market sits at about \$20 an hour a craft"; Card 10: "an hour, though the Director's 500 m may make it long"). Add a priced trial at the venue after cover as the observation that settles price, turns, payment and walk-up against booking.

## Verdict

Page B is an improvement on page A for this owner. It removes the echo and the sales-as-preference reasoning that run through A's buyer, positioning, price, days and channel cards, and it departs from him only where the market carries the departure. It costs him decisiveness: 20 of 28 cards come back as work, and six of those are safety rules the market cannot settle. I am fairly sure of this, about 70 per cent.
