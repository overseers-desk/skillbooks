# SAGE Adjudicate — the owner rules, on cards

Adjudicate is where the run's human hours go, and it exists for one reason: an AI asked to design or sell will offer what nobody decided to offer. Told to sell a pen, it promises a million dollars with the pen, because nothing tells it what it may not promise. The decisions table is the bounding box that stops this, for every downstream designer, absorber, claim-writer and outreach message.

## The card

Every product parameter is decided on a card, not carried in prose, and every card is a question. The market never returns one answer: comparables sell the same kind of thing in several formats, at several unit shapes, to several buyers, and a card that reaches the owner with a single answer got it from a document, because that is the only source that speaks in one voice. The card form is `forms/card.md`, and `tools/card-check.py` reads its fields. A card shows, in this order:

- the **question**: the parameter, in the words a buyer meets it;
- the **options**: each a value the market shows, carrying a demand figure with its source (a taxonomy prevalence, a search volume, an enquiry or rival count), a capability leg (the mechanism or resource, or the cost line of building it), and a seller's sentence: what the person at the gate says to a named roster profile on a Saturday, and that buyer's likely answer. A seller's sentence without a figure is not an option. Where the offering is already on sale, the terms it sells under are an option like any other, and its figure is the operator's own record of what buyers did at those terms (the bookings, the refusals, the enquiries that did not convert) with the condition line beside it. That record is censored by what was on sale: a sale taken at the only terms on offer shows that buyers accept those terms, not that they prefer them to terms nobody offered, as the condition line already says of an enquiry count. So the sales figure carries the on-sale option against another only where both were on sale at once; against an option never offered, what the record can show is what buyers did for want of it: asked for it, stalled, refused, or left. A value that only an internal document, a sibling run or an unsold present practice holds is not an option; it is a prior, and the priors clerk lists it last;
- the **recommended ruling**: one option, with its margin over the runner-up stated in the runner-up's unit; where the options are values (a length, a price) the margin is the count lead behind the recommendation, since a distance between two values says nothing about the evidence for either. A recommendation is carried when its leg could have shown the runner-up winning and its lead is more than a tie once the card's corrections are counted, and the test is asked alike of a recommendation that lands on what the owner holds and of one that leaves it. Where no option is carried the line reads *withheld*, naming the two readings and the one observation that would carry either;
- the **ruling**: the owner's decision, which may pick an option, combine two, or leave the parameter open as design freedom;
- a **provenance chip** on the value the ruling started from: *inherited* (copied with no market derivation), *anchored* (evidence fitted around a pre-held value), *derived* (traced from evidence independently), *radical* (a new decision that breaks the structure, not just the number). Compound chips are allowed and honest, and a card wearing *inherited* or *anchored* is the card to spend the most minutes on;
- the **measured-in line** under the demand leg: the stratum, the cell or cells, the numerator over the denominator, and the measured population's shape on the four dimensions of the operator's shape note, each marked *shared* or *differs* against this operator. A finding carries only as far as the shape it was measured in. Where the population differs on the dimension this card rules, the rate may not stand as the leg, and the clerk names on the card which of three things was done: re-measured within the sub-population that shares the shape, reporting that count even where it is a handful; kept the rate as context and taken the leg from the rival register or the demand notes, which read this operator's own catchment; or marked the card open with the one fact that would close it. A rate whose population differs is still quotable as a rate. Where the leg is an enquiry count rather than a corpus rate, the line instead names the condition the count was taken under: what was offered, under what name, on what surface. A count for an offering not offered, or not named as buyers name it, is provenance and not a leg; an unsolicited enquiry for something not offered is a leg on its own;
- the **cost lines**: the existence facts from the capability record that bear on this card, each named (a role not yet rostered, a system with no such product), so they enter as costs and never as constraints written into the question;
- the **four boundary tests**, argued between the options: *opposite* (why not the opposite direction), *further* (why not further in the chosen direction), *joint* (proof it was decided together with its neighbours rather than one at a time), *buyer* (where one exists, a prediction against a named live buyer from the rival register's demand data);
- the **prior values**, last, appended by the priors clerk and by nobody else: what old drafts, any earlier definition, a sibling run or local practice held, with the file and its stated range or word;
- the **third derivation**, where one exists: its file and where it landed against the recommendation (agrees, differs, or declines to recommend);
- later corrections as dated ruled-lines on the same card, so the card stays the whole history of that parameter. A correction that withdraws the leg under the recommendation rewrites the recommended line in the same act, to the other option or to withheld, and its dated line says what stood there before.

A card with fewer than two figured options is refused by the card check before the set is assembled and goes back to Survey as a named gap, since the corpus, the search findings and the rivals between them always show more than one way the market sells this. A parameter earns a card only where its ruling would change the seller's sentence; a parameter that would not is clerical, and it is left to Game as design freedom rather than put to the owner.

## The card set

Day one produces a strike list, not a card, on `forms/strike-list.md`: every buyer the offering could have is a row, with columns for what they type (a volume with its source and date, or "not searched"), who addresses them (how many of the comparables' own pages read speak to that buyer), the operator's records split by buyer (a count, its date range, and the condition line: what was on offer, under what name, on what surface), which rivals already sell to them, and a strike column. The form carries no recommendation field. Every figure names its source or reads "not searched", and "not searched" is a value the owner sees, not an empty cell. The owner's example, where he gives one, is one row's evidence and a comparability prior, never the scoping. The owner strikes the buyers the frame need not reach; an unstruck buyer is in. The list asks the only question day one can answer, and a list with nothing struck is the default. Card 0, one offer or several and for whom, is ruled at the second gate beside the buyer card, since its options need seller's sentences and a sentence needs a ruled buyer; on day one it exists only as the questions the owner's words raise, in the run record.

The second owner gate, the ruling round after the corpus is coded (the first is the returned strike list), opens with the buyer card: which buyer the product is for. Its options are every unstruck buyer, each with a figure from the source its strike-list row named (the corpus's buyer variable where the operators publish whom they sell to, and the market map's cluster for that buyer's phrasing, the enquiry record or the rival register's buyer mix where they do not), a capability leg and a seller's sentence. A buyer whose row read "not searched" on day one and is still unsearched at the second gate is an option marked so, not an option dropped. The owner rules one buyer or several. Only then is the seller's sentence written, one per ruled buyer, two sentences at most: what the person at the gate says to that buyer on a Saturday. The parameters those sentences contain get cards; a parameter absent from them is design freedom by construction. Several ruled buyers means one sentence and one card set per buyer, and the run record says so.

The order exists because the buyer named in a frame-builder brief shapes the population the whole corpus is drawn from, and a codebook frozen around that population cannot be un-frozen by a later ruling. Every brief line that fixes the buyer, the count of offers or a price shape carries RULED with the strike-list row or card behind it, or DEFAULT with its finding; `tools/launch-check.py` fails a brief that carries one without, and on day one no mark can yet be written, so no brief names a buyer at all.

## The two legs

A ruling is legitimate when it can name the demand evidence it answers and the capability that delivers it, both by citation: a finding number, a register section, a distributor note on one side; a standing capability record on the other. Where the operator keeps no capability records, Survey writes down the capability facts the run needs, so this leg has something to cite. A card missing a leg is not ruled; the parameter stays open.

The capability leg reads what exists; it does not police what the offering has not yet created. A role, a training record, a listing in the booking system, a licence that would exist only once the offering launches is not a bound on the offering, and never a standing caution over a review: its absence today is the state of every unlaunched product. It enters the card as the cost line of building it, or as the card's open question. The flip to watch for is "we do not yet have what the product needs" turning into "the product is constrained by not having it".

A search-demand leg is a demand leg whose citation is a numbered finding from what buyers type: the volumes, difficulty, intent and captures of Survey's fifth demand signal. It is not a search of the corpus, of forum posts, or of anyone's opinion of demand. Four cards carry it. The name card carries the candidate names priced against each other: volume, difficulty and the collision verdict for each. The surface card, whether the operator will own the page the demand lands on or sell through a third party, carries the cluster's volume and the operator's current share of it, and is the one card that cannot be ruled without this leg; where the run has no keyword instrument, the clerk raises that to the owner as the card's open fact rather than ruling without the leg or standing in for the instrument with judgment. The unit card, a booked and capped session against a walk-in, carries the market's own words: whether the demand phrasing runs to book, tickets and session, or to near me and free, says which unit the market expects, and it is labelled as the weaker evidence it is. The season card carries the monthly shape of the cluster where the rank database gives one. Ranking the offering against the operator's existing ones needs the value-per-conversion weights from the operator's fee schedule. Where LAMP's market map already holds them, the card cites the map; where the map is absent or past LAMP's refresh conditions, the card cites Survey's own study of the offering's cluster and the fee schedule directly.

Search demand is refused in three places. Not as a build-or-not gate: an offering sold on the property to a buyer already present can be right to build against no search demand at all, and the figure tells the run whether being found can be the plan. Not on the price card: cost per click is an advertising price, and no conversion rate turns volume into revenue. Not as a capability signal, which is the other leg's job.

This bounds the phase on both sides. Over-ruling, decisions with no demand leg, is the owner guessing, and it costs the Game phase its inventiveness, because every ruled parameter is one the arms may not explore. Under-ruling leaves the million-dollar pen open. The card discipline makes both visible: the legs are written on the card, and an unruled parameter is listed as design freedom, on purpose.

## The table

The output is the decisions table: the single register of what is ruled against what is merely recommended, compact enough to hand to any agent. The full cards, with their reasoning, live beside it; the table repeats only the rulings.

The rule that keeps every later phase honest: **a parameter absent from the table is design freedom, and a brief to any agent may not harden it.** An agent that writes "fixed" or "never" about an open parameter has invented a ruling; that is the self-infantisation failure, and the fix is to reopen the brief, not to obey it.

## Conduct

The owner rules; AI clerks, in three roles that do not share files.

Three **derivation clerks**, by area, write the question, the options, the legs, the seller's sentences and the recommendation. The fenced files, every sibling run and the venue's present-practice documents are closed to them by blacklist. The records they read hold behaviour and no opinion: what buyers did, with the terms they did it under where the offering is on sale, and no value from a draft, a plan, a meeting note, an earlier definition or an earlier run; for an offering not yet sold they carry no price, length, name or session shape for it. A deriving clerk therefore has no opinion to be honest about. Its job is selling the thing to a stranger, not reconciling documents, and each card's seller's sentences are the proof it did that job.

One **priors clerk**, in a single pass over every card, opens the fenced files and the operator's standing records, appends the priors line at the bottom of each card (every held value with its file: the owner's rulings, the terms on sale, whatever a draft holds), and stamps the chip by a stated rule: a recommendation matches a prior when it falls inside the prior's stated range or uses the prior's own word, and the card check applies the rule by script wherever there are numbers. The pass decides nothing; it appends and stamps. A match is not a defect and a departure is not a merit: what the owner holds (a ruling, the terms on sale, a figure in a draft) is a hypothesis the evidence may confirm as readily as overturn, so staying with it and leaving it are asked for the same proof. Every card on which the pass found a held value takes a third derivation, whichever way its recommendation landed, by a fresh clerk who saw neither the fenced files nor the first derivation; the price card always does, since the operator's own fee ladder is the one prior a clerk cannot unsee, and the ladder reaches that card last. The third clerk reads the market side alone, without the operator's own sales record, so the card shows what the market says with the venue's own buyers and without them. It reads no capability record either, so where it lands on an option the record says cannot be delivered, the card puts that cost line beside the landing. The chip is stamped from the two derivations by one rule on either side: where they agree, on the held value or away from it, the agreement is evidence about the market, the chip reads derived, and the card carries both citations; where they differ the card holds two readings, and its recommended line is tested as any other. A defect is a first derivation that matched with the files open, and the third derivation is what tells the two apart. How often the set matches what is held, and how often it leaves it, is reported to the owner as counts; neither rate returns a set. A held value with no third derivation recorded does.

The **integrator** assembles nothing by hand. `tools/assemble-review.py` builds the review from the card files, `3-decisions/integrator-fields.md` and, where present, `3-decisions/joint-ledger.md`, on the template `forms/review.md`; the integrator's prose enters only through the template's fields: the counts, the unlock section quoting cards by number, and the collisions, where the tool quotes each card's Joint line beside the integrator's own. The review opens with the ruling sheet, which the tool builds from the cards: one row a card giving the question, the recommendation or its withholding, the margin with its sign, whether the recommendation keeps or leaves what the owner holds, where the third derivation landed, and whether the card is ruled; the order of ruling and the groups ruled together follow it, so the owner can rule from the front and open a card only where he doubts a row. The cards come after as reference: an open card prints its question, the recommended option and its runner-up in full, and the other options as a name and a figure; a ruled card prints its ruling and its recommendation; corrections stay in the card file. Prose outside a field is not in the review, and a caution the integrator did not receive from a card does not exist. The counts, from the card check: figured options per card; recommendations that match a held value, that leave one, and that are withheld, each reported and none gated; held values with no third derivation recorded, which return the set to the clerks rather than to the owner; margins stated in a unit other than the runner-up's; demand legs citing a population marked differs on the dimension the card rules.

A brief or review relays an invariant by carrying its test whole. Flattening a conditional test into a prohibition rewrites the invariant in transit: a rule admitting a status mark where buyers are shown to consult it, relayed as "no marks", forecloses the very conclusion the study exists to be able to reach ("buyers want an accredited guide, get accredited"), and it does so upstream, by stopping the clerk from ever collecting the buyer evidence the test's condition asks for. The question behind a test's condition is always in scope. Any surface the owner rules from, the cards as presented to him or a sheet derived from them, states each cited fact in plain words with its number beside it, never the number alone; finding numbers and card codes belong in the internal record.

Expect correction rounds: cards come back, chips get re-stamped, and the count of rounds is part of the run's record. A card the owner cannot rule for lack of evidence goes back to Survey as a named gap, not into the table as a guess.
