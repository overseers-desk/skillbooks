# LAMP — whether the market can find one product, and what that is worth

**Phases:** Locate, Attribute, Move, Prove

## What LAMP is

LAMP answers four questions about one product the operator sells: can the people who would want this find us, what is stopping them, what should we do about it ranked by money, and did what we did last time work. It is inward-facing and longitudinal, and its answers are denominated in money, because the operator decides between products out of one budget and a rank position does not tell him which product to spend it on.

The four questions map one to one onto the four phases, and that is the reason the phases keep their names and letters through the restructure of 13 September 2026: Locate answers the first, Attribute the second, Move the third, Prove the fourth. Where a run cannot answer one of the four, it says so in that phase's own words and states what it would take, rather than answering an easier question in the same space.

LAMP is not a ranking audit of a whole site, not a content plan, and not a decision to build. It is a measurement discipline around a single product, with the actions as the experiment. Its posture is the one that runs a test: a change is not finished when it is deployed, and not finished when the series moved; it is finished when the movement has been read against what was predicted and against a control that was not touched.

## The unit of study

The unit is the thing the operator sells, decides on and budgets for: a product line with its own price and its own claim on marketing effort. It is not the page, and it is not the query family.

The unit used to be the query family plus the page meant to answer it, which took the unit from what the instrument could cluster rather than from what the operator sells. Two failures followed, named here so a later reading does not reintroduce them. Demand that no single product owned became invisible, because the visitor-job clusters that describe what a venue is rather than what it sells belong to no product's family. Things to do, attractions, day trips, school holidays, events this weekend, rainy-day activities and the region's own name are clusters of that kind, and a family boundary drawn from one product's page cannot contain any of them. And a product the operator sells without a page of its own, or whose booking lives on a third party's site, failed the definition and was ruled out of scope for lacking exactly the thing the run existed to find out about.

A product's demand and its pages rarely correspond one to one. Where they do not, the mismatch is a finding of the run, not a defect in the unit: demand spread across five pages and demand sharing one page with three other products are both studied whole, and the run reports the spread.

Three cases still need a decision, recorded in the operator's product inventory rather than argued again in each run. Similar products are one product when a searcher of either family would be satisfied by the other's page and one page answers both. A by-product is not a product when nobody looks for it on its own and it is bought on site as an extra; it is a line on the parent product's page and is studied there, marked as such so a later reader does not go hunting for its run. A product with no page is still a product, and its run's first finding is that the demand reaching it has nowhere to land, which is a finding about the operator's coverage and not a reason to decline the run.

A product's run is dated, so a product can be merged or split later without rewriting old runs. A run describes its own scope in its own words — the boundary it drew, the merges it accepted, the cap it worked under — and does not cite the brief that launched it, which no later reader holds.

## The market map

The market map is the artefact that says what demand exists and who holds it, above and across products. It is the source of every demand figure a run uses.

A judgement this methodology has settled, and states rather than leaves to the run: the market pass is not a phase and not a prerequisite run. It is a standing artefact the operator keeps beside the capture series, refreshed on a cycle, and no run starts without a current one. As a phase it would be repeated once per product, spending the rank database's quota many times over on one answer. As a prerequisite run it would belong to a call, and a run commissioned outside a call would have no map at all. As a standing artefact it is built once, consumed by every run, and audited by each run against what that run finds, which is how it stays honest.

The map is refreshed when it is older than the rank database's own refresh cycle, when the operator's offer changes, or when a run's own findings contradict it. A run that finds the map missing or stale builds it first; that building is the call's opening act and is dated as its own artefact, not folded into the first product's run.

The map holds, for the geography that can physically buy:

- The demand clusters, constructed from the market side: the rank database, the results pages' own related searches and question blocks, the operator's enquiry and phone language, and the trade's vocabulary. They are not constructed from what the site already ranks for, since a set derived from the site's own performance can only contain demand the site already partly holds. Each cluster carries volume, difficulty, seasonality, the site's own impressions against that volume, and which product or hub page could own it.
- The visitor-job clusters that sit above products, which is the layer the old unit hid. A cluster no product owns is a finding with a value attached and an owner proposed, not an observation parked for a study that may not exist.
- The value weight per product: value per conversion, referenced to the operator's fee schedule rather than restated, and a conversion rate from the operator's booking or point-of-sale systems, marked unknown where those systems do not hold one.
- The market's occupants, taken from the rank database's report of the domains ranking on the same keywords, and from a gap report of what those domains rank on and the operator does not. Non-business occupants count: aggregators, forums, council and tourism-board pages, listicles, the operator's own resellers, and the results page's own features.
- The products the operator sells that have no page, and the trade and institutional offers whose demand the console cannot see by construction, each carried as demand to be sized rather than deferred for lacking a page.

The map is what makes run order a money decision. It supplies the ranking, and the call's product inventory records it: products in order of demand multiplied by achievable click share, conversion and value, with the merges, the pageless products and the instrument cap beside them. A product whose clusters are too small for a study to repay itself is answered by its row in the map, a demand check of a paragraph, rather than by a run.

The map states, when it is built, the vendor refresh cadence it drew on and the date it is next due. A map that cannot say when it goes stale will be trusted past the point where it should have been rebuilt.

## The movement report

LAMP produces two kinds of report. A run studies one product and proposes what to do about it. A movement report studies one unexplained movement in a measured series, and proposes nothing.

A movement report's unit is one movement, named by that movement and bounded by dates: the fall in a metric between one date and another. The unit is not the site. A unit of that size would compete with products for ownership, and nobody can be accountable for a site the way an owner is accountable for a product. A movement competes with nothing, because it is a question rather than a subject, and a question does not need an owner in the way a sellable thing does.

That is also the enforcement. A report whose unit is a question carries no floor and no fork, since both are answers, so levers stay with products by construction rather than by promise. Anything actionable the report turns up goes to the run that owns that surface, or to the operator's register of concerns, and the report keeps none of it. A movement report that ends without choosing a cause has finished, and a movement report that ends by choosing one has exceeded its unit.

What it does instead, and what makes it worth running first: it produces the measurement ground every run then consumes. The retention wall of each instrument, the provider's error windows with their direction, the tag-break dates, the withheld-row fraction for the windows in use, and the control series. That is its output contract, and a run cites it rather than rebuilding it. Rediscovering that ground once per product is the cost the report exists to remove.

The report holds every candidate cause that could produce the movement, each with a status and none selected, and for each the single observation that would settle it, cheapest first, with who can make it. An observation only one party can supply is a finding in itself. Where the report cannot answer at all, it says which instrument would have to exist, which is the same refusal Locate's gate makes for a lever.

Where a call asks for both kinds, the movement report runs before the runs, because its outputs are their inputs. That is an input contract rather than a schedule, and the ordering follows from it wherever the work is placed. A call that asks only for runs leaves them to establish the same ground themselves, once per product, which is the cost the contract removes. Running first costs the movement report the stronger half of its causes, since the evidence that settles a cause often arrives from product work. So its causal section stays open across the call and is revised as runs report, while the measurement ground is fixed at the outset. It is one document in two states, not two documents.

## Runs and the methodology

LAMP runs when the operator calls it, over the range of dates and the set of products he names. It has no cadence of its own and nothing in it repeats on a calendar. A call is the body of work one such request produces: the movement report where one is asked for, the runs, and the comparison across them. The word marks a boundary of scope rather than a period of time, and two calls may cover the same months.

A run is one product studied once, on one date: a dated document in the operator's own repository, beside the operator's capture series and the market map. This methodology holds what every run shares; the run holds everything about one product.

The dividing test, applied to any fact: would a run for a different product use it unchanged? If yes, it belongs in the operator's standing records, the market map or the capture series, referenced by the run. If the fact exists only because of this product, it belongs in the run. Nothing product-specific belongs here, and no figure with a home elsewhere is restated here or in a run: a price is referenced to the operator's fee schedule by section, never copied.

Every artefact is committed to the operator's repository as it is made, and one that is not committed does not exist. It is unreachable from another machine, from a later session and from anyone the operator hands the work to, and the report citing it cites nothing a reader can open. Placing a file correctly is half the job; the other half is that the repository knows it is there.

Captures a run makes — a located results page, a Maps list, a performance export — go into the operator's capture series under that series' naming, never into the run folder. The run cites the capture file. A capture sitting inside one run's folder is invisible to the next product's run and to the site-wide series, and it will be fetched again at a cost.

## The instruments

A run draws on five kinds of source, in the order below, which is also the order of their authority over the question each answers. The run names the instances; this methodology names the kinds, and a run's headings call each by its kind name rather than by its number here.

1. **A rank database** with keyword volumes, difficulty, the positions of any domain on any keyword, an authority score, referring domains, and reports that return the domains ranking on the same keywords and the keywords a set of domains ranks on. This is the demand instrument and the competitor instrument, and it is the only one that can see demand the operator does not capture. Its volumes are modelled and banded, its long tail truncates to zero, its coverage of small geographies is poor, and it cannot see a local pack. It is primary all the same, because the alternative is blindness to exactly the thing being asked about.
2. **The search performance console** of the operator's site: queries, pages, impressions, clicks and average position by day, device and country. This measures the operator's position inside demand the map has already established. It is never a demand instrument: it reports only queries on which the site appeared, its impressions are demand multiplied by the site's own visibility, its withheld rows bias the undercount toward the long tail and are measured for the window in hand rather than carried from another run's figure, since the withheld fraction moves with the window, the filter and how thin the slice is, and its position figure is an impression-weighted blend across locations and devices rather than a rank. It counts a local pack as one position shared by every site in it, and it is subject to the provider's own anomaly notes, which the run reads before charting. Its query and page dimensions combined count an impression once per page the query reached, so a family measured by query and page together reads larger than the same family by query alone, while a page so measured can read smaller than the page alone where the console withholds low-volume combinations. The family's size is read by query, the page's by page, and the split across pages is a separate table that totals to neither.
3. **A located results-page capture**: the page as a searcher at the operator's location sees it. The capture records every occupant that takes space above or instead of an organic result: the local pack with categories and review counts, any AI overview, the questions block, tours and product carousels, the organic order, and the related searches. For an overview it also records whether the operator is cited inside it and what the overview says. An overview stored as a token and a link is not captured; fetching its content is part of the capture and happens in the same pass, because whether the click is taken before organic position matters is the first thing a lever aimed at that page needs to know, and a token redeemed hours later may no longer answer. Citation inside an overview is a form of standing and has its own row. A capture is one searcher's view on one day; the operator keeps a standing set, and a run reuses that set before spending on new ones.
4. **The pages themselves**: the operator's page and each occupant's, fetched raw for title, headings, structured data and the presence of prices, and read for what they answer and whether a page for the intent exists at all.
5. **The money and off-search instruments**: the booking systems, the point of sale, the site analytics, the business profile, the reseller channel, reviews and social. These answer two questions no search instrument can. They give the value per conversion and the conversion rate without which nothing in a run can be ranked by money. And they guard against a product whose demand is real but not search-shaped being read as low demand and defunded. A product converting steadily through a reseller and repeat custom, on a thin search family, is a finding about the channel rather than about the product.

A budgeted instrument is spent under a cap the operator's inventory sets, after the standing capture set has been reused, and every result is saved to the series whether or not it turned out useful. The cap and the spend are recorded in the run. Instrument calls left unspent at the end of a call are a finding about the cap, not a saving.

The cap is set in queries, not in calls, because a query whose page carries an overview costs two calls and a cap set in calls lets one overview eat a third of a run. A call that returns an empty state is spent, and the rule for unspent calls does not cover it: the run records what came back and what that empty state cannot be told apart from, and where one more call would not separate those explanations it names the sequence that would and declines to spend on it. An instrument that answers nothing is a finding about the instrument.

## The phases

**L — Locate.** How much demand exists for this product, what share of it we get, what that share is worth, and whether search can measure this product at all.

The window is the run's choice and not this methodology's, which carries the rule for choosing and no dates. A run names its windows in its own record, with the reason for each. A window opens after the last seam that makes earlier data unusable, and the run names that seam rather than the date it fell on. The reason expires and the date outlives it, so a window pinned to a date will still be opening there long after the seam has stopped mattering. One window rarely serves: a product's own standing and a matched year-on-year comparison usually want different stretches, and a run takes as many as its questions need.

Each instrument's retention wall is a hard floor under any window reaching back, and the run checks it before choosing rather than after drawing the chart. A year-on-year window is matched by calendar day on both sides. Normalising to a per-day rate does not rescue a side that is missing days, because the missing days are rarely the average ones: a month short of its opening fortnight, compared against a full one, can read as a rise where the matched days give a fall.

Locate opens from the market map, not from the console: the product's clusters with their volume, difficulty and seasonality, and the value per conversion and conversion rate the map carries, referenced to their homes. Against that, the console gives the operator's position inside that demand: the family across all pages and the page across all queries, as impressions, clicks and position by day, with the share of searches on which the site appears at all. The two populations differ from each other and both differ from the map, and the run states the gaps rather than averaging them away. Of the two numbers behind a share, the rank database's volume is the softer: it is a banded estimate on a vendor's cycle, while the console counts every impression it served. A share too high for the position to allow — most of a term's searches at page five, say — indicts the volume estimate or the run's cluster boundary, not the console, and is reported as that. A product page that the provider shows as a sitelink under the brand carries brand traffic many times its product demand, so the page's brand share is reported beside the product's standing and never folded into it.

Locate also gives the instance apart from the population: the located capture, with the features that occupy the page, the pack members, the organic order, and the product page's position or absence. The instance and the population disagree by design and are never merged.

Locate lists the dated events on the product's series, taken or suffered: a retitle, a redirect added, a page that died, a listing changed.

Locate ends with two statements. The first is the product's standing in one sentence a stranger could check, carrying the demand, the share and the money. The second is the gate: the minimum detectable effect, computed from the family's own daily counts over the last clean stretch, expressed in the same units a lever's prediction will use. The question the gate answers is whether the smallest effect a plausible lever could produce is larger than the variation the series shows on its own over a window the operator will actually wait.

The calculation, for a daily count series: take the mean daily figure and the day-to-day spread over the last clean stretch, and ask what change in the window mean a window of the length the operator will wait could separate from that spread. For counts, the spread rises with the square root of the count, so the detectable change over a window falls roughly as the square root of the window's total; a family at single-digit impressions a day will rarely clear a fortnight, and the run should reach that from its own arithmetic rather than from this sentence. A run is free to use a stronger test and names which it used. What the gate will not accept is a run that proposes without stating the number, because the number is what makes the refusal a finding rather than an apology.

**A — Attribute.** Why the share is what it is.

Hold at least three candidate causes before naming one, and for each state what the data shows and a status: verified, inferred, or open with the observation that would settle it. Three is the number that forces the work: the first candidate is the obvious one, the second is the obvious objection to it, and the third is where a run stops confirming and starts ruling out. This structure carries the run's validity and the restructure left it alone.

What has changed is that Attribute names which blocker applies from a set, because the blockers carry different fixes at different costs and calling them all "ranking" hides that. No page exists for the intent. A page exists but answers a different intent. The page ranks but is not clicked. The page is clicked but does not convert. The page cannot rank because a results-page feature or an occupant the operator cannot reach owns the space. Or the demand is not search-shaped and arrives through a reseller, the profile, repeat custom or the trade. More than one blocker can apply, and the run says which is binding.

The recurring causes for a local product are the profile's category against what the pack rewards, the profile's review count against the pack's, the page's topical focus and its age on the query, the citations on the pages that outrank every venue, and the site's authority. Two things usually turn out not to be causes and are worth ruling out explicitly: raw authority, and on-page technique.

The competitor set comes from the market map, not from the ladder above us. Naming "the competitors directly above the product" as the target set defined the field relative to the operator's own position on a query the operator had already chosen to look at, and it returned unreachable landmarks that happened to sit in a pack while leaving unnamed the domain converting a smaller keyword footprint far harder than ours. The map's occupants are the field; the located capture shows who holds one particular page; and "directly above us on this query" survives only as a tactical note inside a lever, never as the set. Passing an aggregator the product already appears on is not a target at all.

**M — Move.** What to do, ranked by money, with a prediction attached to each.

Every lever carries five things: the cause or blocker it addresses, the evidence that suggested it, a predicted magnitude with a metric and a date, an expected value in money a year, and a cost. The predicted magnitude takes the form that this change should add this many clicks a month to this cluster by this date. Without it Prove can read that a lever moved, did not move, or cannot be read, but never that it disappointed, and the operator's fourth question becomes unanswerable in the negative.

The expected value is demand times achievable click share times conversion rate times value per conversion, each factor with its source named and its value referenced to its home. Achievable click share is the share of the cluster's searches the lever would turn into clicks on the operator's own page. It reads as one factor, rather than a share times a click-through at the target position, because no instrument in the set supplies a click-through-by-position curve and a vendor's published curve would be a borrowed figure with no home in the operator's records. The methodology settles that here rather than leaving each run to hunt a curve: the share is the analyst's judgement, stated per lever with the reasoning behind it and marked as judgement, and a run that wants it harder says what would supply it.

Where a factor is unknown, an enquiry-to-booking rate no system holds being the usual case, the run names it unknown, carries it as a band, and ranks the lever on the band's lower end. A band says where each end came from, end by end: the operator's record, with the record named, or the analyst's judgement. An end that says neither is the impressions failure wearing a range, and a band with both ends on judgement is named as the run's weakest figure. A band is also checked against what the operator can deliver: where the upper end implies more conversions in a year than the operator has the capacity to serve, that is evidence the upper end is wrong and is reported as such, not carried as upside.

It never substitutes impressions for a missing factor, which is the substitution that made the whole discipline rank by traffic. The money factors are referenced and never restated, so the run publishes the product and names the section a reader opens to check it. The figure's job is to rank, not to forecast: the ratio between two levers survives assumptions that the absolute does not, so a run's annual figure is used inside the run for ordering and is not quoted outside it as a forecast. The predicted magnitude and the expected value run on different clocks and are not meant to reconcile: the magnitude is what should be readable on the series at the read date, and the expected value is the lever's worth over a settled year, which it reaches later than that.

The gate from Locate binds here. A lever whose predicted magnitude sits below the minimum detectable effect is not proposed. A precondition is the exception the gate would otherwise refuse for the wrong reason: a change that unlocks another and has no separable magnitude of its own, a merge whose gain is in position rather than in audience being the usual case. It rides in a bundle with the lever it unlocks; the bundle carries one predicted magnitude, which is what has to clear the gate, and Prove reads it as one event. The parts of a bundle are not credited separately, and where the parts would each be read on their own it is a list of levers and not a bundle. Where no lever clears the gate, the run proposes nothing for this product and says so as its finding, in one of two shapes. Where the off-search instruments show real demand converting through other channels, the finding is that this product's demand is not search-shaped, that further search spend on it is waste, and where the demand does arrive instead. That is a recommendation, and a money-saving one. Where neither search nor the off-search instruments show demand, the finding is that the product has no discoverable demand at all, which is a product question and not a search one, and the run names who owns it. Either way the refusal is pre-registered, reached before the levers are drafted, so it costs the analyst the work rather than arriving as a consolation after it.

The levers are one list in one order, ranked by expected value against cost. The split into floor and fork names who acts; it does not split the list and it does not excuse the analyst from ranking.

A **floor** is a lever whose expected value exceeds its cost on the run's own numbers, whose cost falls inside a resource the operator has already granted, and which is reversible at that cost. The granted resource is whatever standing authority the operator has stated: a budget, an instrument quota, permission to edit the site or a listing without a sitting. The grant's home is the call's product inventory, which records it per product where it differs and names the document that states it, because an operator states authority wherever it happens to arise — a change plan, a register of concerns, a set of design invariants — and a run left to go looking will find a different grant each time, or find one by accident. Hunting for a grant is therefore not a run's job, and a grant a run found for itself does not make a floor. Where the inventory carries none, every lever is a fork, and the run's first item is to ask the operator what the standing grant is and to propose the entry, since a discipline that cannot tell a floor from a fork has nothing to put in front of him in one sitting. Size does not disqualify a floor: the old definition, clearly correct with no judgement needed, routed every large lever to the operator by construction, because large levers always need judgement, and left the analyst with redirects and structured data. A floor is not a cheap action nobody would object to; it is an action that survived the thinking, so the operator does not have to repeat it. An action whose evidence the run cannot state, or whose mechanism the run's own Attribute table ruled out, or whose target leaves no headroom, is not clearly correct but unexamined, and belongs in the forks or nowhere. A page already at position three on the query a lever names has no headroom, whatever the lever costs.

A **fork** is a lever the run cannot settle because it turns on something outside the run's evidence: a resource the operator has not granted, a positioning choice about what the business is, or a genuine branch where two routes sit close enough on the numbers that the tiebreak is preference. A fork still carries its expected value, its predicted magnitude, and the run's own recommendation with the reason. A fork that hands back a question without a number and without a recommendation is the analyst declining to work, and it is the failure this split exists to prevent: the operator is choosing between priced options, not being asked what he thinks.

Move proposes; it does not act. A lever taken is dated in the run. Where the operator already keeps a register of concerns with their status, that register stays the home of status: the run's levers are candidates for it, named as such, and the run does not grow into a second register. A product the register carries no concern for gets its top-ranked levers listed as candidates for a new concern, which is itself the first item put to the operator.

**P — Prove.** Whether what was done delivered what was predicted, against a control.

Prove reads each dated event on the product's series: a lever taken, or a loss suffered such as a page dying, since a loss is the same experiment run backwards and is often the largest effect on the series. A dated movement with no known cause is also an event; its row carries "cause unknown", and Attribute holds it as an open cause with the observation that would settle it, since a gain nobody made can be lost the same way.

Not every event has a recorded date. An operator may keep a whole class of change out of version control deliberately, a redirect held in the web server's configuration being the common case, and no commit then dates it. Such an event still enters the ledger, with its date inferred from the series: the row says the date is inferred and names the observation it rests on. An inferred date is a weaker date and the reading says so. That the operator keeps no dated log of these changes is a finding for the run's closing section, because a change nobody dated can never be read as an experiment.

Every reading is against a control, and a delta not read against one is not a reading. The control is the site-wide non-brand series over the same window, and, where the operator has one, a comparable product left untouched. A lever that met its prediction while the control rose by as much reads as no effect, not as delivered. Organic traffic moves with the season, with the provider's updates and with the rest of the site, and a verification without a control cannot separate the intervention from the weather.

The effect ledger has one row per event, carrying: the event and its date, marked inferred where the date was read off the series; the lever's state, which is proposed, taken on a date, or not taken; the prediction Move recorded, with its metric and read date; the metric read, with the per-day figure before and after over matched windows; the window length; the control's movement over the same window; the seams inside the window, such as a page swap, a tag change, a vendor anomaly window or a redesign; the season, checked against the same weeks a year earlier where the series reaches; and the reading.

The readings are six. **Delivered**: the movement met or beat the prediction and exceeded the control. **Underdelivered**: the movement was real but short of the prediction, or matched it only because the control moved with it; the row states the shortfall and what it implies about the mechanism. **No effect**: nothing moved that the control does not explain. **Too early**: the after-window has not closed, with the read date Move already recorded. **Not readable**: a seam blocks the window; the row names the seam, which is a finding about the timing of the action rather than a gap in the run. **Moved, unpredicted**: the movement was real against the control and nothing was predicted, so whether it is a success cannot be said; the row states the movement and states that the missing prediction is what makes it unreadable as a result. Delivered, underdelivered and no effect were written for an event that carried a prediction, and the first two cannot be reached without one.

That set is deliberately shorter than the one it replaces. "Too thin to read" is gone as a reading, because the minimum detectable effect at Locate now refuses the lever before it is proposed, and a discipline that discovers after the work that the work was unmeasurable has spent the operator's money to learn something a day-one arithmetic would have told it. "Not yet" is gone as a reading, because whether the lever was taken is a field on the row, not a verdict about its effect. What remains is one exit, "not readable", and it must name the seam that blocks it. The sixth reading is not a second exit: it is available only where the row's prediction field is empty, which under this methodology means an event suffered rather than taken, or one predating the requirement. A lever taken always carries a prediction and can never reach for it. A verdict set with an exit for every failure and none for a disappointment flatters whoever ran the work, and across a whole call will never produce the sentence that a thing we did was a waste of money.

A reading is admissible when both windows are the same length, at least two weeks each, the control has been read over the same window, and the season has been checked. Where an unrelated seam sits inside the window an event would take, the window steps back to the last clean stretch of the same length before that seam and the row says so; where no clean stretch exists on one side, the row reads not readable. A recurring event, whose demand spikes before each date, has no clean stretches at all on a calendar reading: its windows are matched by cycle, the same days before one occurrence against the same days before the next, so an action is read across cycles rather than across a fortnight. A change that moved impressions but not position, or position but not clicks, is reported as exactly that.

Where no ledger row is admissible, the product's delta over the window is read off the segment table instead, against the control, and says so: better, worse, unchanged or mixed across the segments, with no action credited for it. A delta names what it is measured against in the same breath as the verdict, because a table of verdicts whose rows silently compare different things reads as one comparison and is not one. A product whose page did not exist through the earlier period has no standing to compare; what its year-on-year figures show is whether the demand was there before the page was, which is a finding about the market and is labelled as one. A seasonal or recurring product is never read early-window against late-window, since that measures the season rather than the product.

Prove is what a later run of the same product opens with.

## Seams

A series that spans a change in how it was measured, or in what it measured, describes neither side. Before charting, list the seams: the provider's anomaly windows, the date a different page took over the query, the date the analytics tag coverage changed, the date the site was rebuilt. Chart each seam as a dated line on the series; never compute an average across one. Where a metric is unsafe inside a window, say so at the chart and use the metric that is not.

Where the provider's note says only that a metric was not accurately reported, the period is kept rather than dropped, and every comparison that crosses it carries a footnote quoting the provider's entry with its date and URL. What the footnote may claim depends on what can be found out, and the run finds out rather than assuming: the provider's own statements to the press, its engineers in public, and the trade coverage often give a direction the help page omits, and sometimes a third-party estimate of size. A common-sense reading that an error this visible would have been caught early is worth holding and worth checking, because it has been wrong: one such error ran about fifty weeks before the provider acknowledged it. Where a direction is known and a magnitude is not, the affected metric carries no delta across the boundary, since the comparison would manufacture a movement of unknown size; the unaffected metric carries the comparison, and the affected one is shown per side, for scale.

## The audit

A run is not delivered as its author leaves it. Between the run document being finished and its artifact being published, a cold agent reads it against the classes below and returns what it finds.

Cold means it holds the run document and the captures the run cites, and nothing else. It does not receive the brief, the analyst's reasoning, the conversation that produced the run, or the run's own account of what it found. An auditor told what the run concluded will confirm it, which is the failure the whole step exists to avoid. It reads the captures and not only the document, because a class of defect shows only when a figure is recomputed from the source it was drawn from.

The classes are what has actually escaped a run and reached a reader, and the list is kept because an auditor asked to find problems returns prose and an auditor given the classes returns defects.

1. **Two sides that are not equivalent.** Any comparison of two windows, populations or segments whose sides differ in length, coverage or composition without the document saying so. A window truncated on one side; a per-day figure standing in for a truncation whose missing days are not random; a year-on-year statement where the source does not hold both years; a set labelled as excluding something it still contains.
2. **A condemned figure used again.** A window or metric the document has already flagged as not comparable, then carried elsewhere in the same document as a delta, a percentage or evidence.
3. **A label that does not match what it sits on.** A population named for what it does not hold; a crossing or turn marked where fewer than two series hold values on both sides of it; a range labelled with dates the series does not cover; a status outside the document's own stated set.
4. **A claim about material not read to the end.** Any characterisation of a source, a sibling document or another artefact the run holds only an excerpt of.
5. **A known instrument defect not applied.** The run lists what is wrong with each instrument, then states a figure the listed defect covers, without the correction or the caveat.
6. **A verification sharing a source with the thing verified.** Two figures agreeing where one derives from the other, reported as confirmation. It passes by construction and reads as a strong result.
7. **A quantity assembled from part of a source, without saying a part was taken.** A demand, family or population figure built from hand-picked members while the source assigns the whole to that unit. The narrowing is often right, since a unit is entitled to exclude what it cannot serve; the defect is that it is silent, and that the excluded part is frequently larger than the part kept.
8. **An event dated or attributed from prose.** A date or cause taken from a narrative summary rather than from the record that fixes it, and not tested against the bulk changes of the same period.

The auditor also returns one finding outside the list, or says it found none. The list is a frame, and a frame returns the defects it was drawn around; the run that first met a defect no class covers is the run that adds the class, named in the commit.

Findings come back split the way Move splits levers. A **floor** finding is arithmetic with no judgement in it: recompute on matched days, drop a delta and show both sides, state a coverage the page omits. It is fixed before publication. A **fork** finding changes what the run concludes, or turns on data the captures cannot settle. It is published with the run and carries the auditor's reading, because what the run concludes is the operator's to settle and an audit that quietly rewrote a conclusion would be a second analyst rather than a check.

The audit is recorded in the run: the classes checked, the findings, and which were floor and which fork. A run whose audit found nothing records that, since a clean audit is evidence about the run and an absent one is evidence about nothing.

## Sequencing runs

Runs go in the order the market map sets, by expected value, not by whose evidence is richest. As the market map section says, a product whose clusters are too small for a study to repay itself is answered by its row and a paragraph rather than a run, and that paragraph is the finding.

Runs go one at a time, not in parallel, while the methodology is young: each run is a test of whether this document reads correctly to a fresh agent, and a flaw found by one run is fixed here before the next run starts, with the run that taught it named in the commit. That discipline has a known blind spot, and it is worth stating so the next call does not trust it too far. A self-audit run from inside the frame returns only defects inside the frame; the restructure of 13 September 2026 came from an audit commissioned from outside it, against the operator's question rather than against this document. A call that produces only in-frame corrections has not been checked.

## Versioning

The restructure of 13 September 2026 is the first change that invalidates a completed run's reading, because runs made before it rank by impressions and carry no prediction, no control and no money. Runs from before that date are read as measurements and not as recommendations; the measurements are sound and stand. A run states its date, which fixes which reading governed it.

Whether an artefact is versioned or rebuilt is decided by what cites it, not by its age. An artefact other artefacts cite by figure is versioned: the market map and the product inventory, because a run takes its run order and its family estimates from them, and overwriting one strands a citation that can no longer be checked. A new dated file goes beside the old.

An artefact nothing cites by figure is corrected in place, and a run and the master comparison are both of that kind. Correcting one strands nothing, because the dependency runs one way: runs cite the inventory, the master cites the runs, and nothing sits below the master.

Most redrafting is correction rather than a new reading. A run redone because it was wrong is an edit of that run: the same file, the same name, and nothing in it pointing at what it said before. Correcting in place rests on the earlier text being recoverable, so the document is committed before it is corrected; where it was never committed, correcting it destroys it. The folder then holds one document per subject and the history holds the rest. A second file invites a reader to open the wrong one, and a reader who has to hold two documents and work out which half of each is live has been handed the history of how the answer was reached instead of the answer.

No document assumes an order of reading. A reader opens whichever one he was handed, and no report of this methodology may rest on his having read another first, whether that other is the version it corrects, a sibling product's run or the map it draws on. So the corrected document carries every fact a reader needs to act on it, and it carries no account of what changed. Citing the earlier report is fine and often right, since provenance is worth having. What fails is a citation standing in place of the fact: pointing at a figure rather than stating it leaves the reader to fetch a second document and work out which half of it is still live. Its published page is rebuilt at the same URL, so a link already circulated resolves to the current answer, and two pages for one subject is the failure at its most visible.

A correction reads what it replaces in full before writing, and carries forward everything the new work does not supersede. A money ranking rebuilt over a standing measurement can silently drop the measurement. That is a merge requirement rather than a versioning one, and it is what makes correcting in place safe.

A date does not version anything, and a correction usually lands the same day as the thing it corrects. Two artefacts on one subject bearing one date cannot be told apart by their names, and a suffix reached for to separate them is the signal that one of them should have been an edit.

## Relationship to the other methodologies

A product LAMP studies is usually one SAGE established or PLACE described; LAMP asks whether the market can find it and what finding it is worth. The value per conversion LAMP ranks by is the operator's own, referenced to its home, and where SAGE has ruled a product's price that ruling is the home.

A site-level observation a run meets and cannot use goes to the call's movement report, whose causal section stays open across the call for exactly that traffic. The run's closing section is where it waits, and the movement report is where it lands. Parking an observation for a document that does not exist discards it, whatever the parking is called, which is why the movement report runs before the runs that feed it rather than being commissioned when someone notices the pile.
