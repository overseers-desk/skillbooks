This is a Kahneman's system 2 question. The sections that bear on it are Writing for a reader (the cold-reader pass and the sorry-im-late discipline), LHD (the push gate on comment lines), and Vocabulary expansion (coined words that name no real thing); I have re-read them in context.

What follows is my reading of the hook message, stated as data.

## What the message is

It is the text of a push-gate block: output a hook emits when it intercepts a `git push` and refuses it. The audience is whoever is driving the push, which in this plugin's setting is normally an AI session acting for the user. The block is one of a numbered series (it is labeled "[2] Cold read"; there is presumably at least a "[1]").

## When it fires

It fires when the diff the push would publish adds more than 2 comment lines or more than 25 source lines, counted across everything the push carries. The counts are measured against some base revision, which the message calls `{base}` without defining how it is computed; the phrase "from where the session's work began, which reaches back past the last push" implies the base is the state of the branch before the current session started its work, not merely the remote tip. So a session that has already pushed once is still measured, for cold-read purposes, from its original starting point, so that the reader sees the whole of the session's change rather than only its most recent tail. How the hook knows where the session's work began is not stated in the message.

## What it requires, of whom

It requires the pushing session to do three things before the push may proceed:

1. Run `/sorry-im-late --since {base} --but-also-you-are-not-my-mum`. This spawns a fresh agent that has the project's files but none of the current conversation, and that agent cold-reads the session's accumulated diff.
2. Fold the cold-reader's findings back into the work.
3. Rerun the same push command with `COLD_READ_DONE=1` included in it. That token is the bypass: with it present, the gate lets the push through.

The cold-reader looks for four things: insider framing (text that presumes the conversation), leftover deliberation (traces of the route rather than the result), coined vocabulary that names no real thing (words invented mid-session that grep to nothing in the project), and references only the conversation can resolve. The `--but-also-you-are-not-my-mum` flag adds a fifth check, accretion: comments that restate the adjacent code or repeat what a name already says. The rationale given for that check is that each rework of a piece of code tends to lay down another such restating line, so they accumulate. The stated repair preference is deletion over explanation: a conversational reference should usually be removed, not annotated into legibility.

## What satisfies it and what violates it

The gate is satisfied by actually performing the pass, incorporating what it finds, and pushing with the token. Nothing in the mechanism verifies that the pass happened; the token is self-certification. The message is explicit that this is by design: "working around this block is the point," meaning the block exists to be bypassed, and the value it produces is the one deliberate pass taken on the way to the bypass. It is therefore violated, in spirit, by adding `COLD_READ_DONE=1` without running the cold read, or by running the cold read and discarding its findings, even though the hook cannot detect either. It is also violated at a smaller scale by keeping a conversation-dependent reference and explaining it inline where deleting it was possible.

## Why it exists

The premise is stated in the message: a diff large enough to trip the thresholds was written inside a conversation, and its comments and naming may be legible only with that conversation attached. The author of the diff cannot detect this, because the author carries the conversation and everything reads as obvious. The remedy is structural rather than exhortative: a reader who provably lacks the conversation reads the work, and the gate forces that reading to happen exactly once, at the moment of publication, when the whole change is assembled. The low comment threshold (2 lines) relative to the source threshold (25 lines) suggests comments are considered the higher-risk surface, which matches the accretion check's focus.

## What I would do on receiving it

I would run `/sorry-im-late --since {base} --but-also-you-are-not-my-mum` with the base the block supplied, wait for the cold-reader's findings, apply them (preferring deletion of conversational references, per the block's stated preference), and then rerun the push with `COLD_READ_DONE=1` prefixed to the command. I would not add the token without the pass, since the token certifies the pass. If the cold-reader returned nothing, the token is still earned, because the requirement is the pass, not a nonzero finding count.

## Parts I cannot fully resolve

- How `{base}` is computed, and how the hook identifies "where the session's work began," is not specified in the message; I take it on trust that the hook supplies a usable ref.
- Where exactly `COLD_READ_DONE=1` goes "in the command" is not spelled out; the form suggests an environment-variable prefix to `git push`, but the message alone does not settle it.
- The flag name `--but-also-you-are-not-my-mum` is opaque as a name; its effect (the accretion check) is stated, but why that name denotes that check is not derivable from the message. My best construal is that a mum-like commenter over-explains, telling the reader things they already know, and the flag rejects that; but that is inference, not text.
- Whether the 2-comment-line and 25-source-line limits are alternatives (either alone trips the gate) or conjunctive is grammatically ambiguous in "past the limit of 2 and 25"; the natural reading, given the purpose, is that exceeding either threshold fires the block.
