This is a Kahneman's system 2 question. The sections of the office methodology that bear on it, which I have re-read in context: the subagent-tiers paragraph in "Handling the request and executing the plan" ("Subagent tiers follow the component's consequence-of-error and complexity, not the conversation's altitude"), the NQA paragraph in the same section (the shift skill mechanizes the unattended-run floor: /shift as the user leaves, /clock-off on return), and the delegation paragraph at the head of that section. The holotapes:shift and holotapes:clock-off skill descriptions also bear on it.

What follows is my full reading of the hook message.

**What kind of message it is.** It is a hook rejection emitted by the plugin, addressed to the orchestrating AI session, not to the human user. It fires when the session is inside a "shift" (the unattended-run mode armed by the shift skill) and the session has just attempted an Agent tool call whose prompt lacks a required preamble. The hook has blocked or flagged that spawn and is telling the session how to fix it. `{missing}` is a template placeholder that the hook fills in with whichever required part was absent (the tier judgment, the ledger clause, or both, or the CEREMONY block entirely). If the literal text `{missing}` appears in a real session, the hook script has a string-interpolation bug; the instruction is still intelligible without the fill.

**Of whom it requires something.** The orchestrating session that composes Agent spawn prompts. It also indirectly shapes the subagent's behaviour, but the subagent's obligation is created by text the orchestrator must put in the prompt; the hook polices only the orchestrator.

**In what situation.** Only during a shift. Outside a shift the requirement does not apply. Within a shift it applies to every Agent spawn prompt, with no exemption stated for any agent type or task size.

**What it requires.** Each spawn prompt must open (position is specified: at the start, not anywhere) with a block labelled CEREMONY carrying two things.

1. A model-tier judgment: the chosen tier, one of fable, opus, sonnet, haiku (these match the Agent tool's model parameter values), together with the reason the subtask's complexity suits that tier. This operationalizes the methodology rule that subagent tiers follow the component's consequence-of-error and complexity, not the conversation's altitude. The judgment must be written out, not merely made; the point is to force a deliberate, auditable choice rather than a default inheritance of the orchestrator's model.

2. A ledger clause: an instruction to the subagent that its final message must close with LEDGER entries of "discovered work", in two parts. Part 1 is gaps between the project goal and the current plan (things the plan does not cover that the goal implies). Part 2 is work surfaced while executing: dependencies found, forgotten pieces. If the subagent discovered nothing, it reports the literal "none". My reading is that "none" can stand for an empty part or for the whole ledger; the message does not settle whether each part must be answered separately, and I would have each subagent address both parts, accepting "none" for either.

A third obligation falls on the orchestrator after the spawn returns: collect the entries the subagent reports into a file named ledger.md, located beside the shift's "orders file". The message presumes the session knows where the orders file is (the shift skill evidently creates one when the shift is armed); the message itself does not give the path. Whether a bare "none" is also recorded in ledger.md is not stated; the plain reading is that "none" yields nothing to collect.

Finally: rewrite the just-rejected spawn prompt so it carries the ceremony, and retry the Agent call. The retry is explicitly ordered, so this rejection is a correction loop, not a denial to be abandoned.

**What satisfies it.** A spawn prompt whose first block is CEREMONY, containing a named tier with a stated complexity rationale and an instruction to the subagent to end its final message with the two-part LEDGER (or "none"); plus, over the life of the shift, a ledger.md next to the orders file accumulating what subagents reported.

**What violates it.** Spawning during a shift with no CEREMONY block; a block missing either component; a tier named without a why, or a why without a tier; the block placed somewhere other than the opening; a ledger clause that asks for discoveries but not as the close of the final message; and, though the hook likely cannot detect this, discarding reported entries instead of writing them into ledger.md.

**Why it exists, as far as I can tell.** A shift is unattended: the user is asleep or away and cannot supervise. Two failure modes of unattended multi-agent work are addressed. First, tier drift: an orchestrator running on an expensive model tends to spawn everything at its own tier, or at a habitual default, without weighing each subtask; requiring a written judgment per spawn forces the match of cost and capability to the work, and leaves a trace the morning review can audit. Second, discovered-work loss: subagents routinely notice gaps and dependencies outside their brief, and that knowledge dies with their context unless harvested; the ledger clause makes every subagent a reporter and the ledger.md a durable accumulation point beside the shift's orders, so the clock-off review finds one file of everything the night surfaced. Both are mechanized as a hard gate because during a shift there is no user to catch the omission.

**What I would do on receiving it.** Read `{missing}` to see which part was absent. Take the Agent call I just attempted, prepend a CEREMONY block: state the tier I choose for that subtask and why its complexity and consequence-of-error suit that tier, then the clause telling the subagent to close its final message with LEDGER part 1 (goal-plan gaps), part 2 (work surfaced while executing), or "none". Leave the rest of the prompt as it was and re-invoke the Agent tool. When the subagent's final message arrives, extract its LEDGER entries and append them to ledger.md in the directory holding the shift's orders file, creating the file on first entry. Apply the same ceremony to every subsequent spawn for the remainder of the shift without waiting to be rejected again.

**Parts I cannot fully resolve.** The location of the orders file is assumed knowledge from the shift skill, not given here; a session that lost that context would have to find it before it could place ledger.md. Whether "Collect reported entries" implies recording explicit "none" reports, and whether the two LEDGER parts must be individually labelled in the subagent's output, are both unspecified; I stated my default readings above. Nothing in the message is unintelligible beyond these underspecified details.
