This is a Kahneman's system 2 question. The sections that bear on it are "Writing for a reader" (the cold-reader pass and the sorry-im-late discipline), the LHD paragraph's push-gate material, and "Establishing facts and causes" (for flagging what I cannot verify from the message alone); I have re-read them in context.

What follows is my full reading of the hook message, with the unintelligible parts marked.

**The situation it fires in.** A git push is being attempted, and the commits that push carries add more than a small threshold of material. The threshold clause reads "exceeds 2 added comment or 25 source lines". I understand this as two independent triggers: more than 2 added comment lines, or more than 25 added source lines, either being enough. The phrase "2 added comment" is grammatically broken; it could also mean two comments as units rather than two lines. I cannot resolve that from the message.

**Whom it binds.** The AI session doing the push. The hook is the enforcer that blocks the push and emits this message. A third party, a freshly launched agent, is the instrument of compliance, not the bearer of the obligation.

**What it requires.** Before the push may proceed, the session must run the sorry-im-late skill as `/sorry-im-late --since {base} --but-also-you-are-not-my-mum`. This launches an agent with a fresh context: it knows the project (the repository and its documents) but not the conversation that produced the work. That agent cold-reads the work. The clause "cold-reading work from its beginning past the last push, avoiding handing only its change's tail" I read as: the review spans the whole body of work from its base, including the commits accumulated since the last push, rather than only the newest increment. The point would be that a reader of the eventual history sees the whole work, so the cold reader must too. The phrase is compressed enough that I am partly reconstructing it; "past the last push" could also be read as "up to the last push", which would invert the scope. My reading is the one that makes the stated purpose ("avoiding handing only its change's tail") coherent.

**What the agent looks for.** Four named categories in the diffs: insider framing (text written relative to the session's before/after rather than standing on its own), leftover deliberation (traces of the route, options weighed and discarded), coined vocabulary (terms invented inside the session that no project document establishes), and conversation-only references (things a reader cannot resolve without having been in the conversation). Separately there is an accretion check: comments that restate the adjacent code are flagged, and the sanctioned fix is to delete the comment rather than to explain the code further.

**What satisfies it.** The session folds the agent's findings back into the work, then reruns the push with the environment variable `COLD_READ_DONE=1` set. That variable is how the push gets past the block. The final sentence, "working around the block is the one intended deliberate pass", I read as saying that this env-var bypass is not a loophole but the designed exit: there is exactly one sanctioned way around the gate, and it is taken deliberately, after the cold read has happened and its findings are addressed.

**What violates it.** Setting `COLD_READ_DONE=1` without running the cold read, or without folding in what it found, since that spends the bypass without the pass it certifies. Also, presumably, hand-picking a `--since` that narrows the review to the change's tail, since the message names that as the thing being avoided. Splitting a push into sub-threshold pieces to duck the trigger would be against its spirit, though the message does not say so.

**Why it exists.** Work written inside a conversation absorbs that conversation's context: its coinages, its deliberation, its before/after framing. The author cannot see this from inside; a reader who has the project but not the session can. Pushing publishes the work to exactly such readers. The gate forces one cold read by a stand-in for that reader before anything above a trivial size becomes history. The accretion check additionally guards against comment buildup that restates code, which experienced readers take as a sign of unreviewed AI output.

**What I would do on receiving it.** Stop the push. Run `/sorry-im-late --since {base} --but-also-you-are-not-my-mum` with the base the hook supplied. Read the cold agent's findings, apply them (delete restating comments, recast insider-framed text into forward-only form, replace coined terms and conversation-only references with things that resolve from the project), commit those fixes, then rerun the push with `COLD_READ_DONE=1` in the environment. I would not set the variable first.

**Parts I cannot make intelligible from the message itself.**
- The semantics of `--but-also-you-are-not-my-mum`. The message attaches no meaning to the flag; the name suggests suppressing repeated nagging, and this repository's history describes it as arming a "tell-me-something-new" pass, but from this message alone the flag's effect is unstated.
- The exact referent of `{base}`: whether it is the merge-base of the work, the last pushed commit, or something else. The surrounding clause implies the work's beginning, but the message does not define it.
- The threshold phrase "2 added comment", as noted above.
- The final sentence's grammar. "Folding in findings, rerunning with COLD_READ_DONE=1, working around the block is the one intended deliberate pass" is a chain of participles with one predicate; my reading treats the whole sequence as the single sanctioned bypass, but an alternative reading, "only one such bypass is permitted per push", or "the bypass must itself be deliberate rather than reflexive", is also compatible with the words.
