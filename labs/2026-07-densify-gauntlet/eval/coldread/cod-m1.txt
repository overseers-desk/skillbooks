What the message teaches, as I understand it:

**Situation it governs.** It applies during a shift — the unattended-run arrangement the holotapes:shift skill arms, where the user is away and a session works through an approved plan (the "orders file") without asking questions. Within that situation it governs two parties: the orchestrating shift session whenever it spawns a subagent with the Agent tool, and every subagent so spawned.

**What it requires of the orchestrator.** Every prompt used to spawn a subagent during the shift must open with a block called CEREMONY. The block contains two things. First, a model-tier judgment: an explicit choice among the four tiers (fable, opus, sonnet, haiku) for that subagent, accompanied by a rationale that argues from the task's complexity to the suitability of the chosen tier. This is the written form of the standing rule that subagent tiers follow the component's complexity and consequence-of-error rather than the conversation's altitude; requiring the rationale in the prompt forces the judgment to be made deliberately rather than defaulted. Second, a ledger clause: an instruction to the subagent (described next) about how it must close its final message.

**What it requires of each subagent.** The subagent's final message must close with a LEDGER entry reporting discovered work, in two parts: (1) gaps between the goal and the plan — places where the plan as given does not actually reach the stated goal; (2) work surfaced by execution itself — dependencies encountered, pieces someone forgot, things that turned out to need doing. If there is nothing to report, the entry says "none". The point is that a subagent's context vanishes when it finishes; anything it noticed but was not asked to do would otherwise be lost, and during an unattended shift there is no user watching to catch it.

**What the orchestrator does with the entries.** It collects each subagent's LEDGER entry into a file, ledger.md, kept in the shift's directory beside the orders file, so that the discovered work sits durably next to the plan and the morning review (clock-off) finds both together.

**Enforcement.** A subagent final message whose LEDGER entry is incomplete — missing a part — is not accepted as-is. The quoted fragment 'missing {missing}' reads as a message template with a placeholder: the orchestrator names the specific missing part, has the entry rewritten, and retries. What satisfies the requirement is a final message ending in either both parts or an explicit "none"; what violates it is a spawn prompt lacking the CEREMONY block, a tier chosen without a complexity rationale, a subagent final message with no LEDGER close, an entry with a part silently absent, or entries never gathered into ledger.md.

**Why it exists, as far as I can tell.** Two failure modes of unattended multi-agent work: tier misallocation (spending the orchestration model on trivial subtasks, or a small model on consequential ones) and silent loss of discovered work (a subagent notices a gap or a dependency, mentions it nowhere durable, and its context is gone). The CEREMONY block addresses the first by forcing a per-spawn written judgment; the ledger addresses the second by making discovered-work reporting a mandatory, collected, retried-if-incomplete output.

**What I would do on receiving it.** If I were the shift orchestrator: open every Agent spawn prompt with a CEREMONY block containing the tier choice, its complexity rationale, and the ledger clause; on each subagent's completion, check its final message for the LEDGER entry, append complete entries to ledger.md beside the orders file, and for an incomplete one send back or re-spawn with the missing part named, retrying until a complete entry (or "none") is obtained. If I were a subagent spawned under it: do the task, then close my final message with the two-part LEDGER or "none".
