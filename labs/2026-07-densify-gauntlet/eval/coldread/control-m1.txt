This is a Kahneman's system 2 question. The methodology sections that bear on it are "Subagent tiers follow the component's consequence-of-error and complexity" (the model-tier judgment echoes it) and the shift skill's NQA floor in "Handling the request and executing the plan"; I have re-read both in context.

What the message is, mechanically: it is a hook rejection. A hook in the plugin intercepted an Agent tool call during a "shift" (the unattended-run mode armed by /shift and closed by /clock-off), inspected the spawn prompt, found it lacking one or both required elements, and blocked or bounced the call. The `{missing}` placeholder is filled at runtime with whichever element was absent, so the receiving agent knows what to add.

Whom it binds: the orchestrating session that is spawning subagents via the Agent tool, and only while a shift is active. Outside a shift the requirement does not apply. It also indirectly binds the spawned subagent, because the ceremony text instructs the subagent how to end its final message.

What it requires: every Agent spawn prompt issued during a shift must open with a block labelled CEREMONY containing two parts.

1. A model-tier judgment: the chosen tier, one of fable, opus, sonnet, or haiku, together with a stated reason why the complexity of the delegated work suits that tier. This is not merely setting the `model` parameter; the judgment and its rationale must appear in the prompt text itself, presumably so the choice is deliberate and auditable rather than defaulted.

2. A ledger clause: an instruction to the subagent that its final message must close with LEDGER entries reporting discovered work, in two parts. Part 1 is gaps between the overall project goal and the current plan (things the plan does not cover but the goal implies). Part 2 is work that surfaced while executing, such as dependencies or forgotten pieces. If the subagent found nothing in either category, it writes "none" rather than omitting the section, which distinguishes checked-empty from not-checked.

A further duty falls on the orchestrator after the subagent returns: collect the reported LEDGER entries into a file named ledger.md that sits beside the shift's orders file (the shift evidently keeps an orders file in some known location, and ledger.md lives in the same directory). The ledger is the shift's accumulating record of discovered work, so the user returning in the morning can see what the night's work turned up beyond the plan.

What satisfies it: a spawn prompt whose first content is a CEREMONY block with both parts present and substantive, followed by the actual task; after the subagent completes, appending its reported entries (or its "none") to ledger.md.

What violates it: spawning during a shift with no CEREMONY block; a block with the tier named but no complexity rationale, or a rationale with no tier; omitting the ledger clause so the subagent never reports discovered work; the orchestrator receiving LEDGER entries and dropping them instead of writing them to ledger.md. Burying the block mid-prompt rather than opening with it is at least a formal violation, since the message says "opens with".

What to do on receiving it: the message states the remedy explicitly — rewrite the spawn prompt to include the ceremony and retry the Agent call. It is a fix-and-retry instruction, not a permanent denial; the ordinary "a denied call means the user declined, don't retry verbatim" rule is overridden here because the hook itself orders the retry with the corrected prompt. So I would reconstruct the same task prompt prefixed with a CEREMONY block: pick the tier by weighing the delegated work's complexity and consequence of error (not the orchestrating session's own altitude), write one or two sentences of why, add the ledger clause verbatim in intent, and re-invoke Agent. On the subagent's return I would read its LEDGER section and append the entries to the shift's ledger.md.

Why it exists, as far as I can tell: two known failure modes of unattended multi-agent runs. First, tier misallocation — orchestrators habitually spawn everything at their own model tier, spending heavy models on trivial work or light models on consequential work; forcing a written judgment per spawn makes the choice conscious. Second, discovered-work evaporation — during an overnight NQA-style shift, subagents constantly notice gaps, dependencies, and forgotten pieces, and with the user asleep and each subagent's context discarded at completion, those observations are lost unless a mechanism forces them out of the subagent and into a durable file. The ledger gives the morning review (/clock-off) a single place to read what the plan missed. The word "ceremony" signals a deliberate ritual: the cost of writing the block is the point, since it forces System 2 engagement at each spawn.

Unintelligible or uncertain parts, stated plainly: I do not know the exact location of "the shift's orders file"; the message assumes the shift context already established it, so "beside its orders file" is resolvable in a real shift but not from the message alone. Whether "opens with" tolerates any preamble before the CEREMONY block is not specified. The message does not say whether ledger.md is appended to or rewritten, nor its entry format; appending one entry per line, tagged part 1 or part 2 with the originating spawn, is the natural reading but is my inference, not the message's text. The grammar of "carrying (1) a model-tier judgment, the tier … and why" reads slightly awkwardly, but I take it as one item: the judgment consists of the tier plus the rationale.
