# Roadmap: what selling has to teach HIRE, and what has been tested

A job application is a large sale to a committee, made in reply to a published requirement, with one unit to sell and the seller as the product. The lessons below are taken from the bodies of sales practice that fit that description. Each is something the method could make a writer do. A round of the lab picks some of them, writes each into a version of the method, and measures the pages that come out. Each round's worklog names the items it tested by the letters and numbers here.

## How the series is run

The work of a round is to find, by relentless experiment, the best way to bring the outcome. That has two parts. One is getting the judges to agree that a page is better. The other is correcting the judges where they do not behave as a person hiring would. Which route to take to either is for the session running the round to work out. The owner is not expected to supply step-by-step suggestions, and a question about how to run an experiment is settled by running one.

## The judges, and what they may know

A judge is a fresh session of the profile the round runs under, told it is the person the post reports to and given only the advertisement and the pages.

Round 1 considered running judges under a new, empty profile, so that nothing about the candidate's own office could reach them. Round 1 ran its comparisons under such an empty profile, eight judges each, and ran six of them a second time under the current profile with four judges each. The same page won in five of the six, and the sixth was a tie under the current profile. The two ways also differed in the wording of the note and the number of judges, so this shows no difference in which page wins and does not isolate the profile. On that evidence the empty profile is not part of this work. The counts in the round-1 worklog are from the empty-profile runs. The judging script kept with the round's material runs under the current profile, and the fairness tests have not been run on it.

Round 1 also asked each judge afterwards what it thought the exchange was for, and counted those who guessed that versions of one CV were being tried, under the name `doubted`. Most did. A judge who sees through the setup is not behaving as a person hiring would, so that count belongs with the second part of the work. The present script does not ask the question.

One case may bring the need back, and it has not been tested: a test that ranks the candidate's page against other candidates' pages. There a judge who can tell whose office it is running in would not treat that candidate like the others. If a round finds that this affects how the ranking test comes out, an empty profile is the thing to consider. On the researcher's computer where round 1 ran, a sign-in token kept at `~/.jail-claude-token` lets an empty profile be used without logging in to it. That file belongs to one computer and is not guaranteed to exist; where it is missing, some other way of creating an empty, signed-in profile would be needed. The owner allows another profile only for a reason stated to him, so a round that comes to need one writes that reason down for him first.

The letters group the lessons by where they come from: A, selling to large accounts; B, tender and proposal practice; C, positioning; D, proof and risk; E, discovery of what is for sale; F, feedback and the seller's stance; G, the pipeline around the page; H, voice.

## What the results so far suggest for the order of work

The top of the page responds to instruction and the body does not yet. B1's grid is the same move applied to the body, in a form a later step can check. A4 and E1 would change what there is to put in that grid. F1 bears on the checking phase, which round 1 did not study. D2 and D3 are small and could ride with any of these. A2 needs a way to show a page to the two readers separately. G1 and G3 wait for the second stage.
