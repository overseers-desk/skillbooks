# The wall that bellied, told by one who knows

In my second year as a waller I built forty metres along the top of a hill farm, and I was proud of it. The joints were tight and the courses ran level. After one wet winter a stretch of it bellied out like a full sack.

I took the fault to be in the face stones, and rebuilt the stretch with heavier ones. The next spring it bellied again. An older waller who was passing pulled a single face stone out of it and put his arm into the wall up to the elbow. There was nothing behind the stone. I had thrown the small stones into the middle loose, as filling, and they had settled.

A dry-stone wall fails from the inside. The faces are what a walker sees, and the hearting, the small stones packed between them, is what holds the faces up. Each of those stones has to be placed and wedged by hand, and every metre or so a long stone has to run through the wall and tie the two faces together. I have built that way for twenty years, and none of those walls has moved.
