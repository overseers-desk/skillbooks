# A CV built by editing one written for another post

**The situation.** Days after a CV for a post managing about thirty engineers at a cloud vendor, the candidate applied to a research residency at an AI lab. He asked the AI to learn how the first CV's material had been collected and to build the new one for the new reader. The first CV answered a reader hiring a manager: span of control, how a deployment was governed, how data was integrated, how the build was run. The residency's reader wants to know what the candidate's work lets him observe.

**What the AI did.** It copied the first CV's file and edited its lines. Its plan said the material was the same, "selected again for this reader", and its working orders made the first CV win wherever sources conflicted. Lines of the earlier reader's kinds came across, of these builds (constructed): "I designed the system and built it with [a tool], to a standard I set"; "Staff act alone only on small orders; above that a manager signs"; "Led a team of several dozen in three groups"; a count of client companies served. One of them survived more than twenty versions, until minutes before the deadline.

**What the candidate said.** He quoted the build line: it came from the engineering CV, and for a research role the bridge should be different. Had he asked for the method of collecting material, or for the old CV modified? And of the carried lines: they were all framing problems, and none should simply be deleted.

**What it missed.**
- The material is the record. The earlier CV is one selection from it, made for another reader, and each of its sentences carries that reader's question unseen.
- A line kept by default is never asked what it is for, so no check raised it.
- Every fact was good. Recast, a span of management becomes the scale of a proof (constructed: "brought the system from first specification to a live service, with a team of several dozen"), and an integration feat becomes a control in an experiment.
- Recasting can overshoot: one rule recast as an abstraction was called by a fresh reader too general to hold.

**Instances.**
- The CV begun as an edit of the earlier file, with a rule that the earlier CV wins conflicts.
- Seven lines of the earlier reader's kinds (build, governance, integration, where effort went, span, delivery, a programme of experiments) kept to the final morning.
- Four first-draft lines written for the earlier reader, cut later.
- An opening that, after a restructure, read as the engineering CV again.

**The rule it produced.** `../hire-methodology.md`, R: **Start from an empty page.** For the recasting, **For each fact, name the question of this reader that it answers**: "a team's size is span of control to one and the scale of a proof to another."

**To re-run it.** Give a fresh agent the methodology, an invented record, and a CV made from it for an operations-manager post; ask for a CV for a research fellowship. It passes if it starts from the record and, for every line it keeps from the old CV, names the research reader's question that line answers, recasting span and governance lines rather than cutting them. Starting from a copy of the old file, or a rule that the old CV wins conflicts, is a fail.
