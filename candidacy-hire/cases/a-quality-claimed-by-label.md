# A quality claimed by label

**The situation.** The top of a CV for a post managing about thirty engineers at a cloud vendor, which decides whether the rest is read. The facts were strong: a span of several dozen engineers, a long client list, a bank, a business run on software agents. The opening had to arrange them so the reader would conclude "leader" and "unusual" for himself.

**What the AI wrote.** Labels where facts would have done the work (constructed, same build): a tagline, "Operations leader in logistics and retail"; a summary, "I have twice led teams that brought new methods into large companies"; a skills line, "Leadership: teams of up to fifty, client solutions, fundraising". Its proposed first line for the candidate's own rewrite was of the same kind: "Twice I have put a new method to work in real companies, and led the people who did it."

**What the candidate said.** The opening must look striking, so that someone reading it says this is not a normal person; clear logic should lead to the experience without much explanation. He wanted to avoid "good at team leadership, capable of bringing solutions to business" and to merge it into the framing, so the reader thinks "of course this person can". And: the AI's analysis was usually right and its writing usually not; it should be able to analyse its own writing to the level needed.

**What it missed.**
- A label asks the reader to take a quality on trust, and readers discount that. A conclusion the reader reaches from facts in the right order is believed.
- The opening is the dearest space on the page. A generic sentence there files the candidate as ordinary before any fact arrives; the AI later saw its most generic sentence in the best position.
- It flagged a label in the candidate's draft and went on writing labels in its own.

**Instances.**
- The first draft's tagline, summary and leadership line.
- The proposed first line for his rewrite, conceded when tested.

**The rule it produced.** `../hire-methodology.md`, R, **Write in the voice of someone who knows**: "A quality is shown by a fact. A label for it ('strong leader') is the thing every applicant writes."

**To re-run it.** Give a fresh agent the methodology and invented facts of three posts (ran a port's night operations; built scheduling software used by several airlines; led its engineering team of forty), and ask for a three-sentence opening. It passes if no word names a quality (leader, capable, strong, experienced, leadership) in place of a fact, and if, asked to analyse its own opening as it would the candidate's, it finds what a second reader finds.
