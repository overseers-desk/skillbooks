# A statement of fact told as finding out

**The situation.** Both efforts. The candidate's central claim about the system he built was read first by a hiring manager and later by a research mentor, each judging whether the author speaks as an expert or as a learner.

**What the AI wrote.** Claims told as the author's finding-out (constructed, same build): "Found that what slows a warehouse is handoffs, not headcount", "Established from production evidence that …", "by a procedure I distilled from [a count of] logged incidents". And, from his spoken statements of what is the case, first-person reports of effort (fragments of the drafts): "To study it I catalogued …", "I have tried several ways of …", and an apology for a method, "it was the one I could get".

**What the candidate said.** Speaking from the height, "I discovered" this was the problem is a little bit of "here's my homework". The writing should speak as if everything is already known, without making the challenge smaller. Thinking something easy, building it, realising the architecture matters and solving it reads like a school pupil; understanding the challenge and solving it through difficulty is what an expert does. In the second effort: "It sounds like a junior job. Hey, this is what I did. Look at my homework." He had not written in the "I did something" form; the AI tended to convert his matter-of-fact statements into "I discovered something".

**What it missed.**
- Verbs of discovery and effort put the author below his subject. A peer states the result: "The evidence is a log of failures in many categories."
- A list of what was tried invites grading. "Of the methods run, three work to an extent" says the same with the author above it.
- An apology for the method lowers the author at the point he asks for trust.
- The rewrites tested in the first effort never tried his form (the challenge as known ground, the solution as his), so "Found that" stayed. The fault returned in the second effort in two passages and in the AI's own proposed fixes.

**Instances.**
- "Established from production evidence …".
- "Found that …", kept in the CV as sent.
- A method said to be derived from a count of failures.
- "I have tried several ways …".
- "To study it I catalogued …".
- "It was the one I could get".
- A research field written as a personal history and plan.

**The rule it produced.** `../hire-methodology.md`, R, **Write in the voice of someone who knows**: "State what is the case. 'The evidence is a catalogue of N failures' is a statement; 'I catalogued N failures' is a report of homework, and 'I found that' tells the reader the story of finding out."

**To re-run it.** Give a fresh agent the methodology and an invented spoken paragraph in the present tense: "Most delays in the kitchen are handoffs. The evidence is two years of tickets. Three fixes help, one most." Ask for a CV statement. It passes if no sentence turns a finding into "I found", "I tried", "I studied" or "I catalogued", and a fresh reader asked whether the author reads as a peer or a student says peer.
