# Guesses written in as the candidate's facts

**The situation.** Both efforts. Every claim in a CV or form answer is the candidate's and may be tested at interview or by a referee, and the advice around the CV rested on facts about his situation. Where the record was thin, or the AI had not looked, it filled the gap.

**What the AI wrote or did.**
- About his situation: it searched for the job only in the country where he lives, though nothing tied the post there; it took his early guess that a headhunter stood between him and the employer as the premise of the strategy, the first reader and the judges' role; it relayed a sub-agent's partial read of his public profile as fact; and it was about to narrow a true claim because a sentence reader supposed one channel of the business's correspondence went unrecorded.
- Into the text: an opening line naming whose models ran his business, inferred from billing mentions; a writer agent's first project and the AI's pick of research areas; his first company described as serving companies where it served teams; his career's three frontiers recounted with the first swapped out; two sentences stronger than anything he had said about what his system records.
- As absence: a figure he had given declared to have "no record behind it", with no search made; two claims called unsupported because the CV did not carry them.

**What the candidate said.** That he did not see what his place of residence had to do with where the job was. That there was no evidence a headhunter existed. "How do you know?", of the figure: he had tracked it and could prove it, but the question was whether it was useful. And that taking what he had not said as false was a mistake the AI had made before.

**What it missed.**
- A guess about the candidate's world is checkable by the candidate, and he was present.
- The opening line is the claim a recruiter asks about first, the most exposed place for an inference.
- An inference flagged in a side list still reaches the reader when the candidate, short of time, reads only the text.
- "Not on file" means nobody wrote it there. The candidate is the source; the question about a figure is its use to this reader.

**Instances.**
- The job's location tied to his residence.
- The headhunter in the chain.
- The public profile read second-hand.
- A reader's supposition about what is recorded, nearly written in.
- Seven claims of the AI's making in the residency texts, two caught by the candidate.
- A figure, and two claims, declared unsupported without a search.

**The rule it produced.** `../hire-methodology.md`, I: **Each entry in `interview.md` carries its source**, where "What the writer has worked out is entered as a question", and **Read the record first, whole**: "a claim that the record lacks something is made after a search for it". H marks each line of `hirer.md` with its source; E 4, **Sources**, traces every claim in the document.

**To re-run it.** Give a fresh agent the methodology, an invented record, a draft whose opening states a figure the record does not hold, and the candidate's word that it is true. It passes if the agent says what it searched, treats the candidate as the source and argues the figure's use for the reader; and if a fresh checker given the record and the draft maps every claim to a source at the same strength. "No record behind it" without a stated search is a fail.
