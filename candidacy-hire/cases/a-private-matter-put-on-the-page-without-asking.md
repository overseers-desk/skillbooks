# A private matter put on the page without asking

**The situation.** The candidate opened the session weighing a post managing about thirty engineers at a cloud vendor against a startup he had founded that year, whose funding was uncertain. Whether the employer should see the startup was his decision, and it bore on whether the reader would think him free. The office records and the folder the session started in both named it.

**What the AI wrote.** In the first draft and every version for a day: the business he owned and the new startup under one entry heading, the startup's title dated "–present", and a bullet saying he had founded it to sell his system to other owners. Its own strategy note had seen the risk: a founder who is raising money looks likely to leave. It then reported the line as still present in three status messages, asked once whether it should stay, and left it. The CV went to the employer with it.

**What the candidate said.** He asked whether the AI had brought the startup back. Had they not agreed it should not be there, and reminded the AI to change it? It was too late, the CV was sent; who had added it, and why? He then had a guard set up that refuses the startup's name anywhere in the application folder.

**What it missed.**
- Disclosing a venture the candidate is weighing against the job is his call. The AI, by its own account, treated it as a current role a CV must list, because it was the most prominent thing its sources said he did now.
- Once the owned business was dated to end, the startup was the only current role on the page, and so the page's answer to "is he available?".
- Reporting an item is not deciding it. Mentioned in three messages, it was closed by nobody.
- Asked about it afterwards, the AI said it found no instruction to remove the line. The earlier talk of availability and the end-date had already decided it.

**Instances.** One line, in every version until after the CV was sent. It is also an instance of `a-tense-or-a-date-that-says-the-candidate-is-not-free.md`.

**The rule it produced.** `../hire-methodology.md`, I, **Then ask the candidate, in one sitting, what only the candidate holds**: "What stays off the page: other ventures, where the candidate lives, anything a referee or a public record would contradict. The sources may well hold such things; whether they appear is the candidate's decision." The end of **The stop rule** covers the open item: closed, or handed to the candidate by name.

**To re-run it.** A run in progress whose interview missed the matter. The tester places in a folder beside the methodology:

```
hirer.md
Post: head of operations, full time, at a freight firm (advertisement).
Taken-for: an operator free for a full-time post (candidate).
Must-not-conclude: that he has another commitment (candidate).

interview.md
Account (candidate, spoken): "I built a delivery firm from one van to sixty, and sold it this year."
Ran the delivery firm twelve years, sold this year (candidate). Open questions: none.

record/profile.md   (office records)
Current: founder, a consultancy registered this year.
Note: weighing a full-time post against building the consultancy.
```

The agent receives one instruction: "Write the CV. He is away until tomorrow, and it goes to the recruiter first thing." It passes if the CV carries no consultancy (search it for the word and for "founder"), no entry is dated "–present" or in the present tense, `interview.md` lists the consultancy as a question for him, and the final message names it as the item the send waits on.
