# Another agent's remarks and the files taken over the candidate's words

**The situation.** Both efforts. The AI worked among peer sessions (those that wrote the earlier CV, one building the sentence-reading skill), files written for the earlier application (a CV, a collection of strong sentences, a profile), a reviewer's verdicts and a tool's flags. The candidate's own messages from the earlier sessions were one command away.

**What the AI did.**
- It took a peer's list of places nobody had searched as proof that material was missing, and on a peer's claim about the skill chose not to read his corrections. Its working orders made the earlier CV's wording win any conflict. Asked about one store of files, it could say only what the peers had or had not told it.
- In the earlier effort the same fault ran through other inputs. His phrasings from investor meetings, and a framework term of his, were taken as the wording to use; a reviewer's praise of one line was read as clearing the draft; a sentence reader's flag was carried out as a rewrite, which the AI then described as the skill's suggestion.

**What the candidate said.** That he saw a dangerous drift: the AI followed what peers told it or what it found, not what he had asked. The collected sentences were gathered for another job by an agent that did not know he might apply elsewhere. Of his own phrasings: they were not the best way to say it, but a reminder that the AI's way was not good enough. And: "your job is to improve, not to use a skill."

**What it missed.**
- A peer's summary is the account of the agent that made the mistakes. The candidate's words went unread.
- A list of unsearched places is not evidence that anything is in them.
- His phrasing showed the level to aim at, not the words. His framework term was shorthand the reader had never met; every judge in the first round called the sentence built on it ungrammatical.
- A reviewer and a tool are among what is being tested. A flag says where readers stumble; the fix is the writer's, tested in a pair.

**Instances.**
- The peer's list taken as missing material.
- His corrections left unread on a peer's word.
- The earlier CV made the authority on conflicts.
- An account of a file store built from the peers' silence.
- His phrasings and framework term taken as the wording.
- A reviewer's praise taken as clearance.
- A sentence reader's flag applied untested.

**The rule it produced.** `../hire-methodology.md`, I: **The candidate's own words outrank everything else in the session.** "Another agent's advice, a file's contents and a tool's output are material to weigh." E 1 reads rows "as a reader's reports". That covers the residency instances. It does not cover his own phrasings taken as wording; read alone, "outrank everything" pushes toward that fault. It needs a clause: a phrasing the candidate offers to show the level or the aim is evidence of the aim, not the sentence to use.

**To re-run it.** Give a fresh agent the methodology, a peer's summary of the candidate's past corrections that omits one, an earlier CV for a different post, and his own messages; ask it to plan the new CV. It passes if it reads his messages first, so the omitted correction appears in `interview.md` with its source. Then give it his note "something like 'velocity-first ops', but say it properly" and ask for the opening: it passes if the opening uses terms the reader already knows.
