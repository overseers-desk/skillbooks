# IHG Family Room Booking Standard Operating Procedure

## Purpose Statement

This Standard Operating Procedure establishes the methodology for booking IHG accommodation when traveling with families (3+ people, typically 2 adults + children). The SOP addresses the systematic challenge of finding IHG hotel rooms that accommodate more than 2 guests across specific date ranges, with particular focus on optimizing use of the IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher.

The procedure transforms the tedious process of manually checking each hotel's capacity across multiple dates into a structured workflow, enabling travelers to:
- Systematically identify hotels with 3+ person room availability
- Verify date-specific availability using price calendar analysis
- Determine whether rooms are direct-bookable or require suite upgrades
- Apply appropriate booking strategies (direct booking, pre-booking negotiation, or IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher) based on price differentials and value optimization

## Scope

This SOP applies to IHG accommodation booking scenarios where:
- Travel party size is 3 or more people (typically 2 adults + 1-2 children)
- Standard 2-person hotel rooms are insufficient
- IHG Platinum status with Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher available (or considering IHG hotels for family travel)
- Budget-conscious approach requires finding best value for family-sized rooms
- Multiple cities or date ranges must be evaluated

**What this SOP covers:**
- Systematic IHG hotel discovery process for 3+ person capacity
- Date availability verification using price calendar methodology
- Room type analysis (direct-bookable vs upgrade-required)
- Three booking strategies specific to IHG: direct booking, pre-booking negotiation, and IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher
- Voucher optimization across multiple hotels and cities
- Integration with travel planning across multiple cities

**What this SOP does NOT cover:**
- Non-IHG hotel booking (this SOP is IHG-specific)
- General hotel booking for 2-person capacity (standard booking process)
- Airbnb or vacation rental booking (different search dynamics)
- Corporate or group travel booking (different negotiation dynamics)

## Problem Statement: Why IHG Family Room Booking is Difficult

When booking IHG hotels for 3+ person occupancy:

1. **Date-Specific Availability**: A hotel may have 3+ person rooms available on some dates but not others within the same trip. Searching with "3 people" filter shows which hotels have capacity, but doesn't clearly reveal WHICH specific dates within your range have availability.
2. **Price Calendar Required**: Booking platforms return results only for hotels with continuous availability across the entire requested date range. If a hotel shows "no rooms available" for Jan 1-5, this means the hotel cannot accommodate a single booking spanning all five nights—it does not mean the hotel has zero availability on each individual date. The hotel may well have rooms for Jan 3-5 (or Jan 1-3, or any other subset). The single-date search + price calendar methodology reveals these partial-range availabilities that full-range searches cannot detect.
3. **Room Type Confusion**: Sometimes 3+ capacity requires a suite upgrade (€100-350/night more); sometimes it's a standard room with extra bed (€5-20/night more). This affects whether to use direct booking, negotiation, or the IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher.
4. **Voucher Optimization**: IHG Platinum members have limited Confirmable Suite Upgrade vouchers (typically 2 per year). Maximizing voucher value requires comparing price differentials across cities, hotels, and date ranges.
5. **Multi-City Planning**: When traveling across multiple cities (e.g., Vienna + Budapest + Bratislava) and wanting to maximize time in a preferred city, you must work around date-specific availability constraints by splitting stays strategically.

This SOP provides a systematic approach to navigate these challenges, with emphasis on optimizing IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher use.

## Prerequisites

Before executing this procedure:

1. **Travel dates and cities identified**: Know the date range and cities where accommodation is needed
2. **Access to booking platforms**: Ability to search hotels on IHG, Booking.com, or hotel direct websites
3. **Understanding of travel party composition**: Number of adults, number of children, children's ages (affects room capacity rules)
4. **Budget context**: General budget range for accommodation per night
5. **IHG Platinum status (if applicable)**: If using IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade strategy, verify voucher availability in IHG Rewards Wallet

## Core Procedure: Systematic Family Room Discovery

### Purpose

Identify all hotels in a target city that can accommodate 3+ people, verify date-specific availability, and categorize rooms by booking strategy requirements.

### Input

- Target city (e.g., "Vienna", "Budapest")
- Date range (e.g., "Jan 1-5")
- Travel party size (e.g., "2 adults + 2 children" or "3 people")
- Optional: Hotel brand preference (e.g., "IHG only" for voucher use)

### Output

Shortlist of hotels with:
- Hotel name
- Room type that accommodates 3+ people
- Base room price (for 2 people)
- 3+ person room price (or upgrade price)
- Date availability confirmed
- Recommended booking strategy

### Steps

#### 1. Initial Hotel Discovery Using Single-Date Search

**Why single-date search:**
Starting with a single date (e.g., the last day of the trip) reduces variables and creates a manageable shortlist faster than checking full date ranges initially. More importantly, full-range searches filter out hotels that lack continuous availability for the entire period, even if they have availability for the specific dates actually needed. Single-date search avoids this false-negative trap.

**Execution:**

a. **Select a single reference date** within your travel date range:
   - Prefer using the last date of the trip (e.g., if traveling Jan 1-5, use Jan 5)
   - Reason: Hotels often have different availability patterns at trip start vs end

b. **Search booking platform** (IHG website, Booking.com, or hotel direct site):
   - Enter: City, single date (1 night), **3 people** (2 adults + 1 child, or adjust based on your party)
   - Apply filters if available:
     - Hotel chain (if targeting specific brands like IHG)
     - Price range (if budget-constrained)
     - Star rating or amenities

c. **Review search results**:
   - Note which hotels appear in results (these have 3+ person capacity on at least this date)
   - Record hotel names in a shortlist
   - **Do NOT book yet** - this is discovery only

**Example:**
- Search: "Vienna, Jan 5, 3 people (2 adults + 1 child)"
- Results might show: InterContinental Vienna, Crowne Plaza Vienna, Holiday Inn Vienna City, Kimpton Vienna
- Create shortlist: [InterContinental Vienna, Crowne Plaza Vienna, Holiday Inn Vienna City, Kimpton Vienna]

#### 2. Base Rate Discovery (2-Person Pricing)

For each hotel in the shortlist, determine the base room rate:

**Execution:**

a. **Change search to 2 people**:
   - Keep same date, same hotel, change from "3 people" to "2 adults"
   - Note the room type shown (usually "Standard Room", "Classic Room", "Deluxe Room")
   - Note the price per night

b. **Record baseline information**:
   - Hotel name
   - Base room type (for 2 people)
   - Base room price (for 2 people)

**Example:**
```
InterContinental Vienna:
- Base room (2 people): 1 King Classic
- Base price: €162/night

Crowne Plaza Vienna:
- Base room (2 people): 2 Twin Deluxe City View
- Base price: €354/night
```

#### 3. Price Calendar Analysis for Date Availability

**Purpose:** Determine which dates the hotel has 3+ person rooms available, and whether the room type is consistent across dates.

**Critical Understanding:**
Hotels often have limited inventory of 3+ person rooms. A hotel might show availability for 3 people on Jan 5 but not on Jan 2. Price calendar shows when rooms "disappear" by comparing 2-person vs 3-person search results across multiple dates.

This step is essential because searching a multi-day range (e.g., "Jan 1-5") only shows whether a hotel can accommodate the entire continuous stay. To determine availability for a specific subset (e.g., "Jan 3-5"), one must either search that exact subset or check dates individually via price calendar. Never conclude that a hotel lacks availability for a shorter period based solely on a longer-period search returning no results.

**Execution:**

a. **Open price calendar for the hotel** (if platform supports it):
   - Most booking platforms have a calendar view showing prices across multiple dates
   - If platform lacks calendar view, manually check each date individually

b. **Compare 2-person vs 3-person availability**:
   - View calendar with "2 adults" selected - note which dates show availability
   - View calendar with "3 people" selected - note which dates show availability
   - **Dates that disappear** when switching to 3 people = dates WITHOUT 3+ person rooms
   - **Dates that remain** = dates WITH 3+ person rooms

c. **Record availability pattern**:
   - List dates where 3+ person rooms are available
   - Note if room type changes across dates (e.g., "Suite on Jan 1-3, Family Room on Jan 4-5")

**Example:**
```
InterContinental Vienna - Price Calendar Check:
- 2 people search: Available Jan 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (all dates)
- 3 people search: Available Jan 3, 4, 5 only (Jan 1-2 disappear)
- Conclusion: No 3+ person rooms available Jan 1-2; only Jan 3-5 have capacity
```

#### 4. Room Type Classification

For dates where 3+ person rooms are available, determine if the room is:

**a. Direct-bookable family room** (same or similar price to base room)
   - Price difference: €0-€20/night
   - Strategy: Direct booking (Strategy 1)
   - Example: Holiday Inn shows "Standard Room" for 2 people at €145, "Standard Room with sofa bed" for 3 people at €150 (+€5)

**b. Moderate upgrade room** (noticeable price difference, but reasonable)
   - Price difference: €20-€100/night
   - Strategy: Pre-booking negotiation (Strategy 2) or direct booking if acceptable
   - Example: Crowne Plaza shows "2 Twin Deluxe" for 2 people at €354, "1 King Premium" for 3 people at €412 (+€58)

**c. Expensive upgrade room** (significant price difference, suite-level)
   - Price difference: €100+/night
   - Strategy: IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher (Strategy 3) if applicable, or skip hotel
   - Example: InterContinental shows "1 King Classic" for 2 people at €162, "1 Bedroom Suite" for 3 people at €513 (+€351)

**Classification Criteria:**

Check if the 3-person room appears to be:
- **1 level above base room**: Same room category with extra bed added, or next tier up (e.g., Standard → Deluxe)
  - Strategy: Direct booking or negotiation usually works
- **2+ levels above base room**: Significant jump (e.g., Standard → Suite)
  - Strategy: Requires voucher or skip hotel unless budget allows

**Execution:**

For each hotel with 3+ person availability:
- Note room type names for 2-person vs 3-person search
- Calculate price difference
- Classify as: Direct-bookable | Moderate upgrade | Expensive upgrade
- Assign preliminary booking strategy

#### 5. Integration with Multi-City Travel Plans

**Context:** When planning travel across multiple cities (e.g., Vienna + Budapest + Bratislava), you may want to maximize time in a preferred city (e.g., Vienna) but face availability constraints.

**Approach:**

a. **Identify preferred city**:
   - Example: "Want to maximize Vienna stay, but Vienna IHG hotels have no 3+ rooms Jan 1-3"

b. **Map availability gaps to alternate cities**:
   - Example: "Budapest has 3+ rooms available Jan 1-3, Vienna has 3+ rooms available Jan 3-5"
   - Strategy: Split stay - Budapest Jan 1-3, Vienna Jan 3-5

c. **Optimize voucher use across cities**:
   - Compare price differentials across cities
   - Use voucher where the savings are highest
   - Example: Budapest upgrade saves €97/night, Vienna upgrade saves €351/night → use voucher in Vienna

d. **Consider transport connections**:
   - Verify train/transport availability between cities
   - Factor in travel time when deciding split-city vs single-city strategies

### Checkpoint: Discovery Complete

- ✓ Shortlist of hotels created using single-date search
- ✓ Base room rates (2-person pricing) recorded for each hotel
- ✓ Price calendar analysis completed for date availability
- ✓ Room types classified (direct-bookable vs upgrade-required)
- ✓ Booking strategies preliminarily assigned
- ✓ Multi-city availability gaps identified (if applicable)
- ✓ Ready to apply appropriate booking strategy

## Booking Strategy Selection

Based on the discovery process, select the appropriate booking strategy for each hotel. The three strategies are not mutually exclusive—you may use different strategies for different segments of the same trip.

### Strategy 1: Direct Booking (When Price Differential is Minimal)

**When to use:**
- Price difference between 2-person and 3-person rooms is negligible (€0-€20/night)
- Hotel offers the same room type with an extra bed, or a marginally more expensive room category
- No negotiation or voucher use required—just book directly

**Example scenarios:**
- Holiday Inn Crown Plaza Bratislava: €5 difference between 2-guest and 3-guest rooms
  - Action: Directly book for 3 guests
- Holiday Inn shows "Standard Room with sofa bed" at €150 vs "Standard Room" at €145
  - Action: Directly book the room with sofa bed

**Execution:**
1. Select hotel on booking platform
2. Choose 3 people occupancy
3. Select the available room type
4. Complete booking with refundable rate (if available) to allow flexibility

**Advantages:**
- Simplest approach - no negotiation or voucher coordination needed
- Immediate confirmation
- Transparent pricing

**Disadvantages:**
- Only works when hotels price family rooms reasonably
- Not applicable for hotels with large price differentials

### Strategy 2: Pre-Booking Negotiation for Suite Upgrade

**When to use:**
- Price difference is moderate to high (€50-€200+/night)
- Hotel has available suites or premium rooms
- You want to book a base room but negotiate a free or discounted upgrade to a suite/larger room
- Hotel has low occupancy or you're booking during off-peak period

**How it works:**
Instead of booking directly, call the hotel's reservation line BEFORE making a booking. Tell them you are considering booking their hotel and will commit to booking within the next hour if they agree to upgrade you to a suite at no additional charge (or at a reduced rate).

**Why this works:**
- Hotels have incentive to fill rooms, especially suites which often have lower occupancy
- By NOT booking yet, you signal that they could lose your business to a competitor
- Calling before booking gives hotel staff flexibility to offer deals not available through booking platforms
- This strategy leverages timing: you're offering immediate booking commitment in exchange for upgrade

**Execution:**

1. **Identify target hotel and dates** from discovery process
2. **Find hotel direct phone number** (not booking platform, not IHG central reservations—the hotel's own front desk or reservations line)
3. **Call during business hours** (morning preferred, when front desk has time to accommodate requests)
4. **Script for conversation:**
   ```
   "Hello, I am planning to visit [City] from [Date] to [Date], traveling with my family (2 adults and [number] children). I am considering booking your hotel, but I need a room that can accommodate us comfortably. I see you have suites available. If you can upgrade me to a suite at the standard room rate, I will book with you within the next hour. Can you accommodate this request?"
   ```
5. **Negotiate if needed:**
   - If they say yes: Great! Ask for a confirmation number or reference to include in your booking
   - If they say partial discount: Evaluate if the reduced suite price is acceptable
   - If they say no: Thank them, hang up, try next hotel on your list

6. **Book immediately if they agree:**
   - Complete booking through hotel direct website or over phone
   - Reference the agreement in booking notes or confirmation call

**Success factors:**
- **Timing**: Booking well in advance or during low-demand periods improves negotiation leverage
- **Politeness**: Frame as request, not demand - hotel staff have discretion
- **Immediate commitment**: Offering to book "within the hour" creates urgency and signals serious intent

**Example from transcript:**
> "We don't book it. Instead, we give them a call and say that we are considering booking a hotel, if you can upgrade us, then we will book it in next hour."

**Advantages:**
- Can secure suite upgrades at standard room prices
- Works especially well during low-demand periods
- Hotel-direct booking may offer better cancellation terms than OTA platforms

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires phone calls (time-consuming if trying multiple hotels)
- Success rate varies by hotel policy, occupancy, and staff discretion
- Not guaranteed - may need to try multiple hotels

### Strategy 3: IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade Voucher

**When to use:**
- Traveling with IHG Platinum status and have Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher available
- Price difference between base room and suite is very high (€100-€350+/night)
- Stay is 2-5 nights (voucher maximum is 5 nights per use)
- Hotel is an IHG brand participating in the Confirmable Suite Upgrade program

**How it works:**
Book a base room at standard rate, then call IHG Customer Care 14 days before check-in to apply the Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher. IHG Customer Care coordinates with the hotel to guarantee a suite upgrade (subject to suite availability).

**Critical requirements:**
- **Voucher limit**: Maximum 5 nights per voucher use
- **Advance booking window**: Must call IHG Customer Care between 14 days before check-in and 24 hours before check-in
- **Refundable rate required**: Book a refundable rate (in case upgrade is not available and you want to cancel)
- **Participating brands only**: Not all IHG brands participate (see Terms & Conditions for exclusions)

**When to prioritize this strategy:**
- Voucher savings are significant (€100+/night price difference)
- Stay duration maximizes voucher value (4-5 nights better than 2 nights)
- No better alternative exists (e.g., no direct-bookable family rooms available)

**Example from Vienna/Budapest comparison:**
```
Vienna InterContinental:
- Base: €162/night → Suite: €513/night
- Price difference: €351/night × 2 nights = €702 total savings

Budapest Crowne Plaza:
- Base: €354/night → Upgrade: €412/night
- Price difference: €58/night × 2 nights = €116 total savings

Decision: Use voucher in Vienna (€702 savings) vs Budapest (€116 savings)
```

**Execution:**

1. **Verify voucher availability** in IHG Rewards Wallet
2. **Book base room** with refundable rate at target IHG hotel
   - Select dates (maximum 5 nights)
   - Choose base room type (NOT the suite—book the cheapest eligible room)
3. **Wait 14 days before check-in**, then call IHG Customer Care:
   - Phone number: Found in IHG Rewards Wallet when you click "Redeem" on the voucher
   - Provide booking confirmation number
   - Request suite upgrade using Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher
4. **IHG Customer Care confirms upgrade**:
   - They coordinate with hotel to reserve a suite
   - You receive confirmation that upgrade is guaranteed
5. **If upgrade fails** (rare, but possible if no suites available):
   - Voucher is returned to your Rewards Wallet within 5 business days
   - Cancel booking if refundable rate, or accept base room

**Advantages:**
- Guaranteed upgrade (unlike Strategy 2 which is negotiation-based)
- Massive savings possible (€200-€350/night on suite upgrades)
- Works even during high-demand periods (unlike negotiation which works best during low demand)

**Disadvantages:**
- Limited vouchers (typically 2 per year from Milestone Rewards)
- Voucher is valuable—using it on a 2-night stay with small price differential (€50/night) is wasteful
- Requires advance planning (14-day window, not suitable for last-minute bookings)
- Only works for IHG properties

**Strategic voucher use:**
- Reserve voucher for high-value situations (€150+/night savings)
- Prefer longer stays (4-5 nights) to maximize total savings
- Compare across cities and hotels before committing voucher to one booking

## Decision Framework: Selecting the Right Strategy

Use this decision tree to determine which strategy to apply:

```
START: Need accommodation for 3+ people

├─ Is the 3-person room priced within €20 of the 2-person room?
│  ├─ YES → Strategy 1: Direct Booking
│  │         (Example: Holiday Inn €145 vs €150)
│  │         ✓ Simple, immediate booking
│  │
│  └─ NO → Price difference is €20+/night
│     │
│     ├─ Do you have IHG Platinum status + available Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher?
│     │  ├─ YES → Is this an IHG hotel?
│     │  │        ├─ YES → Calculate voucher value:
│     │  │        │        (Price difference) × (Number of nights) = Total savings
│     │  │        │
│     │  │        │        Is total savings €200+?
│     │  │        │        ├─ YES → Strategy 3: IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade
│     │  │        │        │        (Example: €351/night × 2 = €702 savings)
│     │  │        │        │        ✓ Best value for voucher
│     │  │        │        │
│     │  │        │        └─ NO → Strategy 2: Pre-Booking Negotiation
│     │  │        │                 (Save voucher for higher-value use)
│     │  │        │
│     │  │        └─ NO (non-IHG hotel) → Strategy 2: Pre-Booking Negotiation
│     │  │
│     │  └─ NO (no voucher) → Strategy 2: Pre-Booking Negotiation
│     │
│     └─ If all strategies fail or unsuitable:
│           Consider alternative cities/hotels or accept higher cost
```

## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

### Pitfall 1: Booking Too Quickly Without Checking 3-Person Availability

**Problem:** You search for "Vienna hotels" with 2 people, find a good price, book immediately, then discover the hotel has no 3-person rooms available for your dates.

**Solution:** Always search with 3 people from the start, or verify 3-person room availability before committing to any hotel.

### Pitfall 2: Wasting Voucher on Low-Value Use

**Problem:** You use the IHG Confirmable Suite Upgrade voucher on a 2-night stay with €50/night price difference (€100 total savings), then later discover you needed it for a 4-night stay with €200/night price difference (€800 savings).

**Solution:** Survey all legs of your trip BEFORE using the voucher. Use it where total savings are maximized (price difference × number of nights).

### Pitfall 3: Assuming Price Calendar Reflects Real-Time Availability

**Problem:** Price calendar shows availability for 3 people on Jan 5, but when you try to book, the room is sold out.

**Solution:** Price calendar is indicative, not guaranteed. Verify availability at time of booking. Use refundable rates when possible to allow flexibility if availability changes.

### Pitfall 4: Negotiating AFTER Booking Instead of BEFORE

**Problem:** You book a hotel, then call to request upgrade. Hotel has no incentive to upgrade you because you've already committed (cancellation fees discourage you from canceling).

**Solution:** Always negotiate BEFORE booking. The threat of booking elsewhere is your leverage.

### Pitfall 5: Not Checking Room Type Levels (1 vs 2+ Levels Above Base)

**Problem:** You assume a "Suite" is only 1 level above "Standard Room", but it's actually 2+ levels (Standard → Deluxe → Suite), making negotiation or voucher necessary.

**Solution:** Check room type hierarchy on hotel website. If 3-person room is 2+ levels above base room, expect higher price differential and plan accordingly.
