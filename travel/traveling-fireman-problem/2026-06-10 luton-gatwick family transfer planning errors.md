# Luton-in / Gatwick-out family transfer — planning errors (fireman post-mortem)

When it happened: 2026-06-10 (day one of the conversation; the planning and the trip itself). Written up 2026-06-17. Party: 2 adults + children aged 12 and 6, with luggage. Shape: arrive London Luton ~midday, one night in London, depart London Gatwick the next day on a 16:00 flight. Hotel chosen by the traveller: Holiday Inn Express London-Croydon (East Croydon).

## What happened in reality

The family took a car from Luton to the Croydon hotel. Google Maps routed the driver across central London (through Westminster) on the congested west/central roads. With an airport meal and afternoon traffic the transfer ran to roughly 4.5 hours; they were still ~1 hour out at 16:30 and arrived ~17:30, losing the planned afternoon (the Crystal Palace dinosaurs outing was abandoned). The direct Thameslink, Luton Airport Parkway to East Croydon (~50 minutes, fixed, traffic-immune), would have avoided all of it.

## The fireman pattern, per case

For each: the first answer fastened on, the alternative not held, the disconfirming observation that was already available, the fix.

### 1. Cleared the car for the arrival leg (the costly one)

- Fastened on: "Uber for Luton to the hotel is fine — only ~£15-35 more than the train, and there is no time pressure on arrival."
- Alternative not held: the direct train is cheaper AND faster AND fixed-time. On Luton to Croydon specifically, train ≈ £49 for the family versus car ≈ £80-110 — the car was dearer, not "a bit more," and far slower.
- Disconfirming observation already in context: for the Gatwick leg I had already established that a cross-London car ride is traffic-variable and prone to blowout. I did not apply that same check to the arrival leg.
- Fix: weight time in the same units as money. When one option is fixed-time and the other traffic-variable, lead with that distinction rather than averaging it into "a bit slower." Run the traffic-risk check on every road leg.

### 2. Applied the traffic check asymmetrically

- For the Gatwick departure I correctly warned about M25/M23 congestion and recommended the train against the 16:00 deadline. For the Luton arrival I called the car "defensible, no time pressure." Same hazard — a car crossing congested London — opposite advice, with no stated reason for the asymmetry. The unexamined leg is the one that failed.
- Fix: a hazard identified on one leg must be tested on the symmetric leg before it is cleared. Symmetry is the cheapest disconfirmation available.

## Classified honestly as NOT a fireman error

- In an apology I wrote "the ~£20 you'd save on the car" — inverted; the car cost more, not less. This is a logic/arithmetic slip in self-correction, not a first-cause-without-verification failure. Different fix: re-check the direction of a comparison before stating it, especially inside an apology where the reader is already primed to distrust the numbers.
- The Croydon hotel itself was not an error. By train it is well placed: ~15 minutes to Gatwick, direct Thameslink to Luton, ~15 minutes to central London. The failure was the transfer mode, not the base — and conflating "bad outcome" with "bad hotel choice" would itself be a fireman move.

## Coda: the first writeup repeated the pattern

The first version of this file (2026-06-17) invented a third "error": that planning had been run against a date a week in the past, because the queries used 2026-06-10 while the session date read 2026-06-17. That conclusion was itself a fireman move. The contradicting fact was that this conversation spans seven days: 2026-06-10 was day one and the correct "tonight"; 2026-06-17 is merely the day the writeup was filed. The trip unfolding in real time during the conversation was the disconfirming observation walked past. The lesson holds inside its own post-mortem: before treating two values that disagree as an error, hold the alternative that explains the disagreement, and check it.

## Root pattern

Money was quantified carefully; time and reliability were treated as footnotes. A hazard found on one leg was not carried to the symmetric leg. The anti-fireman discipline (hold at least three candidates, name the observation that would disconfirm the leading one, check it) was run on the Gatwick leg and skipped on the arrival leg — the place the plan broke. The writeup then skipped it again on the date.
