Two kinds of phrase go on the list however near their answer sits. A pronoun or a demonstrative ("it", "this", "that", "they") goes on whenever more than one thing before it could fill it. Grammar picks one noun, but the recipient reaches the pronoun holding the subject of the sentence before and what that sentence was about, so those are candidates too; a pronoun whose noun sits inside a modifier ("the cost of the repair") rather than as a subject always goes on.
