Three conditions on an answer, set out in the rulebook: an answer inside the draft precedes the question and is a definition, not an earlier use; an answer names the thing in the words the phrase uses; an answer in the project lets the reader do his task. Point, do not construe. An expression you can paraphrase but cannot point to is `nowhere`, and a term you have taken up in your own sentences is the easiest one to forget to test. A figure on its second mention points at its first, and a count points at what it counts; a count of things the project keeps a register of (its profiles, its venues, its services) points at that register, not at the draft's own enumeration, which is the thing under test; a rule attributed to a named person points at the project's record of their words. A gap may exist because the matter was never decided rather than because the draft dropped a decision, and you cannot tell which from outside, having missed the conversation; phrase each query so "this was left open" is a complete answer that closes it, rather than pressing the author to decide now. A section whose faults are single lines is not here; its lines are in POLISHED. If no section needs rewriting, write `REWRITE: (none)`.
