# cold-reader

A colleague walks into a working session already under way and says "sorry I'm late, I see you have started." He knows the project and the document produced by it, but not what this conversation has covered leading to the document. Everything written from here has to land for him. When the document is a code diff, he is the maintainer who inherits it, and he does what a maintainer does with comments that will cost him later: cuts them. This skill is in the family of edit-email and edit-economistly.

## Problem this skill exists to solve

This is a document tool: it makes a finished text read for its reader. The reader here is an outsider who has the project but was not in the conversation that produced the text. The product is the document, not the conversation; the skill does not summarise the talk, it makes the text stand on its own. If most of the conversation is irrelevant to the document, none of it belongs in the document.

Linda Flower called the condition writer-based prose and named its marks: words that carry meaning only for the writer, an order that follows the writer's discovery, a frame that is the writer's own. Here the writer's private context is the conversation, which is why a fresh agent that holds the project and not the conversation can stand in for the reader she describes. A draft written in the middle of a conversation carries that conversation into the text three ways, and all read badly to the person who arrived late:

- **Short of context.** The author holds the whole exchange, so the draft leans on it: "Option C", "the approach we agreed", "the current behaviour", a word the writer loaded with a sense the page never gives, a conclusion whose reason was spoken and not written, a change that never says what becomes of what it replaces. The reader cannot reconstruct any of it.
- **Conversation residue.** The draft also carries the conversation's leftovers: an idea raised and abandoned, an alternative weighed and dropped, deliberation replayed, kept only because it happened. A reader who wants the result, not the transcript, has to wade through it.
- **Pitched at an insider.** The draft can hold every fact yet tell it from the seat of someone who walked the conversation: a present framed as a change from a before only an insider knew, a defence of an objection the reader never raised, an order that follows how the conversation found things. To the newcomer it reads as the middle of a talk he never joined.

The author cannot see the scaffolding it carried in, and fixes what it is shown by patching the sentence that was flagged. A coined term sits in every sentence the author wrote while holding it; replaced at the one flagged mention, it survives at the others, and the replacement, coined under the same pressure, is often the next pass's finding. So the reader reports each coined term with its spread and names the sections to rewrite whole, and the author rewrites rather than patches.

A code diff adds a maintenance cost. A mechanism's name sits in the code, then in its own comment, then in a caller's comment, then in a module doc, and a rename becomes shotgun surgery with comments as many of the wounds. Measured on one 34k-line Rust tree over 246 commits: a rename or signature change touched a median 114 lines across 3 files and 13 hunks; 69% of the comment-only hunks in those commits existed only because a comment had spelled the name. Of the comment lines naming a mechanism, 36% were plain repetition; the other 64% were pointers whose value is the reference itself, and one of those was already stale. An AI editing code also adds a comment wherever it touches logic and removes few, so the comment share climbs with every pass over the same lines, and what it adds restates the operation beside it. The author is poorly placed to make these cuts, having written each comment for the reader it imagined; the fresh reader who inherits the code can tell a pointer from decoration and a reason from a restatement, so on a diff this skill gives him edit tools.

The boundary from edit-economistly: its reader is a generalist outside the project who needs entities glossed and acronyms expanded; here the reader is inside the project and needs none of that. The boundary from edit-email runs the other way: its reader holds the email and its thread and no project at all, so this colleague certifies a file path or a scenario name that the recipient of an email could never resolve.

The rules both the author and the colleague work to are in `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/cold-reader/rulebook.md`.

## Procedure

1. The draft is a file on disk, read whole. The colleague reads it by its path, the same way it reads the project, which is why the draft has to be written first. Never paste the draft into the prompt, and never a condensed or excerpted version: the review is only as thorough as what the colleague sees, and a shortened draft silently blinds it to the sections you cut. With `--staged`, the draft is the commit about to be made: run the diff that shows what the commit form will commit (`git diff --cached` for a staged commit; `git diff HEAD -- <paths>` for a pathspec or `--only` commit; `git diff HEAD` for `-a`; a caller such as a commit gate may hand you the exact command) and write its full output to a temp file outside the repo (a scratchpad path), then use that file as the draft. With `--since <ref>`, the draft is everything the project changed after the dividing line: write `git diff <ref>..HEAD` to the temp file the same way, and name `<ref>` as the base in the agent prompt so the colleague knows where the before-state sits.
2. Spawn a fresh-context general-purpose agent with `model: "sonnet"`, leaving its read and edit tools available: it reads the project, whatever form the project takes (a codebase, a document set, a shared body of work), and on a diff it edits the working tree. The prompt template is at `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/cold-reader/editor-prompt.md`; substitute `$RULEBOOK_PATH` with `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/cold-reader/rulebook.md`, `$DRAFT_PATH` with the absolute path to the draft file, and `$READER` with the draft's addressee as the caller names it with `--for` (the owner a page is written for, the contributor an issue is for), or `a colleague who holds the project` when the caller names none. With `--report`, append one line, `The caller invoked --report.`, and spawn the agent with `subagent_type: "Explore"` instead, whose tool set holds no edit tools: the colleague then lists the edits it would make and cannot touch the tree, whatever it reads into the prompt. Use `--report` for a first run on a tree whose house style you have not seen. Add nothing else that telegraphs what you hope it will catch.
3. The agent returns, in order: GIVENS (the pointing list, every expression that presupposes a referent against where the reader gets it, with each `nowhere` term's count and locations; on a diff a VOCAB list closes it), PAGE for a page draft (what the stylesheet does to the reading, the column cap and which tables it confines, and the labels list), READING (the colleague's interpretive write-back, in his own words), POLISHED for prose (line patches, with before and after) or EDITS for a diff (each comment edit applied, or listed under `--report`, as path:line, before, after, rule), QUERIES (gaps only this conversation can close, and on a diff the comments left alone because their value may sit in domain knowledge it lacks), METRICS for a diff (comment lines, names outside their definition site kept or rewritten, comment/code ratio, before and after), and REWRITE for prose (each section to rewrite whole, with the terms that condemn it).
4. **Planner pass (opt-in via `--plan-agent`, for bug reports and feature requests).** Run this pass only when the caller invoked the skill with `--plan-agent`, and only for a plannable draft; it costs a second agent and sometimes a re-run at wider scope, so it stays off by default. Spawn a second fresh-context agent with `model: "sonnet"` using the prompt template at `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/cold-reader/planner-prompt.md`; substitute `$DRAFT_PATH` with the draft's path. Give this agent the same project access as the cold reader. It returns ERRORS (steps that cannot be executed as written) and MISSING (inputs the draft omits that the project already holds). It does not design a solution the draft has not already described. If a finding names a component, repository, or system boundary it could not reach but needs to settle an ownership or correctness claim, re-run the planner pointed at those materials in addition, and bring the cross-boundary gaps that remain back to the caller. Skip the pass for a diff, an email, release notes, and other non-plannable drafts.
5. Read REWRITE and READING first. Compare each interpretation in READING against what you meant: where the colleague's reading diverges from your intent, that is a defect even if no entry or query fired, because the rule-driven checks cannot catch a confident wrong reading. For prose, rewrite each section REWRITE names, as a whole and from the reader's seat, with the coined terms replaced by what they denote at every occurrence; apply POLISHED line by line elsewhere, where you agree. For a diff, review EDITS against `git diff`: the colleague edits by rule and you hold the domain, so revert any edit that cut a reason it took for restatement, and say why in the commit; a C6 finding (a comment stating an invariant a test or assert could hold) is reported, not applied, and promoting it is your call. Resolve QUERIES from your conversation, folding a settled answer into the draft in project terms and marking an open matter open rather than inventing a decision. When the planner pass ran, fold its ERRORS by correcting the draft and its MISSING inputs by adding them; treat the open decisions it surfaces as you would a query.
6. For prose, run the reader again on the rewritten draft, from step 2. The skill ends on a pass whose REWRITE is empty and whose GIVENS carry no `nowhere`, or when you stop it and tell the user why. A rewrite made under the same pressure as the draft coins its own terms, and only a fresh reading finds them.
7. Show the user the revised draft or the edits and metrics, with any cross-boundary gaps the planner surfaced. Sending, publishing, committing and pushing are the caller's acts; this skill does none. When a push gate sent you here, commit the comment edits before rerunning the push: the gate measures the push range, and edits left in the working tree are not in it.

## Why a fresh-context subagent

The authoring role belongs to the caller, which holds the conversation and wrote the comments. A subagent forked inside the same context inherits the same blindness about which sentences are conversation and which are draft, which comments carry weight and which carry only the name. A brand-new agent that can read the project but never saw the conversation is the colleague the draft is for.

## Anti-cheating discipline

Rulebook examples come from no test fixture and no tree the skill has been measured on, so the subagent applies the rule rather than recognising a remembered phrase. The prompt carries the rulebook path, the draft's path and the reader's seat; no hint about the particular draft is leaked in, and the colleague reads the draft in full from its file, so the review covers every section rather than the ones the caller happened to include.
