# MIND — how the market holds one trading name, and whether a movement is the operator's own

**Phases:** Measure, Index, Name, Decide

## What MIND is

MIND answers four questions about one trading name: whether the name is held better or worse than a year earlier, on instruments and in each market the operator targets; whether a movement in that standing is the operator's own or the market's; what the market calls the name against what the operator claims, and for whom; and what the operator elects to change. The four questions map one to one onto the four phases, which is what the letters are for: Measure answers the first, Index the second, Name the third, Decide the fourth. Where a run cannot answer one of the four, it says so in that phase's own words and states what it would take, rather than answering an easier question in the same space.

A run is a reading against a prior reading. Its figures sit beside the same window a year earlier on the same instrument, beside the prior run's figures, and beside a panel of comparable businesses measured the same way, so that a movement is attributed before anything is said about it. A brand review that describes the name on one date can be written without either, and reads well, and cannot tell a change in the operator from a change in the market.

MIND is not a campaign, not a rebrand and not a content plan. It is a measurement discipline around one name, and its actions are the operator's decisions, read at the next run for whether they moved what they were meant to move.

## The unit of study

The unit is one trading name: the name the market uses to seek, rate and talk about the operator, whether or not the operator owns it as a mark. A sub-brand, a product name or a former name is a row inside the run, carried where the market still uses it, and is not a unit of its own until the market seeks it without the parent name attached.

The unit is not the website, the logo, the audience or a product. A website is one of the surfaces the name appears on. A product is what LAMP studies, and its standing in search is reported there. An audience is who holds the name, and the Name phase says which audiences hold it and how, but the audience changes with the offer and the name outlasts both.

An operator trading under two names that the market treats as separate runs two units, each with its own panel and its own series. Two names the market treats as one are one unit, and the run says so and carries both spellings.

## The panel

The panel is the set of comparable businesses whose series a run reads the operator's against. It comes from the campaign's register of comparable businesses, which rates each candidate for closeness on stated criteria and groups the result into bands by closeness, and the run takes the closest band as its panel. The register is the campaign's artefact, revised as a dated file, and this methodology says only what a member needs to carry to be indexable: a name the shared instruments can measure, the market it sells into, the reason it was admitted, and the window from which it counts.

A panel of one is not a panel; it is a second operator, and a movement it shares with the first is as likely two coincidences as one cause. The smallest useful panel is the band the register produces, and where the closest band holds fewer than three members the run takes the next band in as well and says so.

The market each member sells into is what lets Index tell the operator's market from the whole. Members sharing the operator's market move with that market's conditions; members outside it move only with conditions the whole market shares. A register that does not carry this attribute per member cannot support the second attribution, and Index then reports a movement as market-wide or own only.

A member entering or leaving the panel is a seam in the series, dated, and not a deletion. A member that closes, renames or merges stays in the register with its exit dated, so a prior run's panel figure can still be checked against the members that produced it.

## Windows and target markets

A run reads matched windows: the window the operator names and the same window a year earlier, this year against last, or the year to date against the same dates a year earlier. Two windows a year apart hold the same season, so a difference between them is not the season; a window read against any other is read against the season as much as against the market. Where the run's window differs from the prior run's, both are read: the matched window for the year-on-year figure, and the prior run's window for its open decisions.

The markets the operator targets are named in the campaign's instrument list, with the instrument that can separate each: a rank database and a console by country, a review platform by the reviewer's language, a public interest index by region. Measure reports each figure for the whole and for each target market an instrument can separate, and Index reads the panel on the same markets. A target market no instrument can separate is reported as such, and what Name finds about it rests on the market's words rather than on a figure.

## Runs and the methodology

A run is one trading name read once, on one date, over the windows the operator names, in the campaign folder. It is a dated document that carries every figure it compares against, every open decision it reads, and every finding it makes, so that a reader handed the run alone holds the series for what the run uses. The run names its instruments' instances; this methodology names their kinds, and a run's headings call each by its kind name.

A run reads the prior run before it measures anything. That is the first act of Measure and the reason the series exists. A run that measures first and reads later has redrawn a snapshot, whatever it says about trend.

Before a run is published, a fresh agent holding the run and its captures, and nothing else, reads it and writes its findings into the run. An agent told what the run concluded confirms it, so the reader is given neither the brief nor the reasoning. At this version the cold read is a paragraph and its checks are the invariants; it grows a class list, as a section of this document, when a defect has escaped a run, with the run that taught it named.

A run is committed before it is corrected, since correcting in place rests on the earlier text being recoverable. Captures a run makes, results pages, review exports and profile snapshots, go into the operator's capture series under that series' naming, committed, and not into the run's own folder.

## The instruments

An instrument is named here by its kind and in the run by its instance. The kinds fall into three groups by what they measure about the name, and each kind has a reach: it sees the panel and the operator alike, or it sees the operator only. The reach decides what Index can do with a figure, so it is stated with the figure and not left to the reader.

Demand for the name asks whether the name is sought. A rank database reports the volume of queries on the name and its variants and the positions held on them, for any name above its floor, and sees the panel and the operator alike. The operator's search performance console reports impressions, clicks and position on queries carrying the name, by day, and sees the operator only, since it reports only queries on which the operator's own pages appeared. A public interest index reports the name's share of search over time as a normalised figure, sees the panel and the operator alike, and is read beside absolute counts and never combined with them.

Perception of the name asks what is said about it. Review platforms report count, rating, velocity and text, and see the panel and the operator alike. Public discussion, forums, comment threads and replies, sees both unevenly, and an absence there is not evidence. The modifiers the market attaches to the name in queries, what people put after the name when they search for it, are the market's own account of what the name is for, and come from the console for the operator and from the rank database for anyone. The operator's own feedback channels, enquiry language, survey responses and complaints, see the operator only.

Presence of the name asks where the name appears and how it reads there. The operator's own properties and claimed listings, the directories and intermediaries that carry the name, and the social accounts that speak in it, all see the panel and the operator alike, at the depth a public reader can reach for the panel and at full depth for the operator.

Every instrument has a floor, a retention wall and error windows of its own. A rank database reports nothing, or a band, below a volume floor; a name under the floor is reported as under the floor, at most the floor's value, and never as zero, and it enters no mean, share or delta, with the count of what was struck stated beside the aggregate. A name under the floor on one run and above it on the next has crossed a floor, which is not a measured rise, and the row says so.

Where a run shares an instrument with a LAMP run of the same operator, it cites that run's measurement ground as LAMP's movement report defines it (`seo-lamp/lamp-methodology.md`): the retention wall, the error windows with their direction, the tag-break dates and the withheld-row fraction. It reads the known-instrument-defects section of the same document before drawing any window, rather than re-deriving either. Where there is no such run, the first MIND run establishes the ground for the instruments it uses and states it in its own instruments section for later runs to cite.

## The phases

### Measure

Measure opens with the prior run: its figures on each instrument and window, its open decisions with the series each named and its read date, and its findings by number. The first run of a name states that there is no prior run; the window a year earlier is still read wherever an instrument's retention reaches it, and a figure it cannot reach is reported without a movement.

Measure then reads the name on each instrument the campaign's instrument list names, over the matched windows, for the whole and for each target market the instrument can separate, and reports each figure with its instrument kind, its window, its market, its reach, the figure for the same window a year earlier, and the prior run's figure where the prior run's window differs. A figure whose window straddles a seam is split at the seam or not given. A figure the instrument caps or withholds is reported with the cap or the withheld fraction on the same line.

Measure reports and does not attribute. A movement it finds is a movement in the operator's series and nothing more until Index has read the panel.

### Index

Index reads the panel on the same instruments, windows and markets Measure used, member by member, and reports each member's figure with the same instrument, window, market and reach beside the operator's. A member under an instrument's floor is reported under the floor. An instrument that sees the operator only produces no panel column, and the operator's figure on it is reported under an unindexed heading, which is all Name may then say about it.

Index then attributes each movement in the operator's series. The panel moved with the operator, in the same direction and of a comparable size, and the movement is market-wide. The members sharing the operator's market moved and the others did not, and the movement belongs to the operator's market. The operator moved and the panel did not, and the movement is the operator's own. A movement the panel splits on, or one the floor hides, is reported as unattributed with the observation that would settle it and who can make it.

Index also reports the operator's standing among the panel on each instrument that sees both, as a rank and a share for each window and market, so that better or worse is stated against the panel as well as against the year before, Name has a position to speak from, and the next run has one to move.

### Name

Name states what the market calls the name, against what the operator claims, and for whom, as numbered findings that cite their evidence. Its findings fall under five heads, each a finding rather than a description: positioning and differentiation, what the name stands for in the market's words against the operator's own; the competitor framing, which names the market puts beside this one and on what axis; the audience segments, who holds the name and how each holds it; presence by channel, where the name appears and how it reads there against the panel; and reputation themes, what the reviews and the discussion say and how that has moved.

The evidence for a finding is the run's own measurements and captures, the market's words in queries and reviews, and the operator's claims as published. A finding drawn from the model's own account of the market, with nothing in the run behind it, is marked as opinion, and a finding the prior run also made says whether it moved.

Name ends the run. It numbers the findings, marks each as moved, unmoved or new, and chooses nothing. What to do about a finding is decided at Decide, and a run that ends by recommending has taken that phase from the operator.

### Decide

Decide is the operator's phase. The operator reads the findings and elects what to change, and AI clerks the table: one row per decision, carrying the finding number it answers, the series that would show it worked, the direction and size expected, and the date it can first be read. A decision made between runs, on a finding of the last run, is a dated decision document in the campaign folder, in the same shape.

The next run's Measure reads each open decision by the series it named and reports it as moved as expected, moved otherwise, not yet readable with the read date, or unreadable, where the decision named no series or a seam now blocks the window. A decision that cannot be read is a finding of that run's Measure, not a footnote.

## Seams and floors

A seam is a dated event after which a series cannot be averaged with its own past: a name change, a platform migration, a review import, an account merge, a change in what an instrument counts, a panel member entering or leaving. A run lists its seams with dates and charts them as lines. No mean, share or before-and-after figure straddles one; the figure is split at the seam or not given.

A floor crossing is a seam of the instrument's making. A name that was under a rank database's floor and is now above it has a first measured figure, not a rise, and the row says which.

## Model allocation

| Phase | Tier | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Measure | Sonnet-tier, under the campaign's instrument list | Instrument pulls and table-making under stated rules, with the seams and floors already named |
| Index | Sonnet-tier, under the register | The same pulls for each member, and an attribution that follows from the table; a split panel is reported, not resolved |
| Name | Opus-tier | Reading the market's words against the operator's claims, and holding a finding at opinion where the run has nothing behind it, is where the run's value is made or lost |
| Decide | Human, with AI clerking | The operator elects; the clerk keeps each row readable at the next run |
| Cold read | Fresh context, tier set by what the run will be used to decide | The invariant checks are mechanical; the defect outside them is not |

## Versioning

A run states its date, and the date fixes which reading of this document governed it.

A run is a dated document beside the earlier runs, and the series is the set of them. There is no standing series file appended each cycle, because such a file is cited by figure and rewritten every run, which strands each citation. Instead each run restates, in its own tables, every prior figure and every open decision it compares against, so a reader handed one run holds the series for what that run uses. That restatement is a merge requirement: a run reads the prior run in full before writing, and carries forward what its new work does not supersede.

The register and the instrument list are cited by figure and are versioned, a new dated file beside the old. A run is corrected in place: the same file, the same name, and nothing in it pointing at what it said before, committed before it is corrected so the earlier text is recoverable.

No document assumes an order of reading. A reader opens whichever one was handed over, and no run rests on another having been read first, the prior run included.

## Relationship to the other methodologies

LAMP (`seo-lamp/lamp-methodology.md`) and MIND divide the series between them. LAMP measures whether the market can find one product and what finding it is worth, and reports a page's brand share beside the product's standing without folding it in. MIND measures how the trading name above the products is sought, spoken of and displayed, and reads it against a panel. Where the two share an instrument for one operator, LAMP's movement report holds the measurement ground and MIND cites it.

The claims Name tests the market's words against are the ones SAGE (`pmf-sage/sage-methodology.md`) ruled when it established the offer and PLACE (`market-place/place-methodology.md`) recorded when it positioned what was built; where a claim has a home there, Name cites it rather than restating it. A finding of Name about who holds the name, and how, is what a SPAR campaign's segments are drawn from when the operator goes to market.
